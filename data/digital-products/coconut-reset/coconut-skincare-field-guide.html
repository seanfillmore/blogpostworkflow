The ingredient

Why cold-pressed coconut oil

Not all coconut oil is equal. The difference between what we use and what most brands use comes down to one word: refining.

Cold-pressed & virgin (what we use)

Pressed from fresh coconut without heat or solvents. Keeps its lauric acid, vitamin E, and antioxidants intact — the parts that make it good for skin. It has a real, faint coconut scent because it's a real, whole ingredient.

Refined or fractionated (what's common)

Bleached, deodorized, or chemically split for a longer shelf life and no smell. Cheaper and more stable — but stripped of most of what made coconut oil worth using on skin in the first place.

What that means for your skin

  • Lauric acid — a fatty acid that helps support the skin's natural barrier.
  • Deep, non-greasy moisture — coconut oil is an emollient that softens and smooths rather than just sitting on top.
  • Pairs with organic jojoba — jojoba mimics skin's own sebum, so the lotion absorbs fast without a heavy film.
  • Organic beeswax in the cream — a breathable seal that holds moisture in overnight, unlike petrolatum which suffocates.
A quick note on texture: cold-pressed coconut oil firms up below 76°F. If your lotion or cream feels solid in winter, that's the real ingredient behaving normally — just warm a little between your palms first.

Use it right

Patch test, then layer

Two minutes of setup protects reactive skin and gets you the best results from your reset.

How to patch test (do this first)

  1. Apply a small amount of the lotion to the inside of your wrist or behind your ear.
  2. Wait 24 hours. Don't wash the area or add other products.
  3. Check for redness, itching, or stinging. None? You're clear to use it anywhere. Then repeat with the cream.

Lotion vs. cream — when to use each

Lotion → daytime

Mornings, after showers, after handwashing. Absorbs in minutes, no residue under clothes.

Cream → nighttime

Dry patches, hands, feet, elbows, knees. Heavier repair layer that works while you sleep.

Common questions

Can I use this on my face?

Many customers do — it's fragrance-free and non-irritating. Patch test first, and use the lighter lotion rather than the cream around the eyes.

Is it safe during pregnancy or on kids?

Both formulas are made without fragrance, parabens, phenoxyethanol, or essential oils. Many parents use the lotion on children. We can't give medical advice — check with your provider if you're unsure.

Will coconut oil clog my pores?

On body skin this is rarely an issue. If you're acne-prone on the face, patch test a small area and see how your skin responds over a few days before applying more widely.
