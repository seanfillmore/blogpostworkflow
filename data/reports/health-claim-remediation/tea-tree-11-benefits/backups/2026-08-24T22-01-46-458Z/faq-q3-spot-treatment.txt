<h2>History &amp; Origin of Tea Tree Oil</h2>
<p>The <em>Melaleuca alternifolia</em> tree thrives in the coastal regions of New South Wales and Queensland. Indigenous Bundjalung people crushed its leaves to inhale the healing vapors for coughs and colds, or applied them directly to the skin for healing. European settlers learned of its uses in the 18th century, and by the 1920s, Australian chemists were producing tea tree oil commercially due to its potent antiseptic properties.</p>

<h2>How Does Tea Tree Oil Work?</h2>
<p>Tea tree oil contains more than 100 active compounds, but terpinen-4-ol is its star player. This compound disrupts the cell membranes of certain bacteria and fungi, leading to their breakdown. At the same time, it reduces inflammatory markers in the skin, making it effective for soothing irritation, redness, and swelling. Tea tree oil is also an antioxidant, helping protect the skin from environmental stressors that can damage the skin barrier.</p>
