Plant based toothpaste is toothpaste formulated with botanically derived, often organic or food-grade ingredients instead of synthetic chemicals, petroleum-derived compounds, or animal byproducts. It's part of a broader shift toward cleaner personal care routines — one that's moved from skincare and haircare to the bathroom sink. And for good reason. What goes in your mouth matters just as much as what goes on your skin.

This post covers exactly what you need to know: what plant-based toothpaste is made of, why those ingredients work, what to look for on the label, and how to choose the right formula for your needs.

What Is Plant-Based Toothpaste?

Plant-based toothpaste replaces synthetic surfactants, artificial flavors, petroleum-derived thickeners, and animal-derived ingredients with plant-sourced alternatives. Common animal-derived ingredients in conventional toothpaste include glycerin rendered from animal fat and carmine (a red dye from insects). Plant-based formulas skip all of that.

This makes plant-based toothpaste a subset of "natural toothpaste" — but more specific. Not all natural toothpastes are plant-based. Some may still contain animal-derived glycerin or other non-vegan ingredients. Fully plant-based formulas, by definition, are also vegan.

Many plant-based toothpastes are also organic and fluoride-free, but neither is a requirement. A formula can be plant-based and still contain fluoride, or it can be fluoride-free but not fully plant-derived. The label matters. Knowing what to look for helps you choose with confidence.

Key Plant-Based Ingredients and What They Do

This is where plant-based toothpaste earns its place. The ingredients below are commonly found in quality natural toothpaste formulas. Each one comes from a plant source and brings a specific function to the formula.

  • Coconut oil: Pressed from the meat of the coconut palm, coconut oil has been used in traditional oral care practices for centuries — most notably oil pulling in Ayurvedic medicine. Studies suggest it may help reduce plaque-causing bacteria in the mouth. It also acts as a natural base that carries other ingredients without synthetic binders. Our coconut oil toothpaste uses organic virgin coconut oil as its primary active base.
  • Baking soda (sodium bicarbonate): A mild natural abrasive derived from the mineral trona, baking soda helps scrub away surface stains without damaging enamel. It also neutralizes the acids in your mouth that can erode teeth over time. Research indicates it's one of the most effective natural whitening agents available in toothpaste.
  • Peppermint and spearmint essential oil: Steam-distilled from the leaves of the mint plant, these oils do two things: freshen breath naturally and provide mild antimicrobial properties. They're a clean alternative to synthetic mint flavoring. Our formula includes both organic peppermint and spearmint essential oil — you can explore the full range in our mint toothpaste collection.
  • Myrrh: Harvested from the resin of the Commiphora tree, myrrh has been used in traditional oral hygiene for thousands of years. It's believed to support gum health and has historically been used to soothe irritated mouth tissue. Our formula uses wildcrafted myrrh powder — meaning it's harvested from wild plants rather than farmed.
  • Xylitol: Derived from birch bark or corn cobs, xylitol is a natural sweetener that does more than make toothpaste palatable. Studies suggest it inhibits the growth of Streptococcus mutans — the bacteria most associated with tooth decay. It's one of the most well-researched plant-derived dental ingredients available. Stevia, another plant-derived sweetener (from the stevia leaf), is used in our formula in place of saccharin or artificial sweeteners.
  • Herbal extracts (neem, tea tree, clove): These plant-derived ingredients have a long history in herbal toothpaste traditions. Neem has been used in Ayurvedic oral care for centuries. Tea tree oil is traditionally recognized for its antibacterial properties. Clove essential oil, derived from the flower bud of the clove tree, is used in our formula and has a centuries-old association with oral care. What to Look for When Choosing a Plant-Based Toothpaste

Use this checklist when reading a label. These are the markers of a complete, well-formulated plant-based toothpaste — not just one with a few natural-sounding ingredients bolted onto a synthetic base.

  1. Plant-derived base: Look for coconut oil, aloe vera, or plant-based glycerin as the primary carrier. Avoid petroleum-derived ingredients or animal-rendered glycerin.
  2. SLS-free: If you have sensitive gums or a history of mouth sores, this is non-negotiable. Check the full ingredient list — it's sometimes listed as "sodium laureth sulfate" as well.
  3. Natural sweetener: Look for xylitol or stevia instead of saccharin, aspartame, or other synthetic sweeteners.
  4. Vegan-verified: Not all "natural" toothpastes are vegan. If that matters to you, look for explicit vegan certification or check the ingredients directly. Our vegan toothpaste collection is a good place to start.
  5. Intentional fluoride-free formulation: If you're choosing fluoride-free, look for formulas that compensate with xylitol and antimicrobial botanicals rather than simply omitting fluoride with nothing in its place.
  6. Organic certification: USDA Certified Organic (or equivalent) means the ingredient sourcing has been independently verified. "Natural" has no regulated definition — "organic" does.
  7. Gentle abrasive: Baking soda is one of the gentlest and most effective natural abrasives. It polishes without scratching enamel. Avoid formulas with aggressive synthetic silica as the primary abrasive if you have enamel concerns.

Is Plant-Based Toothpaste Safe for the Whole Family?

For most adults and older children, yes — plant-based toothpaste is a safe and often gentler alternative to conventional formulas. The absence of SLS and artificial additives makes it a preferred choice for many parents.

For infants and toddlers, it's a different conversation. Some formulas include essential oils — like peppermint, spearmint, or clove — that may not be appropriate for very young children. If you're choosing a natural infant toothpaste, look for formulas specifically designed for that age group and check with your pediatric dentist, especially if you have questions about fluoride for your child's specific situation.

The short version: plant-based toothpaste is generally a good fit for families, but age-appropriate formulas and professional guidance matter for the youngest members of the household.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is plant-based toothpaste as effective as regular toothpaste?

Yes, when formulated with proven ingredients like baking soda, xylitol, and antimicrobial botanicals. The key is a complete formula, not just one or two natural ingredients added to a synthetic base. Look at the full ingredient list, not just the front label claims.

Does plant-based toothpaste whiten teeth?

Many formulas include baking soda, a mild abrasive that helps remove surface stains over time. It won't bleach teeth, but it supports a naturally cleaner appearance with regular use. For more, see our guide to coconut whitening toothpaste.

Is plant-based toothpaste the same as vegan toothpaste?

They overlap significantly. Plant-based toothpaste uses botanically sourced ingredients, which by definition excludes animal-derived ones — making it vegan. That said, not everything labeled "vegan" is fully plant-based. Always check the ingredient list to be sure.

Can I use plant-based toothpaste if I have sensitive teeth?

Many people find plant-based formulas gentler because they skip SLS and harsh synthetic abrasives. Ingredients like coconut oil and myrrh are traditionally associated with soothing gum tissue. If sensitivity is a concern, consult your dentist to find the best fit for your situation.
