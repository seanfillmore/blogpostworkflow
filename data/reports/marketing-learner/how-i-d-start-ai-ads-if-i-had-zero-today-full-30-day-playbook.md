# How I'd start AI ads if I had zero today (Full 30-Day Playbook)

**Creator:** Bogdan (@Bogzabs96)  
**Source:** social post — `how-i-d-start-ai-ads-if-i-had-zero-today-full-30-day-playbook`  
**Published:** 2026-11-05  
**Inferred era cues:** Published 2026-11-05. Names GPT Image 2 (via Higgsfield), Kling, Suno, Claude Code skills, and Canva. Refers to an April-to-September (2026) ramp and a 30 September parallel run. Says photoreal 'soap opera' AI is not yet ready. All are current platform and model capability claims.  

An agency operator who went from 26 to 111 AI ad concepts a month lays out a four-week on-ramp for a brand running no AI ads. Every AI ad starts from an already-proven message, and AI changes only the visual. Week 1 is a fast static session of native statics, competitor clones and us-vs-them, built with GPT Image 2, Claude captions and Canva fixes. Week 2 remakes the best existing script word-for-word as a Pixar-style animated ad through a Claude Code skill. Week 3 adds a second visual style and a Suno song ad, with specific rules for scripting and prompting it. Week 4 turns this into a system: parallel production, a per-ad cost log, per-step timing, and remaking winners in new looks. He publishes real per-ad generation costs, from about $7 to $240 with an average of about $30. He warns against skipping a 'what good looks like' reference set, starting with photoreal AI drama, leaving automations unwatched, and making ads look AI for its own sake.

Found 18 tactics: 9 adopted, 9 rejected.

## Adopted

### Run AI song ads (Suno). Write a normal direct-response script with no rhymes and let the music model turn it into a song. — 7/10

**Why it works:** A sung ad reads as entertainment and is sticky, while the unrhymed DR script keeps every selling point and claim intact. Forcing rhymes would bend the copy away from what sells.

**Evidence:** One song ad for a new client went from $500 to over $12K spend in 3 days and passed the account's ROAS target within 5 days. Song ads are now their most-produced AI format. Generation costs about $24-29 per ad.

**Fit:** This format is new to every existing skill. It suits a low-ticket, everyday consumable (deodorant, lotion, lip balm), where a catchy sung benefit fits the category. At about $25-30 per ad it costs less than a day of RSC's ~$50/day test budget. The operator can produce it alone today with existing Trybe UGC or AI visuals. Launching it in the account is a recommendation to David Parrotino. The evidence is a single vendor case, which keeps the score off the top of the range. It is a platform-mechanics-adjacent format, current as of November 2026.

**Target skill:** `marketing-ai-voiceover-production` (edit)

### Choose the animation style to match the gravity of the problem the ad addresses. Pixar-style carries emotion for a serious problem. A health problem rendered in Lego reads as a joke. — 6/10

**Why it works:** The visual style sets the emotional register before a word lands. A style that undercuts the problem's seriousness makes the claim feel unserious and kills belief.

**Evidence:** Their own pattern: Pixar is 'usually the style that works best for us' on serious problems.

**Fit:** RSC already runs AI-generated video (Gemini Omni), so picking a style is a live decision. Skin and odour problems span light (sweaty commute) to tender (eczema-prone dry skin), so the gravity-to-style match matters per angle. This is a durable principle, and the operator can run it today.

**Target skill:** `marketing-ai-video-ad-production` (edit)

### Treat the approved brief as locked. When the AI pipeline splits a script into scenes and prompts, it must never rewrite the copy. — 6/10

**Why it works:** The whole bet rests on reusing a proven message. Any wording the model 'improves' during scene breakdown silently turns a one-variable test into a two-variable one.

**Evidence:** assertion only (stated as a rule of their SOP)

**Fit:** This is the guardrail that makes re-rendering a Trybe UGC winner's script in a new AI style a clean test. It applies to any skill-driven pipeline the operator runs. It is a durable principle and runnable now.

**Target skill:** `marketing-ai-video-ad-production` (edit)

### Before anything is animated, check the generated characters and frames against the strategic details in the brief. A senior dog that must look senior, or a 60-year-old lead who renders as 35, decides whether the ad wins. — 6/10

**Why it works:** The buyer's self-recognition depends on the on-screen subject matching their age and situation. Image models drift toward young, generic subjects, and catching that at the still-frame stage is cheap while catching it after animation is expensive.

**Evidence:** Their own failed and corrected examples (the senior dog, the 60-year-old lead).

**Fit:** RSC's body-cream buyers skew older than models' default subjects, so age and skin-condition fidelity in AI frames is a real risk. Existing claims settle the storyboard but do not name this demographic-fidelity gate. It is a durable principle and runnable now.

**Target skill:** `marketing-ai-video-ad-production` (edit)

### In a song ad, the hook must open the first verse, and the style prompt must end with 'vocals start immediately, no instrumental intro', because the model sings intros softly. — 6/10

**Why it works:** Music models default to an instrumental lead-in and soft opening vocals. That spends the first seconds of a feed placement, where the scroll decision is made, on nothing audible.

**Evidence:** The team's production rules from their Suno ads (assertion from practice).

**Fit:** A precise and cheap fix that decides whether a song ad survives its first two seconds in Reels and feed, where RSC posts daily. It is platform mechanics (fast decay): a model-behaviour quirk that is current now and should be re-checked as Suno versions change. It is runnable today.

**Target skill:** `marketing-ai-voiceover-production` (edit)

### Keep a production log with one row per ad code: image cost, clip cost, rework cost, total, and start/end time of each step. Review it weekly to catch automations silently burning credits. — 6/10

**Why it works:** Without per-ad cost and time there is no answer to 'is AI actually cheaper?' and no input to a creative-return benchmark. The weekly review catches runaway auto-generation, which once burned their credits unnoticed.

**Evidence:** Their published cost log (from $6.67 to $239.59 per ad) and an auto-generation setting that burned credits until someone caught it.

**Fit:** RSC runs a pay-per-generation AI pipeline (Gemini Omni video, the ad-batch renderer). The existing claim 'attributed revenue per concept divided by the cost to produce it' needs exactly this denominator, and nothing records how to capture it. Per-step timing is the solo operator's own capacity budget. It is a durable principle, runnable today in a spreadsheet.

**Target skill:** `marketing-paid-media-measurement` (edit)

### Do not start with photoreal AI drama. Start with stylised formats (Pixar-style, claymation, songs), because realistic AI drama comes back with jarring cuts and robotic voices and needs heavy rework. — 6/10

**Why it works:** Current video models fail most visibly at realistic human acting and dialogue. Viewers forgive stylised worlds, while they read photoreal flaws as fake, so stylised formats ship with far less rework per dollar.

**Evidence:** Their realistic AI drama ad cost $239.59, versus about $20-30 for Pixar and Suno ads, and needed about 45% rework.

**Fit:** RSC's Gemini Omni output includes dialogue scenes, so this tells the operator where to point first attempts. It is platform mechanics (fast decay) and reflects November 2026 model capability, so it should be revisited as models improve. It is runnable today.

**Target skill:** `marketing-ai-video-ad-production` (edit)

### Route any static or starting frame that shows your product to the image model that renders the packaging label correctly most often (currently GPT Image 2). Use the LLM only for captions and Canva only for the last 10% fix. — 5/10

**Why it works:** A wrong label is the most common reason a product render cannot ship. Picking the model on label fidelity cuts regeneration and correction rounds. A static dropped from 90 minutes and three revision rounds to minutes.

**Evidence:** The team's own before/after production time (90 minutes and three rounds down to minutes). The label-accuracy claim is their observation.

**Fit:** RSC's ad-batch pipeline renders statics of labelled deodorant, lotion and balm tins, where legible on-pack text matters. This is the platform-mechanics class (fast decay). The model ranking is current as of November 2026 but should be re-checked within months. It is runnable today by the operator.

**Target skill:** `marketing-ai-product-imagery` (edit)

### Run a strip-away test on every AI ad: remove the animation and ask whether it is still a real ad. If a phone could have shot the hook, the AI version needs a reason to exist, because cute is not proof. — 5/10

**Why it works:** AI novelty can mask a weak message. Stripping the visual exposes whether the script carries a selling argument, and the phone test stops credits being spent where cheaper real footage would read as more credible.

**Evidence:** assertion only

**Fit:** Useful as a pre-ship gate, since RSC has abundant approved Trybe UGC that could carry many hooks a phone could shoot. It partly overlaps with 'Shoot the real footage you can actually get, then use AI only for shots you could not capture', but it adds a ship/no-ship audit of the finished ad. It is a durable principle and runnable now.

**Target skill:** `marketing-ai-video-ad-production` (edit)

## Rejected

**Fit reasoning:** Sound and directly applicable to RSC's Trybe UGC winners, but the claim is already recorded.

**Fit reasoning:** Relevant, but already recorded.

**Rejected because:** Duplicate of marketing-short-form-video-production 'Record by modelling a reference video that already crushed in the exact format you are making' and marketing-ai-product-imagery 'build the reference board from ads that are already converting'.

**Fit reasoning:** Sensible, but already recorded.

### In week 1, put three people in a room for an hour or two, make 20-30 statics across 4-5 angles, launch them all, and read which angle gets the cheapest clicks. — 4/10

**Rejected because:** Duplicate, and in conflict with recorded rules. It matches marketing-paid-creative-testing 'Start creative testing with static ads rather than video' and the raw-volume native statics claim. It conflicts with marketing-paid-media-measurement 'never treat click-through rate as a performance verdict' and with marketing-paid-creative-testing 'Stop continuously launching new ads alongside a campaign that is already running'.

**Fit reasoning:** Producing statics fast is already covered. The read it prescribes is cheapest clicks, which the existing measurement skill explicitly bans as a performance verdict. Launching 20-30 at once also runs into the recorded doom-cycle rule.

**Rejected because:** Duplicate of marketing-paid-creative-testing's eight named native formats (candid photo, us-vs-them). Cloning a competitor static with your product swapped in contradicts marketing-ai-product-imagery 'never upload a competitor's asset and ask the model to swap your face or product into it'. The permitted version, recreating an out-of-category ad, is already recorded.

**Fit reasoning:** The native and us-vs-them parts are already held. The competitor-clone part crosses a recorded line.

**Rejected because:** No distinct mechanism. It restates the outputs of the other tactics as a planning conclusion, and its measurement inputs are covered by the adopted production-log tactic and the existing creative-return benchmark in marketing-paid-media-measurement.
