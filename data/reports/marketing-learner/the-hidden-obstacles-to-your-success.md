## Adopted

### When you are about to raise the price or withdraw an offer, announce it to existing customers and tell them explicitly that they are grandfathered in at their current rate. — 5/10

**Why it works:** The same announcement does two jobs at once: for prospects it puts a real deadline on the current price, and for existing customers it converts a price rise — normally a churn event — into a loyalty reward. The locked rate becomes an asset the customer forfeits by cancelling, so commitment lengthens rather than shortens at exactly the moment a price change would otherwise trigger a review of the subscription.

**Evidence:** Demonstrated, not taught, and stated twice in the same passage: 'we're going to be removing my reports from Founder's Club shortly (don't worry you'll still get them - you're grandfathered in)... we're going to be offering them at a much higher price... once again you're grandfathered in.' Assertion only, no retention or conversion figures.

**Fit:** Durable-principle class (retention and pricing psychology), so 2010 is irrelevant. Directly relevant because marketing-offer-construction already prescribes stepping prices upward in committed increments on Shopify, and this names what to do at the moment that step lands: email the subscribe-and-save base to say the new price applies to new subscribers only and their rate is locked. Free, one email, solo-executable, aimed at the binding retention constraint. Capped at 5 because it is close kin to the milestone-rate rule already in marketing-retention-offers — the additive part is narrow (the trigger is a price-change event rather than a tenure milestone, and the announcement itself is the retention mechanism) — because the recurring base here is small, and because the urgency half only works if the increase is genuinely honoured, the same failure marketing-scarcity-urgency-framing warns about.

**Target skill:** `marketing-retention-offers` (edit)

### Before accepting or acting on any claim from an outside source, run it through an explicit written skepticism checklist — separate the actual information from what you are inferring, ask whether the speaker has an ulterior motive or hidden agenda, whether there is sufficient evidence, whether this represents the common experience or an isolated occurrence, and whether any part of it is something you want to be true. — 5/10

**Why it works:** Beliefs are acquired passively from whatever you read and watch, and humans draw conclusions automatically and unconsciously, so a single anecdote is silently upgraded into a general rule and a self-interested source is silently trusted. Motive, evidence sufficiency and representativeness are the three places a persuasive claim most often fails, and wishful thinking is what stops you checking — naming the thing you want to be true is what restores skepticism.

**Evidence:** Assertion only — enumerated question lists (eight questions for misleading information, four for wishful thinking, four for jumping to conclusions) plus one illustrative example: wanting to believe others are 'crushing it' at adwords arbitrage. No data, no worked case of the checklist catching a bad claim, and the document itself is a sales piece for the author's own courses.

**Fit:** Durable-principle class (evidence evaluation), so the 2010 date is irrelevant. Free, purely editorial, and it lands on a real recurring failure here: almost every tactic in this library rests on an unquantified practitioner assertion — 'recovers up to 30% of abandoned carts', '50–60% of promotion sales land in the final hours', a 5% budget-scaling step, a $100/day ASC floor — each produced by a creator with an ulterior motive from an account nothing like a $2,700/mo solo operation. 'Is this the common experience or an isolated occurrence?' is exactly the question that stops an imported benchmark being treated as a forecast at ~54 orders/month, and it also governs reading the business's own small-sample numbers. Capped at 5 because it is a vetting habit rather than a revenue mechanism, it partially overlaps the existing discipline of running the arithmetic yourself rather than inheriting a rule of thumb, and the source offers no evidence the checklist works.

**Target skill:** `marketing-performance-pattern-analysis` (edit)

### Expect a delay between cause and effect: correct actions produce 'invisible results' that are undetectable for a long time, so fix the evaluation horizon in advance and do not abandon an activity that is working but has not yet shown feedback. — 5/10

**Why it works:** Most business actions give no immediate signal, so the operator gets no confirmation that a correct decision was correct. Combined with impatience for feedback, this causes people to quit programs that are in fact working, mistaking the absence of a visible result for the absence of a result.

**Evidence:** Analogies rather than business data: heart disease as 25.4% of US deaths, seat belt fatality statistics, and the joke about a man who swam 90% of the English Channel and turned back because he was tired. No account figures, no worked example of a business program abandoned prematurely.

**Fit:** Durable-principle class (feedback timing), age irrelevant. Real translation here rather than pure encouragement: at ~54 orders/month and a sub-1,000 list, the highest-value work — post-purchase and transition-period flows, review accumulation, the subscribe-and-save cadence — produces no readable signal for months, and the standing risk for one operator is killing a flow in week three because nothing visibly moved. The usable form is to fix the evaluation horizon and review date in writing before starting slow-feedback work, so continue-or-stop is decided against a stated condition rather than impatience. Capped at 5 because it heavily overlaps restraint rules already recorded (learning-phase freeze, do-nothing and pre-committed degradation signals, don't-retire-an-angle-prematurely), and because as delivered it is closer to encouragement than mechanism; the additive part is only setting the horizon in advance for non-paid slow-feedback work where no learning phase exists to anchor it.

**Target skill:** `marketing-performance-pattern-analysis` (edit)

### Keep a daily journal as the single place where deliberate thinking happens — recording problems, causes, candidate solutions, chosen plans, goals and the reasons a goal was missed — then scan back over previous entries to spot recurring positive and negative patterns, and use it to hold yourself accountable and process what you are avoiding. — 5/10

**Why it works:** A written record forces thinking to be explicit and makes it retrievable, so past decisions, patterns and misses can be re-read and checked rather than reconstructed from memory; a fixed daily slot converts thinking from occasional to daily. Working alone means nobody else holds you accountable, so the record plus a required reason for every miss is the only accountability mechanism available, and patterns invisible in the moment become visible across the record.

**Evidence:** Twenty years of personal practice plus self-reported life outcomes (grades from D's and F's to summa cum laude, partnerships with Jay Abraham, Gary Halbert, John Carlton, building Strategic Profits) and the claim that the journal birthed the 'YOU' diagram behind the Internet Business Manifesto. Anecdotal and entirely self-reported; no data connecting journaling to any business result.

**Fit:** Durable-principle class (work and decision discipline), so the 2010 date is irrelevant. The honest translation for a solo operator is narrow but genuinely useful: a written marketing decision log — what was changed, on which surface, why, what was expected, what happened, and the reason for any miss. That matters more here than at scale precisely because nothing is statistically readable at ~54 orders/month and a sub-1,000 list, so memory is otherwise the only record, and marketing-performance-pattern-analysis Step 4 warns that 'we tried that' is an unproven verdict until you can say how it was executed. Capped at 5 because it substantially overlaps Step 2's tag-every-asset-at-production-time rule and marketing-product-image-stack's written record of which lever changed on each frame — the additive part is only that the log covers offers, prices, emails and campaign edits too. The personal-development uses (feelings, relationships, self-awareness) have no marketing translation and are not adopted.

**Target skill:** `marketing-performance-pattern-analysis` (edit)
