## Adopted

### Build the opt-in / lead-magnet landing page by prompting an AI website-builder agent with the page's purpose and required elements and telling it to use your existing site styles, then spend ~20 minutes swapping in your real copy and images. — 7/10

**Why it works:** Once a visual identity exists on the site, the agent can inherit type, colour and section styles and generate desktop, tablet and mobile layouts in minutes, collapsing what used to require a designer and a developer into a single prompt — so page production stops being the reason a funnel never gets tested.

**Evidence:** Live demo — the creator types 'I'm making a landing page for a free ebook about interior design, I need a title, subtitle, placeholder for a video, use my existing website styles' and the page builds on screen, including a clarifying question about which calendar link to use; he also regenerates an existing sprint landing page in a Swiss-grid style holding the copy fixed.

**Fit:** Directly on point: a Meta campaign is being stood up to drive soap-giveaway entries, and that campaign needs an entry page. One person can produce and revise it in an afternoon with no designer. Platform-mechanics class, but the tooling described is current (August 2026), so no staleness discount. Existing lead-capture claims cover what the page must contain and the congruency chain but not how to actually produce the asset; the AI-storefront skill is about redesigning the Shopify theme, a different surface.

### Run a tripwire entry offer instead of a pure free lead magnet: sell something at a very low price ($1–$20, or free-plus-shipping), then present a higher-priced order immediately on the next page — it outperforms a free opt-in because a completed transaction breaks the psychological barrier and the card details are already in the system. — 7/10

**Why it works:** Paying anything, however small, converts a browser into a buyer: the trust barrier and the payment-entry friction are both cleared and the buyer has an 'achievement', so the immediately-following larger ask requires no new decision about whether to buy from you and no re-entry of card details — the remaining question is only quantity/upgrade.

**Evidence:** Assertion plus historical pattern examples — 2000s free-plus-shipping book funnels that upsold software and $79 packages on the following page, agencies selling a cheap landing-page build as an entry point, ecommerce loss leaders.

**Fit:** Runnable today: 12 SKUs include cheap units (lip balm, single deodorant) that can act as a paid entry, and Shopify supports a post-purchase offer page. The current front end is a free giveaway entry, so the paid-entry alternative is a live, testable contrast against it with the Meta $30/day campaign already standing up. Durable offer-construction principle, so its age is irrelevant. Overlaps the existing splinter-and-upsell and 'don't re-enter payment' claims, but the distinct assertion — paid beats free at the entry because the transaction barrier is already cleared — is not recorded and changes which front-end offer the ads point at. Low AOV ($50.46) means tripwire margin is thin, so the gain has to come from the second offer, which is exactly what the tactic prescribes.

**Target skill:** `marketing-upsell-offer-design` (edit)

### Keep a running swipe file of landing pages that actually sold you — deliberately click ads, screenshot the pages, save them in a folder — then paste a screenshot into the AI design agent and ask it to recreate that layout with your own product information. — 7/10

**Why it works:** You are stealing a structure that already converted on you rather than inventing one, and the screenshot is a complete brief the design agent can execute directly, collapsing the design step to a paste and a paragraph of product copy.

**Evidence:** The creator demonstrates it live in Framer, including a bento-layout screenshot he handed the agent along with asset placement instructions, which produced the full designed page and rollover effects.

**Fit:** Free, solo-runnable, and immediately applicable: the Meta campaign needs a giveaway entry page and the store needs PDP work, and existing skills already cover briefing an AI design canvas but not where the reference comes from. The existing storefront skill says 'name at least three specific stores whose design aesthetic you want borrowed' — this adds the ongoing capture habit and the screenshot-as-brief mechanism, which is a different action. Translate off Framer to whatever canvas feeds the Shopify theme; the swipe file itself is tool-agnostic.

**Target skill:** `marketing-ai-storefront-design` (edit)

### Every offer should have three distinct copy angles written for it — a human-driven narrative told from your own perspective, an evidence-driven data-backed version, and a direct-response version that names the problem, attaches real numbers, and pitches — and you should be testing those against each other on your pages. — 7/10

**Why it works:** The three angles appeal through different routes (identification, proof, urgency), so covering all three finds which route your market actually buys on instead of betting the page on one.

**Evidence:** Assertion; references his own prior videos on the same three angles.

**Fit:** A concrete generation frame a solo operator can execute today for the giveaway entry page, the deodorant PDP, and the 3-2-2 flexible ad's two primary texts. It is coverage-by-angle-type, which is orthogonal to the existing claims about awareness level and creative jobs, and cheap: three drafts of the same offer, no production cost. Read as directional at ~54 orders/month rather than as a significance-tested winner — pick by which one you can write with real proof behind it.

**Target skill:** `marketing-conversion-copy-angles` (edit)

**Merged from:** part 2 of 2

### Set up the first two emails after any opt-in as fixed easy wins: an immediate email confirming exactly what they signed up for, introducing the person behind it and stating what happens next, then a next-day email that hands over your most popular existing content on that subject. — 7/10

**Why it works:** The immediate email closes the loop the opt-in opened so the subscriber does not feel stranded, and the day-two email delivers real value with zero new production by reusing content that has already proven it holds attention on that topic.

**Evidence:** Assertion — described as 'easy wins' from the creator's own setup.

**Fit:** Directly on the critical path: the Meta campaign is driving giveaway entries right now, and every entrant needs a first-touch sequence or the paid spend buys addresses that go cold. Runnable today in the existing ESP by one person. The confirmation-email half overlaps marketing-email-list-health's opt-in claim, but the day-two rule — seed the second email from your best-performing existing content rather than writing new material — is a distinct, production-free instruction that suits a solo operator.

**Target skill:** `marketing-lifecycle-email-flows` (edit)

### If you are producing content but getting no leads, the missing step is that you are not DMing anyone — go to the profiles of people commenting on your content, qualify them from what their profile shows, and open a personalized conversation referencing what you saw. — 6/10

**Why it works:** Engagers are self-selected, pre-warmed prospects; their public profile supplies a qualifying signal (location, home ownership, employer) and a personal detail, so a one-to-one opener that references it reads as social rather than as a pitch and starts a rapport-building conversation that converts far better than passive posting.

**Evidence:** Assertion plus operator experience — the creator says his Cut 30 program trains thousands of experts and that the 'no leads from content' complaint almost always resolves to zero active DMs; cites a Pacific Northwest roofing company client as the worked example.

**Fit:** Runnable today by a solo operator at zero cost, and it is a genuine acquisition lever with a Meta giveaway campaign about to generate comments and engagement to work. Capped by economics rather than feasibility: at a $50.46 AOV, a hand-typed DM conversation buys a small ticket, so it is a supplement to paid rather than a primary channel. Existing warm-outreach claims cover contact inventories and opener structure but not sourcing prospects from your own content's commenters and qualifying them off their profile.

**Target skill:** `marketing-warm-outreach` (edit)

### Look up the canonical published structure for the format you are writing, load your existing product pitch material into a persistent LLM project, and have it draft against that structure — then re-edit — instead of writing from scratch. — 6/10

**Why it works:** The format's proven skeleton supplies the sequence and your own pitch corpus supplies the substance, so the model is assembling known-good structure over known-true material rather than inventing either.

**Evidence:** Assertion — 'Google video salesletter structure and you will see web pages that break down the exact flow', then paste into a ChatGPT project with your pitches.

**Fit:** Runnable today by one person at zero cost, and directly useful for the long-form assets already on the board — advertorial pre-sell pages for the Meta giveaway campaign, product page rewrites, nurture emails. It sits alongside rather than on top of the existing LLM guardrails (golden-thread audit, section-by-section approval), adding the upstream step those claims assume: where the structure comes from and that the model should be fed a persisted corpus of your real pitch material, not briefed cold.

**Target skill:** `marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats` (edit)

### Test the structural variables of a landing page against each other — long page with more info versus short and direct, light mode versus dark mode, with a video versus without. — 6/10 · parked until `scale`

**Why it works:** These are the page-level variables that change how much a visitor has to process before deciding, and which one wins is market-specific, so you find it by running the variants rather than by reasoning about it.

**Evidence:** Assertion only.

**Fit:** The variable list is sensible and the surface is live — a giveaway entry page is being built for the Meta campaign right now. But an A/B verdict on page length or dark mode needs readable volume, and at ~54 orders/month on $30/day this returns noise; existing guidance already says to ship unmeasured rather than attach a verdict to an underpowered null. Park behind scale: worth running once entry/order volume through a single page is high enough to separate the arms, and until then pick one variant and ship it. Trigger: sustained traffic to one entry page producing enough conversions per arm to read a difference.

**Target skill:** `marketing-lead-capture-landing-pages` (edit)

### Manufacture inbound DM conversations by using Instagram Stories to ask questions and explicitly invite replies ('is anyone looking for X?', 'what's your opinion on Y?'). — 5/10

**Why it works:** A story prompt converts passive viewers into repliers, and every reply lands in the DM inbox as an open thread with an implied conversational opening — so the operator is answering rather than cold-starting, which lowers the social friction on both sides.

**Evidence:** Assertion only.

**Fit:** Free, takes minutes, and a solo operator can run it today; it also pairs directly with the soap giveaway (a story asking which scent people want is both a reply generator and an entry driver). Score is moderate rather than high because DM volume from a small following will not move ~$2,700/mo materially, and retention — not top-of-funnel conversation count — is the binding constraint.

**Target skill:** `marketing-warm-outreach` (edit)

### Make a lead-magnet opt-in the standing call to action in organic content, because leads and emails collected are the number that makes social media output measurable. — 5/10

**Why it works:** Views and follows are unattributable to revenue, but a free thing gated behind an email produces a countable, dated conversion per piece of content — which lets you compare posts, defend the time spent, and feed a list you can sell to later.

**Evidence:** Assertion plus the Van Group case: heavily promoted ebooks brought in thousands of leads for a six-figure-website business with a long sales cycle, where only a handful needed to convert.

**Fit:** Runnable now by one person and well-timed: the soap giveaway is exactly this kind of countable opt-in and can be the standing content CTA while the Meta campaign runs. Held to a moderate score because the existing organic-content skill already instructs promoting the lead magnet as one of only two asks, so the genuinely new part is narrow — using captured emails as the measurement unit for content — and because a sub-1,000 list means the downstream revenue read stays weak for a while.

**Target skill:** `marketing-organic-content-program` (edit)
