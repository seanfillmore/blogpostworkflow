## Adopted

### Write the headline as a swap inside a routine the buyer already performs — 'your after-work wind down, minus the wine' — instead of asking them to adopt a new behaviour or add a step. — 9/10

**Why it works:** Behaviour change is the expensive part of a purchase decision, not price. If the copy keeps the buyer's existing ritual, its emotional payoff and its slot in the day intact and only substitutes one component, the ask collapses from 'change your life' to 'swap one item', so the perceived effort/sacrifice cost approaches zero while the promised outcome stays the same.

**Evidence:** Line-by-line teardown of a live ad on the call; Sarah states the principle explicitly ('we never want to try to change someone's behavior... this is just the same routine, just tweaked a little') as a recurring rule from the show.

**Fit:** This is the natural-deodorant purchase decision exactly. Every RSC SKU replaces something the buyer already uses daily — deodorant, soap, toothpaste, lip balm, lotion — so 'your morning routine, minus the aluminium' is a directly writable headline for the PDP hero, Amazon bullets and Meta primary text, and it is a specific verbal structure rather than a vague positioning idea. It also protects against the switching-cost objection that kills natural-deodorant conversions.

### Place every static concept deliberately at one end of a clarity spectrum — either uber-clear (the image alone communicates the point with no copy needed to explain it) or deliberately surreal/abstract purely to scroll-stop while staying relevant — rather than drifting into a muddled middle. — 8/10

**Why it works:** The two ends win for different reasons: maximum clarity converts because comprehension is instant and the copy is free to do other work, and abstraction wins the scroll because the brain stops on something it cannot immediately parse. The middle gets neither — it is not instantly legible and not arresting, so it is scrolled past by people who would have bought.

**Evidence:** Assertion from agency practice across many accounts, with two example ads walked through on screen; Seb says the choice between the two poles is 'informed ultimately by performance data' and historical patterns across accounts.

**Fit:** A concrete, testable selection rule for the exact asset class this operator already produces — he builds statics himself in an AI image tool and runs them in his own Meta account. It sharpens the existing eight native static formats by giving a prior decision (which pole is this concept aiming at?) that can be made before any production time is spent, and it applies equally to Amazon listing frames and Shopify gallery slots. Durable creative-construction principle, not platform mechanics.

**Target skill:** `marketing-paid-creative-testing` (edit)

### Audit every ad as if you had no brand context at all, because roughly 80% of the people it reaches have never heard of you — if it only makes sense to someone who already knows the product, it does not work. — 8/10

**Why it works:** Whoever makes the creative has months of accumulated context and reads meaning into the frame that is not physically present in it. Cold traffic has none of that, so an asset that feels obvious internally reads as noise externally and fails at the comprehension step before any persuasion happens.

**Evidence:** Seb describes the internal check ('do I know what this is saying, because I have all the previous context?'); Nate calls it an underrated thing to ask of the team and frames creative strategy as being for people who have no clue who you are.

**Fit:** A solo operator who formulates, photographs, writes and buys media for his own 12 SKUs is the single most context-poisoned reviewer possible, so this guardrail has more force here than in an agency. It is a distinct check from the existing 1-second phone-size comprehension test in marketing-product-image-stack: that one measures speed of reading, this one measures whether the meaning exists in the frame at all for someone with zero brand knowledge. Runnable today on every static, listing image and organic short-form thumbnail.

**Target skill:** `marketing-paid-creative-testing` (edit)

### Stop making literal visuals (a happy customer, the product on its own) and instead depict progression, upgrade or evolution — an evolution-style chart that shows the buyer's current option as the under-evolved version, a character, a before-during-after sequence. — 8/10

**Why it works:** Visual comprehension is far faster than reading, so an image that encodes a comparative claim ('what you use now is the primitive version of this') delivers the whole argument before the headline is read. A literal product shot or smiling face encodes no claim at all, so it consumes the fastest channel the brain has and says nothing with it.

**Evidence:** Sarah's critique of the category ('they show a happy customer or the product; they don't show progression, storytelling, levelling up, upgrades, characters'), demonstrated against an on-screen evolution-chart static the panel reads instantly; supported by the claim that visuals are processed far faster than text.

**Fit:** RSC sits in a category defined by a switch away from a conventional incumbent — mainstream antiperspirant, drugstore lotion, fluoride toothpaste — which is precisely the claim an evolution or progression frame encodes without words. It is a concrete addition to the existing static format rotation and to the Shopify gallery slot order, buildable today in the AI image workflow he already uses, and honest here because the comparison is to a product class rather than a named competitor.

**Target skill:** `marketing-product-image-stack` (edit)

**Merged from:** part 1 of 2

### Start every creative from the messaging pillars or personas you have decided to test, never from a template or existing ad format — write the brief with zero visual direction in it (angle, persona, trigger events, desires, consequences only) and derive both the image and the headline from that brief afterwards. — 8/10

**Why it works:** If the brief already specifies a visual, or the work starts inside somebody else's template, everything downstream is executing a picture or a container instead of executing an argument, and the output converges on what the category already runs. Starting from messaging means the same groundwork informs the image, the headline set and later variants, so the whole batch stays congruent and any winner is readable as 'this message worked' rather than 'this template worked'.

**Evidence:** Seb describes his agency's actual brief format ('the brief doesn't contain anything visual related at all... here's our angle, here's the persona, here are different trigger events, here are desires, consequences'); Nate reacts that this foundation is what most brands skip. Restated as the one tactical action to take this week: 'start with figuring out what are the messaging pillars or personas you're looking to test... don't start from a template, try to build something from scratch.'

**Fit:** This is the missing upstream step between the evidence corpus RSC already assembles from reviews and the AI image generation workflow it already runs. One person can write a five-line messaging brief before opening the image tool, and doing so makes the difference between generating twelve pretty renders and generating twelve assets that all argue one buying motive. With a 12-SKU catalogue split between the skin line (the paid front end) and low-margin retention SKUs, naming the pillar or buyer first is how the operator keeps a static aimed at the acquisition job. Directly usable for Meta statics, Amazon image sets and organic short-form scripts alike.

**Target skill:** `marketing-paid-creative-testing` (edit)

**Merged from:** part 1 of 2; part 2 of 2

### To generate a specific visual, find a strong reference image, reverse-engineer that reference into a generation prompt, then mix that prompt with your own visual idea — which gets you roughly 80% of the way to the image you wanted. — 8/10

**Why it works:** Describing an abstract idea to an image model cold leaves composition, lighting and treatment undefined, so the output drifts. A reference already encodes all of those decisions; converting it into prompt language transfers them wholesale, and layering your own subject and idea on top gives a controllable image that is original in concept while borrowing execution quality.

**Evidence:** Given as the summarised prompting workflow: 'figure out exactly what it is you're trying to visualize, find a really good reference, reverse engineer the reference in terms of a prompt — that'll give you 80% of the way there'. Assertion from practice.

**Fit:** Directly operational for a solo operator who already generates his own product and ad imagery with AI models, and it slots straight into the existing AI-imagery workflow between reference collection and generation. It also names a specific weakness that skill does not yet cover — how to get a chosen visual STYLE out of a model rather than just an accurate product render. Costs nothing but the operator's own time and runs on today's tooling.

**Target skill:** `marketing-ai-product-imagery` (edit)

### Write headlines per-image after the visual exists — roughly eight candidates for each individual image, then a second round whittling down — and where the image is especially vivid, force the headline to reference what is in the frame rather than standing alone. — 7/10

**Why it works:** A headline written before the image is generic to the angle and lands on the frame arbitrarily; written against a specific finished image it can either carry a standalone claim or close the loop the picture opened, which is the only way the two elements compound instead of repeating each other. Generating eight and culling produces range to choose from rather than defending the first line written.

**Evidence:** Seb's stated working process ('I'll write maybe like eight per image and then kind of do a second round and start whittling it down... in some cases the headlines work as standalone, in other cases the image is so vivid we have to figure out how to incorporate it').

**Fit:** A concrete production sequence for the solo operator who writes his own copy and generates his own images: it fixes the order of operations (image first, headline against it, cull in rounds) and gives a volume target per asset. It adds the image-headline relationship decision, which the existing hook skill does not cover — its culling rules are about hooks in isolation. Runnable in an afternoon on the current static and listing-image set.

**Target skill:** `marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats` (edit)

**Merged from:** part 1 of 2

### Keep a standing bank of proven statics running permanently in the account as its low-volatility base, so when video winners decay the statics absorb the spend and account performance stays stable. — 7/10

**Why it works:** Video creative is volatile — an individual winner rips hard then dies unpredictably within weeks, leaving the account exposed. Proven statics degrade far more slowly and perform moderately well predictably, so a set of them left running acts as a performance floor: when the top video collapses, delivery shifts onto the statics and results hold rather than falling off a cliff.

**Evidence:** Operator experience across multiple accounts and categories: 'we have seven statics that have been running for a year that are gonna take up more spend today and performance will be fine' — 'statics were always there for us in our time of need'; Seb confirms statics almost always play at least a supporting role even when video is king.

**Fit:** RSC's paid account is one CBO campaign collapsed into a single ad set of four to eight ads, and this tells the operator how to allocate those slots: proven statics hold the base and are left alone rather than swapped out each time a new idea arrives. It directly supports the existing rule against continuously launching new ads alongside running ones and gives a reason to protect winners instead of churning them. Distinct from the existing 80/20 proven-versus-experimental split, which governs format proportions rather than the static-as-ballast role. The solo operator produces the statics himself, so building the bank is his own work.

**Target skill:** `marketing-paid-creative-testing` (edit)

### Test any genuinely new idea, message or concept as a static first, because the fixed unmoving frame forces you to say the thing clearly instead of hiding a vague message inside characters, story and script. — 7/10

**Why it works:** Video gives the marketer unlimited room — characters, scripts, scenes — and that room lets an unclear message survive because the production covers for it, which is how brands 'accidentally overdo it'. A static has one frame that does not move, so the constraint acts as a forcing function: if the message cannot be stated inside that frame, it has not actually been decided yet. Statics also cost almost nothing to produce, making them the cheapest instrument for finding out whether an angle has pull before production effort goes into video. The clarified message then transfers to every other surface.

**Evidence:** Assertion from operator experience: 'if you restrict the amount of storytelling elements you can work with as a marketer, you often get closer to what you're trying to say'.

**Fit:** A durable craft principle about message discipline, distinct from the existing 'statics have two failure points' claim — that one is about who controls production, this one is about the frame as a clarity test. It is directly useful to a solo operator who also writes his own organic short-form scripts: drafting the message as a one-frame static before filming stops a woolly angle from being papered over by editing. Runnable on a phone and a Canva file today.

**Target skill:** `marketing-paid-creative-testing` (edit)

**Merged from:** part 2 of 2

### When judging whether statics work, separate offer/discount statics out of the comparison — offer ads sit near the top of essentially every account, so leaving them in the static bucket hides whether your top- and middle-funnel statics are actually performing. — 6/10

**Why it works:** An offer ad harvests demand that already exists and converts on price, so its cost per purchase reflects the discount, not the creative. Averaged into the static category it inflates the whole bucket and produces a false read — you conclude statics are fine while every non-offer static is quietly failing, or you conclude video beats static when the real comparison was never made.

**Evidence:** Seb names the confound directly ('the difficulty with this question is statics includes offer ads, and of course in every account offer ads are gonna be close to the top; what we do is we intentionally don't do offer ads') as the reason his agency scores its work separately.

**Fit:** A reading discipline that costs nothing and prevents a real misdiagnosis: RSC runs discount and giveaway-style creative alongside brand and problem-led creative, and reading them in one bucket would make the cold-acquisition creative look healthier than it is. It is a tagging-and-segmentation rule the operator applies when he opens Ads Manager, so it complements the existing fixed column set and highest-spend-first triage rather than duplicating them.

**Target skill:** `marketing-paid-media-measurement` (edit)

**Merged from:** part 1 of 2

### When you need someone who can conceive visual ideas, recruit outside the people who label themselves creative strategists or marketers — songwriters, entertainment and script writers conceptualise progression and imagery in a way trained marketers often cannot. — 6/10 · parked until `team`

**Why it works:** The scarce skill is originating the idea, not prompting the tool or knowing ad formats — AI has collapsed the execution cost but not the conception cost. People trained in narrative and lyric craft habitually visualise progression and emotional escalation, which is exactly the input a static needs, and they are not competing for marketing salaries.

**Evidence:** Sarah reports she now tells brands to look outside self-labelled creative strategists and knows people hiring entertainment scriptwriters over traditional marketers; Nate attributes his best copy ideas to two years spent around songwriters. Assertion from practice, no performance data.

**Fit:** Sound principle with a real mechanism, and it names a sourcing pool the existing creator-sourcing skill does not have (it currently covers UGC creators, influencers and actor networks). It is parked behind `team` because carrying it out to its stated outcome means engaging and paying another person — the operator can shortlist writers alone, but the tactic only lands when someone is hired. The trigger is when concept origination, rather than production or media buying, is the demonstrated bottleneck on creative output — which is a later problem than today's conversion-rate constraint.

**Target skill:** `marketing-creator-content-sourcing` (edit)

**Merged from:** part 1 of 2

### Build the visual by collecting references from Pinterest and similar sites, then deliberately closing the laptop (or closing the AI research output) and generating your own visual ideas from blank before you touch a tool. — 6/10

**Why it works:** References prime the visual vocabulary, but staying inside them means you only ever iterate on something that already exists, which produces derivative work. Closing the source and starting from blank forces an original concept, and the collected references then serve as raw execution material for that concept rather than as its shape. Sarah Levinger applies the identical move to AI: let the model do the research, read the output, close it, open a blank doc.

**Evidence:** Both speakers describe it as their own practice — 'go on Pinterest, find various visual inspirations, then maybe close the laptop and think of really good visual ideas' / 'I force myself to read that output and then click X and open up a new blank doc'. Assertion only, no performance data.

**Fit:** Durable creative-process principle with a concrete, named mechanism (collect, close, ideate blank), so it is not bare motivation. It is cheap and entirely solo-runnable — the operator already produces his own statics, organic short-form and listing imagery. Scored below the message-and-pillar tactics because the prescribed output is a better concept rather than a testable change to a surface, which makes it the weakest link to verify.

**Target skill:** `marketing-ai-product-imagery` (edit)

## Rejected

### Stop trying to engineer a better AI prompt — the prompt is the easy part, and the model is only as good as the person driving it, so the effort belongs in conceiving the idea. — 0/10

**Rejected because:** Duplicate. marketing-copy-body-structure already holds 'Put roughly 90% of your copy effort into the concept and the hook — the body is a formulaic outline', and marketing-ai-product-imagery already holds 'Write short plain-language prompts instead of engineering page-long prompts specifying camera make, lens, and lighting'. Together those cover both halves of this claim, and re-recording it as a third variant would degrade skill triggering.

**Fit reasoning:** Fully covered by two existing claims across marketing-copy-body-structure and marketing-ai-product-imagery.

### Vet creative on instinct — show the batch to the room and watch the reaction, because if everyone in a room responds to an image, a lot more people will too. — 0/10

**Rejected because:** No stated mechanism that can be acted on or tested — 'trust your gut, you can pretty much tell' is a defence of instinct rather than a procedure, and the one operational version described (a group reaction on a review call) requires a room of other people that does not exist here. The usable residue, filtering creative on whether it provokes a feeling, is already held as the emotion-wheel hook prompt in marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats.

**Fit reasoning:** Not actionable for a solo operator and the usable residue is already held elsewhere.

### Once you crack a new visual format, iterate on it immediately and stack wins rather than hunting for the next fresh concept. — 0/10

**Rejected because:** Duplicate of an existing claim in marketing-paid-creative-testing: 'When you find a breakthrough ad, dissect it into components to understand exactly why it works, then produce many deliberate iterations of it instead of testing new concepts.' Same mechanism, same action, weaker wording.

**Fit reasoning:** Already recorded verbatim in substance in marketing-paid-creative-testing.

### Pick one thing you want the viewer to feel and take away from a creative — do not try to communicate everything on a single asset. — 0/10

**Rejected because:** Duplicate. marketing-product-image-stack already holds 'Every creative asset should have exactly one goal and one persona — and that persona must be specified narrowly', and marketing-paid-creative-testing holds the creative-jobs claim that gives each ad exactly one job. Re-recording it would add a third near-identical claim across two skills.

**Fit reasoning:** Already held twice across two existing skills.

### Funnel raw creative ideas through guardrails derived from historical performance patterns across many accounts — look at what has worked before and let those patterns constrain which concepts get produced. — 0/10

**Rejected because:** Duplicate in substance of marketing-performance-pattern-analysis Step 7, which already prescribes contrasting top and bottom deciles of comparable assets and naming the discrepancies as the transferable pattern. The only new element is that the pattern library comes from many client accounts, which is the cross-account agency strategy-layer purchase already recorded in marketing-paid-media-measurement.

**Fit reasoning:** Substantively covered by marketing-performance-pattern-analysis Step 7 and the cross-account note already in marketing-paid-media-measurement.
