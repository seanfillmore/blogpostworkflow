# The hook system (X thread, 2026-09-08)

**Creator:** Nick Theriot (@nicktheriot_)  
**Source:** social post — `the-hook-system-x-thread-2026-09-08`  
**Published:** 2026-09-08  
**Inferred era cues:** Published 2026-09-08. References current-generation tooling by name (Claude, TrendTrack ad-intelligence), Facebook ad auction economics, and a $4M/month spend claim. No stale platform mechanics — the claims are hook-craft (durable principle) rather than campaign structure.  

A media buyer running $4M+/month in Meta spend lays out his system for writing hooks at volume: a definition of the hook as the first three seconds of visual + text (or visual + spoken line), two non-negotiables (stop the scroll from the IDEAL customer, trigger curiosity), two traits (be different from everything else in the category, and aim at the biggest reachable audience), a four-point pre-launch checklist, a two-step creation process (start from what is top-of-mind for the buyer today, then pick a differentiation angle from a named menu — speed, result-without-the-hated-thing, price reframe, guarantee, new mechanism, exaggeration), a three-level LLM prompting ladder (basic prompt → paste your swipe file and ask for style transfer → paste an emotion wheel and ask for a hook that makes the buyer angry), a volume-then-refine loop ('write me 10 more like this', then hand-polish), a list of swipe-file input sources (TrendTrack, surveys, YouTube, Reddit, competitor ads) and a cross-niche rule: never copy your own category, import hook structures from unrelated ones.

Found 14 tactics: 8 adopted, 6 rejected (1 of the adopted parked behind a stage gate).

## Adopted

### Generate the 'different' half of the hook from a fixed menu of six angles: speed of result, the result without the thing they hate, a price reframe of the result, an exaggerated guarantee, a genuinely new mechanism, or dramatised exaggeration of the outcome. — 8/10

**Why it works:** Differentiation is not a mood, it is a small set of predictable places a claim can be twisted so it stops sounding like every competitor — 'end cigar breath without quitting cigars' works because it removes the sacrifice the buyer assumes is mandatory, and each of the other five angles removes a different assumed constraint.

**Evidence:** Assertion plus one named winner ('End cigar breath without quitting cigars') and one price-reframe example ('for less than the cost of one premium cigar…').

**Fit:** Directly translatable to a 12-SKU natural body care catalog where the category's hooks are interchangeable: 'result without the thing they hate' is the natural-deodorant buyer's exact objection (works without the rash/burn/aluminium/reapplying), and the price reframe fits a $50.46 AOV. He writes the spoken and on-screen hook for his own videos himself, so this menu is used on the next batch of organic short-form first, then on Meta primary text and Amazon bullets when spend resumes. Adjacent to the existing 'find the differentiator in one of four predictable places' claim but operates on the hook line rather than the mechanism block, and names two angles (guarantee exaggeration, dramatisation) that claim does not.

**Target skill:** `marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats` (edit)

### Do not prompt an LLM for hooks cold — paste in a swipe file of hooks that actually grabbed YOUR attention and instruct it to rewrite your hooks in those styles. — 8/10

**Why it works:** A cold prompt returns the median of the training distribution (generic 'get rid of X fast'); supplying real examples that already earned a stop transfers their structure and rhythm onto your product, which is what lifts the output from level-1 mediocrity to usable lines.

**Evidence:** Before/after examples given in the thread: level-1 output described as mediocre versus level-2 output ('The 3-second ritual cigar guys do in the driveway before walking inside so she never smells the lounge on you').

**Fit:** Zero-cost, one-person, runnable this afternoon: he already keeps and can build a swipe file from the feed, and the output feeds his own filmed short-form hooks first, then Meta primary text and email subject lines. Distinct from the existing 'pull up your own past hooks sorted by performance' claim, which requires a performance history he barely has at ~54 orders/month — this one works from other people's proven hooks instead, which is exactly the right substitute at this volume.

**Target skill:** `marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats` (edit)

### Never copy hooks from inside your own niche — everyone there has already copied each other — instead pull hook STRUCTURES from unrelated niches and apply them to your product (a watch brand's structure on supplements, a pet brand's debate format on underwear). — 8/10

**Why it works:** Within a category, every advertiser has converged on the same phrasing, so copying it guarantees invisibility; a structure imported from a category your buyer does not shop in reads as novel to them while still being a proven format.

**Evidence:** Assertion, plus two named cross-niche transplant examples.

**Fit:** Natural deodorant is a saturated, heavily-copied category where 'aluminium free, actually works' is the universal hook — this is the cheapest available route out of that sameness, and browsing other categories' top organic short-form and ad libraries costs nothing and needs no second person. He applies the imported structure to his own filmed short-form first, then to Meta primary text. The existing teardown skill records only 'research competitor ad libraries and run a gap analysis' — deliberately sourcing structure from OUTSIDE the category is not in the claim list.

**Target skill:** `marketing-competitor-messaging-teardown` (edit)

### Run every hook through a four-point checklist before it goes live — it must appeal to the ideal customer, trigger curiosity, address a large addressable audience, and say/show something different — and rewrite it if it misses any one. — 7/10

**Why it works:** A hook is the only thing that gets bought in the feed; each of the four failures kills delivery in a different way (wrong person stops, right person is not curious, audience is too small to scale spend, or the hook blends into every other ad in the category), so a fixed pre-launch filter catches all four before money or shooting time is spent.

**Evidence:** Assertion plus the claim that 'this is the filter every $100K+ ad passed' across $4M/month in spend; one worked example ('What would happen to your acne if the oil glands feeding it were shut down for a few days?').

**Fit:** A free go/no-go checklist the solo operator applies to the hooks he already writes for his own short-form videos, Meta primary text and email subject lines — no spend, no volume, no second person required, so it is runnable today even with paid paused. Two of the four points overlap existing claims (topic clarity + on-target curiosity; aim at the shared center of the market), but the differentiation screen and the packaging as one pre-ship filter are additive, and a filter is more usable than four scattered principles. Durable-principle class, so the 2026 date is irrelevant either way.

**Target skill:** `marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats` (edit)

### Screenshot an emotion wheel, paste it into the LLM, and prompt it for a hook that makes your ideal customer upset — because a hook that provokes an emotion holds attention better than one that describes a benefit. — 7/10

**Why it works:** Provoked emotion (indignation, being contradicted, mild shame) is a stronger reason to keep watching than curiosity about a benefit; the emotion wheel gives the model a concrete vocabulary of feelings to aim at instead of defaulting to neutral benefit language.

**Evidence:** Assertion plus a generated example ('Most cigar guys think their wife is overreacting. She's not. You actually smell worse than you think. Here's why you can't tell.') and the claim that some of his best ads ever were built this way.

**Fit:** Free, solo, runnable today, and the emotional territory is unusually available to this catalog — deodorant and oral care sit on top of real social self-consciousness ('you don't smell what everyone else smells'), which is exactly the wheel-driven hook this produces. He films and publishes the resulting hook himself on RSC's own social video before it ever becomes Meta primary text. Not a duplicate of the existing negative-framing or 'name a shared enemy' claims — this is a named generation procedure, not an angle.

**Target skill:** `marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats` (edit)

### Make the viewer feel the emotion through a depicted scene before you offer the solution — never sell directly to the emotion by naming it ('do you feel sad that dogs are in shelters?'). — 7/10

**Why it works:** Naming an emotion asks the viewer to self-diagnose and gives them an easy 'no'; showing a concrete scene induces the feeling first, and only a viewer already in that state is receptive to the fix. Feeling first, selling second.

**Evidence:** Analogy to shelter-dog commercials; assertion that 'nobody stops' for the direct-question version.

**Fit:** A durable structural principle, and directly runnable by one person with a phone: the deodorant/oral-care equivalent is filming or narrating the specific moment (the hug, the car ride, the reapplication in a work bathroom) rather than asking 'worried about body odor?'. Applies to his own short-form scripts first, then to email openers and PDP copy. Adjacent to 'open in the middle of the action' but distinct — that governs where to start, this governs the order of emotion and offer across the whole piece, which is a body-structure decision.

**Target skill:** `marketing-copy-body-structure` (edit)

### When an AI-generated hook lands, do not ship it — ask for ten more like it to get variety around the winning idea, then take the core idea into a doc and do the final polish by hand. — 6/10

**Why it works:** The first acceptable output is a direction, not a finished line; re-prompting around it explores the neighbourhood of a working idea instead of jumping to a new concept, and the human pass supplies voice and specificity the model cannot.

**Evidence:** Assertion only ('AI gives you direction; YOU give it the final polish. Never just copy and paste.').

**Fit:** Free and immediately runnable by the solo operator, and it is the correct habit at this volume — at ~54 orders/month and $30/day he will never get a readable per-hook signal, so exploring around a promising idea by hand is cheaper than testing fresh concepts. Scored 6 rather than higher because it partially overlaps existing claims about generating several variants then cutting them against explicit tests and never shipping LLM copy as-is; the additive part is the specific amplify-the-winner re-prompt.

**Target skill:** `marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats` (edit)

### Subscribe to an ad-intelligence tool (TrendTrack) to see which ads are winning right now across every niche, and use it as the standing input to your hook swipe file. — 5/10 · parked until `scale`

**Why it works:** Winning-ad data across categories keeps the swipe file supplied with structures that are currently working rather than ones you happen to remember, which is what makes the cross-niche import repeatable instead of occasional.

**Evidence:** Named as one of five swipe-file sources; assertion only.

**Fit:** Right in kind but a paid research subscription against ~$2,700/mo combined revenue and $30/day of (currently paused) spend — park behind scale per the paid-analytics rule, not behind traffic. The free scaled-down version is already adopted above: browse Meta Ad Library and TikTok/Reels top performers in other categories by hand. Trigger to unpark: monthly ad spend is consistently back above roughly $900/mo and hook production has become a weekly batch rather than an occasional push, so the subscription cost is small against the creative volume it feeds.

**Target skill:** `marketing-competitor-messaging-teardown` (edit)

## Rejected

### Every hook must do exactly two things: stop the scroll from your IDEAL customer, and trigger curiosity so they keep watching or reading. — 3/10

**Rejected because:** Duplicate of the existing marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats claim: 'A hook has exactly one job — get the reader or viewer to opt in — and it does that by delivering exactly two things: topic clarity (they know precisely what this is about) and on-target curiosity (they believe it is for them and want what comes next). Everything else in the hook is decoration.'

**Fit reasoning:** Directly relevant but already recorded almost verbatim, so adopting it again would degrade skill triggering.

### The hook is not just the text — for photo ads it is the visual and the text working together, and for video ads it is what people SEE and what you SAY in the first 3 seconds. — 3/10

**Rejected because:** Duplicate: marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats already holds 'Treat the spoken hook and the on-screen text hook as two separate deliverables for the same video', and marketing-paid-creative-testing already holds that a static has two failure points, the copy and the image, that one person controls end to end.

**Fit reasoning:** True and relevant, but already covered from both directions in the existing skills.

### Always write hooks for the biggest possible audience — great creative aimed at a micro-market (400 neurosurgeons rather than 1.2M doctors) caps how far it can scale. — 3/10

**Rejected because:** Duplicate of the marketing-awareness-level-messaging claim 'Aim at the shared center of the market, not at demographic slices' (and its companion, 'As a small advertiser, pick a splinter of the market defined by motive — then widen only if it works').

**Fit reasoning:** Sound and relevant, but already recorded — and the recorded version is better calibrated for a small advertiser because it is paired with the motive-defined splinter allowance, which this blanket 'always biggest TAM' rule would contradict at $30/day.

### Before writing, answer who exactly the customer is and what is high-priority in their mind RIGHT NOW, and make that the point of entry (the cigar smoker whose wife nags him about the smell when he walks in). — 3/10

**Rejected because:** Duplicate: marketing-awareness-level-messaging holds 'Rank the candidate desires on urgency, staying power and scope, then check the winner is a pain felt today' and 'When a segment does need naming, spend one situational opening line on it', and marketing-conversion-copy-angles holds 'Write the benefit from the perspective of the people who confer status on the buyer — partner, kids, parents, colleagues'.

**Fit reasoning:** Highly applicable in kind (the partner-noticing-the-smell moment is the natural deodorant equivalent) but already recorded twice over.

### Build the hook swipe file from customer surveys, YouTube searches on the problem and Reddit threads, because great hooks come from great inputs. — 3/10

**Rejected because:** Duplicate: marketing-review-mining already holds 'Harvest first-person language from Reddit comments: have an LLM generate a list of search phrases, run them through Google to surface threads, and mine the individual comments', and marketing-creative-evidence-map already holds the six-source weighted customer-evidence corpus including surveys and reviews.

**Fit reasoning:** Correct for this business and free to run, but the corpus-building instruction is already recorded in two skills with more operational detail than the thread offers.

### Level 1 of the AI hook system: prompt the model with 'here's my product, I want to target [audience], their problem is [problem], write me 5 hooks'. — 2/10

**Rejected because:** Not actually advocated — presented as the deliberately weak baseline the rest of the thread improves on, and generic 'ask an LLM for five hooks' carries no mechanism beyond what marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats already records about LLM hook rewriting.

**Fit reasoning:** The creator disavows it himself, and the levels above it (swipe-file style transfer, emotion wheel) are the actual advocated tactics and are adopted separately.
