# It’s Boring, but It Will Fix 99% of Your Amazon PPC Problems

**Creator:** Marketing by Emma  
**Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=twDUexmWaws  
**Published:** 2026-09-09  
**Inferred era cues:** Published 2026-09-09. Names current Amazon surfaces: Brand Analytics → Search Query Performance report (brand-registered only), A+ content, the Amazon advertising console's built-in AI 'creative partner' for generating ad creative at scale. Competitor examples are Neutrogena, Kiehl's and MediCube.  

Emma argues that most Amazon PPC problems are not PPC problems at all. She opens with a live search for 'silicone-free SPF' where the top sponsored results are all silicone-heavy products, then builds a diagnosis: compare a listing's PPC conversion rate on each search term against its organic conversion rate (same listing, same price, same reviews — only the path differs), pull category benchmarks from the Brand Analytics Search Query Performance report, and place yourself in one of four quadrants (bad/bad, good PPC + bad organic, good organic + bad PPC, good/good). She then gives a three-part fix: (1) the right search terms — including proactively negating attribute-qualifier terms your formula does not actually match, because a cheap-looking broad-match sale to a mismatched buyer buys you a return and a bad review; (2) the right ad creative — creative written for one specific search intent rather than a general line built to survive every query; (3) the right listing — her '3-second test', where the attribute the shopper searched for must be findable in three seconds, which means repeating the deciding information across images, bullets and A+ content, and never forcing a shopper off Amazon (Kiehl's hides its ingredient list on its own site behind two clicks) to verify the claim your ad just made.

Found 12 tactics: 10 adopted, 2 rejected.

## Adopted

### Proactively build a negative-keyword list of attribute-qualifier searches your formula does not satisfy ('silicone-free' when the product contains silicones), sourced from category keyword research rather than waiting for the search-term report to show waste. — 9/10

**Why it works:** Broad targeting sweeps you into qualifier searches where the shopper has a hard, non-negotiable criterion. Serving them costs clicks you cannot convert, and the ones who do buy discover the mismatch at home — producing returns and negative reviews. Negating the mismatched qualifier up front prevents both.

**Evidence:** Demonstrated live: Neutrogena and MediCube ads served on 'silicone-free SPF' for silicone-heavy products, plus a silicone sun-patch and a non-SPF silicone gel appearing on the same query.

**Fit:** Near-perfect fit for this exact catalogue: natural deodorant, soap and toothpaste searches are dominated by qualifier terms — 'aluminum-free', 'baking soda free', 'fluoride-free', 'unscented', 'plastic free'. Any of those RSC does not satisfy should be a standing negative before spend touches it. Distinct from the existing claim about negating zero-order and high-ACoS terms after the fact: this negates on formula mismatch before any data exists, and the mechanism is review damage, not wasted clicks. Runnable today at $30/day-scale Amazon spend by one person in an afternoon.

**Target skill:** `marketing-amazon-ppc-management` (edit)

### Apply a 3-second test to every listing: a shopper must be able to confirm the specific attribute they searched for within three seconds, which means repeating that deciding information in the images, the bullets and the A+ content rather than stating it once. — 9/10

**Why it works:** Shoppers arrive at the listing on different devices and scroll different sections, so any single placement is missed by a large share of them. Repeating the deciding fact across all three content surfaces means every entry path hits it fast; if it takes longer, the cheaper action is to click back to the results and buy a competitor.

**Evidence:** Demonstrated by failing Kiehl's live — images, highlights, bullets, features and A+ checked in sequence, no ingredient list anywhere.

**Fit:** Conversion rate is the named binding constraint and the CRO gate is open, and RSC's buyers are ingredient-led: 'aluminum free', 'baking soda free', 'fluoride free' are the whole reason someone chooses natural deodorant or natural toothpaste. Existing claims cover front-loading deciding information and a 1-second comprehension test on a single frame, but nothing records the rule that the qualifying attribute must be redundantly placed across images, bullets and A+ so every entry path finds it. Runnable today on 12 listings by one person.

**Target skill:** `marketing-conversion-friction-audit` (edit)

### Publish the verification detail a buyer needs — for a formulated product, the full ingredient list — on the listing itself, never only on your own website behind clicks. — 9/10

**Why it works:** A discerning shopper who has 'been burned' will not take the ad's word for a claim; they look for the proof on the page. If verifying requires leaving the marketplace, the cost of checking exceeds the cost of going back to the search results, so the click is earned and then thrown away.

**Evidence:** Live demonstration: Kiehl's ingredient list absent from the Amazon listing entirely, then two clicks deep on the brand's own site — 'they earned the click but failed to confirm the click'.

**Fit:** This is the single highest-leverage cheap fix for an ingredient-driven natural body-care catalogue, and it is pure CRO on a gate that is open. It sharpens rather than duplicates the existing product-image-stack rule about honest 'featured' ingredient frames: that rule governs the hero frame, this one requires the complete list to exist somewhere findable on-listing so a sceptical buyer can close their own loop. One person can add an ingredient panel image and an A+ module to 12 listings without spending anything.

**Target skill:** `marketing-conversion-friction-audit` (edit)

### Diagnose whether your Amazon problem is PPC or the listing by comparing the listing's paid conversion rate against its organic conversion rate, because the listing, price and reviews are identical and only the path the shopper took differs. — 8/10

**Why it works:** Holding every variable constant except traffic source isolates the cause: if paid converts far worse than organic, the mismatch is in targeting or ad promise; if both convert badly, the listing itself is failing every visitor regardless of how they arrived.

**Evidence:** Assertion plus a reasoned control argument (same listing, price, reviews); creator cites 10 years and 3,000 brands, and a client conversation that prompted the video.

**Fit:** Amazon is the larger of RSC's two channels (~$1,800/mo of ~$2,700) and the binding constraint named for the business is conversion rate, not traffic. This is exactly a conversion-vs-acquisition split and the CRO gate is open. Per-search-term paid-vs-organic rates need more term-level volume than the account has, so run the honest scaled-down version now: compare paid CVR to organic CVR at whole-ASIN level per parent product, which Seller Central already reports, and move to term level as spend and units grow.

**Target skill:** `marketing-amazon-ppc-management` (edit)

### Get brand-registered and mine the Brand Analytics Search Query Performance report for your category's real conversion benchmarks and for what shoppers search more broadly in your category, rather than importing a generic 'good conversion rate' number. — 8/10

**Why it works:** What counts as a good conversion rate varies hugely by category, and the report shows impressions, clicks, cart-adds and purchases per search term for your own ASINs and the category — so it supplies both the benchmark and the term list without guessing.

**Evidence:** Assertion; creator calls the report 'an absolute goldmine' and gives the exact navigation path (Seller Central → Brands → Brand Analytics → Search Query Performance).

**Fit:** RSC sells its own branded 12-SKU line on Amazon, so this report is available and free once registry is in place — a one-person admin step, not a headcount or budget one. It also directly answers the benchmark question the quadrant diagnosis needs, and complements the imported-claim vetting rule already in marketing-performance-pattern-analysis by replacing borrowed benchmarks with own-category data. Platform mechanics class, and the report and menu path are current as of the 2026 publication.

**Target skill:** `marketing-amazon-ppc-management` (edit)

### Write ad creative for one specific search intent and reinforce that the shopper's exact criterion is met, instead of one general line built to survive every query the campaign might match. — 8/10

**Why it works:** A shopper typing a qualifier search has already fixed their criteria; an ad that restates the criterion confirms 'you are in the right place' and wins the click at lower effort, whereas a broad line ('SPF 50 with no cast, just glow') speaks to a different desire and is ignored by everyone whose criterion it does not name.

**Evidence:** Live walkthrough of MediCube's no-white-cast creative appearing against 'silicone-free' searches, and a 'goodbye to oily sunscreen' ad taking top placement on 'no white cast sunscreen' — the wrong promise for that query.

**Fit:** Directly runnable: Sponsored Brands headlines and Sponsored Display creative are per-campaign, and RSC's campaigns already sort by parent product. The one-creative-per-intent rule pairs with the existing awareness-level messaging skill but is not recorded anywhere as a paid-Amazon rule, and the operator writes all copy himself, so headline variants per keyword theme are today's work, not a hiring problem.

**Target skill:** `marketing-amazon-ppc-management` (edit)

### Let keyword selection and listing content constrain each other: only target a qualifier search term if the listing can confirm that qualifier fast, and when a term is worth having, fix the listing to confirm it before scaling spend on it. — 8/10

**Why it works:** PPC and the listing are one journey — the term states the criterion, the creative promises it, the listing must confirm it. Bidding on a term the listing cannot confirm converts the click into a bounce back to the search results; conversely, a term you want and cannot yet confirm is a listing task, not a bid task.

**Evidence:** Reasoned from the two demonstrations (mismatched ads, Kiehl's unconfirmable claim); creator states PPC strategy should inform listing strategy and vice versa.

**Fit:** This is the sequencing rule the business already runs on — CRO before traffic — expressed as a concrete Amazon gate rather than a principle, and it gives a specific ordering for the 12-SKU catalogue: harvest the qualifier terms RSC genuinely wins on, build the confirmation into images/bullets/A+, then bid. Runnable now at current Amazon spend by one person; nothing here needs volume, budget or another pair of hands.

**Target skill:** `marketing-amazon-ppc-management` (edit)

### Place the listing in one of four quadrants — bad PPC/bad organic, good PPC/bad organic, bad PPC/good organic, good PPC/good organic — and let the quadrant dictate the next action. — 7/10

**Why it works:** Each combination has a different cheapest fix: bad/bad means every step of the framework pays, good PPC/bad organic means the listing is throttling an already-working ad, bad PPC/good organic means the foundation is sound and the job is putting correct spend behind it, and good/good means iterate against category drift.

**Evidence:** Assertion only; framework presented from the creator's client work.

**Fit:** A concrete routing rule for a two-surface Amazon account, and it prevents the classic error of spending on ads to fix a listing problem — the exact sequencing spine (Tracking → CRO → Offer/AOV → Traffic) this business runs on. Lower than the underlying comparison because the quadrant labels depend on category benchmarks that must be sourced separately.

**Target skill:** `marketing-amazon-ppc-management` (edit)

### Treat a cheap-looking ACoS on a broad, poorly-matched keyword as an artificial signal carrying a delayed cost, not as a win. — 7/10

**Why it works:** Broad match buys volume at low CPCs, so surface metrics look good. But the orders it produces come from shoppers who assumed a criterion was met; when the product arrives and it is not, the cost lands later as returns, one-star reviews and a damaged listing — which then depresses both paid and organic conversion.

**Evidence:** Reasoned scenario walked through in detail (customer assumes silicone-free, gets home, skin reacts, leaves review); assertion, no numbers.

**Fit:** The existing skill already negates zero-order and high-ACoS low-order terms; this is the opposite and unrecorded case — a term that looks profitable and is not. For a low-review natural-deodorant catalogue where reviews are the main proof asset and switching from conventional deodorant already produces a rocky transition period, one wave of mismatched buyers is disproportionately expensive. Durable principle class, so age is irrelevant.

**Target skill:** `marketing-amazon-ppc-management` (edit)

### Use the AI creative generation built into the Amazon advertising console to produce many intent-specific ad creatives cheaply, instead of defaulting to one general creative because targeted creative is too expensive to produce. — 6/10

**Why it works:** The reason brands ship broad creative is production cost per variant; generating variants inside the ads console collapses that cost, so per-search-term creative becomes affordable without a design budget.

**Evidence:** Assertion; creator points to the feature existing natively in the Amazon ads console with its creative partner.

**Fit:** Real and runnable — a solo operator with no design budget is the exact case it exists for, and it makes the per-intent creative rule above affordable. Scored moderate because marketing-ai-product-imagery already owns the broader 'generate assets with AI rather than hiring a photographer' workflow with much stronger anti-fabrication and QA guardrails, so this only adds the console surface, and those guardrails still have to be applied by hand to anything it outputs. Platform mechanics class — the feature is named as of 2026 and should be re-verified before it is relied on.

**Target skill:** `marketing-amazon-ppc-management` (edit)

## Rejected

### Stop responding to a sales problem by adding more campaigns and raising budgets, and stop looking at PPC in isolation, because that only accelerates the waste. — 4/10

**Rejected because:** Duplicate. Restates marketing-performance-pattern-analysis Step 1 ('name the binding constraint before you critique copy, offers or creative... split advertising problems from conversion problems before touching either') and the triage claim in marketing-paid-media-measurement ('before changing anything, ask can I spend more money and stay profitable right now').

**Fit reasoning:** Correct and relevant, but already fully recorded: marketing-performance-pattern-analysis Step 1 says to name the binding constraint before critiquing assets and to split advertising problems from conversion problems before touching either, and marketing-paid-media-measurement already prescribes the two-question triage of 'can I spend more profitably, or replace the single worst-performing highest-spend ad'. Adding it again would degrade skill triggering.

### Treat listing and PPC optimisation as a constant cycle rather than one-and-done, because trends shift, categories evolve and new competitors enter, so last month's data is not a guarantee. — 3/10

**Rejected because:** Motivational framing with no stated mechanism, cadence or trigger, and duplicative of the existing scheduled re-audit claims in marketing-amazon-ppc-management ('audit the rules themselves every month or two') and marketing-competitor-messaging-teardown ('re-scanning on a schedule because a winning lead claim decays').

**Fit reasoning:** No stated cadence, threshold or artifact — it is framing, and the mechanism it gestures at is already recorded twice: marketing-amazon-ppc-management prescribes auditing the automation rules every month or two, and marketing-competitor-messaging-teardown prescribes re-scanning on a schedule because a winning lead claim decays as the category copies it. Rejected on merit, not on timing.
