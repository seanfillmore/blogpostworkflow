## Adopted

### Pick one persona and specify it narrowly — not 'small business owners' but which type, at what stage, with what objection — because the persona determines the vocabulary, the proof you must supply, and the objections the asset has to answer. — 6/10

**Why it works:** Vocabulary, proof type and objection set are all persona-dependent. A broad persona gives you no basis for choosing any of the three, so the copy defaults to generic language that identifies nobody and answers nothing.

**Evidence:** Assertion from practice; presented as the second of four strategic prerequisites. No data.

**Fit:** Durable principle class, age irrelevant. Additive to the existing one-goal-one-persona rule, which says how many personas per frame but not how narrowly to specify one. Concretely usable and free: 'someone who already switched off aluminum and had a natural deodorant quit by noon' produces different Amazon bullets and PDP copy than 'natural deodorant buyer' — different vocabulary, different proof, different objection. personas.json already exists to draw from. Not higher because it is a specification discipline layered on a rule already adopted, and with ~54 orders/month the resulting choice is made by judgment, never measured.

**Target skill:** `marketing-product-image-stack` (edit)

### Match your messaging to where the market sits on the sophistication scale — a mature category has moved past plain benefit claims to improved-mechanism and identification angles, and a newer brand cannot copy the messaging of an incumbent playing a different game. — 6/10

**Why it works:** As a category matures, buyers have heard every basic claim, so the same benefit line that worked earlier no longer registers; what still moves people is a specific mechanism explaining why this one works, or an identity they can see themselves in.

**Evidence:** Thin. She reads a market-sophistication section out of an AI-generated audit ('this is where the market is now — improved mechanism and identification') and repeatedly notes that Dr. Squatch is 'playing a much different game than a newer brand.' Observation, no data.

**Fit:** Durable principle class (positioning), age irrelevant. Genuinely additive: the existing awareness-level skill mentions market sophistication only inside an evidence note and no tactic owns it. It matters unusually here because natural deodorant is a saturated category where 'aluminum-free' is table stakes — Native and Dr. Squatch have already exhausted it — so Real Skin Care's PDP and Amazon copy has to lead with a named mechanism (the specific formula, the baking-soda-free variant, why it survives the transition period) or an identity angle rather than the category claim. Free, editorial, no traffic or budget. Held at 6 because the creator asserts it in passing rather than teaching it, and it also warns against copying incumbent messaging — which is a constraint on the competitor-teardown practice, not a mechanism of its own.

**Target skill:** `marketing-awareness-level-messaging` (edit)

**Merged from:** part 1 of 2

### Proofread every AI-generated image for text defects before it ships — misspelled product words, stray em dashes, and other typography artifacts — and fix them with a named correction prompt ('deodorant is spelled wrong, please spell it correctly'). — 6/10

**Why it works:** Generators produce plausible-looking but wrong text at full size, not only in tiny type, and a misspelling of the product category on a listing image reads as a counterfeit or careless seller, undermining the trust the image was meant to build.

**Evidence:** Two live catches in the demo: 'Dio Durant' rendered instead of deodorant (she attributes it to the tool's German origin) and an unwanted em dash in a headline, both fixed with one-line named edits. Anecdotal.

**Fit:** Durable QA principle (asset review), age irrelevant. Additive in a narrow direction: the existing image skills cover hallucinated product features, wrong scale, and garbled *small* text patched in Canva, but nothing owns proofreading legible, full-size copy on a generated frame. Stakes are real on the ~$1,800/mo Amazon channel, where a misspelled 'deodorant' on a secondary slot is a credibility problem on the larger revenue source. Free and purely editorial. Capped at 6 because it is a checklist item close in spirit to the existing hallucination audit rather than a revenue mechanism.

**Target skill:** `marketing-ai-product-imagery` (edit)
