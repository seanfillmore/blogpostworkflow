## Adopted

### Use your own customers as creators — they will often do it in exchange for product alone, and the result is either your best-performing ad or a flop, because VIP customers frequently cannot show up on camera. — 6/10

**Why it works:** A real customer carries credibility no hired creator can fake; the cost is only product, and the risk is purely delivery quality, which you can screen for before using the footage.

**Evidence:** Assertion with an explicit caveat that she is nervous recommending it because outcomes are bimodal. She reports having seen it work and having seen customers accept product as the only compensation. No figures.

**Fit:** Durable principle class, and the one item in this video with a genuine solo-operator translation. Repeat customers are 45-52% of revenue and at ~54 orders/month the operator can personally identify and email the handful of enthusiastic repeat buyers — the same top-customer cultivation already in marketing-retention-offers, extended to asking for a short video. Compensation is a stick of deodorant, not a fee, so it clears the budget constraint entirely, and the output has real homes: the Shopify PDP, the Amazon listing video slot on the larger ~$1,800/mo channel, and post-purchase email. Held at 6 because delivery quality is a coin flip, the video still has to be edited by a solo operator with no designer, and there are hard compliance limits: product-for-content is a material connection that must be disclosed under FTC rules, and it must never be routed into an Amazon review, which is a TOS violation.

### Judge whether a person can carry a video within the first 2-3 seconds of a clip, on delivery rather than looks: energy and vocal variety (speeding up and slowing down), eye contact and magnetism, opening mid-conversation rather than on product features — and rule out anyone whose cadence reveals they are reading a script. — 5/10

**Why it works:** Delivery is what holds attention; a monotone script-read registers as an ad and gets skipped, while vocal variety and direct eye contact pull the viewer in regardless of production quality. Leading with product features before establishing any story loses the viewer before the claim lands.

**Evidence:** Six teardowns of real clips, including two of her own — a gummy creator praised for vocal variety and 'girl talk' vibe, Emily D'Angelo for eye contact and magnetism, two 'good but not great' clips flagged for scripted intonation, one apparel clip criticized for cutting straight to product features, and a side-by-side of her own flat versus high-energy videos. Practitioner judgment, no view or conversion data.

**Fit:** Durable principle class (on-camera delivery), age irrelevant. Executable today and free: the only realistic on-camera source here is repeat customers or the founder, and this gives concrete accept/reject criteria for a video before it goes onto a Shopify PDP or an Amazon listing video slot — the surface that carries the larger ~$1,800/mo channel. Capped at 5 because it is a selection filter, not a revenue mechanism, and the 'reject anyone who reads a script' bar is harsh when your candidate pool is a handful of willing customers rather than a paid roster; the practical version is to prompt for conversational answers rather than to reject the person.

**Target skill:** `marketing-creator-content-sourcing` (create)

### Scale brief depth inversely to the creator's experience: give UGC and small creators a fully prescriptive storyboard with the script written out line by line; give established influencers only campaign context plus content pillars, shooting guidelines and explicit dos and don'ts so they write and send back their own script for approval; and with celebrities and high-caliber talent write the script yourself close to verbatim. — 5/10

**Why it works:** Inexperienced creators do not know what converts, so leaving the script open produces weak content; experienced creators know their own audience's voice better than you do, so guardrails constrain the strategy without flattening the native delivery that makes UGC read as authentic; with celebrity talent the brand needs to control message and hook while borrowing the face, and talent will follow a well-written script closely.

**Evidence:** Teardown of her agency's own brief for a top-performing video (full clip-by-clip storyboard) contrasted with the briefs she personally receives as an influencer (campaign context only, she returns POV and script), plus a Jones Road Beauty brief with content pillars, shooting guidelines and dos/don'ts but no script. Notes she personally scripted celebrity ads verbatim at a nine-figure beauty brand and most followed them 'to a T'. No test or performance data.

**Fit:** Durable principle class (briefing), age irrelevant, and it resolves directly into the customer-as-creator path adopted above: a repeat customer filming a testimonial is the least experienced case on the scale, so the brief must be fully prescriptive — the exact question to answer, the sentences to open on, the length. Free and executable today. Capped at 5 because only the fully-prescriptive end of the scale will ever apply here — the influencer-guardrail and celebrity-verbatim ends presuppose paid talent that is unreachable at ~$2,700/mo — and it is a production-quality lever rather than a revenue mechanism.

**Target skill:** `marketing-creator-content-sourcing` (create)

### Build every brief from the same fixed set of components: a description of the video type wanted, the product linked and already shipped, a reference ad, the setting and wardrobe/style guidelines, a storyboard table of clips with the script and the visual for each, upload instructions, and recording guidelines. — 5/10

**Why it works:** A fixed component list removes the decisions the creator would otherwise make badly and eliminates the round trips caused by missing information — the reference ad in particular communicates the target faster than description does.

**Evidence:** Walkthrough of a real brief her agency used to produce a recent top-performing video, section by section. Single example, no comparison against a looser brief.

**Fit:** Durable principle class (asset briefing), free, and it is the concrete artifact that makes the customer-testimonial path produce usable footage instead of a shaky one-take. The reference-example component is the highest-value part and echoes the exemplar rule already in marketing-email-design-production and the reference-photo rule in marketing-ai-product-imagery, so it transfers with a known mechanism. Held at 5 because the wardrobe/setting and camera-spec sections are agency overkill for a customer filming on a phone, and this is a production checklist, not a revenue mechanism.

**Target skill:** `marketing-creator-content-sourcing` (create)

### Match production value to the product's price point — high-AOV products need elevated, higher-production creative to earn consumer trust, while lower-priced products can be served by raw phone-shot content. — 5/10

**Why it works:** Polish is a trust signal that scales with the size of the ask: when a buyer is spending a lot, visible production value signals a legitimate brand; when the ask is small, raw and unpolished reads as authentic rather than cheap, so the extra spend buys nothing.

**Evidence:** Observation that Caraway retains creators with 'alternative production value' beyond 'yapping out an iPhone' specifically because their AOV is high. Single named example, no conversion data.

**Fit:** Durable-principle class (trust psychology and positioning), so age is irrelevant. It resolves a real decision for a solo operator with no photographer and no designer: at a $50.46 AOV, phone-shot and plainly-lit imagery is the correct register, and money or time spent chasing studio polish is misallocated — while over-polished, generic-looking frames actively read as stock on a small body-care brand. Held to 5 because it is a calibration judgment rather than a revenue mechanism, the evidence is a single observed brand, and it partly overlaps the raw-phone-reference discipline already in marketing-ai-product-imagery. Additive only in giving a price-point rule for how much polish an asset needs.

**Target skill:** `marketing-product-image-stack` (edit)

### Activate creator content through Meta partnership ads because the delivery starts with the creator's own seed audience and expands from there, reaching net-new people your pixel keeps missing. — 4/10

**Why it works:** A partnership ad is served first to the creator's engaged followers and then to lookalikes of them, so it escapes the audience rut an account develops after years of optimizing against its own pixel — and Meta's persona-based Andromeda targeting rewards content that maps to new personas.

**Evidence:** Assertion plus secondhand claims: a performance summit statement that creator content is the future of selling, and named brand interviews (Pacted, Caraway said small creators/UGC performed best on partnership ads; Jones Road said bigger influencer and celebrity deals performed better for them). No spend, CPA, or incrementality figures.

**Fit:** Platform-mechanics class — published fresh (2026-08) so no decay discount today, but it is exactly the kind of claim (Andromeda behavior, partnership-ad delivery, in-Reels affiliate links) that will likely be obsolete by the time this business reaches the traffic gate, which is what caps it at 4. It also needs three things RSC lacks simultaneously: an ad budget, a media buyer, and cash to pay a creator. The mechanism is real and worth recording so it is not re-derived, but it is a long park on a fast-decaying claim.

## Rejected

### Classify creators as UGC creator, creator, influencer, or celebrity based on how they cultivate community (readable in their comments), not on micro/nano/macro follower tiers. — 2/10

**Rejected because:** There is no honest translation of this four-tier paid-creator classification to a solo-operator catalog with no creator budget and no media buyer.

**Fit reasoning:** A taxonomy for choosing between four tiers of paid creator relationships. Real Skin Care will never be choosing among those tiers — at $2,700/mo with no media buyer and no creator budget, the only realistic on-camera source is its own repeat customers.

### Match creator tier to how competitive your category is — in saturated categories you have to buy additional trust at scale with a bigger influencer or celebrity. — 2/10

**Rejected because:** The remedy requires funded influencer or celebrity deals; the usable 'borrow authority' insight (credentialed attribution on copy) is already owned by marketing-conversion-copy-angles.

**Fit reasoning:** Natural deodorant is genuinely a saturated category, so the diagnosis is right — but the prescribed remedy is an influencer or celebrity deal, which is agency-retainer territory and has no honest version at $2,700/mo revenue, at any stage of the gate sequence.

### Reserve 5-15% of every influencer campaign budget for creators who deliberately break your best practices — a different demographic, an older or younger person, someone outside your niche. — 2/10

**Rejected because:** No campaign budget and no per-creator signal at ~54 orders/month; the general 'keep one off-pattern variant in test' idea is already covered by the standing image-test cadence in marketing-product-image-stack.

**Fit reasoning:** Requires a campaign budget large enough that 5-15% funds a meaningful number of additional creators, and readable per-creator performance to identify the off-pattern winner. Neither exists here at any gate — at ~54 orders/month no creator-level result would ever be distinguishable.

### Source creators by browsing TikTok and Reels inside your niche looking for hyper-viral posts from accounts that did not already have millions of followers. — 2/10

**Rejected because:** Produces a shortlist of strangers who must then be paid; no creator budget, no ad account, and no platform presence to make outreach credible.

**Fit reasoning:** Free to do, but the output is a shortlist of strangers you then have to pay — and Real Skin Care has no creator budget, no ad account to run the resulting content through, and no presence on either platform to make an outreach credible. The creator's own caveat is that this channel alone rarely works even for a funded agency.

### Use performance-pay UGC platforms (Tribe, Influy) where creators produce content for free and are compensated on backend performance, especially if you are just starting out or want volume without strict brand guidelines. — 2/10

**Rejected because:** Fails three preconditions simultaneously — order volume creators can earn from, working attribution, and a paid channel for the content — and the named platforms are fast-decaying specifics.

**Fit reasoning:** Superficially the best fit in the video for a cash-poor brand, but it fails on three preconditions at once, not one timing gate: creators self-select onto brands with enough order volume to earn from, paying on performance requires attribution that does not work yet, and the resulting content needs a paid channel to run in. At ~54 orders/month no performance-pay creator would earn enough to participate.

### Post an open call on Twitter and LinkedIn to let UGC creators opt in, because large UGC communities live on both platforms and the posts circulate well. — 2/10

**Rejected because:** No existing Twitter/LinkedIn audience for the open call to circulate from, and no budget to pay responders.

**Fit reasoning:** Depends on having an audience on Twitter or LinkedIn for the post to circulate from, which this brand does not have, and on being able to pay the creators who respond. Both are missing.

### Stop doing one-off 'churn and burn' creator deals: work with multiple creators simultaneously and move to long-term partnerships — creators on monthly retainer, given internal email addresses, titles, and standing deliverables. — 2/10

**Rejected because:** Recurring creator retainers are fixed creative headcount against ~$2,700/mo total revenue with no team to manage them; not a stage problem but a structural one.

**Fit reasoning:** Presumes an ongoing paid roster and recurring per-creator commitments — effectively a retainer structure and recurring creative headcount. A solo operator at ~$2,700/mo cannot fund or manage multiple concurrent creator relationships at any point in the gate sequence, and the variance insight adds nothing once you have already adopted the rule to screen delivery before using footage.

### Before paying a creator whose rate is based on follower count, run them through a tool (Modash) to check what percentage of their followers are fake, and use the same tool to manage the inbox, tagging, campaign tracking and global payouts of the creator program. — 1/10

**Rejected because:** Sponsored vendor placement with no scaled-down version: it presumes paid follower-count-priced creators and a program large enough to need inbox, tagging and multi-currency payouts.

**Fit reasoning:** Sponsored tool plug whose entire premise is that you are paying creators by follower count and running enough of them to need an inbox, tagging and multi-currency payouts. No team, no agency, no creator budget, and a subscription cost that is non-trivial against $2,700/mo.

### Recruit from actor networks such as Backstage when UGC creators and influencers keep underdelivering, because trained actors know how to emote. — 1/10

**Rejected because:** Requires casting budget, a studio and a shoot to run — a production operation unfundable and unstaffable at $2,700/mo with no team.

**Fit reasoning:** Casting and paying actors, with a studio and a shoot to run them through, is a production operation a solo operator with no team and $2,700/mo in revenue cannot staff or fund at any stage.

### Give yourself a consistent visual signature that appears in all your content — Hormozi's nose feature is the example — so audiences instantly recognize whose content it is. — 1/10

**Rejected because:** No founder content channel or feed presence for a recognition cue to accumulate on, and no stated mechanism touching a PDP, listing or email.

**Fit reasoning:** This is personal-brand advice for someone building a recurring on-camera presence in a feed. Real Skin Care has no founder content channel and no plan for one, so there is no surface on which repeated recognition could accumulate — and the tactic states no mechanism that touches a product page, listing, or email.

### Gate creator payment on approval — both the creator manager and the creative strategist review the returned raw content against the brief, and only after sign-off does the creator get paid. — 1/10

**Rejected because:** Requires a paid external supplier and two reviewers; the equivalent QA discipline for self-produced assets is already owned by marketing-ai-product-imagery and marketing-product-image-stack.

**Fit reasoning:** A vendor-management control that only has meaning when there is a paid external content supplier and two reviewers. Real Skin Care has neither, and commissioning content at $250–$1,000 per asset is not reachable at ~$2,700/mo.

### Source creators continuously rather than only when a brief exists — maintain an always-on pipeline, and when you spot a creator you want, write a brief retroactively to fit them. — 1/10

**Rejected because:** Always-on sourcing is dedicated labor a solo operator lacks, serving a paid creative supply chain that does not exist and cannot be funded at ~$2,700/mo.

**Fit reasoning:** Ongoing creator sourcing is dedicated labor a solo operator does not have, in service of a paid creative supply chain that does not exist and cannot be funded at ~$2,700/mo with paid spend still gated behind tracking, CRO and offer work.

### Keep the creative strategist and the creator project manager as separate roles with clear SOPs — brands that combine them get bad results. — 0/10

**Rejected because:** Entire content is dividing work between two headcounts; a solo operator has no executable form of it at any stage.

**Fit reasoning:** Real Skin Care is a solo operator with no team. A tactic whose entire content is how to divide work between two headcounts has no executable form here at any stage of the sequence.
