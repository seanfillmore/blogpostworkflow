### Before asking an LLM for any content strategy, pipe real social data into it — your own post-level performance plus your competitors' top-performing videos with transcripts — because without that context the model is guessing and produces generic slop. — 8/10

**Why it works:** The model has no knowledge of what is actually working in your niche, so it falls back on training priors and generic advice. Feeding it deep-analyzed real posts (what got views, what the hooks were, what topics are covered) turns generic content advice into recommendations specific to the niche's live behaviour.

**Evidence:** Assertion, plus the creator's claim that this data loop is why he has grown faster than most and why the business owners he coaches are 'crushing in their niche'. No numbers on the specific claim.

**Fit:** The solo operator already films, edits and publishes RSC's own short-form video, so the planning input for that surface is a live surface today. He also already uses LLMs across the business, so the failure mode being described — briefing a model cold and getting slop — is one he will actually hit. The paid MCP tool is not required to run the principle: the scaled-down version is pasting the transcripts/captions of 15-20 top natural-deodorant and body-care short-form posts plus his own post-level view counts into the chat before asking for topic or hook ideas. Runnable today at $0.

### Map your audience as five concentric rings of breadth — from the single hyper-specific bullseye buyer out to the entire category — deliberately pick which ring to post at, and refuse to make content aimed at the outermost ring even though it would get the most views. — 8/10

**Why it works:** Reach and buying intent trade off against each other as topic breadth increases. Too narrow and there is never enough volume to produce a credibility spike or meaningful lead flow; too broad and views arrive from people with no relationship to the product, which both wastes reach and teaches the algorithm to serve future posts to the wrong audience. Rings 2-4 sit close enough to purchase that a viewer can still walk down the funnel, and naming the five rings makes altitude a deliberate choice rather than an accident.

**Evidence:** Worked example with five named rings for the plastic-surgery persona (bullseye = specific revision candidate, ring 5 = all beauty and skincare), the assertion that generic skincare-routine videos would go viral but never sell a $100k facelift, plus the pattern the creator says business owners report — going broad produces views but 'I never see sales'.

**Fit:** Directly on point for RSC's own organic short-form, which the operator films and publishes himself, and it enforces the prime directive of revenue over views. Concrete translation: bullseye = someone whose armpits react to the aluminum-free brands they already tried; ring 2 = natural-deodorant switchers three weeks into the detox who are about to quit; ring 3 = anyone curious about switching off conventional antiperspirant; ring 4 = non-toxic/clean-beauty curious; ring 5 = all skincare and beauty, which is exactly where a viral video brings zero deodorant buyers — with rings 2-3 as the working altitude and ring 5 banned. Extends rather than restates the existing 'puddle before the pond' claim, which sequences breadth over time; this supplies the explicit five-ring artifact, the simultaneous mix, and the two-sided failure mode.

### Build your topic list from other people's proven winners: pull the top-performing short-form videos from named competitors over the last ~180 days, screen out everything off-niche (lifestyle vlogs, adjacent products you don't sell), and keep a working top-50 list ranked by views and outlier score with each video's topic bucket attached. — 8/10

**Why it works:** Certain topic categories systematically outperform inside a given niche because of what the average viewer in that niche cares about, so a ranked list of already-validated topics beats guessing which subject matters — and screening off-niche outliers out first stops you from copying a hit that attracts the wrong audience.

**Evidence:** Worked run: 400 videos on the watch list over 180 days, top 130 screened, 73 qualified, 57 excluded as lifestyle vlogs or wrong-procedure, leaving a top-50 doc with topic bucket, outlier score and link per video.
