# We 38x'd Meta Spend in 4 Months. Here's What Actually Did It

**Creator:** Spencer Pawliw  
**Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=goedDlD00T0  
**Published:** 2026-07-13  
**Inferred era cues:** Published 2026-07-13. References AI-animated ad styles as a current wave, Claude used to turn scripts into song lyrics, cost cap vs lowest cost bidding, CBO/ABO debate, Meta 'Andromeda' (referenced as a prior video topic), Pet Lab Co. exit, creator's Evolve/Origins communities and an unreleased 'iteration playbook'. Very recent platform-era content.  

A media buyer walks through how his agency took a Meta ad account from ~$30k/month to ~$1.1M/month in four months, attributing it to three things: relentless, intentional iteration on ads that already win (changing format, hook, bridge, or authority while holding the proven angle fixed), heavy use of AI-animated ad formats (talking ingredients, talking body parts, AI songs, AI founder stories) to produce volume when a brand is short on real footage, and the claim that media buying mechanics — CBO vs ABO, cost caps, campaign count — contributed almost nothing, since the same three campaigns ran the whole time. The entire video is framed around a brand spending $65k/day; nearly every mechanism assumes an ad account with enough volume to identify winners statistically.

Found 7 tactics: 2 adopted, 5 rejected.

## Adopted

### Hold the proven angle and body copy fixed and change only the entry point — swap the hook and bridge to enter at a different awareness level, e.g. open unaware ('our dogs can't talk to us when they feel off') and funnel into the same problem-aware angle. — 6/10

**Why it works:** A hook that names the problem directly only catches people who already recognize the problem. A less-aware entry point catches people upstream and walks them into the same working argument, expanding the addressable audience without discarding the message that converts.

**Evidence:** Side-by-side of the direct 'if your dog eats grass, do this' hook versus the unaware 'our dogs can't talk to us' hook leading into the identical script; both described as winners. Practitioner assertion, no numbers.

**Fit:** Durable principle class, age irrelevant. It's a distinct operation from what marketing-awareness-level-messaging currently holds: that skill diagnoses which awareness level a surface serves; this adds the derivation move — keep one proven angle and re-enter it at a higher or lower awareness level to make a second asset. Free and solo-executable across an Amazon title vs. an A+ block vs. a PDP hero vs. an email subject line, all of which serve different awareness levels for the same natural-deodorant angle. Capped at 6 because Real Skin Care cannot measure which variant wins — it can only reason about coverage, not test it.

**Target skill:** `marketing-awareness-level-messaging` (edit)

### Raise the authority behind the same message — replace the anonymous everyday presenter with a credentialed figure (vet, trainer) and stage it in a setting that itself signals authority, like a podcast set. — 5/10

**Why it works:** The identical claim carries different weight depending on who says it and where; a credential and an authority-signaling context remove the 'why should I trust this person' objection that the message itself cannot answer.

**Evidence:** Two Pet Lab Co. iterations of the same grass-eating angle, one with a vet in a podcast room and one with a dog trainer, both described as winners. Assertion.

**Fit:** Durable persuasion principle, age irrelevant, and it fills a genuine gap — marketing-conversion-copy-angles has specificity, call-out, taboo, trigger words, primal desire, transformation, negative framing, one-thing and curiosity, but nothing on who the claim is attributed to. Real Skin Care can borrow authority cheaply on Amazon bullets, PDP copy, and education emails (dentist-relevant framing on oral care, cited testing or sourcing, named reviewer quotes, founder formulation credentials). Held to 5 because the video's actual execution — hiring a credentialed spokesperson and shooting a staged podcast set — is impossible for a solo operator with no team and no budget, and fabricated authority in a body-care category is a real compliance risk. Only the honest, already-true version transfers.

**Target skill:** `marketing-conversion-copy-angles` (edit)

## Rejected

### When you find a breakthrough ad, dissect it into components to understand exactly why it works, then produce many deliberate iterations of it instead of testing new concepts. — 3/10

**Rejected because:** Depends on scale that does not exist: requires an ad account with enough spend and volume to identify a winning ad and read the results of dozens of variations.

**Fit reasoning:** The premise is that you already have a statistically identifiable winner. Real Skin Care does ~$2,700/mo combined with no working attribution and traffic too thin to declare any asset a winner — and paid is gated behind Tracking -> CRO -> Offer/AOV. Durable-principle class, so age is not the issue; the issue is that the input to the process (a measured winner at volume) does not exist here.

### Media buying mechanics are irrelevant to scale — keep the account to a handful of campaigns and put all effort into creatives, offers, and funnels rather than CBO/ABO, cost caps, or structure. — 3/10

**Rejected because:** Platform-mechanic advice premised on a large ad budget, and its general residue is unmechanized motivational framing that restates the existing Tracking -> CRO -> Offer/AOV -> Traffic sequence.

**Fit reasoning:** Two halves, neither usable. The specific half (campaign counts, cost cap vs lowest cost, CBO/ABO) is platform-mechanic class and would decay fast, and it presumes an ad budget orders of magnitude above current spend. The general half — 'focus on creative, offer, and funnel' — is motivational framing with no stated mechanism at this scale, and the parts of it that are actionable (offer construction, CRO) are already the explicit sequence Real Skin Care is following. Adopting it would restate existing guidance.

### Take a winning ad's exact script, change nothing but the visual format, and ship it as a new ad — format-only swaps generate net-new reach and new winners. — 2/10

**Rejected because:** Requires an ad account and ad budget; the transferable remainder duplicates the static-format rotation guidance already in marketing-product-image-stack.

**Fit reasoning:** The causal mechanism is Meta's delivery system distributing formats to distinct audiences — a fast-decaying platform-mechanic claim that also requires an ad account Real Skin Care isn't running. The off-platform residue ('say the same thing in multiple formats') is already covered by the format-rotation entry in marketing-product-image-stack, so adopting it would duplicate an existing skill.

### Label every iteration and keep a count, so you can tell deliberate iteration apart from 'brainless' hook-spamming and know when an angle is exhausted. — 2/10

**Rejected because:** Depends on test volume that does not exist here; without readable results a naming convention produces no learning.

**Fit reasoning:** Testing hygiene is a durable practice, but it presumes a test cadence that produces readable results. At ~$2,700/mo across two channels with no working attribution, Real Skin Care cannot distinguish a winning variant from noise, so a labeling convention records activity with no signal attached to it. Nothing to adopt beyond generic discipline.

### Use AI-animated ad formats — talking ingredients, talking body parts, personified symptoms, AI-generated songs, animated founder stories — to produce winning creative when you have little real footage. — 2/10

**Rejected because:** Requires ad budget and a distribution platform Real Skin Care is not currently running; also a fast-decaying creative-format trend.

**Fit reasoning:** Fast-decaying platform/format class — AI creative tooling and what Meta rewards will look different in 18 months. More decisively, the entire distribution assumption is a Meta ad account with real budget, which is gated behind Tracking -> CRO -> Offer/AOV, and the production still requires editing skill the solo operator doesn't have time for. Real Skin Care is not stated to be on any short-form social platform where this could run organically.
