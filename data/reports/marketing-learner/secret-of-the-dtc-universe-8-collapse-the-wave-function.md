# Secret of the DTC Universe #8: Collapse the Wave Function

**Creator:** Stefan Georgi  
**Source:** social post — `secret-of-the-dtc-universe-8-collapse-the-wave-function`  
**Published:** unknown (not supplied via --published)  
**Inferred era cues:** No explicit year. Platform and format cues place it firmly in the current era: Meta as the ad platform, UGC creators, advertorials and quiz funnels as standard DTC funnel steps, telemedicine quiz funnels called out, and long-form ad copy generated 'in about 15 minutes with an AI like StefanBrain' (a 2023+ tool). It is entry #8 in the same 'Secret of the DTC Universe' series whose #9, #10 and #11 posts already in the library are dated mid-2026, so treat this as roughly contemporaneous — 2025-2026.  

A short essay arguing that the paralysis of new-product marketing comes from an effectively infinite option space (UGC vs founder vs spokesperson, static vs video, advertorial vs quiz funnel vs direct-to-PDP, discount vs bundle vs subscription) and that the operator's job is not to solve for the correct combination in advance but to pick one reasonable starting path and let the market answer. His prescribed starting point is an 'Occam's Razor' funnel: one type of ad pointing straight at a PDP or lander, with no advertorial and no quiz funnel in between, so there are as few variables as possible while a baseline is established. He argues for long-form native static ads over video for the same reason (video adds creator, script, hook, footage, and editor as failure points), and warns that advertorials and multi-step quiz funnels make failure unattributable — you cannot tell whether the ad, the headline, the design, the CTA, or question seven lost the sale. Complexity gets added only after a simple funnel has a working baseline and known economics.

Found 5 tactics: 3 adopted, 2 rejected.

## Adopted

### Start every new product, offer or brand with the simplest possible funnel — one type of ad pointing directly at a PDP or landing page, with no advertorial and no quiz funnel in between — establish whether it makes sales and what its economics are, and only then add another step. — 7/10

**Why it works:** Every extra funnel step is an extra variable, and variables are multiplicative: with an ad, an advertorial and a lander in the path, a flat result has half a dozen plausible explanations (wrong ad, wrong advertorial format, wrong headline, wrong design, wrong CTA placement, wrong offer) and none of them can be isolated. A two-element funnel produces a result you can actually attribute, which is what a baseline is. Once the baseline exists and the economics are known, added steps are measured against something rather than guessed at.

**Evidence:** Assertion plus an extended worked-through-in-prose illustration of the failure mode: a hypothetical skincare launch spending six months debating founder ads vs UGC vs dermatologist spokesperson vs wrinkles vs before-afters vs advertorial vs quiz, and the spiral of unanswerable questions that follows a failed listicle advertorial. No account data, no figures.

**Fit:** Durable-principle class (test design and attribution), so age is irrelevant. Directly live: a $30/day hand-run Meta campaign is being stood up this week by one operator with no designer and no media buyer, and this is the rule that keeps the build to something that person can actually maintain and read. It has a specific, concrete bite here — marketing-offer-construction records a 'scent/SKU match quiz' as one of three lead-magnet options, and this argues to defer that until a plain ad → page path has a baseline, because a solo operator cannot debug a multi-step quiz and a new ad account simultaneously. Attribution matters more at this scale, not less: at ~54 orders/month and ~$2.50 modelled cost per entry there is barely enough signal to explain a two-element funnel, let alone a four-element one. Two honest adjustments. First, the live giveaway does have an intermediate page, but that is not a violation — the entry page IS the optimised conversion event, not a stepping stone, and the allowable-cost-per-entry arithmetic already in marketing-paid-campaign-structure is what connects it to revenue. Second, 'establish a baseline' here will be a directional read over weeks, not a significant one.

### Start creative testing with static ads rather than video, because video immediately introduces a long chain of dependencies — finding the right creator, getting the right performance, the script, the opening hook, the opening caption, the footage, the editor, the edit supporting the idea, and format and length testing — each of which is a way the system can break. — 7/10

**Why it works:** A static ad has two failure points (the copy and the image) that one person controls end to end. A video ad has nine or more, most of which depend on other people, so a flat result cannot be attributed and a good idea can die from a bad edit or a weak performance rather than a weak concept. Fewer dependencies means faster iteration and a cleaner read on whether the message itself works.

**Evidence:** An enumerated nine-item dependency list for video versus a two-element static setup, plus the concession that video ads can work extremely well. Assertion, no comparative performance data.

**Fit:** Durable-principle class in substance (dependency counting in asset production), so age is not the limiter. It is an unusually exact match for the standing constraints rather than a gate: there is no videographer, no creator roster, no editor and no budget for any of them, agents/ad-studio produces static plates, and marketing-paid-media-measurement already scored hook rate and hold rate at 4/10 explicitly because this business barely produces video. What this adds is a reason to prefer static that is not 'we cannot afford video' — with $30/day and one operator, the static path is also the only one whose failures are attributable. It pairs directly with the 3-2-2 flexible ad already adopted, which wants three creatives of the same format and aspect ratio. Note the long-form native-style ad format the creator names is already recorded in marketing-conversion-copy-angles at 5/10 — only the static-before-video dependency argument is additive here, and it should be written as a format-selection rule, not a second copy of the native-ad entry. Held at 7 because it is a production-and-attribution rule rather than a revenue mechanism, and a repeat-customer testimonial clip may still be worth shooting later.

**Target skill:** `marketing-paid-creative-testing` (edit)

### Do not launch a new DTC product or offer with a quiz funnel — quiz sequences often run 15 to 20 steps, so a single weak page anywhere in the chain can lose the sale even when the ads and the product page are both right, and you cannot tell which page is at fault. — 5/10

**Why it works:** Conversion through a long sequence is the product of every step's pass rate, so one bad question silently caps the whole funnel. Worse, the diagnostic space explodes: is the ad attracting the wrong person, is question one weak, is question seven causing drop-off, is the recommendation page unclear, is the offer wrong, or is the PDP losing it? With that many candidate explanations, optimisation stops being possible.

**Evidence:** Assertion, with the explicit caveat that quiz funnels work well in the right situation and may be necessary in categories like telemedicine. No drop-off data, no named accounts.

**Fit:** Durable-principle class (funnel attribution), age irrelevant, and it lands on a real recorded temptation rather than a hypothetical one: marketing-offer-construction lists a scent/SKU match quiz across the 12-SKU catalog as one of three lead-magnet types, and marketing-paid-media-measurement's restraint rules say a solo operator will build things during the do-nothing period. This is the counterweight — a 15-step quiz is not something one person hand-running $30/day can build, populate with logic for 12 SKUs, and then debug against ~54 orders/month. Held at 5 because it is a refinement of the simplest-funnel rule above rather than an independent mechanism, and because it is a decision not to act, which protects hours rather than producing revenue. Record it as a note under that rule, not as its own section.

**Target skill:** `marketing-paid-campaign-structure` (edit)

## Rejected

### Acknowledge that an infinite number of marketing paths could reach your goal, then deliberately 'collapse the wave function' by choosing one single path to start walking down today instead of trying to identify the correct combination before spending anything. — 3/10

**Rejected because:** Motivational framing whose only actionable content is fully carried by the simplest-funnel rule below; adding it separately would duplicate staging discipline already recorded in marketing-awareness-level-messaging, marketing-lifecycle-email-flows and marketing-upsell-offer-design.

**Fit reasoning:** Durable principle class (decision discipline), so age is irrelevant — the problem is that the framing has no separable mechanism of its own. Everything actionable in it ('pick one starting point', 'don't solve the combination in advance') is stated concretely in the Occam's Razor funnel rule extracted below, and the surrounding library already carries the same discipline in three places: the cheap-reversible-surface-first rule in marketing-awareness-level-messaging, the build-one-flow-at-a-time rule in marketing-lifecycle-email-flows, and the one-offer-type-at-a-time staging in marketing-upsell-offer-design. Recording it again as its own entry would add a motivational paraphrase and degrade triggering across all four.
