## Adopted

### Treat the absence of fast, frequent response data as a constraint in its own right and fix it by instrumenting the store — real-time analytics, session/visitor recording and on-site surveys — then change the page based on what you observe rather than on the plan you wrote last month. — 6/10

**Why it works:** Without observation you are guessing at why visitors do not buy, so you optimize whatever you happen to believe, and you cannot double down on a winner or kill a loser before it drains the bank. Watching real sessions and asking visitors directly surfaces the specific confusion, missing information or friction that no amount of internal reasoning produces — and it does so as qualitative evidence, which does not require statistical volume to be informative.

**Evidence:** Named as Silver Bullet #2 in overview, then detailed later with tool name-drops (Woopra for real-time analytics, ClickTale for session recording, 4q.iperceptions for on-page survey) and the claim that today's reports are graphically robust enough that 'a sixth-grader could understand them'. No account data, no before/after, no instrumentation procedure.

**Fit:** The named tools are platform-mechanics class and 17 years stale — ClickTale is now Contentsquare, 4Q is defunct — so treat the stack as dead and the practice as durable. The practice itself is free and solo-executable today (Microsoft Clarity or similar for recordings, a one-question exit survey on the Shopify PDP) and lands on the ~$875/mo Shopify side where CRO work is live and the tracking gate is open. Its real value is that qualitative feedback is readable at ~54 orders/month where a quantitative funnel report is not: twenty session recordings show a broken variant picker or an unanswered transition-period question even though no conversion-rate delta would ever reach significance. Held at 6 because Amazon — the larger ~$1,800/mo channel — cannot be instrumented this way at all, it produces diagnosis rather than revenue, and marketing-conversion-friction-audit already tells you what to look for once you are looking.

**Target skill:** `marketing-conversion-friction-audit` (edit)

### Before deciding, prospects leave your site to check consumer-generated media — user reviews, blogs, discussion forums — and trust those opinions more than your advertising copy, so monitor what is being said about you there and make sure nothing derogatory stands without a rebuttal. — 6/10

**Why it works:** Third-party accounts are read as disinterested, so they outrank seller claims in the buyer's evaluation; an unanswered negative account off your own property therefore does more damage than any on-page copy can repair, and a visible, reasonable response neutralizes it for every later reader of the same thread.

**Evidence:** A cited Deloitte & Touche survey claiming more than 80% of people who seek out these opinions believe them more than advertising copy. Secondhand citation, no link, no figures on the effect of responding.

**Fit:** Durable trust-and-proof principle whose described behaviour has only intensified since 2008. Genuinely additive: marketing-review-mining owns extracting language from reviews you already have and choosing which to display, and the voice-of-customer agent already mines Reddit and Judge.me monthly, but nothing owns the response side — replying to a negative Amazon review on the ~$1,800/mo channel, or answering a 'has anyone tried this brand' thread in a sensitive-skin or zero-waste subreddit. Free, purely editorial, solo-executable, and it lands on the exact objections (baking soda burned, quit by noon, gave up during the transition) that drive the 18–22.5% repeat rate. Held at 6 because it protects trust rather than producing revenue, it will never be measurable at this volume, and it carries a hard guardrail: Amazon permits a seller response to a review but prohibits anything that reads as review manipulation, and forum participation must be disclosed as the brand rather than posted as a customer.

**Target skill:** `marketing-review-mining` (edit)

### Counter buyer's remorse immediately after the order: re-sell the buyer on the benefits of what they just bought, restate the money-back guarantee, and tell them how to reach you if a question arises. — 5/10

**Why it works:** Doubt forms in the minutes and hours after payment — did I need this, can I afford it — and an unanswered doubt turns into a refund or a cancellation. Most buyers only need to know the money-back option exists rather than to use it, so restating the guarantee removes the risk without costing anything, and re-stating the benefits gives the decision a reason to survive the doubt.

**Evidence:** Assertion within the seventh buying-process step, plus the observation that 'most buyers simply need the reassurance that they can get their money back if need be'. No refund or retention figures.

**Fit:** Durable retention psychology, free, and aimed at the binding constraint — repeat customers are 45–52% of revenue and the churn event is a first-time buyer deciding during the transition period that the product failed. marketing-post-purchase-onboarding already owns the first-48-hours promise schedule and the pre-arrival education series, and marketing-offer-construction owns writing and placing the guarantee on the PDP; the additive part is narrow but real — the guarantee should be restated inside the order-confirmation and first post-purchase email, not only on the page that sold them, and that email should re-sell the specific benefit rather than just confirm shipping. Capped at 5 for that overlap and because at ~54 orders/month a change in refund rate will never be readable. Note the tension with the fear-handling rule in marketing-conversion-copy-angles: the guarantee restatement is evidence, but bare 'don't worry' reassurance is not, so the same email still owes the dated transition timeline.

### Influence is highest at the earliest stage, before the prospect has framed their own need — so help them frame the problem in a way that projects your strengths and minimizes your weaknesses, rather than waiting until they are comparing products on price and features. — 5/10

**Why it works:** A buyer who has not yet defined what a good solution looks like will adopt the definition offered to them; whoever supplies the evaluation criteria first shapes every later comparison, because the shortlist is filtered against those criteria. Once the prospect has built their own criteria from competitors' messaging, you are arguing against a frame instead of setting one.

**Evidence:** Assertion within the first buying-process step, with the added claim that framing the need also lays the foundation for a long-term relationship. No examples, no data.

**Fit:** Durable positioning principle, free and purely editorial. The honest form here is concrete: a 'how to choose a natural deodorant that actually works' education piece or A+ block that establishes baking-soda irritation and surviving the transition as the criteria a buyer should judge on, written before they compare Native and Dr. Squatch on scent and price. Capped at 5 because it substantially overlaps three recorded rules — the 'how do I pick' solution-aware stem and the market-image bridge in marketing-awareness-level-messaging, and the us-vs-them attribute cap in marketing-product-image-stack. The additive part is narrow: those govern the comparison surface, this says the criteria should be set upstream in problem-aware content before comparison begins. Any criterion named must be a defensible formulation fact under the body-care claim rules, and at ~54 orders/month the effect will be judged rather than measured.

**Target skill:** `marketing-awareness-level-messaging` (edit)

### Solve a recurring problem permanently by running it through a fixed sequence — identify the problem, reframe it from a person to a system, probe with specific questions, name the real constraint, state the outcome you wanted, complete the sentence 'the solution is to install a system that…', design that system, then implement it. — 5/10

**Why it works:** A one-off fix repairs an instance while leaving the process that produced it intact, so the problem recurs and consumes attention indefinitely. Forcing the answer into the form 'install a system that…' converts a repair into a documented process, and each such process permanently removes a category of problem from the operator's plate, which is what frees capacity to grow.

**Evidence:** An eight-step procedure worked through on a single hypothetical (hiring an incompetent employee → the real constraint is the absence of a hiring and training system), plus a ten-item sub-checklist (A–J) for designing the system. No business outcome data, no named case.

**Fit:** Durable process-design principle and free. Roughly half does not transfer — steps on staffing, accountabilities, non-compliance enforcement and employee notification presuppose people who do not exist here, and the worked example is a hiring problem for a solo operator with no team. What transfers is the terminal instruction: when a marketing problem recurs, the output is a written process, not another fix. Real recurrences here are concrete — a deodorant SKU stocking out (which caps everything downstream and no marketing work repairs), a post-purchase flow silently breaking, listing copy drifting out of sync across 12 SKUs and two channels, review requests never going out. A checklist file costs an hour and prevents the recurrence. Capped at 5 because the first three steps duplicate Step 1 of marketing-performance-pattern-analysis and the sequencing duplicates Step 3, the evidence is one hypothetical, and it protects hours rather than producing revenue.

**Target skill:** `marketing-performance-pattern-analysis` (edit)

### Because buyers now shop you by asking each other, put the credible comparisons, third-party studies and endorsements on your own page so the prospect can satisfy their need to have made a prudent decision without leaving to check you out. — 5/10

**Why it works:** Buyers trust each other more than they trust you, so they will go elsewhere to verify before buying — and every departure is a chance to be sold something else. Hosting the comparison and the third-party proof on your own page lets the verification happen inside your funnel, and buyers buy the product that is easiest to decide to buy.

**Evidence:** Assertion, supported by the general Web 2.0 argument that prospects now ask each other for advice and 'shop' you looking for similar products at a lower price. No conversion figures.

**Fit:** Durable positioning-of-proof principle, free and purely editorial, landing on the two surfaces that carry revenue: the Shopify PDP and the Amazon A+ block on the larger ~$1,800/mo channel. Natural deodorant is a category where the buyer will open another tab and compare against Native and Dr. Squatch, so an on-page comparison block plus third-party testing and certification is a real retention of the session. Capped at 5 because it overlaps the credibility-asset placement and every-subpage-is-an-entry-page rules in marketing-conversion-friction-audit and the us-vs-them attribute cap in marketing-product-image-stack, and because the additive claim is narrow: the proof must be sufficient to end the comparison shopping, not merely be present. Any competitor comparison must stay to defensible formulation facts under the body-care claim rules.

### Survey buyers at the end of the buying process to find out which factors actually helped them decide, and keep probing until you have isolated the specific constraint limiting order count or average order value. — 5/10

**Why it works:** Analytics show you where people abandon but never why; asking the people who did buy which information closed them names the deciding factor directly, so the fix targets a known constraint instead of a guessed one.

**Evidence:** Process instruction only — monitor each step, note abandonment points, scour forums and blogs, and 'better yet, conduct a survey at the end of the buying process'. No example survey, no findings, no data.

**Fit:** Durable diagnosis-before-action principle, free and solo-executable — a one-question post-purchase survey appended to the existing Shopify thank-you page or the first post-purchase email costs nothing and needs no traffic. Genuinely additive: marketing-problem-solution-inventory sources objections from public complaints and competitor reviews and marketing-review-mining shapes the review request, but nothing asks buyers what actually closed them, and that answer is the input the friction audit is currently guessing at. It also feeds Step 1 of marketing-performance-pattern-analysis, which demands the binding constraint be named before anything is rewritten. Capped at 5 because at ~54 orders/month the responses are a handful of qualitative comments rather than a measurement, it is a data-gathering step rather than a revenue mechanism, and Amazon buyers on the larger ~$1,800/mo channel cannot be surveyed this way.

### Study your most successful competitors' sales process on their own sites — which product comparisons, third-party studies, endorsements, testimonials and customer forums they supply to help the prospect decide — and use the gap to work out what your own page is missing. — 5/10

**Why it works:** A competitor who is winning has already worked out which proof assets a prospect in this category needs before deciding; enumerating what they put on-page tells you what your prospect arrives expecting to find, so production targets a known gap instead of a guess.

**Evidence:** Process instruction: 'study your competitors' sales process to see how they work… study the kinds of information… that your most successful competitors offer'. No worked example, no data.

**Fit:** Durable competitive-reference gathering that extends a recorded practice onto a surface it does not cover: marketing-competitor-messaging-teardown reads Native, Every Man Jack, Dr. Squatch and Harry's ad libraries and email archives, but nothing reads their actual PDPs and Amazon listings for which proof assets sit on the page. Free, solo-executable, and lands on the two revenue surfaces. Capped at 5 because it is a coverage checklist rather than a revenue mechanism, it is a close cousin of the ad-library and email-archive gap analyses already recorded, and copying a much larger brand's proof stack is not honestly available here — RSC has no press logos or thousands of reviews, so the output must be an honest gap list (transition-period timeline, third-party testing, certification) rather than an imitation.

### Announce the offer in advance with a fixed opening date, make it explicitly unavailable until then, and fill the gap with free content and answered questions so anticipation accumulates toward the launch. — 5/10

**Why it works:** Withholding availability while continuing to deliver value converts waiting time into engaged attention rather than dead time — the reader keeps opening emails because more free material is promised, and the fixed date gives the eventual ask a moment of concentrated demand instead of a trickle of individual decisions.

**Evidence:** Demonstrated rather than taught: 'you can NOT sign up for Guided Profit System now. Not yet... between now and Tuesday, September 23 2008, keep your eyes glued to your inbox. There are more reports, videos and blog postings... all free. Plus, as we get closer to the release date, I'll provide more details and personally answer your questions.' No open rates, no conversion figures, no comparison against launching immediately.

**Fit:** Durable offer-sequencing principle, free and solo-executable, landing on the one owned surface where the 18–22.5% repeat rate is decided: a small email list. Genuinely additive next to marketing-scarcity-urgency-framing, which owns closing deadlines, reason-why wrappers and the last-hours reminder but has nothing on an opening date — a dated launch for a new scent, a seasonal bundle or the next giveaway round, with two or three value emails in between, is an honest form that needs no discount and no new SKU. Capped at 5 because at a sub-1,000 subscriber list and ~54 orders/month the concentration effect will never be measurable, because a runway of 'coming soon' emails on a $12 consumable overshoots if it runs more than a few sends, and because the mechanism collapses if the stated date is not honoured.

### When the price is high, say so plainly and give explicit reasons why it has to be — rather than apologising for it or softening it. — 5/10

**Why it works:** An unexplained high price reads as greed and invites the buyer to negotiate it down mentally. Naming the reasons converts the number into a consequence of something the buyer values — the cost of what is inside, the size of the outcome — so the price becomes evidence about the product rather than an obstacle to it. Stating it before the buyer objects also signals a seller who is not embarrassed by the number.

**Evidence:** Demonstrated: 'Securing a place will NOT be cheap... And that's the way it needs to be — for three important reasons', followed by the three reasons (seriousness filter, peer quality, value delivered). Assertion only, no price test, no take-rate figures.

**Fit:** Durable pricing psychology, free and purely editorial. Narrowly additive inside marketing-offer-construction: that skill owns re-anchoring the comparison class, funding a premium by raising price, and stepping a price upward against a fixed sample, but nothing owns stating the reasons behind the number on the page. The honest form here is real and already true — small-batch production by one person, ingredient cost, no filler — and it belongs on the Shopify PDP and in founder-voice emails where a $12 stick sits next to Native and Dr. Squatch. Capped at 5 because it is a one-paragraph craft habit rather than a revenue mechanism, it sits close to the reason-why rule in marketing-scarcity-urgency-framing, and the source's version — price as a screening device for a six-figure coaching client — has no form at a $50.46 AOV, so only the stated-reasons half transfers. Every reason given must be a real cost fact, not a manufactured justification.

### When the reader is skeptical, endorse the skepticism as a sign of intelligence rather than rebutting it, and shrink the ask to 'hear me out before you decide'. — 5/10

**Why it works:** Rebutting an objection makes the reader defend it, hardening it. Complimenting the objection removes the need to defend it — the reader gets to keep their guard and their self-image at once — and reframes the doubt as a shared standard the seller also holds. Reducing the immediate ask from 'believe me' to 'keep reading' then lets a skeptical reader continue without conceding anything, so belief can be built by the copy rather than demanded up front.

**Evidence:** One worked passage: 'I know — you're skeptical. You've heard thousands of big promises from business coaches over the years... That's good. Skepticism is a sure sign of intelligence. But please hear me out before you decide.' No test, no data.

**Fit:** Durable persuasion psychology, so the 2008 date is irrelevant. Free, purely editorial, and it lands on the exact buyer this catalogue faces: virtually every natural-deodorant prospect has already tried one that quit by noon and arrives disbelieving, so 'if you're skeptical, good — you should be, most of these don't work' is both true and available on the PDP hero, the non-buyer nurture flow, and Meta primary text on the live $30/day giveaway campaign. Capped at 5 because it sits close to the admit-flaws rule already recorded ('voice the reader's own disbelief out loud') — the additive part is narrow but real: that rule says name the doubt, this says validate it as correct and then reduce the ask to continued attention rather than agreement. Also capped because at ~54 orders/month no version of the opening line will ever be measurably better, and the flattery must be genuine rather than a tic.

### After a large claim, state a much smaller fraction of it as the floor — 'if I can deliver on even one-tenth of my promise, this will be the best investment you've ever made'. — 5/10

**Why it works:** A big claim asks the reader to accept the whole thing before taking any action, and if they discount it the action dies with it. Explicitly discounting the claim yourself makes the small action justifiable on the discounted version alone, so the reader no longer has to resolve their doubt about the headline claim before proceeding. It also signals a seller confident enough to argue against their own promise, which raises the credibility of the undiscounted version.

**Evidence:** One line in the lead: 'If I can deliver on even one-tenth of my promise to you, the time you spend reading this report will be the best investment you've ever made.' Assertion only, no test.

**Fit:** Durable claim-construction principle, age irrelevant, free and purely editorial. It has an honest, low-risk form here because the ask is small and the guarantee already exists: 'even if it only gets you through the first two weeks, it's done its job' is defensible against a $12 stick with a stated 30-day promise, and it lands on the transition-period objection that drives the 18–22.5% repeat rate. Capped at 5 because it overlaps the claim-calibration rule ('pitch the claim at the edge of credibility') and the admit-flaws rule beside it, and because the discounted floor is still an efficacy statement in body care — it must clear the specificity and claim rules, and the fraction must be one the product genuinely delivers rather than rhetorical hedging. Unmeasurable at ~54 orders/month.

### Find a freelancer by first writing down the exact skill combination the job needs and the prior career path an ideal candidate would have, then searching for that specific combination of terms plus 'resume' rather than browsing job boards. — 4/10

**Why it works:** A generic search or job-board browse returns a large undifferentiated pool you cannot filter; specifying the skill combination and the experience path first turns the search into a filter that only people who already have both can pass, so the shortlist is pre-qualified before you contact anyone.

**Evidence:** A worked example for a website build (CSS + video editing, ex-graphic-designer and ex-webmaster) with the literal query string and three named results, plus a Firefox extension recommendation. No hit-rate data.

**Fit:** Platform-mechanics class for the execution detail — a 2008 Google resume-search string and a Firefox extension are ~18 years old and the pool of freelancers with indexed HTML resumes has moved to LinkedIn, Upwork and portfolio sites — so the recipe must be rebuilt rather than trusted. What survives is the durable kernel: derive the exact skill combination and prior-role path from the job before searching, and search for that combination rather than for a category. Parked under `team` because it exists only to hire someone who is not the solo operator. Trigger: the first time there is budget for a paid outside asset (a product photographer, or the video editor implied by the static-first rule in marketing-paid-creative-testing). Scored on the merit it will have then: a narrow sourcing heuristic that overlaps the existing TikTok-browsing and Backstage entries in marketing-creator-content-sourcing, though it pairs usefully with the track-the-winners rule already parked there.
