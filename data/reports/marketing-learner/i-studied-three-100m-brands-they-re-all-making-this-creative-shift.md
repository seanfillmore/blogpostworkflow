## Adopted

### Make an iteration 'meaningful' by keeping a winning ad's angle and storyline and re-rendering it in a different format (animation, storytelling native, green-screen yapper over phone-shot B-roll, static, podcast, story post). Do not produce look-alike variations of the same format with a reworded script. Each new format captures more share of the same market. — 7/10

**Why it works:** Meta groups variations that look and sound the same into the same delivery pocket, so they find no new buyers. Different segments of one avatar prefer different formats. Some go from memes into an advertorial, some from animation straight to the product page, and others respond to podcasts or story posts. A proven angle in one format reaches only the slice that consumes that format. Porting the market-validated angle and story into a new format opens new audience pockets without the risk of testing an unproven message. Example: dissect a native long-caption text-post ad into its angle and storyline, then reshoot it as a green-screen authority talking head over phone-shot B-roll with a rewritten script.

**Evidence:** One client winner spending $4–5.2K/day at 2.9x ROAS. It came from converting a native long-caption text-post ad into a green-screen authority talking head over phone-shot B-roll. He says this happened in five or six other instances this year, and a second excerpt repeats the same native-to-green-screen-yapper example as working 'super well'. He also points to the thousands of active ads at the three nine-figure brands.

**Fit:** Fits the live $50/day creative-test campaign directly, and it targets the binding constraint, conversion rate. Re-using an angle that already converts reduces message risk, so only the format is under test. Sean films, green-screens and edits himself, so this is runnable today with a phone and CapCut. Steps: (1) Take a SKIN-line angle that has performed (lotion, cream or sets), such as a static, a native text-post or story post, or a Trybe UGC clip. (2) Reshoot it as a founder green-screen yap over phone-shot product B-roll on RSC's own Reels/TikTok first. (3) Port it to a static and to Meta primary text, one transplant per test. Deodorant, soap and lip balm should not be cold-paid fronts. This is distinct from existing claims: 'dissect a breakthrough ad' varies components within a format, the Andromeda claim crosses avatars, 'change the entry point' shifts awareness level, and visual distinctness covers static batches only. None of them names format porting as the expansion axis.

### Build a creative matrix: a sheet of every winning concept (yours or a competitor's), broken down by avatar, hook style, hook messaging, body messaging, winning pain point and winning outcome. Build new ads by keeping about 80% of the winning elements and changing one element. — 6/10

**Why it works:** Breaking winners into named, swappable components shows which elements carry performance. New ads then reuse proven parts and vary one thing deliberately, which raises hit rate compared with writing concepts from scratch.

**Evidence:** Assertion, plus his agency's own process and a downloadable sheet.

**Fit:** A solo operator can maintain this in one spreadsheet today. Competitor winners from the ad library fill it out even while RSC's own winners are few. It works as a generation and recombination tool, distinct from the after-the-fact tagging in performance-pattern-analysis Step 6 and from the what×who×when value matrix. The partial overlap with those keeps the score from going higher. It feeds the one-variable iteration discipline the $50/day test campaign needs.

**Target skill:** `marketing-paid-creative-testing` (edit)

### Run long-form (3–8 minute) Pixar-style animated story ads, which work especially well with women 40+. — 5/10

**Why it works:** The format reads as entertainment rather than advertising, so viewers watch, engage and even praise the ad. Older demographics find the animation charming where younger viewers find it silly.

**Evidence:** A Rise animation ad with about 50M views and comments such as 'the only reason I bought the product is this ad'. He also says the format is working for his own clients targeting 40+ women.

**Fit:** This is a real format with an engagement mechanism. Existing AI video and B-roll skills cover solo production, so it is runnable today. One animated story can enter the $50/day test as a single concept, with a short cut posted on RSC's own Reels first. Two things hold the score at 5: the source does not establish whether RSC's buyer skews 40+ women, and the 3–8 minute length is unproven for a low-price consumable.

**Target skill:** `marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats` (edit)

**Merged from:** part 1 of 2

### Never use stock footage in ads. Use only phone-shot organic B-roll so the ad looks like someone is actually recording. — 5/10

**Why it works:** Stock footage signals advertising and triggers the 'ick' scroll-past. Phone-shot B-roll keeps the piece native to the feed.

**Evidence:** Assertion. It is part of the winning green-screen format he describes.

**Fit:** Runnable today, since filming his own B-roll on a phone is within his current capability. It is a concrete video production rule the short-form production skill lacks. Image-side equivalents already exist ('camera roll, not stock', and raw phone frames for low-price products), so the added value is moderate.

**Target skill:** `marketing-short-form-video-production` (edit)

**Merged from:** part 1 of 2

### Make podcast-style ads, because people watch three-hour podcasts but skip a one-minute ad. — 4/10

**Why it works:** Copy the format of content people voluntarily spend hours on. The ad delivers the education and entertainment they came to the feed for, and the product comes up organically inside it.

**Evidence:** Assertion only.

**Fit:** A solo version works today: he films himself in a podcast-mic setup talking through an ingredient or the switch to natural deodorant, posts it on RSC's own channels, then runs the best cut. The existing 'camouflage as the medium' claim covers the principle but names no format. This is a specific format, but the evidence is assertion only, so it scores modestly.

**Target skill:** `marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats` (edit)
