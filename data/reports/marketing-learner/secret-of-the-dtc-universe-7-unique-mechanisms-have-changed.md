# Secret of the DTC Universe #7: Unique Mechanisms have changed

**Creator:** Stefan Georgi  
**Source:** social post — `secret-of-the-dtc-universe-7-unique-mechanisms-have-changed`  
**Published:** unknown (not supplied via --published)  
**Inferred era cues:** Post-ChatGPT era: buyers 'increasingly relying on LLMs to answer all of their health questions'; TikTok, Reels, YouTube and podcasts named as primary health-info channels; a GLP-1 brand scaled from $20M to $32M to $50M over 2.5 years, which places the writing in roughly 2024–2025 given the GLP-1 telehealth boom; posted on X. No explicit year stated.  

Georgi, who popularised the 'unique mechanism' in direct response, argues the classic long-form Unique Mechanism of the Problem — 1,500–2,000 words explaining the hidden physiological root cause before you ever mention the product — is now mostly dead weight. His reasons are that buyers are far more informed (LLMs, TikTok, podcasts, decades of ads) and that even older demos no longer consume long-form VSLs natively. His replacement is narrower: the mechanism today is a compare-and-contrast device whose only job is to say 'other solutions don't do X, ours does', with the problem-mechanism and solution-mechanism collapsed into a single move. He walks a UV-light robot vacuum example to show where the mechanism should stop (name the culprit and why competing products miss it; do not go on to explain inflammation or the science of UV), gives three places to find a differentiator in a supplement (delivery format, a genuinely complementary ingredient, concentration of the active), and closes with the commodity case: if the product has no differentiator at all, either stop selling it or compete on price, service and delivery — citing his GLP-1 brand scaling $20M→$50M with no unique mechanism copy whatsoever.

Found 6 tactics: 5 adopted, 1 rejected.

## Adopted

### Write the mechanism as a compare-and-contrast statement — 'the real problem with other products is that they don't do X, and ours does' — collapsing the problem-mechanism and the solution-mechanism into a single move. — 7/10

**Why it works:** When the prospect already accepts the problem exists, the only remaining job of mechanism copy is differentiation, so the mechanism should be framed against the alternatives rather than against the buyer's ignorance. Naming the specific thing competing products fail to do converts an abstract formulation detail into a reason to choose you, and it does the work of both mechanism halves in one sentence instead of two sections.

**Evidence:** Worked illustration only: a $500 robot vacuum where the buyer needs no convincing the floor is dirty, so the mechanism reduces to superior suction or a UV light that kills bacteria most competitors lack. Plus a turmeric example and a hair-loss example. No performance data.

**Fit:** Durable-principle class (positioning), age irrelevant, and it fills a specific hole. marketing-awareness-level-messaging already tells RSC that a mature category demands a named mechanism, but never says what that mechanism copy's job is — this says it is differentiation, not education. It also lines up with assets RSC already builds: marketing-product-image-stack caps the us-vs-them graphic at three or four attributes sourced from competitor one-star reviews, and this is the sentence-level version of the same move for PDP body copy and Amazon bullets ('most natural deodorants rely on baking soda, which is what burns; ours does not, it uses X'). Free, editorial, no traffic or budget required. Held at 7 because the differentiating attribute must be a defensible formulation fact rather than an invented one in a body-care category, and because it partly overlaps the prior-attempt walkthrough already in marketing-conversion-copy-angles — the additive part is that the *mechanism* is the comparison, not a separate education block preceding it.

**Target skill:** `marketing-awareness-level-messaging` (edit)

### Find the differentiator in one of a small set of places — the delivery format, a genuinely novel complementary ingredient, the concentration of the active, or intervening at an earlier stage of the problem than competitors do. — 7/10

**Why it works:** A named mechanism has to be anchored to something physically true about the product, and in a commoditised category the true differences cluster in a few predictable places. Enumerating them turns 'find a mechanism' from a creative act into a checklist you run against your own formulation, and the earlier-stage variant works because it reframes competitors as arriving too late rather than as simply worse.

**Evidence:** Turmeric worked example listing pill vs liquid vs liposomal delivery, a complementary ingredient, and 99% curcumin/curcuminoid concentration. Plus a hair-loss P.S. example: most treatments target the Z stage of the hair-loss cycle, but hair loss really starts at Y. Illustrative only, no data.

**Fit:** Durable-principle class (positioning), age irrelevant, and it supplies the missing generator for the sophistication rule already in marketing-awareness-level-messaging, which demands a named mechanism in a saturated category without saying where to find one. Every slot has an honest form here: the baking-soda-free formulation is the complementary-ingredient swap, stick vs cream vs bar is the delivery axis, and the earlier-stage angle is unusually exact — competing natural deodorants are judged on whether they work once you have switched, while RSC can position around surviving the transition period itself, which is both the known churn point and the binding retention constraint. Free, editorial, feeds PDP copy, Amazon bullets and A+ blocks. Held at 7 because every candidate differentiator must be a real, defensible formulation fact, and the specificity and body-care claim rules in marketing-conversion-copy-angles have to gate anything this generates.

**Target skill:** `marketing-awareness-level-messaging` (edit)

### Stop writing the long root-cause education section — the market is already informed enough that explaining the hidden physiological cause of their problem is counterproductive rather than persuasive. — 6/10

**Why it works:** Buyers have seen thousands of ads and now get their category education from LLMs, short-form video and podcasts, so they arrive already holding a working explanation. Telling them the 'REAL' cause is something exotic contradicts what they already believe and reads as a sales device, and the long-form formats that used to carry that education no longer feel native even to the older demos that used to sit through them.

**Evidence:** Assertion plus two observations: that 'Grandma Sue' is now on TikTok so long-form VSLs no longer feel native to her either, and that in the GLP-1 category — where awareness is near-total — literally nobody spends time on mechanism copy. Self-reported scaling numbers on his own GLP-1 brand ($20M→$32M→$50M over 2.5 years) but no copy test comparing a long UMP against a short one.

**Fit:** Durable-principle class (positioning and market sophistication), so age is irrelevant — and the era cues here actually support it rather than date it. It lands on a real temptation: marketing-awareness-level-messaging already tells RSC that natural deodorant is a saturated category requiring a named mechanism, and a solo operator drafting PDP or A+ copy with an LLM will produce exactly the long apocrine-gland-and-bacteria explanation this rule says to cut. Buyers in this category already know what aluminium is, already know baking soda irritates some people, already know there is a transition period. Free, purely editorial, applies to PDP body copy, Amazon A+ blocks and the non-buyer nurture emails. Held at 6 because it is a 'stop doing' rule that protects hours and page space rather than a revenue mechanism, and because at ~54 orders/month the effect of shortening a mechanism block will never be measurable.

**Target skill:** `marketing-awareness-level-messaging` (edit)

### Stop the mechanism copy the moment the differentiator has landed — do not continue into background science or category explanation the market already knows. — 6/10

**Why it works:** Every additional sentence past the differentiator is explaining something the reader already accepts, so it adds reading cost with no persuasive return and actively kills momentum toward the sale. The stopping point is the sentence where you have said what competing products fail to do and what yours does instead; anything after that is 'wasting breath'.

**Evidence:** Demonstrated by negation on the vacuum example: he sketches the mold-and-bacteria mechanism, then explicitly refuses to continue into bloodstream, organs, inflammation, immune response and chronic inflammation ('BORING ALERT. Nobody cares'), and equally refuses 300–1,000 words explaining UV technology as a category. Assertion with a worked illustration, no data.

**Fit:** Durable-principle class (copy editing discipline), age irrelevant, and it is the practical rule that makes the two entries above executable — it names the exact line at which to stop. Directly useful on PDP body copy and Amazon A+ blocks for a 12-SKU catalogue where slot count and scroll depth are both constrained. Free and solo-executable. Held at 6 because it substantially overlaps the information-fatigue rule already in marketing-conversion-friction-audit ('cut page content that forces unnecessary scrolling before the buyer can decide') — the additive part is narrow but real: that rule governs page length in general, this governs where a mechanism explanation specifically terminates, which is the block an LLM will happily overrun.

**Target skill:** `marketing-awareness-level-messaging` (edit)

### Audit each product for whether it has a differentiator at all — if it is a pure commodity, stop writing mechanism copy for it and compete on price, customer service or delivery time instead (or stop selling it). — 5/10

**Why it works:** Mechanism copy only has something to say when there is a real difference to name. Forcing a unique mechanism onto a generic product produces copy the market disbelieves, so the honest options collapse to competing on the non-product vectors buyers can still feel, or exiting the product. The GLP-1 case is the extreme: awareness is total, the molecule is identical across sellers, and the winners competed on price, economics and customer success rather than on mechanism.

**Evidence:** The generic-turmeric case stated as a rule, plus the GLP-1 anecdote — his own brand scaled $20M→$32M→$50M in 2.5 years with no unique mechanism work at all, and more aggressive competitors scaled further. Self-reported figures, no comparison against a mechanism-led competitor.

**Fit:** Durable-principle class (positioning and offer construction), age irrelevant. It has an honest translation as a per-SKU audit across the 12-SKU catalogue: the baking-soda-free deodorant has a real differentiator, an unscented body bar or a plain lip balm may not, and pretending otherwise wastes the copy slot. Capped at 5 for three reasons. It substantially restates the rule already in marketing-offer-construction — 'if your category is easy for buyers to comparison-shop, win on at least one of three value vectors: faster, less risky, or easier' — which is the same argument with a better-fitting vector list. The 'stop selling it' half is dangerous advice for a solo operator whose undifferentiated lip balm is explicitly the low-COGS entry and bundle-filler SKU under the splinter and lead-magnet rules, so it must be read as 'stop writing mechanism copy for it', not 'delist it'. And competing on delivery time is largely out of RSC's hands under FBA. Adopt only as the audit step that decides which SKUs get mechanism copy and which get value-vector copy instead.

**Target skill:** `marketing-offer-construction` (edit)

## Rejected

### To reach a wider audience than category shoppers, re-enter the same product with a classic problem-aware or unaware mechanism hook (allergies and fatigue caused by mold on your floors) rather than a product-aware one. — 4/10

**Rejected because:** Duplicates two existing entries in marketing-awareness-level-messaging — the re-entry-at-a-different-awareness-level rule and the headroom tradeoff — and adding a third variant would degrade skill triggering. The only additive fragment (that a broad entry hook may carry root-cause framing the body should not) is already captured by the mechanism stopping-point tactic adopted from this same video.

**Fit reasoning:** Reject on duplication rather than on merit. marketing-awareness-level-messaging already owns this move twice over: 'Hold the proven angle fixed and change only the entry point to make a second asset' is precisely re-entering the same body copy from a broader hook, and 'Judge an angle by its headroom, not only by how fast it converts out of the gate' already supplies the reason to do it. Adding a third near-identical entry would degrade triggering on a skill that is already long. The one genuinely new element — that the broad entry hook may use classic root-cause framing even though the body should not — is captured by the stopping-point entry adopted above. Worth noting separately that the creator's own example is invented physiology he admits he has not verified, which is exactly the failure mode a body-care catalogue cannot risk.
