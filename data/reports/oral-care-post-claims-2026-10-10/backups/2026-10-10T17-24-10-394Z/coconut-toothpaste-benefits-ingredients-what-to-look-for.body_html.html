What Is Coconut Toothpaste?

Coconut toothpaste combines coconut oil with other natural ingredients — usually baking soda, essential oils, and a natural sweetener — to clean and freshen your mouth in one ready-to-use formula. The key difference from conventional toothpaste: no SLS, no synthetic foaming agents, and often no fluoride. That matters when you want to know exactly what you're putting in your mouth twice a day.

Coconut oil has a long history in oral care through oil pulling — swishing it around your mouth to reduce bacteria. A well-made coconut toothpaste takes that idea further by pairing coconut oil with ingredients that also polish, clean, and freshen.

How Does Coconut Oil Work in Toothpaste?

The active component is lauric acid — a fatty acid that makes up about 50% of coconut oil. Lauric acid breaks down the cell membranes of harmful oral bacteria, including Streptococcus mutans, the main bacteria linked to tooth decay and plaque. When those membranes break down, the bacteria can't survive.

A 2015 study in the Journal of Clinical and Diagnostic Research found coconut oil showed significant activity against S. mutans and reduced plaque and gingivitis scores comparably to chlorhexidine — a common prescription mouth rinse. You can find the study on PubMed.

Coconut oil also has mild emollient properties, meaning it soothes soft tissue and is gentler on gums than formulas built around harsh detergents. One honest note: it works best as part of a consistent routine. No toothpaste does all the work on its own, and regular dental checkups still matter.

Which Coconut Oil Toothpastes Actually Work?

Not all coconut toothpastes deliver. Many use coconut oil as a marketing claim rather than a functional ingredient. Here's how to tell the difference:

  • Coconut oil near the top of the ingredient list. Ingredients are listed from highest to lowest concentration. If it's near the bottom, there's very little of it in the formula.
  • Complementary active ingredients. Baking soda adds polishing power. Essential oils like peppermint, spearmint, clove, and cinnamon add antimicrobial support and natural freshness. Wildcrafted myrrh is a traditional oral care ingredient with a long antibacterial history.
  • No SLS, saccharin, artificial dyes, parabens, or carrageenan. These appear in conventional and even some "natural" toothpastes — always check the label. Our post on 7 ingredients to avoid in natural toothpaste is a useful reference.
  • A format that fits your routine. Gel or paste, squeeze bottle or jar — texture is personal. A squeeze bottle keeps things hygienic and makes dosing easy.

Is Coconut Toothpaste Good for Your Teeth Every Day?

Yes — when formulated without harsh abrasives, coconut toothpaste is gentle enough for daily use. Coconut oil is mild on both enamel and gum tissue, which makes it a good fit if you have sensitivity. The baking soda in a well-made formula is a low-abrasion polisher, not a scrub. Use it morning and night like any other toothpaste and you'll be fine. If you have specific concerns about cavities, talk to your dentist — they know your mouth best.

How to Make Homemade Coconut Oil Toothpaste with Baking Soda

If you want to try a DIY version first, this simple recipe works as a starting point.

What you need:

  • 2 tablespoons coconut oil (softened)
  • 1 tablespoon baking soda
  • 10–15 drops peppermint or spearmint essential oil
  • Optional: a small amount of xylitol for sweetness

How to make it: Mix everything until smooth. Store in a small glass jar. Use within 2–3 weeks.

Making your own gives you full control over ingredients. But there are trade-offs: short shelf life, variable texture batch to batch, and no way to guarantee consistent ratios. A well-formulated commercial coconut toothpaste gives you the same clean ingredients in a stable, ready-to-use form.
