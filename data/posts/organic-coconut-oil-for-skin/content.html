Why Organic Coconut Oil Is Good for Skin

Organic virgin coconut oil is pressed from coconuts grown without synthetic pesticides. It is not bleached or heavily processed. That means it keeps its natural mix of fatty acids, mainly lauric acid (about 50%), plus caprylic and capric acid.

These fatty acids are what make coconut oil feel so rich on skin. They soften it, help seal in water, and support gentle cleansing.

You can also turn the oil into soap. In our Bar Soap, the oil is "saponified," which simply means it has been combined with a natural alkali to become real soap. You get a clean lather that works with your skin rather than stripping it.

The oil and the soap do different jobs. The oil stays on your skin as a moisturizer. The soap rinses clean and leaves skin feeling soft without a greasy film.

Virgin vs. Refined Coconut Oil: What's the Difference?

  • Virgin (unrefined): Minimal processing, mild coconut scent, full antioxidant and fatty acid profile. This is the base for our Bar Soap and our lotion, because it keeps more of the beneficial compounds intact.
  • Deodorized (refined): Steam-processed to remove scent. Same core fatty acids, slightly less antioxidant activity. Common in mass-market products.
  • Fractionated: Coconut oil with some fatty acids removed so it stays liquid at room temperature. It feels lighter, but it is a more processed oil. It is often used as a carrier oil for essential oils.
  • Cold-pressed: Describes how the oil is pulled from the coconut, without high heat. Cold-pressed and unrefined together means the most nutrient retention.

Bottom line: For skin, virgin organic coconut oil is the better choice. It brings the full fatty acid profile plus natural antioxidants.

Benefits of Organic Coconut Oil for Skin

Moisture and Barrier Support

Coconut oil is rich in medium-chain fatty acids. These are shorter fats that soak in easily, and lauric acid is the best known. They help your skin hold on to water, so it feels softer and less tight. Many people find virgin coconut oil a comforting option for mild to moderate dry skin. It's especially useful for extremely dry skin.

Antimicrobial Properties

Lauric acid has been shown in studies to work against certain bacteria and fungi. That's one reason coconut oil is a popular choice for everyday skin care, including our Bar Soap with added tea tree oil. It's not a substitute for medical treatment, but it's a nice bonus.

How to Use Organic Coconut Oil on Your Skin

As a Body Moisturizer

Warm a small amount of oil between your palms. Smooth it over damp skin right after your shower. A little goes a long way. If you'd rather skip the greasy feel, use a coconut oil body lotion instead.

As Your Daily Body Wash

Lather a bar in your hands or onto a washcloth, then apply to damp skin. The coconut oil base rinses cleanly. It's also gentle enough for frequent hand washing, without the drying effect of synthetic detergent soaps.

As a Shaving Aid

Work up a lather from the bar, or smooth on a thin layer of oil, before shaving legs. The coconut oil softens hair and lubricates skin, reducing razor drag and irritation. Rinse thoroughly after.

On Dry Lips and Cuticles

A tiny dab of oil softens dry cuticles and chapped lips. Around the lips and chin, a gentle lather also removes residue without harsh surfactants (the strong cleansers in many washes).

For Baby Skin

A little organic coconut oil can be used on dry patches on baby skin. Always do a patch test first and check with your pediatrician before using on newborns or broken skin.

When to Be Careful With Coconut Oil

Coconut oil works well for most skin types. Here are the situations where you should use caution.

  • Acne-prone or oily facial skin: Coconut oil has a comedogenic rating of around 4 out of 5. That's a scale of 0 to 5 for how likely an oil is to clog pores, so 4 means it often can. Our tea tree bar soap is better suited for acne-prone skin, but still patch test first. Keep heavy oils off acne-prone facial skin.
  • Coconut allergy: Rare, but real. Check with your allergist before using any topical coconut oil product if you have a tree nut or coconut allergy.
  • Essential oil sensitivities: Our bars contain organic lavender, tea tree, or lemongrass essential oils depending on the variety. If you react to any of these, choose accordingly or patch test first.
  • Diagnosed skin conditions: Coconut oil can support mild dryness, but it's not a treatment for eczema, psoriasis, or other conditions. Work with a dermatologist for persistent issues.

Patch test tip: Apply a small amount to the inside of your wrist or elbow. Wait 24 hours. Redness, itching, or irritation means don't use it on larger areas.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is organic coconut oil as moisturizing as a body lotion?

They work a little differently. Pure oil seals in moisture but contains no water, so it can feel heavy. A lotion blends oil with water so it spreads easily and absorbs faster. For very dry skin, you can use coconut oil soap to cleanse, then lotion to seal.

Does coconut oil clog pores?

Coconut oil carries a comedogenic rating of around 4 out of 5, meaning it can clog pores for some people. As a rinse-off product, bar soap poses less risk than a leave-on oil. If you have acne-prone facial skin, use caution and patch test first.

Can I use coconut oil bar soap every day?

Yes, for most skin types. It's gentle enough for daily use. If you notice dryness or irritation, reduce frequency or follow up with a moisturizer after each wash.

Is coconut oil safe for sensitive skin?

Generally yes, especially varieties without added essential oils. Our bars use organic essential oils rather than synthetic fragrance, which makes them easier to tolerate for many sensitive-skin users. Always patch test before wide use.
