The best Ivory soap alternatives are pure castile, unscented natural bar soap, coconut oil bar soap, goat milk soap, tallow soap, and foaming hand soap. Each has a short ingredient list and rinses clean without the harsh stripping many people associate with conventional bars. Check the label on any bar you buy, since formulas can change over time.

Most Ivory loyalists want the same things: a simple bar, little or no scent, a mild feel, a fair price, and a soap that rinses clean. We've matched each option below to those needs. Updated October 2026 by the Real Skin Care team.

1. Pure Castile Soap

Castile soap is a plant-oil soap, traditionally made with olive oil. Soap is made by saponification, which means mixing oils with an alkali so they turn into soap (FDA overview of soap). Best for: people who want a very short ingredient list. Trade-off: some people find it drying or a little harsh. Keep it on your body rather than your face unless you know it suits you.

2. Unscented Natural Bar Soap

This is the closest match for anyone who wants a plain bar. Look for saponified plant oils, maybe glycerin, and no synthetic fragrance or dyes. Best for: sensitive-feeling skin and fragrance-averse households. Trade-off: "unscented" and "fragrance-free" aren't always the same thing. An unscented bar can still contain ingredients that cover up odor, so read the whole label.

3. Coconut Oil Bar Soap

Coconut oil makes a rich lather and a squeaky-clean feel, which is exactly what many Ivory fans miss. Our coconut oil bar soap is our pick for a simple, effective everyday bar. Best for: normal to oily skin and anyone who loves a big lather. Trade-off: bars with a lot of coconut oil clean strongly. If your skin is very dry, they may feel drying, so moisturize afterward.

4. Goat Milk Soap

Goat milk soap is usually a creamier, softer-feeling bar, and it's often handmade. Many people find it gentle. Best for: dry skin that wants a more cushioned feel. Trade-off: handmade bars vary from maker to maker, and many are scented or colored. Check the label. For more detail, read our guide to goat milk soap benefits and ingredients.

5. Tallow Soap

Tallow soap is made from rendered beef fat. It's a traditional soapmaking ingredient, and it's the old-fashioned bar you may picture on a farmhouse sink. Best for: people who like a classic, hard, long-lasting bar. Trade-off: it isn't vegan, and some people dislike the animal source. Here's the full rundown on tallow soap benefits and alternatives.

6. Foaming Hand Soap

If you mostly used Ivory at the sink, a foaming pump may suit you better. Our organic foaming hand soap starts with saponified organic virgin coconut oil. It's lightly scented with organic citrus, spearmint, and lavender essential oils, so it isn't fragrance-free. One pump makes plenty of foam, which makes it easy for frequent washing. When you run low, a foam soap refill lets you top off the same bottle. Trade-off: if you need zero scent, choose a bar from option 2.

How to Choose the Right Ivory Soap Alternative

Match the soap to your main need:

  • Sensitive skin: Start with an unscented natural bar and patch test it.
  • Dry skin: Try goat milk soap, and moisturize after washing.
  • Frequent hand washing: Use a foaming hand soap.
  • Budget: Bars tend to last a while, and refills can cut down on packaging.
  • Simple ingredients: Choose castile or a short-list coconut bar.

On the label, look for a short list of saponified oils, no dyes, and little or no fragrance. To patch test, dab a little lathered soap on your inner arm, rinse, and wait a day. You can find more ideas in our gentle natural soap options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the closest alternative to Ivory soap? An unscented natural bar soap made with saponified plant oils is the nearest match. It's plain, simple, and rinses clean. A coconut oil bar is a good pick if you like a bigger lather.

Is Ivory soap natural? We can't say what's in the current formula. Check the label or the manufacturer's page for the latest ingredient list, since formulas can change over time.

Is castile soap good for sensitive skin? Many people tolerate it well. Others find it drying because soap is naturally alkaline. Patch test first, and ask a dermatologist if you have a skin condition.
