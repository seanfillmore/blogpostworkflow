How to make natural toothpaste: mix baking soda with a little water and, if you like, add coconut oil or peppermint for a gentle cleaning paste that takes about five minutes. Homemade versions are not a replacement for fluoride toothpaste, which has the strongest evidence for protecting teeth from cavities. Below are three simple recipes, plus the safety facts most DIY posts skip.

Quick Facts

  • Time: About 5 minutes.
  • Difficulty: Easy.
  • Yield: A few days' supply (Recipes A and B) or 2–4 weeks (Recipe C).
  • Cost: Roughly $1–3 per batch if you already have the basics.
  • Materials: Baking soda, fine sea salt, coconut oil, peppermint oil, water, a small bowl, measuring spoons, and a glass jar with a lid.

What Homemade Toothpaste Can (and Can't) Do

Homemade toothpaste can help you scrub away surface film and leave your mouth feeling fresh. That's about it. It has no fluoride, and that matters. A Cochrane review of fluoride toothpastes found they help prevent cavities (evidence: strong). The NIDCR and the American Dental Association both explain why fluoride is a cornerstone of cavity care.

Baking soda is a mild abrasive. That means it gently scrubs. Some research suggests it may help remove plaque, but results vary from study to study (evidence: mixed). A homemade paste is best thought of as a simple way to freshen your mouth, not a way to kill germs or rebuild enamel. So we won't make those claims. source

Talk with your dentist first if you have cavities, gum disease, sensitive teeth, or you're making this for a child. Best Ingredients for Natural Toothpaste (and What to Avoid)

A good homemade toothpaste keeps things short and simple. Here's what works, and what to leave out. Each item has an evidence label so you know how much weight to give it.

Use These

  • Baking soda: A mild abrasive that gently cleans. Some studies suggest it may help with plaque, but findings are mixed. See the research on dentifrices and plaque. (Mixed)
  • Fine sea salt: A tiny pinch adds a little scrubbing power and flavor. More is not better. (Weak)
  • Coconut oil: Gives the paste a creamy texture. One small study looked at coconut oil and gum inflammation, but the evidence is limited. Read the study. Think of it as a base, not a treatment. (Weak/mixed)
  • Peppermint or spearmint oil: A few drops for flavor. Use food-grade oil. (Flavor only)
  • Stevia or xylitol: Optional sweeteners that soften the salty taste. (Taste only)

Skip These

  • Lemon juice and other acids: Acid can wear down enamel over time. Studies on dental erosion point to acidic drinks and foods as a risk. (Strong concern)
  • Activated charcoal: A 2017 JADA literature review found its safety and effectiveness are not well established. (Weak)
  • Undiluted cinnamon oil: It can irritate your gums and the inside of your mouth. (Safety concern)
  • Strong hydrogen peroxide: It can be harsh on tissue. Skip it.
  • Too much salt or baking soda: More grit means a harsher scrub, not a cleaner mouth.

One bonus: these recipes are naturally free of SLS (sodium lauryl sulfate, a foaming agent) and glycerin, because you never add them. If that matters to you, see why glycerin-free toothpaste matters.

Step 1 — Gather Your Ingredients and Tools

Set everything out before you start so the mixing takes minutes. Clean, dry tools keep your paste fresh longer.

  • Bowl: A small glass or stainless steel bowl.
  • Spoon: A clean, dry spoon or spatula. Use a fresh one each time you scoop.
  • Jar: A small airtight glass jar (for Recipe C).
  • Measuring spoons: Teaspoons and tablespoons.
  • Ingredients: Baking soda, fine sea salt, organic virgin coconut oil, peppermint oil, and water.

Coconut oil turns solid below about 76°F, so your paste may be firm in winter and soft in summer. That's normal.

Step 2 — Choose and Mix Your Base Recipe

Here are three options, from simplest to creamiest. Pick the one that fits your texture and taste preferences. These are easy homemade toothpaste recipes, and none take more than five minutes.

Recipe A — Baking Soda and Water (Simplest)

This is the basic way to use baking soda as toothpaste. It tastes salty and a little bitter, so expect that.

  1. Add 2 teaspoons of baking soda to a small bowl.
  2. Stir in about 1/2 teaspoon of water.
  3. Mix to a thick paste. Add water a few drops at a time if needed.
  4. Use it right away. Make it fresh each time. Don't store it.

Recipe B — Baking Soda, Salt and Peppermint

This one tastes better and still takes under two minutes. It's a small-batch, fresh-use paste.

  1. Mix 2 teaspoons of baking soda with 1/8 teaspoon of fine sea salt.
  2. Add 2–3 drops of food-grade peppermint oil.
  3. Stir in water, a few drops at a time, until it's a thick paste.
  4. Toss any leftovers within a few days.

Don't add extra salt for "extra scrubbing." It can make the paste harsh and may irritate your gums.

Recipe C — Coconut Oil Toothpaste (Creamiest)

This is the homemade toothpaste recipe that feels most like store-bought. It makes about 1/3 cup.

  1. Soften 3 tablespoons of coconut oil until it's spreadable, but not fully melted.
  2. Stir in 3 tablespoons of baking soda until smooth.
  3. Add 5–10 drops of peppermint oil and, if you like, a pinch of stevia.
  4. Spoon it into a clean glass jar and close the lid.

It keeps about 2–4 weeks. Toss it if the smell or look changes. Scoop with a clean spoon each time. Coconut oil can harden in pipes, so spit into the trash or wipe the sink afterward. Step 3 — Flavor and Sweeten Safely

Flavor is about taste, not treatment. Use food-grade peppermint or spearmint oil, a few drops only. Never swallow the paste, and keep essential oils away from young children and pets. The ASPCA Animal Poison Control has guidance if a pet gets into them.

Want cinnamon? Use a tiny pinch of ground cinnamon, less than 1/8 teaspoon. Skip cinnamon essential oil, which can burn. And if you sweeten with xylitol, remember it's very toxic to dogs, per the ASPCA. Store it well out of reach. Stevia is a simpler choice if you have pets.

Step 4 — Mix, Test the Texture and Adjust

Aim for something like thick frosting. It should hold on a brush without dripping.

  • Too dry or crumbly: Add a few drops of water, or a bit more coconut oil in Recipe C.
  • Too runny: Stir in a little more baking soda.
  • Test first: Dab a small amount on your gum and wait a minute. If anything stings, don't use it.

Step 5 — Store and Use It Properly

Store Recipe C in an airtight glass jar in a cool, dry spot. Write the date on the lid. Always use a clean spoon and never double-dip.

Use a pea-sized amount. Brush gently for two minutes. Don't scrub hard, because the grit can wear on gums and enamel if you press too much.

Stop using it if your gums burn, your teeth feel more sensitive, or you notice any irritation. Kids shouldn't use DIY pastes unless a dentist or pediatrician okays it. See our guide to SLS-free toothpaste for kids for gentler options.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is homemade toothpaste safe?
It can be low risk when made with simple ingredients and used gently. Avoid acids, charcoal, and strong essential oils. Check with your dentist, especially if you have cavities, gum disease, or sensitivity.

Can I use baking soda as toothpaste every day?
Some people do, but baking soda is mildly abrasive. Brush gently, and stop if your teeth or gums feel irritated. A dentist can tell you whether it suits your mouth.

Does homemade toothpaste have fluoride?
No. These recipes don't contain fluoride, which has strong evidence for helping prevent cavities, per the NIDCR. Ask your dentist what's right for you.

How long does it last?
Recipes A and B should be used fresh or within a few days. Recipe C lasts about 2–4 weeks in a clean, sealed jar. Throw it out if the smell or look changes.

Can I make it for kids?
Check with a dentist or pediatrician first. Kids may swallow paste, and they have different fluoride needs. Our kids' SLS-free toothpaste guide can help.

Is coconut oil toothpaste effective?
The evidence is limited. One small study looked at coconut oil and gum inflammation, but more research is needed. It works best as a creamy base.

Wrapping Up

You now have three easy ways to make natural toothpaste: baking soda and water, baking soda with salt and peppermint, and a creamy coconut oil version. Keep the mix gentle, skip acids and charcoal, and stop if anything irritates. Homemade paste has no fluoride, so ask your dentist what your teeth need.
