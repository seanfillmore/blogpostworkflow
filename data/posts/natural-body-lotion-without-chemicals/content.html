Harmful Ingredients to Avoid in Conventional Body Lotion

These are the most common offenders in mainstream lotions. None of them are necessary for effective hydration.

  • Parabens (methylparaben, propylparaben, butylparaben): Synthetic preservatives that mimic estrogen in the body, potentially disrupting hormone function. Clean formulas achieve the same stability with plant-based alternatives.
  • Synthetic fragrance / "parfum": One word that can hide hundreds of undisclosed compounds. Under current FDA rules, fragrance formulas are trade secrets — brands don't have to list individual ingredients, including phthalates, which are known endocrine disruptors. Clean brands list each essential oil by name instead.
  • Mineral oil and petrolatum: Cheap petroleum byproducts used as occlusives. They seal the skin surface but deliver no nourishment — no fatty acids, no antioxidants. Plant-based occlusives do the same job and more.
  • Propylene glycol: A penetration enhancer that can irritate sensitive or eczema-prone skin. Gentle plant-derived humectants do the same job without the irritation risk.
  • Formaldehyde-releasing preservatives (DMDM hydantoin, imidazolidinyl urea): These slowly release small amounts of formaldehyde over time. Formaldehyde is a known irritant and classified as a human carcinogen at high exposures by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC). Low-level exposure in skincare is unlikely to cause acute harm, but there's no skin benefit that justifies including them.
  • Synthetic colors (FD&C dyes): Petroleum-derived colorants that add nothing to how a lotion performs. Some people react to them with skin sensitization.

Avoiding these doesn't mean settling for a less effective moisturizer. Our non-toxic body lotion collection shows exactly what's possible without any of them.

Natural Ingredients That Actually Moisturize

Good moisturization works on three levels: humectants attract water to the skin, emollients soften it, and occlusives seal moisture in so it doesn't evaporate. The best clean body lotions cover all three.

Humectants: In general, plant-derived humectants such as glycerin and aloe vera gel draw moisture from the environment into the upper layers of skin. These are general examples of the category. Neither one is in our lotion, which relies on its plant oils instead.

Emollients — where plant oils shine: Organic virgin coconut oil is rich in lauric acid, a medium-chain fatty acid with mild antimicrobial properties, and caprylic/capric acid, which absorbs quickly without a greasy feel. How to Choose a Natural Body Lotion for Your Skin Type

Dry or Extremely Dry Skin

Look for formulas where coconut oil or another rich plant oil appears high on the ingredient list. Apply right after showering — while skin is still slightly damp — to lock in surface moisture before it evaporates. For very rough or flaky skin, layer: apply a plant-based body oil first, then follow with a richer lotion or body butter on top.

Sensitive Skin

Fragrance is the most common trigger. Even "natural fragrance" can cause reactions. Your safest options are a fragrance-free body lotion or one that uses named individual essential oils at low concentrations. Keep the ingredient list short and stick to recognizable, plant-derived ingredients. Avoid formulas where ethanol appears in the first half of the list — it can disrupt your skin barrier. For more guidance, see our best body lotion for sensitive skin roundup.

Aging or Mature Skin

Aging skin produces fewer natural lipids and repairs its barrier more slowly. It needs both deep emollients and antioxidant-rich ingredients. Consistent daily use of a clean, plant-oil-based formula will do more over time than occasional use of a high-tech product.

Body Lotion vs. Body Butter vs. Body Oil — Which Format Is Right?

Body lotion is a water-and-oil emulsion. It's lighter, absorbs quickly, and works well for daily all-over use on normal to dry skin. Most people do well starting here.

Body butter contains little to no water — mostly oils and butters. It's richer and slower to absorb, making it best for very dry patches (elbows, knees, heels) or overnight use.

Body oil is pure lipid — no water. Applied to damp skin, it absorbs well and leaves a healthy glow. It's excellent for dry and mature skin, either alone or layered under a lotion. Clean body oils use plant-based alternatives to the mineral oil found in most conventional formulas.

The format matters less than what's inside it. A clean lotion will outperform a chemical-laden body butter every time.

How to Read a Body Lotion Ingredient Label

You don't need a chemistry degree. You need to know four things:

  1. Order matters. Ingredients are listed from highest to lowest concentration. If your "coconut oil lotion" lists coconut oil tenth, there's very little of it in the bottle.
  2. "Fragrance" or "parfum" is a red flag. Clean brands list individual essential oils by their botanical names (e.g., Lavandula angustifolia oil). A single "fragrance" entry means undisclosed ingredients.
  3. Certifications help. USDA Organic, EWG Verified, COSMOS, and Leaping Bunny all require third-party verification. They're faster than auditing every ingredient yourself.
  4. "Natural" and "clean" are unregulated terms. The FDA doesn't define either for cosmetics. The ingredient list is the only reliable source of truth.

The best practice: find a brand whose entire formulation philosophy you trust, so you don't have to audit every bottle. That's why we publish the full ingredient list for every product we make.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there really such a thing as a body lotion without chemicals?

Not literally — every ingredient is a chemical compound. But a natural body lotion without harmful chemicals is completely achievable. It means avoiding synthetic preservatives, petrochemical fillers, and undisclosed fragrance compounds, and replacing them with plant-based ingredients that your skin can actually use.

Are natural body lotions as effective as conventional ones?

Yes — when formulated correctly. Plant oils cover all three moisturization functions (humectant, emollient, occlusive) without the additives. The key is finding a formula where beneficial ingredients appear high on the list, not as an afterthought.

What preservatives are used in natural body lotions instead of parabens?

Common clean alternatives include vitamin E (tocopherol), rosemary extract, and combinations of naturally derived compounds like sodium levulinate or gluconolactone. Our lotion uses organic grapefruit seed extract. Some formulas also use a high concentration of plant oils, which have natural antimicrobial and antioxidant properties, to extend stability.

Can I use a natural body lotion on my face?

Some people do, but most body lotions — including clean ones — are formulated for thicker body skin. If you have oily or acne-prone facial skin, a face-specific formula is usually a better fit. Jojoba-based products are among the least likely to clog pores if you do want to use one product for both.
