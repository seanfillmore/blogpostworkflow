What Makes Coconut Oil Work on Skin?

Virgin coconut oil is rich in lauric acid, a medium-chain fatty acid that makes up about half its composition. Lauric acid fights bacteria, calms inflammation, and helps your skin hold onto moisture by reducing transepidermal water loss (TEWL). A 2018 review in the International Journal of Molecular Sciences (Lin et al.) confirmed that plant oils rich in these fatty acids support the skin barrier and reduce TEWL.

The rest of the profile includes caprylic acid, capric acid, vitamin E (tocopherols), and plant polyphenols — each adding antioxidant and skin-soothing effects.

Virgin vs. refined: Always choose virgin, cold-pressed, organic coconut oil for skin. Refined coconut oil is processed at high heat, which strips antioxidants and fatty acids. For daily use, that difference matters.

Core Benefits of Coconut Oil on Skin

Deep Moisturization and Barrier Support

Coconut oil works as both an emollient (softens skin) and an occlusive (slows water loss). A randomized controlled trial by Agero and Verallo-Rowell (2004), published in Dermatitis, found that virgin coconut oil significantly improved hydration and reduced TEWL compared to mineral oil in patients with mild-to-moderate dry skin. Apply to slightly damp skin right after your shower for best absorption.

Anti-Inflammatory and Soothing Effects

Lauric acid has shown anti-inflammatory activity in lab research, which may help calm redness and irritation. It's often useful for eczema, dermatitis, or reactive skin. Most studies are small or lab-based, so the evidence is promising but not definitive. That said, a thin layer of virgin coconut oil is generally well-tolerated on sensitive skin — including as a potential option for coconut oil for rosacea on the face (see FAQ below).

Antimicrobial Properties

A 2009 study by Verallo-Rowell et al. in the Journal of Drugs in Dermatology found virgin coconut oil showed antimicrobial activity against Staphylococcus aureus comparable to mupirocin, a standard topical antibiotic. This makes it a solid daily option for body skin prone to minor bacterial or fungal irritation.

Important caveat: Coconut oil has a comedogenic rating of around 4 out of 5 — meaning it can clog pores. It's better suited to body skin than daily facial use for acne-prone people.

Antioxidant Protection

Virgin coconut oil contains tocopherols and polyphenols that neutralize free radicals from UV exposure and pollution. Think of this as a complement to sunscreen — not a replacement. Coconut oil provides minimal sun protection on its own. Always wear SPF.

Wound Healing and Skin Repair

Early research — mostly animal studies and small human trials — suggests coconut oil may help accelerate wound healing and support collagen synthesis. It's a practical choice for dry cracked heels, rough elbows, or irritated skin after shaving. Skip it on open wounds or serious skin conditions without checking with your doctor first. source

Is Coconut Oil Good for Your Skin? Depends on Your Type.

  • Dry skin: One of the best natural daily moisturizers available. Apply morning or night. If raw oil feels too heavy, a coconut oil body lotion is a lighter, easier alternative.
  • Sensitive skin: Generally well-tolerated. Patch test first — apply a small amount to your inner wrist and wait 24 hours. Avoid broken or actively inflamed skin.
  • Oily or acne-prone skin (face): Not recommended for daily facial use. The high comedogenic rating can block pores and trigger breakouts. Use lighter, low-comedogenic oils like jojoba on your face instead.
  • Normal skin: Works well on the body, hands, and hair. Use on the face with caution and watch for breakouts.
  • Baby skin: A study by Evangelista et al. (2014) in the International Journal of Dermatology found virgin coconut oil safe and effective for managing atopic dermatitis in pediatric patients. Always patch test first, and check with your pediatrician before ongoing use.

Can You Use Coconut Oil as a Moisturizer Every Day?

Yes — with conditions. For body skin, daily use is safe and effective for most people. Apply a small amount to damp skin after your shower. On the face, daily use suits dry or normal skin types best. Oily or acne-prone skin types should use it sparingly on the face or skip it there entirely.

How to Use Coconut Oil on Skin Every Day

As a body moisturizer

Apply right after your shower while skin is still slightly damp. Warm a palmful between your hands and work it in from your legs up. This gives you the smoothest, most hydrated results — especially on legs year-round.

As a hand and cuticle treatment

Keep a small jar by your bed. Massage a pea-sized amount into your hands and cuticles at night. By morning — especially in dry seasons — the difference is noticeable.

As a lip balm or coconut oil balm

One of the simplest natural lip conditioners available. Apply a tiny amount before bed or throughout the day. It also works as an all-over skin balm for rough patches on elbows, knees, and heels.

As a makeup remover

A small amount on a cotton pad breaks down most makeup, including mascara. Rinse thoroughly after. Gentle enough for daily use around the eyes for most skin types.

As a scalp and hair treatment

Work a small amount into your scalp and through the ends of your hair. Leave for at least 30 minutes — or overnight — then shampoo out. Especially useful for a dry, flaky scalp.

What's the Best Coconut Oil for Skin?

The best coconut oil for skin is virgin, cold-pressed, and organic — unrefined and minimally processed. Look for those three words on the label. Refined, bleached, or deodorized versions are not ideal for skincare because processing removes key fatty acids and antioxidants.

Fractionated vs. virgin: Fractionated coconut oil has its long-chain fatty acids removed, leaving mainly caprylic and capric acid. It stays liquid at room temperature, absorbs faster, and works well as a carrier oil in formulations. The trade-off: it's lower in lauric acid, so you get fewer antimicrobial and barrier-support benefits. For the full range of benefits, virgin coconut oil is the better choice.

Organic vs. conventional: Organic virgin coconut oil retains its full fatty acid profile, antioxidants, and natural plant compounds. For daily skin use, organic virgin is the better choice — not just a marketing label.

  • Sweet almond oil: Gentle, light. Good for sensitive skin and a solid body oil.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does coconut oil clog pores on the face?

For many people, yes. Coconut oil has a comedogenic rating of about 4 out of 5. That doesn't mean it will definitely clog your pores, but the risk is real — especially if your skin is already oily or breakout-prone. If you have dry or normal skin and have used it on your face without issues, you're likely fine. If you're acne-prone, use it on your body and choose a lighter facial oil instead.

Why use coconut oil on your face?

If your skin is dry or sensitive and not prone to breakouts, coconut oil is one of the most affordable and effective natural face moisturizers available. Its anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties can help calm redness and support the skin barrier — especially in cold or dry climates. For oily or acne-prone skin, the risk outweighs the benefit on the face.

What does coconut oil do for your skin?

It moisturizes, reduces water loss, fights bacteria, and soothes inflammation. The main driver is lauric acid, which makes up about half of virgin coconut oil's composition. Can you use coconut oil for rosacea on your face?

Possibly, in small amounts. Coconut oil's anti-inflammatory properties may help calm redness, and it's generally gentle on sensitive skin. That said, some people with rosacea find oils — even gentle ones — can trigger flares. Patch test first, and check with a dermatologist if your rosacea is active or worsening.

Can you use coconut oil for dermatitis on your face?

Yes, with care. Many people find virgin coconut oil helpful for soothing atopic dermatitis, and it's often used to support skin comfort in sensitive and reactive skin. Apply a thin layer to clean, damp skin. Avoid refined oil, and stop use if redness or itching worsens.

Can virgin coconut oil remove stretch marks?

There's no strong clinical evidence that coconut oil removes stretch marks. It can improve skin hydration and elasticity, which may make stretch marks look slightly less pronounced over time. Regular moisturization may help support skin suppleness during pregnancy — but it won't erase existing marks. source

Does coconut oil make skin shiny or greasy?

It can, especially if you apply too much. Use less than you think you need, and apply it to damp skin — that helps it absorb faster and leaves less shine. On legs after a shower, a light coat gives a natural glow. On the face, it can look greasy if over-applied.

Does coconut oil make your skin darker?

No. Coconut oil has no melanin-stimulating properties. It may give skin a slight sheen when first applied, but this fades as it absorbs. It provides almost no UV protection — sun exposure without SPF is what causes darkening, not the oil itself.

Does coconut oil affect cholesterol when used on skin?

No. The dietary cholesterol debate around coconut oil doesn't apply to topical use. When applied to skin, coconut oil does not enter the bloodstream in meaningful amounts.
