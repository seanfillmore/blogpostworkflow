How a Body Moisturizer Actually Works

A body moisturizer helps your skin hold onto water and maintain a healthy protective barrier. Most effective formulas combine three types of moisturizing ingredients:

  • Humectants draw water into your skin — aloe vera and glycerin work this way.
  • Emollients soften skin by filling gaps between cells — jojoba and coconut oil are both emollients.
  • Occlusives seal moisture in with a light barrier — coconut oil and red palm oil do this naturally.

Our Coconut Moisturizer uses purified spring water as the hydrating base, jojoba as the emollient, and coconut oil as both emollient and occlusive. It's built to work, not just feel nice.

Ingredients to Avoid

Reading a label shouldn't require a chemistry degree. Here are five common ingredients worth knowing about:

  • Parabens (methylparaben, propylparaben): Synthetic preservatives linked to hormone disruption in some research. Used because they're cheap.
  • Synthetic fragrance: Listed as "fragrance" or "parfum." A single entry can represent dozens of undisclosed chemicals, including phthalates. One of the most common contact allergens in personal care.
  • Mineral oil and petrolatum: Petroleum-derived occlusives that sit on top of skin without adding any nutritive value. Cheap filler, not a feature.
  • Phthalates: Often hidden inside "fragrance." Flagged by researchers as potential endocrine disruptors.
  • SLS/SLES (sodium lauryl sulfate / sodium laureth sulfate): Harsh surfactants occasionally used in lotion bases that can strip your skin barrier over time.

None of these appear in our formula. A non-toxic body lotion should be clean all the way through — not just on the front label.

How to Apply Body Moisturizer So It Actually Works

  1. Apply within three minutes of showering. Damp skin absorbs moisturizer more effectively. Don't wait until you're fully dry.
  2. Use circular motions. This encourages absorption and gently boosts circulation, especially on legs and arms.
  3. Focus on problem areas. Elbows, knees, heels, and hands lose moisture fastest — give them an extra pass every time.
  4. Exfoliate 1–2 times per week. Dead skin cells block absorption. A gentle scrub before moisturizing means your lotion reaches living skin.
  5. Layer in winter. For very dry areas in cold months, apply a body oil first, then a body cream on top. The oil seals moisture; the cream adds a protective layer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between body lotion, body cream, and body butter?

Body lotion is the lightest — water-based and fast-absorbing, ideal for daily use on normal to dry skin. Body cream has more oil and less water, better for dry or very dry skin. Body butter is the thickest — almost entirely oil-based — and works best for rough, cracked, or extremely dry areas.

How often should you apply body moisturizer?

Once a day is enough for most people. The best time is right after showering. In winter or very dry climates, twice a day on rough spots like knees and heels can help.

Is natural body moisturizer better for sensitive skin?

Generally, yes. Synthetic fragrances and preservatives are among the most common triggers for contact dermatitis. A short, plant-based ingredient list removes most of those triggers. If you're highly reactive, start with a genuinely fragrance-free formula — like our Pure Unscented variant — rather than one that uses essential oils as fragrance. See our full guide to the best body lotion for sensitive skin.

Can you use coconut oil body lotion on your face?

Coconut oil can clog pores for some people, especially those prone to breakouts. It's generally better suited to the body than the face. If you have acne-prone or oily facial skin, stick to a non-comedogenic face moisturizer and use your body lotion from the neck down.

When is the best time to moisturize your body?

Right after showering is the most effective time — your skin is still slightly damp and more receptive to absorbing moisture. Morning application fits naturally into a daily routine and keeps skin hydrated through the day.
