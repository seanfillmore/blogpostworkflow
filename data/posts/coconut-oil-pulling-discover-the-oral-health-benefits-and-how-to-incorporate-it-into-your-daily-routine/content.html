What Is Oil Pulling?

Oil pulling means swishing edible oil around your mouth for several minutes, then spitting it out. It works like a natural mouth rinse that reaches between teeth and along the gumline — spots a toothbrush often misses.

Coconut oil is the most popular choice. It tastes mild, stays stable at room temperature, and contains lauric acid — a well-studied antibacterial compound. At roughly 50% lauric acid by weight, coconut oil has a higher concentration than almost any other natural oil. source

A 2019 review in the Journal of Traditional and Complementary Medicine confirmed that oil pulling can reduce the bacteria responsible for plaque and bad breath — making it more than just folk wisdom.

How Oil Pulling Works

When you swish coconut oil, it mixes with saliva and thins out enough to reach between teeth, along your gumline, and across your tongue. Dental plaque has a fatty outer layer the oil can bind to and carry away when you spit.

Lauric acid also disrupts the outer membranes of certain bacteria, making it harder for them to stick to teeth and gums. Less bacterial buildup means fresher breath and a cleaner-feeling mouth over time.

Oral Health Benefits of Coconut Oil Pulling

These are the most well-supported benefits — whether you're oil pulling or using a coconut oil toothpaste daily.

1. Reduces Harmful Bacteria

Lauric acid works against Streptococcus mutans — the bacteria most responsible for tooth decay. A study in the Nigerian Medical Journal found that coconut oil pulling significantly reduced S. mutans counts in saliva after just two weeks of daily use.

2. Freshens Breath

Bad breath comes from bacteria on your tongue, between your teeth, and along the gumline. These bacteria release sulfur compounds — that's the smell. Coconut oil removes those bacteria and the debris they feed on. Most people notice fresher breath within the first week.

How to Do Coconut Oil Pulling

Traditional oil pulling is simple. Here's how to incorporate it into your daily routine:

  1. Start with 1 teaspoon of organic coconut oil. It may be solid — let it melt in your mouth for a few seconds.
  2. Swish gently for 5–10 minutes. You don't need to hit 20 minutes. Most studies showing benefit used 10 minutes.
  3. Spit into the trash, not the sink. Coconut oil solidifies below 76°F and can clog pipes.
  4. Rinse with water, then brush normally.

Do this once daily, ideally in the morning before eating. Start with 5 minutes if the texture bothers you and work up gradually.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main benefits of coconut oil pulling for oral health?

The main benefits are fewer harmful bacteria (especially S. mutans), fresher breath, less plaque, and cleaner-looking teeth. These come primarily from lauric acid, which makes up about half of coconut oil's fatty acid profile. Most people notice fresher breath within the first week of daily use.

Can you brush with coconut oil instead of toothpaste?

You can, and it will reduce bacteria and freshen breath. But plain coconut oil lacks abrasives, fluoride, and remineralizing agents. For a more complete clean, a coconut oil toothpaste is the better daily choice. Learn more: Can You Use Coconut Oil as Toothpaste? Here's What Science Says.

Does oil pulling replace brushing?

No. Oil pulling is a helpful addition, not a replacement. Mechanical brushing physically removes plaque in a way swishing alone can't. Use oil pulling or coconut oil toothpaste alongside regular brushing and flossing — not instead of them.

How long should I oil pull?

Traditional protocols call for 10–20 minutes, but 5–10 minutes of consistent daily use can make a real difference. If it's hard to stick to, a coconut oil toothpaste puts coconut oil into a two-minute brush instead.
