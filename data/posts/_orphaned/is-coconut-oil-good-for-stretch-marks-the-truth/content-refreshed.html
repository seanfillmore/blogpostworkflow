Coconut oil — aceite de coco — is not clinically proven to remove stretch marks, but research does show it improves skin hydration and elasticity in ways that can reduce their appearance, especially when used early. It works best as a preventive tool and early-stage treatment, not a cure for established marks.

Used consistently during the red stage — before marks mature — coconut oil can meaningfully soften their severity. It won't erase white or silver marks. But with realistic expectations, it's one of the most useful natural options available.

This guide covers what the evidence actually says, when coconut oil works best, and exactly how to use it.

What Are Stretch Marks and Why Do They Form?

Stretch marks — medically called striae — form when skin is stretched faster than it can produce new collagen and elastin. The middle layer of skin (the dermis) tears slightly under that tension. Those small tears are what you see on the surface.

There are two stages, and they matter a lot for treatment:

  • Striae rubrae: Red or purple marks. These are newer. The skin is still actively remodeling, and this is your best window for treatment.
  • Striae albae: Silver or white marks. The tissue has become fibrous and scar-like. Topical products have limited effect here.

Common causes include pregnancy (especially the third trimester), rapid weight gain or loss, puberty growth spurts, and significant muscle building. All share one thing: the skin is stretching faster than it can keep up.

Do Stretch Marks Go Away on Their Own?

Red or purple stretch marks often fade significantly over 6 to 12 months as your skin heals naturally. Many people find them much less visible a year after they first appeared.

White or silver marks are different. At that stage, the structural change in the dermis is largely permanent — but "permanent" doesn't mean "always visible." They typically flatten and lighten over time, blending closer to your skin tone.

No topical product has been shown to fully remove mature stretch marks. Clinical options like laser therapy, microneedling, or prescription tretinoin can produce significant improvement, but even those rarely achieve complete removal. What topical care can do is meaningfully improve texture, hydration, and the look of newer marks — and that's a realistic, worthwhile goal.

Is Coconut Oil Good for Stretch Marks? What the Evidence Says

Coconut oil has real properties that support skin health — and research backs several of them. Here's what the evidence actually shows, and where the limits are.

1. Hydration and skin barrier support

Coconut oil is a highly effective emollient. A 2014 study in the International Journal of Molecular Sciences found it performs comparably to mineral oil as an emollient while also strengthening the skin's lipid barrier. A 2016 study in Dermatology Research and Practice found virgin coconut oil significantly improved skin hydration and surface lipid levels in participants with dry skin.

Well-hydrated skin is more supple and better able to handle the tension of stretching. When marks do form on hydrated skin, they tend to be less severe.

2. Lauric acid and anti-inflammatory properties

Coconut oil is roughly 50% lauric acid, a medium-chain fatty acid with documented anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties (Kabara et al., Journal of the American Oil Chemists' Society). For stretch marks, the anti-inflammatory action is key. Red, newer marks involve active inflammation in the dermis — and applying coconut oil may help calm that response.

3. Collagen environment support

Coconut oil doesn't directly stimulate new collagen the way prescription retinoids do. But keeping skin deeply hydrated creates a better environment for existing collagen and elastin to function. Dry, depleted skin is less resilient. Consistent moisture support helps preserve the structural integrity your skin already has.

Bottom line on the evidence: Coconut oil for stretch marks has meaningful support for prevention and early-stage treatment. The coconut oil stretch marks evidence from dermatology research points to hydration and barrier function as the real mechanisms — not collagen synthesis. That distinction matters. It won't reverse mature, white marks, and no authoritative dermatologist or dermatology source claims otherwise.

Virgin Coconut Oil vs. Refined Coconut Oil for Stretch Marks

Not all coconut oil is equal for skin use. Virgin (unrefined) coconut oil is cold-pressed from fresh coconut meat without bleaching or high-heat processing. It retains more antioxidants, polyphenols, and vitamin E — which contribute to skin health beyond basic moisturization. This is the preferred choice for stretch marks.

Refined coconut oil still provides solid emollient and moisturizing benefits and works fine if that's what you have. But if you're choosing specifically for skin application, virgin coconut oil for stretch marks delivers a broader range of active compounds.

How to Use Coconut Oil for Stretch Marks

  1. Choose virgin or organic coconut oil. Look for "cold-pressed" and "unrefined" on the label. A coconut oil body product with coconut oil as a core ingredient also works.
  2. Warm it between your palms. Coconut oil is solid at room temperature. Scoop out about a teaspoon and rub it between your hands until it melts. Warm oil absorbs more readily.
  3. Massage in circular motions for 2–5 minutes. Don't just spread it on. The massage increases circulation and helps the oil absorb deeper. This step matters.
  4. Apply twice daily — morning and before bed. Consistency is everything. Twice daily produces noticeably better results than once.
  5. Give it 4–8 weeks. Expect improved texture and reduced redness within 4–6 weeks of twice-daily use. Fading of more established marks takes longer.
  6. Layer under a body lotion for extra moisture. Apply coconut oil first, then a clean body lotion on top. This extends hydration throughout the day — especially useful for dry skin. See our guide to the best coconut oil body lotions for extremely dry skin for options that pair well.

Coconut Oil for Pregnancy Stretch Marks

Pregnancy is the most common context for stretch marks — and also where coconut oil is most effective, because prevention is always easier than treatment.

Coconut oil is considered safe for topical use during pregnancy. There's no evidence that external application poses any risk, and its simple ingredient profile makes it a reassuring choice compared to products with synthetic additives or fragrances.

When to start: Begin in the second trimester, around weeks 13–16. Don't wait until you see marks forming. Apply daily to the belly, hips, lower back, and breasts — all the areas where the most dramatic stretching happens.

Many red pregnancy stretch marks fade significantly within 6 to 12 months postpartum. Your skin is still actively healing during that window. Continued application after birth is worth maintaining. Most marks won't disappear entirely, but they often become much less noticeable.

Other Natural Oils That Work Well With Coconut Oil

Coconut oil pairs well with a few complementary oils:

  • Rosehip oil: Rich in naturally occurring vitamin A precursors and linoleic acid. One of the better-studied natural oils for supporting skin cell regeneration and reducing the appearance of scars and stretch marks. Add a few drops to your coconut oil before massaging in.
  • Argan oil: High in vitamin E and essential fatty acids. Supports elasticity and adds a lightweight finish.
  • Sweet almond oil: Lightweight, easily absorbed, and gentle enough for use during pregnancy. Enhances the emollient effect of coconut oil.

A simple DIY blend: warm a teaspoon of virgin coconut oil and add 3–5 drops of rosehip or argan oil before massaging in.

Side Effects and Who Should Be Cautious

Coconut oil is well-tolerated by most people, but a few things to know:

  • It can clog pores for some skin types. If you're prone to body acne on your back, chest, or shoulders, do a patch test on a small area before applying broadly.
  • Allergic reactions are rare but possible. If you have a tree nut sensitivity, check with your doctor before use.
  • It feels greasy on very oily skin. If that's a concern, apply at night and rinse off any excess in the morning, or blend with a lighter oil like sweet almond.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does coconut oil actually help with stretch marks?

Yes — with an important caveat. Coconut oil helps most with newer, red stretch marks. Its moisturizing and anti-inflammatory properties can reduce severity and support faster fading. It has very limited effect on mature white or silver marks.

What do dermatologists say about coconut oil for stretch marks?

Dermatologists generally acknowledge coconut oil as a safe, effective moisturizer with anti-inflammatory properties. Most dermatology sources note that while it won't remove established marks, consistent use during the early stage can support skin hydration and reduce severity — which is consistent with the available coconut oil stretch marks evidence from dermatology research.

Is virgin coconut oil better than regular coconut oil for stretch marks?

Yes. Virgin coconut oil retains more antioxidants and vitamin E than refined coconut oil. For skin application, those extra compounds are worth having. If refined is all you have, it will still moisturize — but virgin is the better choice.

How long does it take for coconut oil to work on stretch marks?

For newer marks, most people notice improved texture and reduced redness within 4–6 weeks of twice-daily use. Fading of more established marks takes longer — often 3 to 6 months of consistent use. There are no overnight results with any topical product.

Can I use coconut oil on stretch marks during pregnancy?

Yes. Coconut oil is considered safe for topical use during pregnancy. Start around week 13–16 and apply daily to the belly, hips, lower back, and breasts. Starting early — before marks appear — is the most effective approach.
