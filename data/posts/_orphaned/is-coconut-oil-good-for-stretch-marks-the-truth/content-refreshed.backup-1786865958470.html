What Are Stretch Marks and Why Do They Form?

Stretch marks — medically called striae — form when skin stretches faster than it can produce new collagen and elastin. The middle layer of skin (the dermis) tears slightly under the tension. Those tears are what you see on the surface.

There are two stages, and they matter for treatment:

  • Striae rubrae: Red or purple marks. These are newer, with active inflammation still present. This is your best window for topical treatment — the skin is still remodeling.
  • Striae albae: Silver or white marks. These are mature. The inflammation has resolved and the tissue has become fibrous. Much harder to treat with topical products alone.

Common causes include pregnancy (especially the third trimester), rapid weight gain or loss, puberty growth spurts, and significant muscle gain. All involve skin being asked to stretch faster than it naturally can.

Do Stretch Marks Fade on Their Own?

Red or purple stretch marks often fade significantly over 6 to 12 months as your skin's natural healing process runs its course. Many people find them much less noticeable after a year.

White stretch marks are different. At that stage, the structural change in the dermis is largely permanent — but "permanent" doesn't mean "always visible." They typically become lighter and flatter over time, blending closer to your skin tone.

No topical product can fully remove established stretch marks. Clinical options like laser therapy, microneedling, or prescription tretinoin can produce significant improvement, but even those rarely achieve complete removal. What topical care can do is meaningfully improve texture, hydration, and the appearance of newer marks — and that's a worthwhile goal.

Is Coconut Oil Good for Stretch Marks? What the Evidence Says

There's a fair amount of coconut oil stretch marks evidence to work with — not a single definitive clinical trial, but a consistent body of research on its skin properties that supports its use. Here's what the science actually shows.

1. Moisturization and skin barrier support

Coconut oil is a highly effective emollient — it fills the spaces between skin cells and locks in moisture. A 2014 study in the International Journal of Molecular Sciences found coconut oil performs comparably to mineral oil as an emollient while also strengthening the skin's lipid barrier. A 2016 study in Dermatology Research and Practice found virgin coconut oil significantly improved skin hydration and surface lipid levels in participants with dry skin.

Well-hydrated skin is more supple and elastic. When skin maintains its moisture barrier, it handles tension better — and when marks do form, they tend to be less severe. Dermatology reviewers consistently cite hydration as the most accessible lever for stretch mark prevention.

2. Lauric acid and anti-inflammatory properties

Coconut oil is roughly 50% lauric acid, a medium-chain fatty acid with documented anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties. For stretch marks, the anti-inflammatory action matters most. Red, newer marks involve active inflammation in the dermis — applying coconut oil may help calm that response and support faster visual improvement.

3. Collagen environment support

Coconut oil doesn't directly stimulate new collagen the way prescription retinoids do. But keeping skin deeply hydrated creates a healthier environment for existing collagen and elastin to function. Dry, depleted skin loses resilience. Consistent moisture support helps the skin maintain the structural integrity it has.

Bottom line: Coconut oil stretch marks evidence from dermatology research supports its use for prevention and early-stage treatment. It cannot reverse mature, white marks. That's not a reason to skip it — it's a reason to start early.

Virgin Coconut Oil vs. Refined: Which Is Better for Stretch Marks?

Virgin coconut oil for stretch marks is the better choice. It's cold-pressed from fresh coconut meat without bleaching, deodorizing, or high heat — so it retains more natural antioxidants, polyphenols, and vitamin E. Those compounds support skin health beyond basic moisture.

Refined coconut oil still provides solid emollient benefits and is a fine option if that's what you have. But if you're choosing specifically for skin application, look for "cold-pressed, unrefined" on the label.

  2. Warm it first. Coconut oil is solid at room temperature. Scoop about a teaspoon and rub it between your palms until it melts. Warm oil absorbs more readily.
  3. Massage in circular motions for 2–5 minutes. Don't just spread it on and leave. The massage increases circulation and helps drive the oil deeper into the skin. This step genuinely matters.
  4. Apply twice daily. Morning and before bed. Consistency is everything — twice daily produces noticeably better results than once.

Coconut Oil for Pregnancy Stretch Marks

Pregnancy is where coconut oil is most effective — because prevention is always easier than treatment. Coconut oil is considered safe for topical use during pregnancy, with no evidence that external application poses any risk.

When to start: Begin in the second trimester, around weeks 13–16. Don't wait until marks appear. Apply daily to the belly, hips, lower back, and breasts — the areas that will stretch most.

Do pregnancy stretch marks go away? Many red marks fade significantly within 6 to 12 months postpartum. Your skin is still actively healing during that window, so continuing application after birth — especially while nursing — is worth maintaining. Most marks won't disappear entirely, but they often become much less noticeable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there clinical evidence that coconut oil helps stretch marks?

No single clinical trial has tested coconut oil directly on stretch marks. But coconut oil stretch marks evidence from dermatology research supports its use — studies confirm it improves hydration, strengthens the skin barrier, and has anti-inflammatory properties. These are the mechanisms most relevant to stretch mark prevention and early treatment. A dermatologist reviewing the evidence would generally describe it as a reasonable, low-risk option for early-stage marks.

Can coconut oil prevent stretch marks during pregnancy?

It can reduce severity, not guarantee prevention. Starting daily application in the second trimester — before marks appear — gives you the best chance of keeping new marks mild. Hydrated, elastic skin handles stretching better. There's no evidence that any topical product fully prevents stretch marks during pregnancy, but consistent moisture support is the most practical tool available.

What's the difference between virgin and refined coconut oil for skin?

Virgin (unrefined) coconut oil retains more antioxidants and vitamin E because it's processed without high heat or bleaching. For skin use, it delivers a broader range of beneficial compounds. Refined coconut oil still moisturizes well and is a reasonable backup, but virgin is the better choice if you have the option.

How long does it take to see results?

Most people notice improved skin texture and reduced redness in 4–6 weeks of twice-daily use. Visible fading of more established marks takes longer — typically 3 to 6 months of consistent application.

Can coconut oil remove old, white stretch marks?

No. White, mature stretch marks involve fibrous scar tissue in the dermis. Topical oils — including coconut oil — cannot reverse that structural change. Clinical options like laser therapy or microneedling can improve their appearance, but even those don't fully remove them. Coconut oil can help improve the surrounding skin texture and tone, but expectations should be realistic.
