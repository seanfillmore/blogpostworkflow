What Does "Organic Toothpaste" Actually Mean?

The word "organic" on a toothpaste label can mean almost anything. There are three distinct tiers:

  • USDA Certified Organic: At least 95% of ingredients are certified organic. This is the gold standard — you'll see the official green-and-white USDA seal on the packaging.
  • "Made with organic ingredients": At least 70% of the formula is organic. Brands can say this on the label but cannot display the full USDA seal.
  • "Natural" or "with organic extracts": Completely unregulated. No certification required, no minimum percentage, no oversight. Any brand can use it.

Why is it hard to reach 100% organic in a toothpaste? Because water, minerals like baking soda, and functional binders like xanthan gum are not organically certifiable — they're not grown on a farm. Even the best all natural organic toothpaste will have a ceiling below 100%. What matters is that the remaining ingredients are truly organic and that the formula is free from synthetic additives.

When evaluating certified organic toothpaste brands, look for a legitimate organic seal, a short ingredient list you can actually read, and no synthetic surfactants, artificial flavors, or petroleum-derived thickeners.

Is Mainstream Toothpaste Actually Safe? What to Know Before You Switch

A common question: do popular mainstream toothpastes — including brands like Crest Pro-Health — have safe ingredient lists? The honest answer is: it depends on what you're trying to avoid.

Many conventional toothpastes contain sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS), artificial sweeteners like saccharin, and synthetic dyes. None of those are required to clean your teeth. SLS is a foaming agent — it makes paste feel sudsy, but it has no cleaning function and has been linked to increased mouth sores in some people.

Crest Pro-Health specifically contains stannous fluoride, which is clinically supported for cavity prevention, but also uses artificial flavors and colorants that you won't find in a best natural organic toothpaste. If avoiding synthetics is your priority, conventional formulas generally don't clear that bar — regardless of how they market themselves.

Ingredients to Avoid

These are the ingredients that disqualify a toothpaste from being genuinely organic — regardless of what the front label says.

  • Sodium Lauryl Sulfate (SLS): A synthetic foaming agent with no cleaning role. Linked to increased mouth sores in some people. See: Best Toothpaste Without SLS.
  • Artificial dyes and synthetic flavors: Serve marketing purposes, not oral health. A label that just says "flavor" with no further description is a red flag.
  • Saccharin and other artificial sweeteners: Synthetic compounds added for taste. Stevia achieves the same goal without synthetic processing.
  • Polyethylene glycol (PEG) and petroleum-derived compounds: Used as humectants and solvents in conventional formulas. No place in an organic product.
  • Carrageenan: Sounds natural (it's derived from seaweed), but is considered controversial by some researchers due to potential effects on soft tissue. Worth watching for.
  • Glycerin: Some oral health advocates argue it leaves a coating on teeth that may interfere with natural remineralization. It's not in RSC's formula. See: Why Glycerin-Free Toothpaste Matters.

A note on fluoride: Fluoride is a separate personal decision — not a synthetic additive. Whether to use organic fluoride toothpaste or go fluoride-free is covered in the next section.

Fluoride or Fluoride-Free?

Fluoride is strongly supported by mainstream dentistry for cavity prevention. It helps harden enamel and has decades of clinical evidence behind it. If your dentist has flagged you as high cavity risk, talk to them before switching to a fluoride-free formula.

That said, many people choosing the best natural toothpaste prefer to avoid fluoride for real reasons. Fluorosis — white spots on teeth — can result from too much fluoride in young children whose teeth are still developing. Others simply want a formula made entirely from recognizable ingredients.

What do fluoride-free formulas offer instead? Baking soda alkalizes the mouth to disrupt acid-producing bacteria. Coconut oil has been studied for its effect on certain oral bacteria. Myrrh powder adds botanical antimicrobial support at the gum line. Some broader-market formulas also use hydroxyapatite — a mineral naturally found in teeth — as a remineralizing agent. That's not currently in RSC's formula, but it's worth knowing about if remineralization is your primary goal.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a toothpaste truly 100% organic?

No toothpaste can be literally 100% organic because water and minerals are not certifiable under the USDA National Organic Program. A genuinely organic formula will carry a USDA Certified Organic seal (95%+ organic ingredients) and use only natural, recognizable ingredients for the rest.

Is natural toothpaste as effective as conventional toothpaste?

Yes — for daily cleaning and freshening breath. Baking soda removes surface stains effectively. Coconut oil and myrrh provide antimicrobial support. The one functional difference is fluoride: if cavity prevention is your primary concern and you're fluoride-free, talk to your dentist about your specific risk level.

What is the best natural toothpaste ingredient for gum health?

Myrrh powder is the standout. It has well-documented antimicrobial properties in lab studies and has been used for centuries specifically for gum support. Coconut oil also contributes as an antibacterial base.

Are certified organic toothpastes safe for daily use?

Yes. A formula built on baking soda, coconut oil, and botanical extracts is designed for daily use. The RDA score of baking soda is low enough that it won't wear down enamel with regular brushing.
