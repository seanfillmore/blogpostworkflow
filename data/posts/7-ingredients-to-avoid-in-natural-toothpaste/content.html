Many people switch to “natural” toothpaste hoping to avoid harsh chemicals. But not every clean-labeled product is as gentle as it looks. Some formulas include ingredients that may irritate your mouth or add nothing helpful. Here are 7 common ones to avoid, plus a simpler way to care for your smile.

1. Sodium Lauryl Sulfate (SLS)

What it is: A foaming agent that helps toothpaste spread and feel “active.”

Why to avoid it: Linked to mouth irritation, dryness, and increased canker sores. It can also upset the balance of the good bacteria in your mouth (your oral microbiome) and bother delicate tissues.

Better alternative: Non-foaming formulas using coconut oil, baking soda, or herbal blends to cleanse without lather.

2. Glycerin

What it is: A thickener that adds moisture and sweetness.

Why to avoid it: It may coat your teeth and get in the way of enamel remineralization. That's the natural process where minerals from saliva strengthen the surface of your teeth.

Better alternative: Glycerin-free pastes with simple plant-based binders and natural humectants (ingredients that help hold in moisture).

3. Artificial Sweeteners (e.g., Saccharin, Sucralose)

What they are: Chemical sweeteners for taste without sugar.

Why to avoid them: They can irritate sensitive users and may disrupt gut health. They're often found in products for children.

Better alternative: Natural sweeteners like stevia or no sweetener at all.

4. Triclosan

What it is: A synthetic antibacterial agent.

Why to avoid it: It's banned in many personal care products because of concerns about hormone disruption (it may interfere with the body's natural hormones) and antibiotic resistance.

Better alternative: Natural antimicrobials like coconut oil or myrrh extract.

5. Carrageenan

What it is: Seaweed-derived thickener.

Why to avoid it: May trigger inflammation in sensitive individuals.

Better alternative: Xanthan gum or bentonite clay for texture without controversy.

6. Artificial Flavors & Dyes

What they are: Lab-created additives for flavor or color.

Why to avoid them: They provide no functional value and may trigger allergic reactions or sensitivity in children.

Better alternative: Essential oils like peppermint, cinnamon, or clove for natural flavor and benefits.

7. Fluoride

What it is: A mineral to prevent cavities.

Why you might skip it: It's effective in small amounts but can be harmful if overused. Some families choose fluoride-free for young children.

Better alternative: A gentle fluoride-free formula plus good brushing habits. If you're cavity-prone, ask your dentist about hydroxyapatite or fluoride.

FAQs: Ingredients to Avoid in Natural Toothpaste

Why do natural brands still use SLS or glycerin?
Often for texture, foaming, or shelf stability — but they’re not necessary for an effective product.

Are all sweeteners bad in toothpaste?
No. Natural sweeteners like stevia are generally well-tolerated in small amounts.

How can I find a truly clean toothpaste?
Look for short ingredient lists, no dyes, no artificial flavors, and no alcohol, SLS, or preservatives.

Do I need fluoride?
It depends on your dental history. Many people do well without it when they pair good brushing with a balanced diet. Your dentist can help you decide.
