What to Look for in a Natural Deodorant Alternative

First, a quick vocabulary lesson. Deodorant cuts down underarm odor. Antiperspirant reduces sweat, and the FDA regulates antiperspirants as over-the-counter drugs, while deodorants are treated as cosmetics (FDA). Natural deodorants do not stop sweat. They work on odor.

Here's a simple checklist for any natural deodorant:

  • Aluminum-free: Aluminum compounds are what make antiperspirants block sweat. A true aluminum-free deodorant leaves them out.
  • Baking soda approach: Baking soda neutralizes odor, but it's alkaline and bothers some skin. Check whether it's in the formula and how you react.
  • Scent source: Look for essential oils listed by name, not just "fragrance."
  • Texture: Roll-on, cream, stick or spray. Choose what you'll actually use every morning.
  • Sensitive-skin fit: Shorter ingredient lists make it easier to spot what bothers you. Browse our non-toxic deodorant collection to see what that looks like.

Skin reacts differently to every ingredient, even natural ones. So patch test first. Put a small amount on your inner arm and wait a day before using it under your arms.

2. Baking Soda Free Deodorant for Sensitive Skin

Baking soda is alkaline, and your skin is slightly acidic. For some people, that mismatch means redness, itching or a rash. The American Academy of Dermatology explains that underarm rashes can come from contact dermatitis, a reaction to something that touched your skin. That something can be baking soda, a scent or another ingredient. If you've had that reaction, a baking-soda-free formula is worth trying. Read more in our guide to baking soda in natural deodorant: benefits, risks and alternatives.

No formula is guaranteed to be free of irritation, so patch test any new one.

4. Vegan Deodorant

"Vegan" means no animal-derived ingredients, so no beeswax, lanolin or similar. Plant-based waxes usually take their place. Many people choose these for ethical reasons or simply prefer plant-based products. But vegan doesn't automatically mean gentler. 5. Natural Deodorant Spray or Mist

Sprays suit people who want a light, no-residue feel. They dry fast and are easy to apply. The trade-offs: some contain alcohol, which can sting fresh-shaved skin, and scents can be strong.

And is deodorant a liquid? It can be. Deodorants come as sticks, creams, sprays and roll-ons. Just don't confuse a deodorant spray with a fragrance body spray. A body spray only adds scent. A deodorant is made to reduce underarm odor.

How to Switch to a Natural Deodorant (and What to Expect)

Switching is easy, but your underarms may need time to adjust. Many people say it takes two to four weeks. That's anecdotal, not guaranteed.

  1. Patch test first: Try it on your inner arm for a day.
  2. Start clean and dry: Apply to fully dry skin so it sits well.
  3. Use a thin layer: More doesn't mean better, and extra can feel sticky.
  4. Reapply when needed: After a workout or a heavy sweat, add a little more.
  5. Watch your skin: If you get redness, itching or a rash, stop using it. See a dermatologist if it doesn't clear up.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is natural deodorant as effective as conventional?
It depends on what you need. Natural deodorants help with odor, but they don't reduce sweat. Antiperspirants do that, and the FDA regulates them as drugs (FDA). If odor is your concern, natural can work well. Results vary from person to person.

Are deodorants FDA approved?
Deodorants are regulated as cosmetics, which don't need FDA approval before sale. Antiperspirants are over-the-counter drugs with their own rules. So no deodorant, ours included, should be called "FDA approved." Read the FDA's explanation.

Is baking soda bad for sensitive skin?
Not for everyone, but it can be. It's alkaline, and some people get redness or itching. Rashes like this can be contact dermatitis, according to the AAD. If you react, stop using it and consider a baking-soda-free formula.

Can I use the same deodorant on my whole body?
Only if the label says it's meant for that. Our roll-on is made for underarms. If you want to try other areas, patch test first and avoid broken skin and mucous membranes.
