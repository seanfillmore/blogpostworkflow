Toothpaste without propylene glycol does exist, and most natural formulas skip it. Propylene glycol is a common ingredient that keeps paste from drying out. The only reliable way to know what's in your tube is to read the full ingredient list. Here's how to do that quickly, and what to check while you're at it.

What Is Propylene Glycol and Why Is It in Toothpaste?

Propylene glycol is a synthetic liquid that holds moisture and dissolves other ingredients. In toothpaste, it keeps the paste from drying out and helps carry flavors. It also shows up in foods, cosmetics, and medications.

Some people simply prefer to avoid it. Maybe they want shorter ingredient lists, or maybe they've noticed a sore mouth with certain pastes. Both are fair reasons.

Is Propylene Glycol Actually Harmful? What the Evidence Says

Here's the balanced answer. The FDA lists propylene glycol as generally recognized as safe for use in food. The CDC's toxicology arm, ATSDR, also has a plain-language summary of what's known about exposure.

A small number of people do report skin or mouth irritation. You can read more in the NIH PubChem entry. So skipping it is a personal-preference or sensitivity choice. It isn't a proven health necessity.

How to Spot Propylene Glycol on a Toothpaste Label

Ignore the front of the tube. "Natural" is a marketing word, and it doesn't guarantee a clean list. Flip it over and try this checklist:

  1. Find the full list: Look at the complete ingredients, including the inactive ones.
  2. Search the exact name: Look for "propylene glycol." Also watch for other glycol terms, like "PEG" or "polyethylene glycol."
  3. Don't mix it up with glycerin: Glycerin is a different ingredient. It's a plant-derived humectant, so avoiding propylene glycol doesn't mean avoiding glycerin. If you want to skip both, our guide on glycerin-free toothpaste explains why.
  4. Scan for SLS: Look for sodium lauryl sulfate, the foaming agent. Our SLS-free toothpaste collection is a good place to start.
  5. Check other add-ons: See label checks before you buy and toothpaste without titanium dioxide for more.

What to Look For Instead

A simple toothpaste has a short ingredient list you can actually read. Look for plant-based thickeners, no artificial colors or sweeteners, and a flavor that comes from real oils. Some people also look for vegan toothpaste.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is propylene glycol in all toothpaste?
No. Many conventional pastes use it, but plenty of natural ones don't. Always check the ingredient list.

Is glycerin the same as propylene glycol?
No. Glycerin is a separate, plant-derived humectant. They do similar jobs, but they're different ingredients.

Can I use fluoride-free toothpaste if I avoid propylene glycol?
Yes, the two choices are unrelated. But fluoride matters for cavity prevention, so ask your dentist. See what parents need to know about fluoride-free kids' toothpaste.
