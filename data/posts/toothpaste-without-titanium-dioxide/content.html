What Is Titanium Dioxide, and Why Is It in Toothpaste?

Titanium dioxide is a white mineral-based pigment. Makers use it as an opacifier, which means it makes a product look solid, bright and even. In toothpaste, that's all it does.

It is cosmetic. It doesn't scrub teeth, fight cavities or whiten enamel on its own. A paste without it simply looks less stark white.

Size matters in the debate. TiO2 comes as larger "micro" particles and tiny "nano" particles, and most of the worry centers on the nano-sized fraction. A 2012 paper in Environmental Science & Technology found that TiO2 is widespread in personal care products, including toothpaste, and that some of it is nano-sized (Weir et al., 2012). That paper measures exposure. It doesn't show harm from brushing.

On labels, you may also see it as CI 77891, which is its color index number.

Is Titanium Dioxide in Toothpaste Safe? What the Evidence Says

Here's the honest picture, graded by strength. Short version: toothpaste isn't banned, and no authority has concluded toothpaste TiO2 harms people.

  • EU food ban (strength: strong, but about food): In 2021, the European Food Safety Authority said TiO2 could no longer be considered safe as a food additive (E171) because concerns about DNA damage couldn't be ruled out. The EU then banned it in food. This covers swallowed food additives, not toothpaste (EFSA, 2026).
  • IARC Group 2B (strength: relevant to inhalation only): The International Agency for Research on Cancer classes TiO2 as "possibly carcinogenic to humans" based on inhaled dust, such as in workplaces. That's not the same as brushing your teeth (IARC Monographs Vol. 93).
  • Harm from toothpaste use (strength: weak): You spit toothpaste out, so swallowed exposure is small. Direct evidence of harm from TiO2 in toothpaste is limited.

As of October 2026, we're not aware of a U.S. ban on TiO2 in toothpaste. The FDA lists approved color additives on its color additives page.

So skipping it is a reasonable precaution, a personal preference. It isn't a medical necessity.

What Do Titanium Dioxide-Free Toothpastes Use Instead?

Mostly, they use nothing. TiO2-free pastes are usually off-white, cream or slightly translucent, and that's normal.

Approach What you'll see
TiO2 whitener Bright, uniform white paste
Natural off-white base Cream or tan tone, sometimes a little speckled

Formulas without TiO2 rely on other things for the cleaning job. Common choices include gentle abrasives such as baking soda, hydrated silica or calcium carbonate. Others use coconut oil bases and plant flavors like mint or cinnamon. Some use hydroxyapatite, a mineral found in teeth. Research on it is still emerging and mixed, so we don't make claims about it. These ingredients may lift mild surface stains, but none of them changes your natural tooth color.

Titanium dioxide-free and fluoride-free are two separate choices. A large Cochrane review found strong evidence that fluoride toothpaste prevents cavities (Walsh et al., 2019). Some people still choose a fluoride-free toothpaste. If that's you, talk with your dentist first. How to Choose by Need: Sensitivity, Kids, and Whitening

Sensitive teeth or gums

Start with your dentist, since sensitivity can have many causes. Gentle, low-abrasion, SLS-free pastes are a reasonable thing to try. Evidence linking SLS to canker sores is mixed. One small, early study suggested a connection (Herlofson & Barkvoll, 1994), but it doesn't prove SLS causes them. Our sensitive teeth guide has more.

Kids

Ask your dentist or pediatrician about fluoride for your child. Whatever you choose, supervise brushing and use a pea-sized amount. Our kids' toothpaste without fluoride: what parents need to know walks through it.

Whitening

Set your expectations. TiO2-free paste won't look dramatically whitening, and no paste changes your natural tooth color. Be cautious with charcoal and other trendy abrasives, because rough scrubbing can wear on teeth.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is titanium dioxide in toothpaste dangerous? No authority has concluded that it harms toothpaste users. Evidence on oral-care exposure is limited, so avoiding it is a fair precaution, not a medical need.

Does toothpaste have to contain titanium dioxide? No. Toothpaste without titanium dioxide cleans just as well as paste with it, because the pigment is only cosmetic. Without it, paste simply looks off-white.

How do I know if my toothpaste has titanium dioxide? Check the inactive ingredients list for "titanium dioxide," "TiO2," "CI 77891," or "white pigment." Check the back of the package, not just the front.

Is titanium dioxide-free toothpaste also fluoride-free? Not necessarily. They're separate choices, and many pastes with fluoride skip TiO2. Read the label for both, and ask your dentist about fluoride.
