Why People Look for Antibacterial Soap Alternatives

Soap works because each soap molecule has two ends. One end grabs oil and dirt. The other end grabs water. Together, they lift grime and germs off your skin so water can rinse them away. Soap is made from fats or oils plus an alkali, through a reaction called saponification.

The CDC explains that washing hands with plain soap and water is effective. In 2016, the FDA issued a final rule on consumer antiseptic washes. It said companies had not shown that triclosan and 18 other added ingredients were any better than plain soap and water, and it restricted their use in consumer washes.

That doesn't make antibacterial soap harmful. It just means "antibacterial" isn't automatically better. And harsh cleansers can strip skin oils, which may leave skin feeling tight or dry.

3. Tea Tree Oil Soap

Tea tree oil is a popular plant ingredient in soaps for oily or blemish-prone skin. People often look for it as a natural antibacterial soap alternative. Laboratory studies suggest tea tree oil has antimicrobial properties. But lab results don't prove a soap will work the same way on your skin.

Our coconut oil bar soap comes in a Nourishing Tea Tree option, scented with organic essential oil of tea tree.

The NIH's NCCIH notes that tea tree oil can cause skin irritation in some people. Patch-test first. And don't treat it as a replacement for medical antisepsis.

  • Best for: Oily or acne-prone skin, if you tolerate botanicals well.
  • Trade-off: It may irritate sensitive skin.

4. Goat Milk Soap

Goat milk soap is a creamy, mild option. Many people find it gentle, which is why it's a go-to for dry or sensitive skin. It usually has a soft, smooth lather.

  • Best for: Dry skin and people who want a mild soap.
  • Trade-off: It's animal-derived, so it won't suit vegan readers. Scents and add-ins vary by bar, so read the label.

5. Tallow Soap

Tallow soap is made with rendered animal fat, usually from beef. Some people choose it for the short ingredient list, the old-fashioned approach, and the creamy lather. It's a classic bar-soap style.

  • Best for: People who like traditional soap and don't mind animal-derived ingredients.
  • Trade-off: It's not vegan. Plant-based readers can choose a bar like coconut oil soap instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is antibacterial soap better than regular soap?

Not necessarily. The CDC says plain soap and water is effective for handwashing. In 2016, the FDA found companies hadn't shown certain added antibacterial ingredients worked better than plain soap and water in consumer washes.

What is soap made of?

Soap is made from fats or oils, such as coconut oil, plus an alkali. When they react in a process called saponification, they form soap and glycerin. The soap molecules then help lift oil and dirt away.

Is bar soap or body wash better?

Neither is better for everyone. Bars often use less packaging and have shorter ingredient lists. Body wash is convenient and some people find it easier to use. Pick the one that cleans well without leaving your skin tight.

Can natural soap clean as well as antibacterial soap for everyday handwashing?

For everyday handwashing, plain soap and water is effective, according to the CDC. What matters most is washing long enough and rinsing well. A gentle soap you like using may help you wash more often.
