What Makes a Toothpaste Truly Vegan?

A toothpaste is vegan when it meets two criteria: no animal-derived ingredients in the formula, and no animal testing at any stage of development. Both matter. A toothpaste can be cruelty-free (no animal testing) but still contain animal-derived ingredients — like beeswax or honey. That's not vegan. Conversely, a formula could theoretically be free of animal ingredients but still be tested on animals.

Here's the part that trips most shoppers up: "natural toothpaste" and "organic toothpaste" labels do not automatically mean vegan. Those terms refer to ingredient sourcing and processing methods — not to whether animal products were used. A toothpaste with organic beeswax is organic. It is not vegan.

Even ingredients that sound plant-based can come from animals. Glycerin — one of the most common toothpaste ingredients — can be derived from animal fat or plant oils. There's no way to tell from the word "glycerin" alone on an ingredients list. The same goes for flavor compounds and certain softeners. The only reliable signals are a certified vegan logo, a clear "vegetable glycerin" callout, or a brand that publishes its full ingredient sourcing transparently.

Hidden Non-Vegan Ingredients to Watch Out For

This is the section most toothpaste guides skip. These six ingredients appear in "natural" and conventional toothpastes more often than you'd think — and none of them are vegan.

  • Glycerin: One of the most common toothpaste ingredients. It can be derived from animal fat (tallow) or plant oils — the label usually just says "glycerin." Look for "vegetable glycerin" as the confirmed plant-based version. Our formulas use no glycerin at all. (Here's why that matters.)
  • Carmine / CI 75470: A red pigment made from crushed cochineal insects. It's used to give gel toothpastes and some herbal toothpaste formulas a pink or red color. If your toothpaste has any reddish tint, check for "carmine" or "CI 75470" on the label.
  • Propolis: A resin collected by bees. It shows up in some herbal toothpaste and "natural antibacterial" formulas because of its antimicrobial properties. It sounds wholesome — but it's 100% bee-derived and not vegan.
  • Lanolin: A wax secreted from sheep's wool. It's more common in lip balm and whitening strip kits than in paste itself, but it appears in some "conditioning" toothpaste formulas and combination oral care products.
  • Bone char: This one is especially tricky because it won't appear on any label. Bone char is a processing agent made from animal bones, used to refine and whiten cane sugar to a bright white color. If a toothpaste contains refined white sugar or a sweetener processed through conventional sugar refineries, bone char may have been used upstream. It's never listed as an ingredient — which is exactly why it's easy to miss.
  • Casein / Whey: Milk-derived proteins that appear in some remineralizing toothpaste formulas. These are marketed as supporting enamel strength, but they're animal-derived dairy proteins. Look for "casein," "sodium caseinate," or "whey" in the ingredients if you're evaluating a remineralizing or "healthy toothpaste" product.

Vegan vs. Natural vs. Organic Toothpaste — What's the Difference?

These three terms are used interchangeably all the time. They're not the same thing.

  • Vegan: No animal-derived ingredients. No animal testing. This is a binary standard — a product either qualifies or it doesn't.
  • Natural: Minimally processed ingredients derived from plants or minerals. There is no universal regulatory standard for "natural" in cosmetics, which means any brand can use the word freely.
  • Organic: Certified organic ingredients, verified by a third-party body like the USDA. Organic certification says nothing about whether a product is vegan — an organic formula can still contain organic beeswax or organic lanolin.

The gold standard is all three: vegan + natural + organic. That's what we aim for at Real Skin Care. Our natural toothpaste formulas use certified organic ingredients where possible, skip all animal derivatives, and leave out the synthetic additives that make a product "natural" in name only.

It's also worth noting that vegan and eco-friendly toothpaste often go hand in hand. Brands that care about animal welfare tend to also care about packaging waste and environmental impact — so if you're looking for an eco-friendly toothpaste, starting with a vegan formula is a smart place to begin.

Should Vegan Toothpaste Be Fluoride-Free?

Fluoride itself is a mineral — it's not animal-derived. So fluoride is technically vegan. The fluoride question is separate from the vegan question entirely.

That said, many people who seek out vegan toothpaste are also drawn to fluoride-free options. The reasons vary. Some prefer to avoid fluoride because of concerns about ingestion, especially for young children. Others are drawn to the philosophy of using the fewest and most recognizable ingredients possible. And some are exploring natural remineralizing alternatives like hydroxyapatite or nano-calcium phosphate as part of a broader holistic oral care routine.

We offer fluoride-free toothpaste options for exactly this reason. Our formulas rely on coconut oil, baking soda, and plant-based essential oils rather than synthetic additives.

One important note: we recommend consulting your dentist about the right approach to cavity prevention for your individual needs. Fluoride-free toothpaste is a popular and well-tolerated choice for many people, but your dental professional is the right person to advise on what's best for your specific oral health.

Can Vegan Toothpaste Actually Whiten Teeth Naturally?

Yes — vegan toothpaste can help remove surface stains and brighten your smile through plant-derived and mineral-based mechanisms. Here are four of the most effective ones found in clean, natural formulas:

  • Baking soda (sodium bicarbonate): A mild abrasive that buffs away surface stains without scratching enamel. It's one of the most well-studied natural whitening agents available. Our toothpaste uses baking soda as a core ingredient.
  • Coconut oil: Associated with the traditional practice of oil pulling, coconut oil may help break down the bacteria that contribute to plaque and surface discoloration. Our coconut oil toothpaste makes this a central part of the formula, not an afterthought.
  • Activated charcoal: Binds to surface stains through adsorption — a process where compounds stick to the surface of the charcoal — and carries them away when you rinse. Common in natural whitening toothpaste formulas.
  • Kaolin clay: A gentle polishing mineral that smooths tooth surfaces and may help lift stains over time without harsh abrasives.

None of these require bleaching chemicals, and all are vegan-friendly. For natural teeth whitening that fits a clean ingredient philosophy, these are the mechanisms worth knowing. Just use language that matches reality: these ingredients help remove surface stains over time — they won't replace professional whitening treatments for deep discoloration.

How to Read a Vegan Toothpaste Label (In Under 60 Seconds)

You don't need a chemistry degree. Here's a four-step check you can do at the store or on any product page:

  1. Look for a certified vegan logo. The Vegan Society's sunflower, the Certified Vegan logo from Vegan Action, and PETA's cruelty-free and vegan bunny are the most widely recognized. A logo means a third party has verified the formula — not just that the brand says so.
  2. Scan for the hidden non-vegan ingredients covered above. Check for glycerin (is it specified as vegetable?), carmine or CI 75470, propolis, lanolin, casein, or whey. If any of these appear without a plant-based qualifier, the product may not be fully vegan.
  3. Check the sweetener. Sorbitol and xylitol are plant-derived and vegan-friendly. Stevia (like what we use) is also plant-based. Be cautious with formulas that just list "sugar" — unspecified refined sugar may have been processed with bone char.
  4. Check for SLS. Sodium lauryl sulfate isn't a vegan issue per se — it's a synthetic foaming agent. But it's a common concern for natural toothpaste shoppers because it can irritate mouth tissue and disrupt oral microbiome balance. Our formulas are entirely SLS-free.

Four steps. Under a minute. That's all it takes to evaluate any toothpaste label with confidence.

Frequently Asked Questions About Vegan Toothpaste

Is all natural toothpaste vegan?
Not necessarily. "Natural" refers to how ingredients are sourced and processed — not whether they're animal-free. A natural toothpaste can still contain glycerin from animal fat, propolis from bees, or casein from milk. Always check the label for specific ingredient sources, and look for a certified vegan logo when in doubt.

What is the healthiest toothpaste to use?
The healthiest toothpaste for most people is one that cleans effectively without unnecessary additives. Look for a formula that is vegan, SLS-free, free of artificial sweeteners and synthetic dyes, and made with plant-based or mineral active ingredients. What counts as "healthiest" also depends on your individual oral health — your dentist is the best person to advise on specifics like fluoride needs or sensitivity concerns.

Can I find vegan toothpaste at Whole Foods?
Some vegan toothpaste options are available at Whole Foods and natural grocery stores, though selection varies by location and the labeling isn't always clear. For a curated, fully vetted selection, you can shop Real Skin Care's vegan toothpaste online with complete ingredient transparency.

Is fluoride-free toothpaste safe?
Fluoride-free toothpaste is generally considered safe and is a popular choice among those pursuing a natural oral care routine. Many people use it successfully for everyday cleaning and freshness. That said, if you have specific concerns about cavity prevention or enamel health, it's worth consulting your dentist to find the right approach for your needs.

What are good toothpaste alternatives to conventional paste?
Common toothpaste alternatives include tooth powder, oil pulling with coconut oil, and tablet toothpaste — many of which are naturally vegan and come in plastic-free packaging. A squeeze bottle formula like ours offers a middle ground: the convenience of conventional paste with the clean, plant-based ingredients of a true alternative.
