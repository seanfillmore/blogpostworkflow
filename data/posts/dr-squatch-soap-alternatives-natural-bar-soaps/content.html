Why People Look for Dr Squatch Soap Alternatives

Most shoppers who switch aren't unhappy with bar soap. They just want something that fits their routine and budget better. Here are the most common reasons.

  • Cost per wash: A premium bar or a subscription can add up fast, especially if the bar doesn't last.
  • Soft or crumbling bars: A bar that sits in a puddle turns mushy. Some bars also crack or crumble before you're done with them.
  • Strong fragrance: Novelty scents are fun for some people. Others want something quieter, or no scent at all.
  • Dry skin: Any cleanser can leave skin feeling tight, depending on the recipe and your skin.
  • Shorter ingredient lists: More people want to read a label and recognize every word.

Plenty of people are also moving from body wash to bar soap. Bars often come with less plastic, and they're easy to pack for travel. We'll use the five reasons above to sort the options.

How to Choose a Natural Bar Soap (Quick Checklist)

You don't need a chemistry degree to read a soap label. Run through this list before you buy.

  • Base oils: Coconut oil makes big lather. Olive oil makes a milder, less bubbly bar. Tallow makes a dense, long-lasting bar. Shea and cocoa butter add a creamier feel.
  • Fragrance source: "Essential oils" come from plants. "Fragrance" or "parfum" can be a blend of undisclosed ingredients. Essential oils can still bother some skin, so patch test.
  • Glycerin: This is a natural byproduct of soapmaking. It attracts water to the skin. Handmade-style bars often keep it in.
  • Skin sensitivity: Soap is naturally more alkaline than skin's surface. Research suggests that can leave some people feeling dry or irritated, so go gentle if your skin is reactive (AAD).
  • Lather and hardness: Hard bars last longer. Soft bars feel luxurious but wear down faster.
  • Cost per wash: Divide the price by the number of washes. As an estimate, assume 40 to 60 washes per bar. A $10 bar at 50 washes is about $0.20 a wash. Your numbers will vary with bar size and how you store it.

One more thing about lye. Real soap is made by saponification, which means oils react with lye (sodium hydroxide). The lye is used up in that reaction, so the finished bar doesn't contain it when properly made (FDA). So "made with lye" on a label isn't a red flag. 2. Tallow Soap

Tallow soap is made from rendered beef fat. It makes a dense, long-lasting bar with a creamy, mild feel. If you've had bars that crumble early, a hard tallow bar is worth a look.

The downsides are clear. It's animal-derived, so it isn't vegan, and the scent is often mild. We wouldn't promise any specific skin results from it either. Read more in our guide to tallow soap benefits and ingredients.

3. Pine Tar Soap

Pine tar soap smells woodsy and a little smoky. It's a good match if you want a rugged, "outdoorsy" scent without a novelty fragrance. It's also a very traditional soap, so the scent comes from the ingredient itself.

The smell is strong, and some people with sensitive skin react to it. Patch test first by dabbing a little lather on your inner arm. For more, see pine tar soap benefits and ingredients.

4. Goat Milk Soap

Goat milk soap is a softer, creamier pick. Many people with dry or easily irritated skin like how gentle it feels. It's usually made with goat milk plus oils like coconut, olive, or palm.

We won't claim it treats any skin condition. It just tends to feel mellow and smooth. Because milk sugars can make a bar soften faster, keep it well drained. Learn more in our goat milk soap benefits guide.

5. Plant-Oil (Castile-Style) Bar Soap

Castile-style soap is olive-oil-forward and minimalist. It's mild, low in scent, and works for a lot of jobs. If you want simple and unscented, this is your type.

It usually lathers less than a coconut-based bar. The lather is lighter and slicker, so don't judge it by bubbles alone. Many castile-style bars also sit a bit softer, so drain them well.

6. Extra-Moisturizing Bar Soap

Moisturizing bars are "superfatted." That means extra oils or butters, like shea or cocoa butter, are left in the bar so they don't all turn into soap. The result often feels gentler, which many people like in winter or on dry skin.

These bars tend to run softer, so drying between uses matters. Set the bar on a draining dish, not in a closed holder. Our guide to the best moisturizing bar soap for soft, hydrated skin has more picks.

Bar-Care Tips That Work for Any Soap

  • Use a draining dish: Let water drip away so the bar isn't sitting in a puddle.
  • Dry between uses: Keep the bar out of the direct spray and let it air dry after every shower.
  • Cut the bar in half: Start one half and keep the other dry. You'll use each half fully and lower your cost per wash.
  • Skip the washcloth soak: Lathering on a wet cloth wears the bar down less than rubbing it directly on skin.

Can Natural Soap Help with Body Odor?

Honestly, soap can only do so much. Body odor starts when skin bacteria break down sweat (MedlinePlus). Washing regularly, drying off well, and wearing clean clothes do most of the work.

You don't need an "antibacterial" label for everyday washing. The FDA says there's no evidence that antibacterial soaps work better than plain soap and water (FDA). The CDC also recommends plain soap and water for handwashing (CDC). For armpit odor, a natural deodorant is a better tool than soap. Our guide to natural alternatives to Dial soap covers this more.

See a clinician if you have persistent folliculitis, a skin infection, or a foot condition that won't clear up (AAD). Soap doesn't treat these.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is natural bar soap better than body wash?
Neither is better for everyone. Bars often have shorter ingredient lists and less packaging. Body wash can feel gentler for some skin types. Choose the one you'll use consistently and that leaves your skin comfortable.

Can natural soap be used on the face and body?
Sometimes. Mild, low-scent bars can work for both, but facial skin is more delicate. If your face feels tight or red after washing, switch to a gentler cleanser made for faces, and patch test any new bar first.

Why does my bar soap get mushy and how do I stop it?
Sitting in standing water is the main cause. Use a draining dish, let the bar dry between uses, and keep it out of the shower stream. Cutting a bar in half and rotating the pieces helps too.

Is antibacterial soap necessary for everyday use?
No. The FDA says plain soap and water is enough for everyday washing, and antibacterial versions haven't been shown to work better (FDA).

How do I choose an effective soap if I have sensitive skin?
Look for a short ingredient list and a mild or unscented bar. Patch test on your inner arm for a day or two first. If you have eczema or another skin condition, ask a dermatologist what suits you.
