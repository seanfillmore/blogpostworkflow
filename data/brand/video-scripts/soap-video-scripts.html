Shoot the liquid first

Not because it is the better script — the bar's are stronger — but because the bar is between batches. Every bar order today is a presale shipping by October 1, Refreshing Lemongrass is sold out in both the single and the 4-pack, and the 4-pack variety box is gone too. The liquid is fully in stock in all four scents with the refill available.

So the bar's closes state the presale out loud rather than working around it, and none of them names Lemongrass. Post the liquid now; bank the bar videos and release them as stock lands, or run them with Close 1 as written.

These are not two versions of one video, and the objection data is why. The bar's top complaint is that it disappears — 6 mentions, the single most common thing anyone says about it, plus 5 more that the bar looks small for the money. The liquid's is that it leaves hands stripped, and that it arrives thinner than expected.

One is a value argument. The other is a chemistry argument. A script that tried to serve both would win neither.

Four things worth knowing before you shoot

  • Bar Script B is the tightest measurement in either batch. Hook 05 scores 0.38 on premise dominance against a 0.50 backstop — the closest anything has come. It is fine, and the reason is structural: the hook quotes the objection at length, so its own words necessarily recur when the body answers it. Shoot that one close to the page; it has the least room to wander of any script here.
  • Two tallow comparisons were CUT on 2026-09-05, and the second one was simply false. Script B originally claimed the bar "outlasts tallow bars costing twice as much" (lifted from the live PDP) and that ours is "softer than a tallow bar". Checked properly, two independent mechanisms say a coconut bar with its glycerin retained wears faster than tallow, not slower: lauric and myristic acids make small, highly soluble soap molecules where tallow's stearic and palmitic make larger insoluble ones, and retained glycerin is a humectant that pulls in moisture and speeds dissolution — which is exactly why commercial bars have it extracted. Your own review corpus had been saying so all along: "gone in three weeks" is the single most common thing anyone says about this product, 6 mentions. The claim is gone from the live PDP and the theme; hardness is left unclaimed in either direction because nobody has measured ours. Script B now argues solubility, which is true, supported, and the thing customers actually experience.
  • Bar Script C beat 6 discloses tea tree oil in two scents. That is a direct answer to a real complaint — a buyer got tea tree they were avoiding and said the ingredients weren't findable before purchase. Volunteering it on camera costs a few sales and buys the thing the whole account is built on.
  • The liquid's review quote is paraphrased, not verbatim. The original names two retailers directly; the script says "the big bath chains" instead. If you put the review on screen as written, that's the customer's words and fine. Saying them yourself is a comparative claim about named competitors — a different thing.
