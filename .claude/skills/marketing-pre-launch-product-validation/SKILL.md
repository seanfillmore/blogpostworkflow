# Pre Launch Product Validation

## Put the product in the hands of real users in the hardest use case before you build any promotion — give free samples in exchange for honest opinions and what they would pay — and kill or fix the product if the feedback is not strong.

**Why it works:** No amount of promotion rescues a product that does not deliver; disappointment eventually catches up and destroys the reputation the sales message depends on. Testing in the most demanding real-world conditions surfaces failures the seller cannot see from the inside, and asking testers what they would pay surfaces the price before any money is committed to the campaign.

**Evidence offered:** Impact Gel insoles tested with workers standing 8+ hours a day on concrete at an oil-change shop; the DualSaw tested with the Clearwater Fire Department; Schefren's own VIP customer group receiving products in advance with authority to kill a launch, plus an AWAI joint-venture product in field testing. Anecdotal throughout, no figures.

**Fit here (6/10):** Durable principle (product validation before promotion), age irrelevant, and nothing in the current fleet owns it — marketing-review-mining sources proof from products already shipped, marketing-retention-offers cultivates top buyers for repeat revenue, but nobody covers a go/no-go panel before a variant ships. Honest solo-operator translation: before releasing a new scent, a reformulation or a bundle, send free units to a handful of the top repeat buyers in the hardest use case (12-hour shifts, heavy sweaters, baking-soda-sensitive skin) and act on the answers. Costs a few units of COGS, needs no traffic, no attribution and no team, and it attacks the binding retention constraint at its source, since the 18–22.5% repeat rate is driven by first-use disappointment. Two hard limits: marketing-offer-construction gates catalogue expansion, so this applies to variants and reformulations rather than SKU 13; and product-for-feedback must never be routed into an Amazon review (a flat TOS violation) and must be disclosed as a material connection if the feedback later becomes a testimonial. Held at 6 because the evidence is anecdotal and a panel of five is a signal, not a measurement.

*Source: Rich Schefren — "Billy Mays Marketing Magic" (blog post, part 1 of 3)*
