# Creator Content Sourcing

## Activate creator content through Meta partnership ads because the delivery starts with the creator's own seed audience and expands from there, reaching net-new people your pixel keeps missing.
**Stage:** traffic — gate OPEN as of 2026-08-17. Live; no longer parked.

**Why it works:** A partnership ad is served first to the creator's engaged followers and then to lookalikes of them, so it escapes the audience rut an account develops after years of optimizing against its own pixel — and Meta's persona-based Andromeda targeting rewards content that maps to new personas.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion plus secondhand claims: a performance summit statement that creator content is the future of selling, and named brand interviews (Pacted, Caraway said small creators/UGC performed best on partnership ads; Jones Road said bigger influencer and celebrity deals performed better for them). No spend, CPA, or incrementality figures.

**Fit here (6/10):** Platform-mechanics class published fresh (2026-08), so it is current, and the traffic gate it was parked behind is open — the "long park on a fast-decaying claim" reasoning no longer applies, which is what raises it from 4. Two of the three things it needed now exist: an ad budget and a live Meta account. The remaining one is a creator, and the giveaway is itself the cheapest way to solve that — entrants are self-selected brand-interested people, and paying one in product for a usable clip costs COGS rather than cash (see the sourcing tactics below). Held at 6 rather than higher because a partnership ad requires the creator to grant permission on their own handle, which is a real coordination step a solo operator has not done before, and because the delivery-behaviour claims are unverified here.

*Source: Dara Denney — "How I Source, Brief & Manage Creators (My Exact System)" (_VtpfVeB2Kc)*

## Judge whether a person can carry a video within the first 2-3 seconds of a clip, on delivery rather than looks: energy and vocal variety (speeding up and slowing down), eye contact and magnetism, opening mid-conversation rather than on product features — and rule out anyone whose cadence reveals they are reading a script.

**Why it works:** Delivery is what holds attention; a monotone script-read registers as an ad and gets skipped, while vocal variety and direct eye contact pull the viewer in regardless of production quality. Leading with product features before establishing any story loses the viewer before the claim lands.

**Evidence offered:** Six teardowns of real clips, including two of her own — a gummy creator praised for vocal variety and 'girl talk' vibe, Emily D'Angelo for eye contact and magnetism, two 'good but not great' clips flagged for scripted intonation, one apparel clip criticized for cutting straight to product features, and a side-by-side of her own flat versus high-energy videos. Practitioner judgment, no view or conversion data.

**Fit here (5/10):** Durable principle class (on-camera delivery), age irrelevant. Executable today and free: the only realistic on-camera source here is repeat customers or the founder, and this gives concrete accept/reject criteria for a video before it goes onto a Shopify PDP or an Amazon listing video slot — the surface that carries the larger ~$1,800/mo channel. Capped at 5 because it is a selection filter, not a revenue mechanism, and the 'reject anyone who reads a script' bar is harsh when your candidate pool is a handful of willing customers rather than a paid roster; the practical version is to prompt for conversational answers rather than to reject the person.

*Source: Dara Denney — "How I Source, Brief & Manage Creators (My Exact System)" (_VtpfVeB2Kc)*

## Use your own customers as creators — they will often do it in exchange for product alone, and the result is either your best-performing ad or a flop, because VIP customers frequently cannot show up on camera.

**Why it works:** A real customer carries credibility no hired creator can fake; the cost is only product, and the risk is purely delivery quality, which you can screen for before using the footage.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion with an explicit caveat that she is nervous recommending it because outcomes are bimodal. She reports having seen it work and having seen customers accept product as the only compensation. No figures.

**Fit here (6/10):** Durable principle class, and the one item in this video with a genuine solo-operator translation. Repeat customers are 45-52% of revenue and at ~54 orders/month the operator can personally identify and email the handful of enthusiastic repeat buyers — the same top-customer cultivation already in marketing-retention-offers, extended to asking for a short video. Compensation is a stick of deodorant, not a fee, so it clears the budget constraint entirely, and the output has real homes: the Shopify PDP, the Amazon listing video slot on the larger ~$1,800/mo channel, and post-purchase email. Held at 6 because delivery quality is a coin flip, the video still has to be edited by a solo operator with no designer, and there are hard compliance limits: product-for-content is a material connection that must be disclosed under FTC rules, and it must never be routed into an Amazon review, which is a TOS violation.

*Source: Dara Denney — "How I Source, Brief & Manage Creators (My Exact System)" (_VtpfVeB2Kc)*

## Scale brief depth inversely to the creator's experience: give UGC and small creators a fully prescriptive storyboard with the script written out line by line; give established influencers only campaign context plus content pillars, shooting guidelines and explicit dos and don'ts so they write and send back their own script for approval; and with celebrities and high-caliber talent write the script yourself close to verbatim.

**Why it works:** Inexperienced creators do not know what converts, so leaving the script open produces weak content; experienced creators know their own audience's voice better than you do, so guardrails constrain the strategy without flattening the native delivery that makes UGC read as authentic; with celebrity talent the brand needs to control message and hook while borrowing the face, and talent will follow a well-written script closely.

**Evidence offered:** Teardown of her agency's own brief for a top-performing video (full clip-by-clip storyboard) contrasted with the briefs she personally receives as an influencer (campaign context only, she returns POV and script), plus a Jones Road Beauty brief with content pillars, shooting guidelines and dos/don'ts but no script. Notes she personally scripted celebrity ads verbatim at a nine-figure beauty brand and most followed them 'to a T'. No test or performance data.

**Fit here (5/10):** Durable principle class (briefing), age irrelevant, and it resolves directly into the customer-as-creator path adopted above: a repeat customer filming a testimonial is the least experienced case on the scale, so the brief must be fully prescriptive — the exact question to answer, the sentences to open on, the length. Free and executable today. Capped at 5 because only the fully-prescriptive end of the scale will ever apply here — the influencer-guardrail and celebrity-verbatim ends presuppose paid talent that is unreachable at ~$2,700/mo — and it is a production-quality lever rather than a revenue mechanism.

*Source: Dara Denney — "How I Source, Brief & Manage Creators (My Exact System)" (_VtpfVeB2Kc)*

## Build every brief from the same fixed set of components: a description of the video type wanted, the product linked and already shipped, a reference ad, the setting and wardrobe/style guidelines, a storyboard table of clips with the script and the visual for each, upload instructions, and recording guidelines.

**Why it works:** A fixed component list removes the decisions the creator would otherwise make badly and eliminates the round trips caused by missing information — the reference ad in particular communicates the target faster than description does.

**Evidence offered:** Walkthrough of a real brief her agency used to produce a recent top-performing video, section by section. Single example, no comparison against a looser brief.

**Fit here (5/10):** Durable principle class (asset briefing), free, and it is the concrete artifact that makes the customer-testimonial path produce usable footage instead of a shaky one-take. The reference-example component is the highest-value part and echoes the exemplar rule already in marketing-email-design-production and the reference-photo rule in marketing-ai-product-imagery, so it transfers with a known mechanism. Held at 5 because the wardrobe/setting and camera-spec sections are agency overkill for a customer filming on a phone, and this is a production checklist, not a revenue mechanism.
