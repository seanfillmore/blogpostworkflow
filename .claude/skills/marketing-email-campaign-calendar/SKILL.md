# Email Campaign Calendar

## Send a regular email campaign at least once a week from the start as the baseline. Increase frequency toward daily only as the list grows and revenue warrants more volume.

**Stage:** offer-aov — parked until the offer-aov phase opens. Recorded now so it is not re-derived later.

**Why it works:** Campaigns are active revenue that flows do not replace. A fixed weekly send keeps the list warm and produces recurring revenue from subscribers and buyers. The habit is easier to build early, and tying frequency increases to revenue avoids burning the list.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion with open-rate arithmetic (~20% open rate means subscribers see only a fraction of sends). Brands entering their coaching programme often send none. Regular campaigns were among the foundations in the client turnaround.

**Fit here (7/10):** Repeat customers are 45–52% of RSC revenue, and a standing weekly campaign is the cheapest lever on that revenue. One person can run it today, at any list size. No existing skill records a cadence floor. The open-rate argument is weaker than claimed, because the same engaged minority opens repeatedly. Read frequency increases against unsubscribe and complaint rates per send rather than assuming they are safe.

## Run a 'promo sprint' every four weeks: a 1–3 day promotion to the email list tied to a holiday, product launch or other occasion, to turn non-buyers into buyers and get existing buyers to buy again.

**Stage:** offer-aov — parked until the offer-aov phase opens. Recorded now so it is not re-derived later.

**Why it works:** A short, dated, occasion-tied deal produces a predictable monthly cash injection from the owned list at near-zero acquisition cost. That revenue pulls ad spend as a share of revenue back down while paid scales.

**Evidence offered:** Part of the client turnaround: a monthly promo was among the foundations credited with moving the brand to $8–10K profit months. Not isolated per tactic.

**Fit here (7/10):** This hits retention and the funding problem at once: owned-list cash offsets live Meta spend. One person can run it today. The new parts beyond marketing-scarcity-urgency-framing are the fixed four-week cadence, the 1–3 day window and the ad-spend offset job. Build offers around skin-line items or sets, not deep cuts on $2–5 contribution SKUs.
