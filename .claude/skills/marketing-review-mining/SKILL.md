# Review Mining

## Export your reviews to a CSV, hand it to an LLM, and ask it to surface the 'golden nugget' testimonials that would work as ad or headline copy.

**Why it works:** Customers already articulate the objection, the transformation, and the vocabulary better than you can; borrowing verbatim language beats writing it, and an LLM can scan the whole corpus for the few lines that carry.

**Evidence offered:** 'This again and again has gotten me winners' — repeated practitioner claim, no data.

**Fit here (8/10):** Durable principle, and the single best-fit tactic here for a solo operator. Amazon and Shopify reviews already exist and export as CSV; it costs nothing, needs no traffic, no budget, and no team. It does not require thousands of reviews — the method works on dozens, since the output is a handful of quotable lines for Amazon bullets, PDP copy, and email.

## Choose quotes whose 'before' state is the buyer's situation today and that name the mechanism that fixed it — not the quotes with the highest praise.

**Why it works:** Identification is what makes proof persuasive: a reader who sees themselves in the before state accepts the after state as available to them, while a before state that doesn't match reads as evidence about someone else. Naming the mechanism inside the quote does the second half — a reader who sees an impressive outcome with no stated cause may credit the person's effort or luck rather than your product. A quote that states the starting situation and what fixed it does both jobs; generic praise does neither. The format is not restricted to a photo pair — a testimonial, case study or video all work.

**Evidence offered:** Bill Phillips fitness before/after pages; an events director's testimonial functioning as an implicit before/after for another events director; and a fitness testimonial closing with 'Tim credits the simple yet effective 25-minute workouts, the balanced nutrition and the online peer support community' — described as deliberately making the causal link obvious. Practitioner teardowns, no conversion data.

**Fit here (7/10):** Works on dozens of reviews rather than thousands, which is the situation here. RSC's reviews already contain the before ('every natural deodorant I tried quit by noon') and the after ('made it through a 12-hour shift'), and the mechanism ('the baking-soda-free one is the only thing that got me through the switch'). Free to apply on the PDP, Amazon secondary images and A+ slots. The non-photo permission matters because honest photographic before/afters are hard to produce solo for body care. **Both halves are selection rules, not writing licence — editing a causal sentence into a customer's quote is a real compliance risk in body care, and fabricating the before state is worse.** Not higher because extraction and transformation format are already owned above and by `marketing-product-image-stack`; this only supplies which quote to pick.

## Spread displayed proof across different ages, genders and situations, and lead a case with the attribute that would otherwise disqualify the reader.

**Why it works:** Every visitor silently asks whether the result applies to someone like them. A proof set covering multiple identities means each reader finds their own case, and a case that opens with the disqualifying attribute ('at age 50…') neutralises the objection before it forms.

**Evidence offered:** Observes that the fitness page alternates male and female subjects and opens one testimonial with 'at age 50', with an explanation of what each choice defuses. Practitioner analysis, no data.

**Fit here (7/10):** Unusually well matched to this category's real disqualifiers — 'natural deodorant never works for someone who sweats as much as me', 'baking soda burns my skin', 'I gave up during the transition'. Those are exactly the beliefs blocking first purchase and reorder, and retention is the binding constraint. Executable today by curating existing Shopify and Amazon reviews into a proof set organised **by objection rather than by star rating**, then placing it on the PDP and in the A+ block. Held at 7 because with a small corpus some objection slots will be empty — **an empty slot is a prompt for the post-purchase review request, not something to fabricate.** Note Amazon prohibits directed review solicitation, so only the Shopify-side request can chase a specific gap.
