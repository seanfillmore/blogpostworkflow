# Post Purchase Onboarding

## Use post-purchase emails and a short pre-arrival video/education series to teach buyers how to use the product before it lands, so they reach the result faster — because a long time-to-result is what kills repeat purchases.

**Why it works:** The gap between paying and experiencing the promised outcome is dead time in which enthusiasm decays and misuse happens. Front-loading education compresses that gap, so the buyer succeeds sooner and reorders instead of concluding the product failed.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion plus his own observation that products arriving with no guidance leave customers to figure it out and therefore lack repeat purchases; no numbers.

**Fit here (8/10):** Durable retention principle, age irrelevant, and it names the exact causal chain that binds here: retention is the constraint, repeat customers are 45–52% of revenue, and the known churn cause in natural deodorant is a misunderstood transition period. Shopify post-purchase email flows are already a live, zero-cost surface for a solo operator. Distinct from existing coverage — the awareness skill supplies the 'how to start' headline stem and offer-construction mentions onboarding as an ease vector, but nothing owns the sequence-and-artifact layer (what emails, in what order, containing what).

*Source: Ecommerce Alley — "How To Apply Alex Hormozi's $100M Offers To Ecommerce" (Nxddux6n2-c)*

## Put printed instructions or a getting-started insert in the box instead of shipping a bare product, because customers left to figure it out on their own don't reorder.

**Why it works:** Missing instructions raise the effort and the time delay to the outcome simultaneously; a card that removes the guesswork gets the buyer to a correct first use and therefore to a result.

**Evidence offered:** Anecdote about receiving many products at his office with no instructions at all, plus the assertion that this causes lost repeat purchases.

**Fit here (7/10):** Durable principle class, and one of the few tactics that reaches the Amazon customer — where ~$1,800/mo of revenue lives and where there is no email relationship at all, making the insert the only owned onboarding surface on that channel. Cheap for a solo operator (a printed card), directly aimed at the binding retention constraint. Held at 7 rather than higher because it carries real per-unit print cost at a $50.46 AOV and Amazon restricts what an insert may say (no review solicitation or off-platform redirection that violates policy).
