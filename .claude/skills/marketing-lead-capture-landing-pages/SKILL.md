# Lead Capture Landing Pages

## Build a deliberately simple landing page for the opt-in — one headline, one image, one form, off a template — and ship it rather than agonizing over it.

**Why it works:** A page with few variables is easier to test and diagnose. The words and image carry nearly all the outcome. Complexity adds build time and confounds testing without adding conversion.

**Evidence offered:** Personal anecdote — the author avoided building a landing page for four years, then finished one before lunch.

**Fit here (8/10):** Durable principle and immediately live. A Meta campaign is being stood up to drive entries to a soap giveaway, which needs exactly this page, and a solo operator can build it in Shopify or a drag-and-drop tool in an afternoon. No existing skill covers the lead-capture page as an artifact — the friction-audit skill covers PDPs and checkout.

*Source: Alex Hormozi — "$100M Leads" (book, (part 9 of 16))*

## Produce the page by prompting an AI website-builder agent with the page's purpose and required elements plus 'use my existing website styles', then spend ~20 minutes swapping in your real copy and images.

State the job and the elements in one line — e.g. "I'm making a landing page for a free ebook about interior design, I need a title, subtitle, placeholder for a video, use my existing website styles". Let it generate, answer its clarifying questions (which calendar link, which CTA target), then replace placeholder copy and images with the real ones. This is the production method for the simple one-headline/one-image/one-form page above, not a licence to add sections.

**Why it works:** Once a visual identity exists on the site, the agent inherits type, colour and section styles and generates desktop, tablet and mobile layouts in minutes. That collapses what used to need a designer and a developer into a single prompt, so page production stops being the reason a funnel never gets tested.

**Evidence offered:** Live demo — the creator types the ebook prompt and the page builds on screen, including a clarifying question about which calendar link to use. He also regenerates an existing sprint landing page in a Swiss-grid style while holding the copy fixed.

**Fit here (7/10):** Directly on point: a Meta campaign is being stood up to drive soap-giveaway entries, and that campaign needs an entry page. One person can produce and revise it in an afternoon with no designer. Platform-mechanics class, but the tooling described is current (August 2026), so no staleness discount. Covers how to produce the asset, which the other lead-capture claims (what the page must contain, the congruency chain) do not. The AI-storefront skill is about redesigning the Shopify theme, a different surface.

## Enforce congruency across the whole chain — ad, entry/pre-sell page, offer page — so the click-to-close experience reads as one continuous document rather than a Frankenstein of mismatched pieces.

Match every hand-off on copy, angle, awareness level, imagery, colours, the exact product variant shown, and the people shown. Same look, same language, same promise. On the offer page, the headline must be a value prop, not the product name.

**Why it works:** People click because a specific benefit was promised. If the destination does not visibly restate that promise, the visitor re-evaluates from scratch and bounces, so ad spend is wasted at the handoff rather than at the ad. Each mismatch costs a second of confusion, and confusion compounds across hand-offs — a paw-licking ad landing on a grass-eating page reads as a different product.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion, with the observation that advertisers 'waste money until they remember it'. Also named as a top lever in scaling Nomisk, a period cramp brand, from 0 to $10M in year one, illustrated by the dog-paw-licking vs dog-eating-grass mismatch.

**Fit here (9/10):** Durable principle and the single highest-leverage item for the live situation. $30/day is being spent driving cold Meta traffic through ad → giveaway entry page → offer, which is exactly the three-step chain described, and a promise mismatch anywhere in it silently burns the whole budget. Free to fix, one person, no volume threshold required. Concretely checkable: same scent/variant pictured throughout, same colours, same people, and an offer-page headline that states the benefit rather than the SKU name. The full 'build separate ads, pre-sell pages and offer pages for every variant' version needs multiple cells. At $30/day, scale it down to one angle carried consistently end to end.

## Bridge the second ask to the action just taken using the consistency principle — 'now that you've done A, do B to get the most out of A' — to move more people from click to giving contact information.

**Why it works:** Cialdini's consistency principle: people want to behave consistently with an action they just took. Framing the opt-in as the natural continuation of the click raises step-through, rather than treating the second ask as a fresh decision.

**Evidence offered:** Cites Robert Cialdini's Influence.

**Fit here (7/10):** Durable persuasion principle, concretely applicable to the giveaway entry page and the Shopify opt-in popup. It costs one line of copy and nothing else. Retention is the binding constraint, so growing the sub-1,000 list with people who actually opted in matters. Scored below message match because the lift is smaller and harder to verify at 54 orders/month.

*Source: Alex Hormozi — "$100M Leads" (book, (part 9 of 16))*

## Make the CTA destination itself frictionless — a short, memorable, typeable URL (acquisition.com/training, not a long parameterized path) — because the audience can only act on what they can easily execute.

**Why it works:** Every character of friction between motivation and action loses people. Motivation from an ad is high but very short-lived, so the destination must be executable instantly.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion plus the author's own $370k single-word domain purchase, which he admits may bias him.

**Fit here (6/10):** Durable principle. It scales down honestly to creating a clean /giveaway or /free-soap path on the existing Shopify domain and using it on package inserts, QR codes and any spoken or printed mention. Low ceiling, since most Meta traffic clicks a button. But it is free, and it matters for the printed insert, the only owned surface for Amazon buyers. No recommendation to buy an expensive domain.

*Source: Alex Hormozi — "$100M Leads" (book, (part 9 of 16))*

## Test the structural variables of the landing page against each other — long page with more info versus short and direct, light mode versus dark mode, with a video versus without.

**Stage:** scale — parked until the scale phase opens.

**Why it works:** These are the page-level variables that change how much a visitor has to process before deciding. Which one wins is market-specific, so you find it by running the variants rather than by reasoning about it.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion only.

**Fit here (6/10):** The variable list is sensible and the surface is live — a giveaway entry page is being built for the Meta campaign right now. But an A/B verdict on page length or dark mode needs readable volume, and at ~54 orders/month on $30/day this returns noise. Existing guidance already says to ship unmeasured rather than attach a verdict to an underpowered null. Park behind scale: run it once entry/order volume through a single page is high enough to separate the arms. Until then, pick one variant and ship the deliberately simple page. Trigger: sustained traffic to one entry page producing enough conversions per arm to read a difference.

*Source: orenmeetsworld — "The only funnels guide you'll ever need (Marketing 2.0)" (-8YiaBpl3DU)*

## Treat a proven messaging angle as a funnel, not just an ad concept: when customer testimonials reveal a real use case, make more ads on it and build a dedicated hybrid 'intro offer' landing page per angle that makes the visitor read a short use-case explanation before reaching the buy box.

**Stage:** cro — parked until the cro phase opens.

When reviews show a recurring use case (aluminium-free deodorant for gym sweat, lotion for winter hand-cracking), give that use case its own Meta ads and its own page. Put a short use-case block above the lotion or set offer, then the buy box. This is the congruency principle above applied per angle: one page per angle with education before the buy box, not a separate advertorial step. It keeps the first funnel to two elements: ad → one page.

**Why it works:** Before the buy box appears, the visitor gets more reasons to buy that are specific to the situation the ad promised. The page stays congruent with the angle instead of dropping a use-case-specific click onto a generic PDP. The host also claims, unverified, that educated buyers are stickier.

**Evidence offered:** The 'pre-workout alternative' use case surfaced in testimonials. Ultra built an angle-specific landing page that 'helped a lot', sent about 70% of paid traffic to such pages in spring, and scaled the approach to several messaging funnels. The creator has no retention data for these pages.

**Fit here (7/10):** Conversion rate is the binding constraint for this tactic, and one person can build an angle-specific Shopify page in a day. Reading whether it beats the PDP will be slow at ~16 Shopify orders/month, but that does not block the build.
