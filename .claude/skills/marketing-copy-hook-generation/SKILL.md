# Copy Hook Generation

> Split 2026-09-19 out of `marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats`: this skill covers where candidate hook lines come from and how they are cut before one ships. What a hook must deliver lives in `marketing-copy-hook-construction`; the named opening gambits and the ad formats stay in `marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats`; congruence, the body skeletons, the close and the drafting process live in `marketing-copy-body-structure`. Some "above"/"below" cross-references point across those boundaries.

## Run a written hook through an LLM with a specific clarity prompt rather than editing it by feel: 'I've written a hook for a short form video about X; keep the meaning identical but rewrite it at a sixth grade reading level so the viewer cannot misunderstand it.'

**Why it works:** The rewrite task is mechanical — simplify vocabulary and sentence order while holding the meaning fixed — which is exactly what a language model does reliably, so it returns several clearer framings of the same idea in seconds. Because the meaning is pinned by the prompt, the model is not being asked to invent anything, which is where its output usually goes wrong.

**Evidence offered:** The prompt is given verbatim; no before/after performance data.

**Fit here (7/10):** A named, copy-pasteable prompt one person can run on every hook he writes for his own videos, and equally on the first line of Meta primary text and on email subject lines. Zero cost, no people, no volume requirement, and it is a generic LLM instruction rather than a platform feature, so the current-era tool names do not decay it. It is also the one LLM copy task this skill trusts to come back near-shippable, and worth understanding why: meaning is held fixed and only vocabulary and word order move, so the next-token bias that makes the model summarise an ad in line one or re-theme a body around the hook has nothing to grip. The three LLM *generation* prompts below (swipe file, emotion wheel, amplify-the-winner) do ask the model to invent, which is exactly why each of them ends in a human pass. Even here, trust does not extend to shipping unread — the rewrite still has to pass the withheld-resolution and named-topic check above, the three kill-tests below, the understatement filter below, and the health gate. Add it as a step on the LLM draft audit rather than treating it as a separate ritual.

## Do not prompt an LLM for hooks cold — paste in a swipe file of hooks that actually grabbed YOUR attention and instruct it to rewrite your hooks in those styles.

**Why it works:** A cold prompt returns the median of the training distribution, which is why it hands back 'get rid of X fast' every time. Supplying real examples that already earned a stop transfers their structure and rhythm onto your product instead, which is the whole gap between mediocre first-pass output and lines that are worth polishing. You are not asking the model to be creative; you are asking it to re-cast your subject into a shape that has already been proven to stop someone.

**Evidence offered:** Before/after examples given in the thread: level-1 output described as mediocre versus level-2 output ('The 3-second ritual cigar guys do in the driveway before walking inside so she never smells the lounge on you').

**Fit here (8/10):** Zero cost, one person, runnable this afternoon — the swipe file is built by screenshotting the hooks that stop him in his own feed into one doc, and the output feeds his own filmed short-form hooks first, then Meta primary text and email subject lines. This is the right substitute for the variant-and-kill-test rule below at this volume: that one ranks *his own* past hooks by performance, and at ~54 orders/month with a thin posting history there is barely a ranked list to draw on, whereas other people's proven hooks are available immediately and in quantity. Keep the file tagged by which of the six differentiation angles above each swiped hook is using, so it doubles as the menu's example bank, and it is the same external seed the power-phrase cloud below needs. Two constraints. Swipe the *structure*, not the claim — most high-performing hooks in adjacent categories (supplements, skincare, weight loss) carry an efficacy promise this catalogue cannot make, and lifting one verbatim fails `lib/seo-copy-health-gate.js`. And the output is a draft, not a hook: it still has to clear the withheld-resolution and named-topic rule above, the sixth-grade reading level rule above, second person, the three kill-tests below and the understatement filter below.

## Screenshot an emotion wheel, paste it into the LLM, and prompt it for a hook that makes your ideal customer upset — because a hook that provokes an emotion holds attention better than one that describes a benefit.

**Why it works:** Provoked emotion — indignation, being contradicted, mild shame — is a stronger reason to keep watching than curiosity about a benefit, because the reader has to resolve the feeling rather than merely satisfy an interest. The emotion wheel gives the model a concrete vocabulary of feelings to aim at, which is what stops it from defaulting to neutral benefit language; without the wheel, 'write an emotional hook' returns the same benefit sentence with an exclamation mark.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion plus a generated example ('Most cigar guys think their wife is overreacting. She's not. You actually smell worse than you think. Here's why you can't tell.') and the claim that some of his best ads ever were built this way.

**Fit here (7/10):** Free, solo, runnable today, and the emotional territory is unusually available to this catalogue — deodorant and natural oral care sit directly on top of social self-consciousness, and nose-blindness ('you don't smell what everyone else smells') is exactly the wheel-driven hook this procedure produces. The founder films and publishes the result himself before it is ever tested as Meta primary text, so the line has to survive being said out loud. Not a duplicate of the negative-framing or shared-enemy angles in `marketing-conversion-copy-angles`: those are angles, this is a named generation procedure that can be pointed at any of them. Three governors. Mild shame is the live wire in body care — provoke the buyer's *situation*, never their character, or the piece buys attention and loses the sale. The output still has to be second person per the rule below, still has to withhold its resolution, and still has to clear the understatement rule below, since provocation is not a licence for superlatives. And an 'upset' hook aimed at skin rather than odour is one word away from an unapproved-drug claim — the SKIN cluster is where `lib/seo-copy-health-gate.js` blocks exactly this vocabulary, so run the wheel on the deodorant and oral-care problems, not on eczema.

*Source: Nick Theriot (@nicktheriot_) — "The hook system (X thread, 2026-09-08)" (social post)*

## When an AI-generated hook lands, do not ship it — ask for ten more like it to get variety around the winning idea, then take the core idea into a doc and do the final polish by hand.

**Why it works:** The first acceptable output is a direction, not a finished line. Re-prompting around it explores the neighbourhood of an idea that already worked instead of jumping to a fresh concept, which is where most of the value is; and the human pass supplies the voice and the concrete specifics — the real day, the real product name, the real situation — that the model has no access to.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion only ('AI gives you direction; YOU give it the final polish. Never just copy and paste.').

**Fit here (6/10):** Free, immediately runnable, and the correct habit at this volume: at ~54 orders/month and $30/day there will never be a readable per-hook signal, so mining a promising idea by hand is strictly cheaper than shipping fresh concepts and waiting for data that will not arrive. It closes the loop on the two generation prompts above — swipe file or emotion wheel produces the direction, this rule turns one hit into ten candidates, and the three kill-tests below cut them down. Scored 6 rather than higher because it partly restates rules already here: generating several variants and cutting them against explicit tests is the variant-and-kill-test rule below, and never shipping LLM copy as-is is the standing position of this skill. The additive part is narrow but real — the amplify-the-winner re-prompt, which is the instinct a solo operator lacks, since the natural move on a line you like is to stop.

## Before shipping, read the hook alone, out of the script, and ask how many ways it could be interpreted — if more than one, rewrite until only the intended reading survives.

**Why it works:** The writer reads the hook with the rest of the piece already in his head, so ambiguity is invisible to him. Isolating the one or two sentences and actively hunting for alternative readings surfaces what a cold reader might take from it, and every unintended reading is a chance they decide the piece is not for them.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion only, offered as a 'clarity-proofing' tip.

**Fit here (7/10):** A real QA pass rather than motivational framing: specific input (the hook alone), specific test (count the interpretations), specific action (rewrite to one). Runnable today by one person on his own video scripts, and identical in form to the imagery QA passes in `marketing-ai-product-imagery` and the LLM draft audit — add it there as an item rather than running it separately. Concrete failures this catches here: 'I stopped using deodorant' reads as either 'I switched to a natural one' or 'I now use nothing', and the second reading loses the buyer; 'this fixed my armpits' reads as a skin claim. That second case is why the pass earns its place beyond craft — a hook read in isolation can sound like a disease claim even when the body is plainly cosmetic, which is exactly what `lib/seo-copy-health-gate.js` blocks, so run the isolation read before the gate does it for you. This is the back-end check on a finished hook; the three kill-tests directly below are the front-end filter that stops most ambiguous candidates ever reaching it. One scope note: a headline written against a specific image is not judged in isolation — see the per-image headline rule below, where the picture is part of the intended reading.

## Write hooks by pulling up your own past hooks sorted by performance, adapting the strongest phrasing to today's topic across several variants, then deleting each variant against three explicit tests — too corny, too long-winded, or 'I like the sound of it but I'm not sure what it means'.

**Why it works:** Generating variants off phrasing that already earned attention removes the blank page, and a named kill-test turns the choice between them from inspiration into a repeatable filter. The third test is the load-bearing one: it catches the specific self-deception where the writer likes the rhythm of his own line and therefore cannot see that it names no topic — the same defect the two-part hook spec calls a clarity failure and the four failure modes call confusion.

**Evidence offered:** Live walkthrough of the creator writing and killing four or five variants of one hook out loud.

**Fit here (7/10):** One person, a list of his own past hooks and a blank page — runnable this afternoon on a deodorant Reel, and the same three tests apply to Meta primary text on the $30/day giveaway campaign, to email subject lines and to Amazon bullet leads. Distinct from the isolation read above without restating it: that pass interrogates one finished hook for ambiguity, this one produces several candidates and kills most of them fast, and 'I'm not sure what it means' names the writer's own blind spot rather than a cold reader's. The phrasing to adapt comes from the power-phrase cloud below; 'too corny' is the understatement rule below applied as a kill-test; 'too long-winded' is bounded by the two-to-three-line budget below, which is a length allowance, not a licence to ramble in line one. Honest limit: with a thin posting history the ranked list of past hooks is short, so on day one draw the variants from other people's proven hooks via the swipe-file prompt above — that is the designed substitute at this volume — and seed the rest from the six-angle differentiation menu, the seven interest components and the contrast rule below, ranking your own as posts accumulate. The three tests are also where the ten amplify-the-winner variants above get cut, and where the eight per-image headline candidates below get whittled down.

## On a static ad or listing image, write the headline *after* the visual exists and *per image* — roughly eight candidates for that specific frame, then a second round whittling down — and where the image is especially vivid, force the headline to reference what is actually in the frame rather than standing alone.

**Why it works:** A headline written before the image is generic to the angle, so it lands on whatever frame it is later pasted onto and the two elements merely repeat each other. Written against a specific finished image, the line gets a real choice: carry a standalone claim, or close the loop the picture opened. That is the only way image and copy compound instead of duplicating. Generating eight and culling in a second round produces range to pick from, which is what stops you defending the first line you wrote because it is the only one you have.

**Evidence offered:** Seb's stated working process ('I'll write maybe like eight per image and then kind of do a second round and start whittling it down... in some cases the headlines work as standalone, in other cases the image is so vivid we have to figure out how to incorporate it'). Practitioner process, no data.

**Fit here (7/10):** A concrete production sequence for the one person who both generates the images and writes the copy, runnable in an afternoon on the current static set and the Amazon/PDP listing images: image first, eight headlines against it, cull in a second round. What it adds over everything else in this skill is the *image–headline relationship* decision — the rules above cull hooks judged as text in isolation and say nothing about whether the line should name what is in the picture. So it deliberately qualifies the isolation read above: for a headline written against a vivid frame, the picture is part of the intended reading, and the test becomes 'is it unambiguous *with this image*', not 'is it unambiguous alone'. Eight is the volume target; the three kill-tests above are the filter for the second round, and the four-point checklist above still gates whichever survives. Two category notes. The vivid frames this catalogue actually has are situational, not clinical — the shirt, the gym bag, the 3pm meeting — so the incorporating headline names that situation rather than the product, which keeps it consistent with the product-out-of-the-hook rule below. And an image that dramatises a *result* on skin plus a headline that names it is the pairing `lib/seo-copy-health-gate.js` blocks, so the frame can carry the situation and the line can carry the mechanism, but neither may carry an efficacy outcome. Scope: this is a static-asset rule, which is where the paid side sits per `marketing-paid-creative-testing`; on video the equivalent split is the spoken-versus-overlay rule below.

## Extract a power-word and power-phrase cloud from the hooks that actually performed best, and use that vocabulary directionally in new hooks rather than inventing hook language from scratch.

**Why it works:** The words and phrases that recur across high-performing hooks encode the psychological trigger that is actually stopping the scroll for this audience, independent of the topic they were attached to. Reusing that vocabulary on a new topic transplants the working trigger instead of gambling on a fresh one. Some of what comes out of the cloud will be unusable verbatim — the point is the direction it aims you in, not the phrase itself.

**Evidence offered:** Screen-share of a generated phrase cloud ('hypnotizes the scroll', 'physically cannot scroll away', 'the opposite of slop') pulled from deep-analyzed winning hooks; assertion that some are unusable but the direction is what matters.

**Fit here (7/10):** Directly runnable with no tooling: sort his own Reels/TikToks by views, list the hooks in one document, and mark the emotive phrasing that recurs — then reuse it on his own social hooks first, and secondarily in Meta primary text on the $30/day campaign and in email subject lines. Distinct from the trigger-word injection rule below, which assumes you already know which loaded phrase to swap in; this is the method for *deriving* that vocabulary from performance data rather than from taste. It is also the phrase bank the variant-and-kill-test rule above draws on. Cheap, no volume gate. Three constraints. The cloud is directional: an extracted phrase still has to clear the sixth-grade reading level rule above and the understatement rule below, and the source's own examples ('physically cannot scroll away') are exactly the hyperbole this catalogue kills. An efficacy-flavoured phrase lifted off a competitor's hook can fail `lib/seo-copy-health-gate.js` outright. And with a thin posting history there is not much of your own to rank yet, so seed the cloud from the swipe file above, from competitor hooks per `marketing-competitor-messaging-teardown` and from the voice-of-customer file per `marketing-review-mining` — customer phrasing is the safest source, because it is real, on-register and already passes as a peer talking.

*Source: Kallaway — "How to Use Claude To Grow From 0 to 100K Followers (Full Course)" (vwlK6MbdAto)*

## Inject a trigger word or emotionally loaded phrase ('ruining my life', 'quits by noon') into a hook structure that already converts — don't rebuild the structure.

**Why it works:** The loaded phrase raises emotional temperature and stops the scroll, while the underlying structure that already worked is left intact — so you add attention without discarding the converting logic. This is the word-level lever that makes the taboo and negative-framing angles in `marketing-conversion-copy-angles` land; it is an edit pass on a proven line, not a new concept.

**Evidence offered:** Claims both hooks shown had hook rates above 45%; agency practice with DTC brands. No breakdown of which brands or over what spend.

**Fit here (6/10):** Durable-principle class and transfers off-platform at zero cost: email subject lines and preview text (the live surface, since retention is the binding constraint), Amazon bullets, and PDP headlines. Scored mid because its native measurement is hook rate on video ads, which does not exist here — Real Skin Care can only judge it by email opens on a small list and by retention on the founder's own organic clips. Practical use: take the best-performing existing subject line or bullet and swap one flat noun phrase for a loaded one, keeping everything else fixed. Which phrase to swap in is not a guess — pull it from the power-phrase cloud above rather than inventing it. One loaded phrase is the point; a draft where every noun has been upgraded fails the understatement rule below, and a loaded phrase that costs comprehension fails the sixth-grade reading level rule above. And the loaded phrase is an attention device only — if the body then spends its word count justifying the loaded phrase rather than arguing the buying motive, the golden-thread rule above has been broken.
