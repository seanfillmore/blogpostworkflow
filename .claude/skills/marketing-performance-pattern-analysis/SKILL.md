# Performance Pattern Analysis

## Step 1 — Tag attributes before results arrive: a metrics row is not an analysis, so record each asset's qualitative properties (format, who delivered it, imagery and production quality, setting, messaging angle, awareness level) at the moment you produce it.

**Why it works:** Performance numbers tell you which asset won but carry no information about why, so acting on them alone means producing "more variants of B" without knowing which property of B did the work. Attributes recorded at production time turn a set of results into a set of testable explanations, and recurring attributes across winners become the thing you deliberately reproduce.

**Evidence offered:** Walkthrough of a four-variant test slide, showing what the numeric readout omits, plus one case where the same creator was identified as the recurring driver across multiple winning ads and was re-hired on that basis. Practitioner demonstration, no controlled comparison.

**Fit here (5/10):** Durable-principle class (a feedback-loop discipline, not a platform mechanic), so age is irrelevant. Its native home — a Meta creative test — does not exist here, but the attribute-tagging half translates honestly onto the two surfaces a solo operator actually has: Klaviyo campaign history (tag each send by angle, format, and awareness level *before* comparing opens and clicks) and Amazon/PDP image changes (record which lever changed, which marketing-product-image-stack already asks for, so the frames can be re-run as proper A/B pairs once sessions support it). Capped at 5 because it is close kin to the decile contrast in Step 2 below — the additive part is only the explicit attribute taxonomy recorded ahead of results — and because at ~54 orders/month and a sub-1,000 subscriber list every conclusion is directional judgement, not a finding.

*Source: Dara Denney — "How to Analyze Facebook Ads Data the Right Way (The 2025 Guide)" (CCsty8R0UaA)*

## Step 2 — Run a common factors analysis: put your top 10% and bottom 10% of comparable assets side by side — content pieces, ads, customers, sales call recordings — and name the discrepancies between them, because those discrepancies are the transferable skill.

**Why it works:** Doing volume produces data; the data only becomes skill when you close the feedback loop by contrasting winners against losers. The differences between the best and worst performers isolate the variable that is actually doing the work, so you can deliberately make more of what wins and less of what doesn't, instead of averaging across everything. The tags from Step 1 are what make the discrepancies nameable rather than vibes.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion from personal practice — he says it is how he learns things nobody teaches, and applies it to content, ads, customer avatars, and sales scripts. No worked example, no numbers, no controlled comparison.

**Fit here (5/10):** Durable-principle class (a learning/feedback loop, not a platform mechanic), so age is irrelevant. Executable today at zero cost on two surfaces a solo operator actually has: Klaviyo campaign history (which sends earned opens and clicks vs. which died) and Shopify order history (what the repeat buyers who drove 45–52% of revenue have in common versus one-and-done buyers). Capped at 5 because the deciles are tiny here — ~54 orders/month, 12 SKUs, a small list, and no working attribution — so every conclusion is directional judgement rather than a finding, and the ad-and-content version of the exercise needs volume this business does not have. It also partly overlaps the top-customer identification already in marketing-retention-offers and the winner-dissection rule parked in marketing-paid-acquisition-scaling; the additive part is the explicit bottom-decile contrast, which neither owns.
