# Content Repurposing

## Repurpose a long video script into a blog post with the LLM — letting it strip out the on-camera personality and jokes because the blog reader arrived from a search result rather than from your audience — and publish it on your own site with the video embedded at the top so search traffic can either read or watch.

**Why it works:** The script and the article serve different readers: a subscriber came for you, a searcher came for the answer. Reformatting into sections and headings with the personality removed makes the same researched substance digestible to someone with no relationship to the brand, at near-zero marginal cost since the research is already done; publishing it on a domain you own captures the off-platform search demand and offers both consumption formats.

**Evidence offered:** Demonstration only — he generates the blog post and points out approvingly that the model removed his jokes and personality; the search-capture argument is reasoned from the example query 'best beginner cameras for YouTube'. His additional claim that embedded plays earn more platform distribution is assertion only.

**Fit here (5/10):** Durable principle, and RSC has a Shopify blog it already owns, so this is runnable today by one person. Mid score on merit rather than timing: the prime directive is revenue, and an owned article is several steps removed from an order — its honest value is as the owned primary source AI search surfaces now favour, and as a cheap second use of research already paid for. The personality-stripping instruction is the genuinely useful half because it names who the second asset is for. The embedded-views-earn-more-distribution half is fast-decay YouTube platform mechanics and is discarded.

## Repurpose the same script into a deliberately much shorter email newsletter, then follow up with one more prompt asking it to add a call to action at the end that sends the reader to the full piece.

**Why it works:** The email's job is not to deliver the content but to route an existing subscriber to it, so the instruction has to be 'much shorter' plus an explicit ask — and because the LLM's first pass omits the ask, you add it as a named second turn rather than rewriting from scratch.

**Evidence offered:** Demonstration only — he generates the newsletter, notes it came back with a subject line and merge-field personalisation, then adds the CTA on a follow-up turn.

**Fit here (7/10):** Durable practice and directly on the route to the named target: retention is the binding constraint, repeat customers are roughly half of revenue, and the hard part of emailing the list consistently as one person is having something to send. Turning content he has already made into short routing emails removes that blocker, and the existing lifecycle and email-design skills specify the skeleton this drops into. Runnable today on a sub-1,000 list and improves as the list grows.

## Have the LLM analyse a long script and pull out three to five short-form video ideas from it, each returned with a hook, the substance of the middle, and a CTA back to the long-form piece.

**Why it works:** The long asset already contains several self-contained claims and opinions; extracting them as standalone shorts gets multiple posts out of one research-and-scripting effort, and the CTA at the end routes the short-form audience back to the deeper asset.

**Evidence offered:** Demonstration only — he runs the prompt and notes the model returned hook, body and CTA without being asked, including a specific product recommendation lifted from his own script.

**Fit here (7/10):** Organic short-form is a live surface RSC runs today — he films, edits, writes and publishes it himself — and the recorded content program requires a fixed posting cadence with a 3-2-1 bucket mix, which is a volume problem for one person. Extracting several shorts from one longer piece is a direct answer to that volume problem, and the hook-plus-CTA structure matches the hook construction and give:ask rules already in the skills. Runnable today with free tools.
