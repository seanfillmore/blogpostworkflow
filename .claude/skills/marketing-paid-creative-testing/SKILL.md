# Paid Creative Testing

## Start creative testing with static ads rather than video — a static has two failure points, the copy and the image, that one person controls end to end, while video introduces nine or more.

**Why it works:** Video drags in a long chain of dependencies — finding the right creator, getting the right performance, the script, the opening hook, the opening caption, the footage, the editor, an edit that actually supports the idea, then format and length testing. Each link is a way the system can break, and most of them depend on other people. When a video ad comes back flat you cannot attribute the result: a good idea dies from a bad edit or a weak performance rather than a weak concept. A static collapses the chain to two elements you own, so iteration is faster and the read on whether the *message* works is clean.

**Evidence offered:** An enumerated nine-item dependency list for video versus a two-element static setup, plus the concession that video ads can work extremely well. Assertion, no comparative performance data.

**Fit here (7/10):** Durable-principle class in substance (dependency counting in asset production), so age is not the limiter. It is an unusually exact match for the standing constraints rather than a gate: there is no videographer, no creator roster, no editor and no budget for any of them, `agents/ad-studio` produces static plates, and marketing-paid-media-measurement already scored hook rate and hold rate at 4/10 explicitly because this business barely produces video. What this adds is a reason to prefer static that is not 'we cannot afford video' — with $30/day and one operator, the static path is also the only one whose failures are attributable. It pairs directly with the 3-2-2 flexible ad below, which wants three creatives of the same format and aspect ratio; static plates are the cheapest way to satisfy that constraint, and the native-format list immediately below is what those plates should look like. Note the long-form native-style ad format the creator names is already recorded in marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats at 5/10 — only the static-before-video dependency argument is additive here, and it is a format-selection rule, not a second copy of the native-ad entry. Held at 7 because it is a production-and-attribution rule rather than a revenue mechanism, and a repeat-customer testimonial clip may still be worth shooting later. One caveat from the one-job-per-ad refinement below: the job the ad has to do is decided first, and only then the format — a standing 'we always make statics' default is exactly the reflex that rule guards against, even though static is usually where it lands here.

*Source: Stefan Georgi — "Secret of the DTC Universe #8: Collapse the Wave Function" (social post)*

**Refinement — test any genuinely new idea, message or concept as a static first, because the unmoving frame forces you to say the thing clearly.** Video hands the marketer unlimited room — characters, scenes, script, edit — and that room lets an undecided message survive, because the production covers for it. A static has one frame that does not move: if the message cannot be stated inside that frame, it has not actually been decided yet. Statics also cost almost nothing to produce, so they are the cheapest instrument for finding out whether an angle has any pull before production effort goes into video, and the message that survives the frame then transfers cleanly to every other surface.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion from operator experience — 'if you restrict the amount of storytelling elements you can work with as a marketer, you often get closer to what you're trying to say'. No data.

**Fit here (7/10):** Durable craft principle, and a genuinely different argument from the dependency-counting claim above: that one is about who controls production, this one is about the single frame as a clarity test on the message itself. It is directly useful to a solo operator who also writes his own organic short-form scripts — drafting the angle as a one-frame static before filming stops a woolly message from being papered over by editing, and it costs a phone and a Canva file. Pair it with the messaging-brief rule below (the brief names the argument; the frame proves the argument can be stated) and with the clarity-spectrum rule below (a concept that cannot land at either pole in one frame is not ready). Held at 7 because it governs sequencing and discipline rather than producing revenue directly.

## Build those statics in eight named native formats — text post, screenshot, review card, us-vs-them, candid photo, meme, editorial card, story-post — dressed as iPhone notes, text threads, Reddit posts, Google searches, DMs or Amazon reviews, rather than a product on white with a headline.

**Why it works:** In the first half-second a viewer categorises what they are looking at. A frame that reads as a native artifact of the feed gets read before it is filed as advertising, whereas a product-on-white with a headline is classified as an ad and skipped before a single word lands. The format is doing the work of buying the first beat of attention; the copy only gets a chance once that categorisation has gone the right way.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion plus the creator's own claim that 'the best statics I've pulled this month' are in these formats; a linked swipe board of 150+ examples is offered as illustration, not measurement.

**Fit here (8/10):** Platform-mechanics class published days ago, so no age discount, and it is the concrete rotation list the static-first rule above was missing. Every one of the eight is producible in Canva by one person with no designer, editor or video crew, and they cost nothing extra to run inside the existing 3-2-2 flexible ad — three creatives in three different native formats is a cheaper way to satisfy 'meaningfully different' than three new photoshoots. Natural deodorant and oral care are categories where a text thread ('does natural deodorant actually work or am I going to stink at work'), a Reddit screenshot or an Amazon-style review card is a truthful, native-feeling frame rather than a costume. The general camouflage principle is already recorded in marketing-copy-hooks-and-formats; what is additive here is the named, buildable format list for paid statics. Pair it with the angle-not-audience refinement below: the format is the wrapper, the pain angle is the content, and the two vary independently. The format is also chosen *after* the messaging brief and the clarity-pole decision below, not before them — the container should be picked to carry an argument that already exists. Note that us-vs-them is both a native format and one of the product-aware launch angles below — it is the cheapest single asset to build first.

*Source: Fedot (@FedotOff90) — "Native static ad formats that do not look like ads (X post)" (social post)*

**Refinement — audit each native static against three legs at once, and ship it only if all three are present: it must not look like an ad, it must borrow a format people are already consuming (forum thread screenshot, review stack, notes-app screenshot, text message), and it must carry authority.** Authority means a named professional, a visible credential, or specificity only someone who actually lived the problem would have. Drop the authority leg and you have a native-looking artifact saying nothing worth believing; drop the format leg and you have an honest photo nobody stops on; drop the not-an-ad leg and the other two never get read. The three are not a ranking, they are a pass/fail checklist run before the file is uploaded.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion only, framed as what the agency's converting statics have in common. No data.

**Fit here (7/10):** Durable-principle class, runnable today by one person in Canva, and it closes the obvious hole in the eight-format list above — a correctly dressed text thread or review card can still be empty. The authority leg is unusually cheap for a founder-formulated skin, deodorant and oral care catalogue: the formulating story in first person, one ingredient specific with a reason attached, a dated customer detail lifted from the review corpus. It is a different test from the no-brand-context audit below — that one asks whether a stranger can understand the frame, this one asks whether a stranger has any reason to believe it. Practical sequence: messaging brief, then clarity pole, then native format, then this three-leg audit as the last gate before upload, with 'no authority leg' a legitimate kill reason to write into the concept-selection log below.

*Source: Lorenzo Pravata (@lorenzo_pravata) — "How to exploit GPT-2.5 Images for more winning ads" (social post)*

## Judge an image batch by visual distinctness, not by count — Meta clusters near-identical creatives into a single delivery entity, so only a new scene, subject, format, lighting condition or time of day is a genuinely new ad.

**Why it works:** The retrieval and delivery system treats a creative as an entity and recognises variations that share a visual fingerprint, so fifty copy-swapped versions of one image enter the auction as one ticket — they share a learning pool, share delivery and fatigue on the same curve, and expand reach not at all. Visual re-composition is what produces a separate entity with its own learning and its own fatigue clock. The field test is blunt: would a stranger scrolling with sound off say 'I have seen this one already'? If yes, it is not a new ad no matter how different the headline is.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion about platform behaviour from an agency running AI statics in client accounts since April; one AI-generated static named as the top performer in a new skincare account ahead of every video launched there. No delivery data or controlled comparison.

**Fit here (8/10):** Platform-mechanics class but ten days old and describing current Meta delivery behaviour, so age is not a mark against it, and it changes what gets made rather than what gets spent — fully runnable at $30/day. It sharpens two rules already here rather than restating them. The 3-2-2 flexible ad below wants three creatives of the *same format and aspect ratio*; this says those three must still differ in scene, subject, lighting or native-format type, or the flexible ad is really a one-creative ad wearing three hats. And the breakthrough-iteration rule below is the place this bites hardest: multiplying executions of a proven angle means re-composing the frame — the same baking-soda-irritation pain delivered as a text thread, then a review stack, then a bathroom-counter candid — not regenerating the same plate with new type on it, which is the default output of an AI image pipeline left unsupervised. Practical discipline for `agents/ad-studio`: line the batch up as thumbnails, and count how many visually distinct entities are actually present before counting files.

*Source: Lorenzo Pravata (@lorenzo_pravata) — "How to exploit GPT-2.5 Images for more winning ads" (social post)*

## Give the image exactly one job — stop the right person's scroll with a frame from their own life — and let the copy do the selling.

**Why it works:** Splitting the labour stops you briefing for a beautiful picture and makes you brief for a specific lived moment: concrete evidence of the problem being lived with, not illustrated. Two flattened pillows on an office chair, a hand on the lower back getting out of the car, the dent in a worn seat cushion. A depicted symptom is self-selecting — the people it stops are the ones already having that experience, which is exactly the audience the copy can close. An image asked to also explain the product and carry the offer stops everyone weakly and qualifies no one.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion with worked examples (the pillows, the hand on the back, the seat-cushion dent). No performance data.

**Fit here (8/10):** Durable-principle brief rule, and this catalogue has obvious lived-evidence frames a phone can shoot for free: the pit stain checked in the mirror before leaving the house, a half-used deodorant stick in the bin, the 2pm reapplication at a desk, the toothpaste tube by a sink at the hour someone actually notices the problem. It is a division of labour *inside* a static and therefore distinct from the one-job-per-ad refinement below, which picks the job the whole ad is doing in the account; run that one first, then this one to brief the frame. It is also the practical route to the clear pole below — a lived moment is legible to a stranger without a caption explaining it — and it supplies the raw material for the visual-distinctness rule above, since different lived moments are genuinely different scenes rather than the same plate retyped. Write the moment into the messaging brief below as the trigger event, and let the image render it.

*Source: Lorenzo Pravata (@lorenzo_pravata) — "How to exploit GPT-2.5 Images for more winning ads" (social post)*

## Place every static concept deliberately at one end of the clarity spectrum — either uber-clear, where the image alone makes the point with no copy needed to explain it, or deliberately surreal and abstract purely to stop the scroll while staying relevant — and never let it drift into the muddled middle.

**Why it works:** The two poles win for different reasons. Maximum clarity converts because comprehension is instant, which frees the copy to do some other job instead of explaining the picture. Abstraction wins the scroll because the brain stops on something it cannot immediately parse. The middle gets neither: it is not instantly legible and not arresting, so it is scrolled past by exactly the people who would have bought. Deciding the pole up front is a decision made before any production time is spent, which is what keeps a concept from sliding toward the middle during execution.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion from agency practice across many accounts, with two example ads walked through on screen; the choice between the poles is described as 'informed ultimately by performance data' and historical patterns across accounts. No figures given.

**Fit here (8/10):** Durable creative-construction principle, not platform mechanics, so age is not the limiter — and it is a concrete selection rule for the exact asset class this operator already produces by hand. It sharpens the eight native formats above by inserting a prior question (which pole is this concept aiming at?) that costs nothing to answer and applies equally to Amazon listing frames and Shopify gallery slots, not just Meta statics. At $30/day the muddled-middle failure is the expensive one, because a flat ad judged as a single unit gives no clue whether the message or the execution was at fault. Practical reading: pick the pole in the messaging brief below, then pick the native format that can carry it — a review card and an us-vs-them frame sit naturally at the clear pole, a meme or an unexpected editorial image at the abstract pole. Pair with the no-brand-context audit immediately below, which is the test for whether the 'clear' pole is actually clear to a stranger.

## Audit every ad as if you had no brand context at all, because roughly 80% of the people it reaches have never heard of you — if it only makes sense to someone who already knows the product, it does not work.

**Why it works:** Whoever made the creative carries months of accumulated context and reads meaning into the frame that is not physically present in it. Cold traffic has none of that, so an asset that feels obvious internally reads as noise externally and fails at the comprehension step before any persuasion is even attempted. The check is to ask, of each frame, whether you understand it *because* of prior context — and if so, to put the missing meaning into the frame.

**Evidence offered:** The internal check as described ('do I know what this is saying, because I have all the previous context?'), plus the framing that creative strategy is for people who have no clue who you are. Assertion, no data.

**Fit here (8/10):** A solo operator who formulates, photographs, writes and buys media for the whole catalogue is the most context-poisoned reviewer possible, so this guardrail has more force here than in an agency where at least one colleague is fresh to the asset. It is a distinct check from the 1-second phone-size comprehension test in marketing-product-image-stack: that one measures how fast a frame can be read, this one measures whether the meaning exists in the frame at all for someone with zero brand knowledge. Runnable today, free, on every static, listing image and short-form thumbnail before it ships. It is also the enforcement mechanism for the clear pole above — 'uber-clear' means clear to a stranger, not clear to the person who built it — and it pairs with the product-aware launch order below, since even in-market buyers arriving cold have no idea who this brand is.

## Start every creative from the messaging pillars or personas you have decided to test — never from a template or an existing ad format — and write the brief with zero visual direction in it: angle, persona, trigger events, desires, consequences only, with the image and the headline derived from that brief afterwards.

**Why it works:** If the brief already specifies a visual, or the work starts inside somebody else's template, everything downstream is executing a picture or a container rather than executing an argument, and the output converges on whatever the category already runs. Starting from messaging means the same groundwork feeds the image, the headline set and every later variant, so the batch stays congruent and any winner is readable as 'this message worked' rather than 'this template worked' — which is what makes iteration on a winner possible at all.

**Evidence offered:** The agency's actual brief format described ('the brief doesn't contain anything visual related at all... here's our angle, here's the persona, here are different trigger events, here are desires, consequences'), plus the restated weekly action: 'start with figuring out what are the messaging pillars or personas you're looking to test... don't start from a template, try to build something from scratch.' No performance data.

**Fit here (8/10):** This is the missing upstream step between the evidence corpus already assembled from reviews and the AI image generation this operator runs. One person can write a five-line messaging brief before opening the image tool, and doing so is the difference between generating twelve pretty renders and generating twelve assets that all argue one buying motive. It sits directly above four rules already here: the one-job refinement below (job first, then format) and this (message first, then visual) are the same discipline applied at two levels; the one-job-for-the-image rule above turns the brief's trigger event into the frame; the angle-not-audience refinement below names the axis the pillars should vary along; and the concept-selection log below is where the derived concepts get cut and recorded. It also protects against the native-format list above being read as a starting point — the eight formats are containers chosen after the argument exists, not prompts to fill. Usable identically for Meta statics, Amazon image sets and organic short-form scripts.

## Build a 3-2-2 Flexible Ad — three creatives of the same format and aspect ratio, two meaningfully different primary texts, two meaningfully different headlines — so twelve combinations share a single learning pool and Meta allocates spend among them itself.

**Why it works:** Splitting the same creative ideas across many separate ads splits the data, so each ad accumulates signal slowly and every impression informs only its own tiny pool. Consolidating them into one flexible ad means every impression feeds one shared bucket, so the system reaches confident allocation faster. Keeping all three creatives the same format and aspect ratio means the system is answering one comparison question rather than two at once, and the texts and headlines must be genuinely different angles rather than reworded versions — otherwise there is nothing to learn between them.

**Evidence offered:** Live Ads Manager walkthrough of the setup; the combinatorial arithmetic (3 x 2 x 2 = 12) is definitional. The claim that consolidated learning outperforms split learning is assertion only, with no test against separate ads.

**Fit here (8/10):** The single most useful item here for a $30/day hand-run campaign, and the opposite of the disqualifying '20-creative test cell' pattern — it concentrates a thin budget into one learning pool rather than starving several. The inputs are within reach for a solo operator with no designer: three static plates from the existing `agents/ad-studio` pipeline (see the static-before-video rule above for why static is the default, the messaging-brief rule above for what each plate must argue, and the native-format list above for what the plates should look like), two entry angles and two headlines drawn from the awareness-level and copy-angle work already done. Note the constraint from the visual-distinctness rule above: same aspect ratio, yes, but the three plates must be visually distinct scenes or the ad is really one creative in triplicate. It also fits the arithmetic — one flexible ad in one ad set keeps all ~84 modelled weekly entries in a single bucket, which is what makes learning-phase exit reachable at all. Platform-mechanics class published seven days before the operating date, so current; verify the 'flexible ad' label in the live interface, as it is exactly the name Meta renames.

*Source: Professor Charley T — "The Simple Facebook Ads Strategy Dominating in 2026" (4DutxlMzqgc)*

**Refinement — judge a flexible ad as a single unit.** You will never see per-permutation reporting, and the only question is whether the ad as a whole is good enough to scale. Each creative runs against two headlines and two texts, so no permutation has an isolatable result; the absence of a breakdown is deliberate, because the shared pool is what makes allocation work in the first place. Hunting for the winning permutation reintroduces exactly the micromanagement the structure was built to remove.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion, framed as 'a feature, not a limitation'. No data.

**Fit here (7/10):** Measurement discipline, durable in substance even though it describes a specific reporting surface. It matters more here than at agency scale: at ~54 orders/month and a giveaway modelled at ~$2.50 per entry, twelve permutations would never accumulate enough events to separate anyway, so the honest unit of decision is the whole ad. It also protects against the hand-forcing instinct warned about under the breakdown effect above. Held at 7 because it prevents a wasted analysis rather than producing revenue.

*Source: Professor Charley T — "The Simple Facebook Ads Strategy Dominating in 2026" (4DutxlMzqgc)*

**Refinement — if one combination visibly dominates, harvest it by post ID rather than rebuilding it.** When spend consolidates on a single combination and it collects disproportionate comments and reactions, find the post in the page inbox, click the timestamp, copy the post ID out of the URL and use it in a separate ad. Reusing the post ID carries the accumulated engagement and social proof with the ad instead of resetting it to zero. Leave the original flexible ad running — turning off the source is a separate decision from harvesting from it, and the flexible ad is performing, which is why it produced the winner.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion with a described procedure, plus the creator's own caution that a harvested post ID will not perform identically once separated from the shared pool. No data.

**Fit here (5/10):** Platform-mechanics class, current, and the post-ID reuse mechanic is cheap and real — accumulated likes and comments are free social proof a solo operator otherwise throws away every time an ad is rebuilt. A giveaway also generates engagement more readily than a purchase campaign, so the 'you will see it in your comments' signal is plausible here. Held at 5 because the surrounding architecture it belongs to — a standing control ad set of harvested winners — cannot run on $30/day without splitting the budget below the learning threshold. Only the post-ID reuse half transfers: if a creative visibly wins, reuse its post ID; do not build a second ad set to house it.

## Open a new account with product-aware angles — us-vs-them, why ours is different, 'if you've tried X, try this instead', 'already looking for a solution? here's ours' — to harvest in-market buyers first, and only widen to cold audiences, obscure pain points and curiosity hooks once those conversions have given the pixel real purchase data.

**Why it works:** Product-aware angles select for people who already know they have the problem and are actively comparing solutions, so they convert on the fewest touches. Those early conversions hand Meta's optimizer concrete purchase events to model the buyer from, instead of forcing it to infer the ICP from scratch while the advertiser is simultaneously varying angle, format and copy. With a seeded signal, later prospecting into colder audiences delivers against a known profile rather than a guess.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion only — no account data, spend figures or before/after numbers. Reasoning from how Meta's conversion optimization is understood to work.

**Fit here (8/10):** Platform-mechanics class published 2026-08-29, two weeks old, so no staleness discount, and it lands on the live surface: a Meta account is being stood up at $30/day with paid open as of 2026-08-17. At $30/day and ~54 orders/month, conversion events are the scarcest resource in the account, so spending the first weeks buying the cheapest, most-in-market conversions rather than cold curiosity tests is the right allocation. The named angles are also the cheapest a solo operator can produce as statics — 'if you've tried natural deodorant that quit on you, try this' and a competitor comparison card need no video, no creator and no designer, and us-vs-them is already one of the eight native formats above. This is not a duplicate of the format-rotation or creative-jobs rules: neither of those states a launch *order* by awareness level, or ties that order to seeding the conversion signal. Practical reading: the first flexible ad's three creatives should skew product-aware, with the reserved exploration slot in the double 80/20 below carrying the colder curiosity angle. Product-aware does not mean brand-aware — apply the no-brand-context audit above to every one of these frames. Scored 8 rather than 10 because the giveaway campaign currently being built optimizes for entries, not purchases, so the pixel-seeding half applies to the product campaigns that follow it.

*Source: Khalifa (@EcomKhalifa) — "Run product-aware ads first to feed the pixel (X post)" (social post)*

## Cover distinct creative jobs rather than creative volume — a small set of concepts where several open the conversation with cold audiences in genuinely different ways and one or two close it for people already touched, so no two ads chase the same person.

**Why it works:** Creative diversity is a delivery-sequencing problem, not a volume problem. The system needs to know which message suits which person at which point, and it can only do that if each ad has a distinguishable job. Three different cold openings catch three different people who each ignored the others; the closing message picks up the conversation those openings started. Many near-identical ads instead compete to be the last touch on the same sale.

**Evidence offered:** Framework assertion with a football-team analogy and a paraphrase of Meta CMO Alex Schultz on personalization being about the whole feed experience rather than per-person creative. No campaign data, no comparison against a flat structure.

**Fit here (5/10):** Durable-principle class in its core claim (distinct jobs beat volume), and that half is right for a solo operator who cannot produce many assets anyway. The full five-ring, two-funnel version does not survive the standing constraints: $30/day cannot fund five concepts across prospecting and closing stages, and there is no designer or media buyer to produce and maintain them. The honest scaled-down version is **two or three distinct angles for the giveaway — a founder-voice ad, a taboo/problem angle, a proof or testimonial angle — used as the three creatives inside one flexible ad**, each built in a different native static format from the list above, with the 'close' job handled by the post-entry email sequence rather than a second ad tier, since retention flows are the owned surface and cost nothing to run. Sequence those jobs with the product-aware rule above: the in-market angles run first, and the colder openers come in once there is conversion signal to deliver them against. Scored 5 with that scale-down stated rather than adopted at full size.

*Source: Professor Charley T — "The Simple Facebook Ads Strategy Dominating in 2026" (4DutxlMzqgc)*

**Refinement — give each ad exactly one job (earn attention, clarify value, or convert trust), pick that job from a scale-vs-efficiency diagnosis of your own account, and fix the job before you choose the format.** An ad asked to do several jobs at once does none of them. The job is not chosen from taste or from funnel vocabulary but from what is actually wrong in the account right now: if the problem is *scale* — not enough people entering at all — the job is earn attention; if the problem is *efficiency* — traffic arriving and not converting — the job is clarify value or convert trust. 'Midfunnel' is not an acceptable answer, because it names a stage rather than a job. Only once the job is locked does format get decided, which is what stops a standing production default ('we always make statics', 'we always make video') from silently deciding strategy.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion, demonstrated in a build session: the builder recommended 'earn attention' from the evidence in front of it, refused a midfunnel answer, and only then asked what content could actually be produced. No performance data.

**Fit here (6/10):** Runnable today inside the single campaign — it is a decision rule, not a spend requirement, and it costs nothing to apply before each rebuild of the flexible ad. It overlaps the distinct-creative-jobs claim above, but adds two things that claim does not carry: the job is *derived* from a scale-vs-efficiency read of the account rather than assumed, and it is locked *before* format selection. Both bite here. At $30/day with a giveaway that has almost no top-of-funnel volume, the honest diagnosis today is scale, so the job for all three creatives is earn attention — which is also why the native-format list above matters more than polish. And the static-first rule above is exactly the kind of default this refinement asks you to justify each time rather than inherit: static still wins on attributable failure points, but it should win as a conclusion, not as a habit. Run it in sequence with the messaging-brief rule above — job, then message, then visual — so nothing downstream gets decided by a template. Scored 6 for the overlap, not for timing.

*Source: Professor Charley T — "Claude Has Officially Changed Facebook Ads Forever! (Tutorial)" (4-ApfzxGhYI)*

**Refinement — the axis of difference is angle, not audience.** Differentiate the creatives on pain point inside one market — one ad about the physical symptom, one about appearance, one about confidence, one about the frustration of a body that changed — rather than producing a separate ad per demographic avatar. Different pain points catch different people at different moments without needing a different audience definition behind each ad, so four angles against one audience produce real creative diversity where four avatar-split ads produce four near-identical messages against four thin segments. Splitting by demographic multiplies production work while the underlying message barely moves, and all the angles still hit the same shared centre of the market.

**Evidence offered:** Illustrative list of four gut-supplement angles (menopause, appearance, confidence, body-change frustration) asserted to all speak to the market's centre. No performance figures.

**Fit here (6/10):** Durable-principle class (creative differentiation axis), so age is not the limiter, and it is directly actionable on the campaign going live: the 3-2-2 flexible ad needs three creatives and two meaningfully different primary texts, and this specifies what 'meaningfully different' should mean — different pain angle against the same buyer (transition-period fear, baking-soda irritation, the mid-afternoon shirt check, aluminium avoidance), not different demographic. That is exactly the shape a solo operator with no designer can produce, because the angles are already enumerated in marketing-problem-solution-inventory and the review corpus. These pain angles are the messaging pillars the brief rule above asks you to name before any visual exists. Note that angle and native format vary independently: the same baking-soda-irritation pain can be delivered as a text thread, a review card or an us-vs-them frame — and awareness level is a third independent axis, with the product-aware versions of those angles running first. Held at 6 because it substantially reinforces rules already recorded here — distinct creative jobs above, and the exploit/explore slot allocation below — so the additive claim is narrow (the axis of difference is angle, not audience), and at ~54 orders/month with one flexible ad judged as a single unit the choice will be made by judgement rather than measured.

## Allocate creative on a double 80/20 — roughly 80% of ads in proven formats and 20% experimental, and inside the proven 80%, roughly 80% on market-validated angles and 20% on new or retested ones.

**Why it works:** Keeping the large majority of spend on formats and angles that already work protects performance, while a fixed minority slot guarantees exploration happens continuously rather than only when performance collapses. The account keeps discovering new winners without ever betting the budget on an unproven idea, and the explore budget is decided in advance rather than in a panic after a bad week.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion, explicitly offered as a rule of thumb rather than dogma ('what if I want 75%? yeah bro, that's fine'). No account data, no comparison against other allocations.

**Fit here (5/10):** Durable-principle class (portfolio allocation between exploit and explore), so age is not the limiter — the limiter is the size of the portfolio. At $30/day the whole account is one campaign, one ad set and a 3-2-2 flexible ad, so '20% of ads' is 0.6 of a creative and the percentages are meaningless as stated. The honest scaled-down version: **of the three creative slots in the flexible ad, hold two on whatever has proven itself and reserve one standing slot for a new or retested angle**, refreshed each time the ad is rebuilt. Second real caveat — at launch nothing is proven, so all three slots are exploratory, and the launch-order rule above is what breaks the tie: start them product-aware, then let the reserved slot carry the colder curiosity angle once conversions exist. It is also the correct counterweight to the iterate-on-winners rule below: that rule says multiply executions of a proven angle rather than gambling on fresh concepts, and this one keeps exactly one slot open so the gamble never stops entirely. Note that the reserved slot is a new *angle* against the same buyer, not a new demographic — see the audience-expansion rule below — that it must be a visually distinct scene and not a retyped version of a proven plate, and that an untried native static format is a cheap way to fill it.

*Source: Stefan Georgi — "Secret of the DTC Universe #11: The Double 80/20 Rule in Creative Strategy" (social post)*

## Keep a standing bank of proven statics running permanently as the account's low-volatility base, so when a video or a hot new winner decays the statics absorb the spend and account performance holds.

**Why it works:** Video creative is volatile — an individual winner rips hard and then dies unpredictably within weeks, leaving the account exposed on the day it goes. Proven statics degrade far more slowly and perform moderately well, predictably. A set of them left running acts as a performance floor: when the top performer collapses, delivery shifts onto the statics and results sag rather than falling off a cliff. The bank only works if it is left alone — swapping proven statics out every time a new idea arrives removes the ballast it exists to provide.

**Evidence offered:** Operator experience across multiple accounts and categories — 'we have seven statics that have been running for a year that are gonna take up more spend today and performance will be fine', 'statics were always there for us in our time of need'. No figures, no controlled comparison.

**Fit here (7/10):** The paid account is one CBO campaign collapsed into a single ad set of four to eight ads, and this tells the operator how to think about those slots over time: once a static proves itself, it stays, and new ideas rotate through the reserved slot rather than displacing the base. It is a different claim from the double 80/20 above, which governs the proportion of proven to experimental at any one rebuild — this one governs *tenure*, the instruction to let a proven static run for months instead of churning it. It also gives a constructive reason behind the doom-cycle rule below: the reason not to keep launching alongside a running ad is that the running proven ads are the floor. The one constraint is honest — at $30/day a 'bank' means two or three plates, not seven, and nothing in this account has a year of history yet, so the rule mostly reads today as *do not retire a static that is working just because it is old*. Note the bank should be built from visually distinct entities (see above), since two plates sharing a fingerprint fatigue together and provide one floor, not two. The operator produces the statics himself, so building the bank costs time, not money.

## Generate several ad concepts, cut most of them, and write down the reason each was cut plus the runner-up you are holding, so the selection is arguable rather than assumed.

**Why it works:** A visible kill log makes the selection contestable — you can look at why a concept was dropped and disagree with the reasoning instead of inheriting an unexplained shortlist. It also means the next concept to run is already chosen: when the launched creative fails, the held runner-up steps in without redoing the research. Each surviving concept should carry its own working parts with it — the evidence it rests on, the format, the core message, the objection it attacks, and a testable hypothesis — so a concept that later wins or dies can be traced back to which of those parts was doing the work.

**Evidence offered:** Demonstrated in a build session: five concepts generated, three removed with stated reasons, the top pick and a held second surfaced along with their carried components. No performance data.

**Fit here (6/10):** Runnable now by one person with an LLM and no spend — it is a documentation habit around concepting, not a budget commitment, and it is the cheapest possible insurance at $30/day where each launched creative takes weeks to read. The held runner-up is exactly what fills the reserved exploration slot in the double 80/20 rule above on the next rebuild, and the 'objection it attacks' field lines up with the pain-angle axis in the refinement above, so the artifact drops straight into the existing workflow. The concepts being cut should all have come out of one messaging brief (see above), so the log records which *argument* was dropped, not which picture. It overlaps the decision-log habit recorded elsewhere in spirit, which is why it is 6 rather than higher, but the additive part is real: pre-selection kill reasons are a different artifact from a post-hoc change log — one records why something was never tried, the other records what changed after it was. Pair with the one-job refinement above: the job is the first thing each concept is judged against, and 'does two jobs at once' is a legitimate kill reason to write down — as are 'lands in the muddled middle', 'only makes sense if you already know us', 'no authority leg' and 'visually the same ad as one we already run'.

## Stop continuously launching new ads alongside a campaign that is already running — every new ad is handed your warmest bottom-of-funnel pool to learn from, which makes your existing upper-funnel ads look like they died.

**Why it works:** Every new ad starts at zero estimated action rate, so Meta seeks the fastest path to useful data by serving it to the highest-intent, most-engaged pool available. That pool is pulled away from the ads already running, whose reported performance immediately worsens. The operator reads the dip as fatigue, switches off the upper-funnel ads that were actually building the audience, and concentrates ever more spend on the least incremental bottom-of-funnel placements — the self-reinforcing decline the creator calls the doom cycle.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion plus a claimed billion-plus dollars of managed spend across his agency, his own brands and students. No account data, no before/after, no controlled comparison.

**Fit here (7/10):** Platform-mechanics class but published 2026-08-10, so no decay discount — and it lands directly on the campaign being stood up this week at $30/day. This is the specific failure mode a solo operator checking a brand-new giveaway campaign daily will walk into: performance dips on day three, the instinct is to launch something new, and the launch itself is what makes the reported numbers worse. Note this is not the same as leaving what is running alone — it is the additional instruction not to add anything *alongside* it, with the delivery mechanism for why adding hurts. It also constrains how the reserved exploration slot above is used, and how the widening to colder angles happens: the new angle, the held runner-up concept or the new native format goes into the *next rebuild* of the flexible ad, never into a fresh ad launched beside the running one. The standing-static-bank rule above is the positive form of the same discipline — the proven statics are the floor, and launching around them is what erodes it. At $30/day the cost is concrete: every extra ad competing for the same tiny budget slows the ~84 entries/week that let the learning phase exit at all.

## Do not open a genuinely different demographic or market until you have reached real scale on the current one and further growth on your existing traffic channel has clearly become hard.

**Why it works:** A new demographic is a new research project, a new set of creative and a split of the same budget — all justified only when the existing audience is close to exhausted. Before that point, the incremental buyer inside the current market is cheaper to reach than the first buyer in a new one, because the creative, the proof and the audience signal already exist.

**Evidence offered:** Hypothetical: a gut-supplement brand doing nine figures on Meta to 50+ women deciding to go after 50+ men, where the creator concedes proper research would be essential. No data on where the threshold actually sits.

**Fit here (6/10):** Durable-principle class (audience expansion order), age irrelevant, and it has an honest scaled-down form that is runnable today. Scale-down: at $30/day driving giveaway entries there is one campaign, one ad set and one flexible ad, so a second demographic cell would split the ~84 modelled weekly entries below the level where the learning phase can exit at all — so the rule today reads as **do not open a men's angle, a teen angle or a new age bracket; every creative slot stays pointed at the core buyer until cost per entry stops improving with the existing audience**. That pairs with the refinement above: diversity comes from a new pain angle, a new awareness level, a new lived-moment scene or a new native format against the same buyer, never from a new avatar. It also lines up with the standing read that retention, not new audiences, is the binding constraint. Held at 6 because it is a decision not to act rather than a revenue mechanism, it overlaps the doom-cycle rule above and the restraint rules in marketing-paid-media-measurement, and the source's own threshold (nine figures) is so far from this business that only the direction transfers. The genuine expansion version — researching and building for a new demographic — is what waits for scale.

## When you find a breakthrough ad, dissect it into components to understand exactly why it works, then produce many deliberate iterations of it instead of testing new concepts.

**Why it works:** An angle that works has a finite audience per execution; once you know which component is doing the work, you can multiply reach by re-delivering the same working logic in new wrappers rather than gambling on fresh concepts. The age, gender and placement breakdowns on the winner are part of that dissection: they turn an unexplained winner into a stated audience or format hypothesis for the next asset.

**Evidence offered:** Walkthrough of four Pet Lab Co. ads built on the same 'dog eats grass' angle, plus the creator's own account going from $1,700/day to $65,000/day after taking iteration 'way more seriously'. No isolated test of iteration vs. new-concept testing.

**Fit here (8/10):** Durable-principle class — decompose a proven winner into its working component and multiply executions of that component rather than gambling on new concepts. The gate is open, and the input it needs is closer than it looks: a giveaway generates entry events cheaply enough that a winning creative becomes identifiable in days rather than months, which is the fastest this business has ever been able to find one. The native format list above is the ready-made set of new wrappers — the same working pain angle re-delivered as a text thread, a review card, a meme, an editorial card — and awareness level is a second axis to iterate along, taking a product-aware winner colder once the pixel has signal. Critically, each iteration must clear the visual-distinctness bar above: a re-typed copy of the winning plate is the same delivery entity and buys no new reach, so the iteration has to change scene, subject, lighting or format. The messaging-brief and concept-selection rules above make the dissection easier, because each surviving concept already records its argument, format, core message and the objection it attacked, so a winner arrives with its components named and is readable as 'this message worked' rather than 'this template worked'. Iteration throughput is the real remaining limit — one operator, no designer — though `agents/ad-studio` exists precisely to produce plate variations, so the constraint is Canva/Photoshop time rather than concepting, and the static-first rule above is what keeps each iteration cheap enough to be worth making. Read it alongside the double 80/20 allocation above: iterations of the proven angle fill the majority of the creative slots, but one slot stays reserved for an untested angle so the account never stops exploring. Held at 6 because "statistically identifiable" is still generous at this budget; treat the winner as a strong hint and iterate on it anyway, since the alternative is iterating on nothing.

## Test 3-6 hook variations per creative test and take big swings rather than isolating a single word — ideally reformatting existing top performers across every hook pattern in one large test.

**Stage:** scale — parked until the scale phase opens.

**Why it works:** Larger variation between cells produces bigger performance separation than single-variable changes, so winners surface faster than clean A/B isolation would allow. A one-word change on a low-traffic account spends the entire budget proving nothing, because the effect size is smaller than the noise; swinging between genuinely different hook patterns makes the gap large enough to read.

**Evidence offered:** Personal testing practice; says she has done the reformat-all-top-performers test repeatedly with great results. No numbers.

**Fit here (6/10):** The underlying principle is durable and correct: big variation between cells separates faster than single-word isolation. Reformatting a proven top performer across several distinct hook patterns is the cheapest way to extend a winner, and it is the natural next move after the breakthrough-ad dissection rule above once there is volume to support it. It is a multi-cell test, though — 3-6 hook cells cannot resolve at $30/day, where a single 3-2-2 flexible ad judged as one unit is the right structure. Trigger: enough purchase volume per week that separate hook cells accumulate readable results inside a fortnight, roughly the point where one ad set clears double-digit weekly purchases. This parks alongside the live rule against hunting per-permutation winners inside a flexible ad — it is what replaces that discipline when volume arrives, not a contradiction of it at current spend.
