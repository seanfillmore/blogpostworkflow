# Ai Research Workflows

## Before asking an LLM to research anything, state the asset you are building — its exact title, who it is for, and its intended structure (how many items, what price range, what order) — so the output comes back as usable specifics rather than a generic overview.

**Why it works:** The model's answer is only as constrained as the brief. Naming the deliverable, the audience level and the structural spine converts an open question into a fill-in-the-blanks task, so what comes back maps one-to-one onto sections of the thing you are about to write.

**Evidence offered:** Demonstration only — he pastes the full framing ('a buyer's guide for beginners outlining five cameras from under $500 up to $3-4,000') and gets a usable five-item list on the first pass.

**Fit here (6/10):** Durable prompting principle, not platform mechanics, so age is irrelevant. The solo operator writes every script, PDP block and Amazon bullet himself, and he routinely needs researched specifics (aluminium-free deodorant alternatives, oral-care ingredient comparisons, what a natural-soap buyer is choosing between). Framing the brief with the surface, the buyer and the structure is free and runnable today. Not higher because the surrounding skill set already holds several stronger grounding tactics, so this is an incremental discipline rather than a new lever.

## When comparing your product against a competing option, have the LLM write a paragraph arguing for one side and then a paragraph arguing the opposite side, specifically to flush out your own preferences before you decide what the comparison copy claims.

**Why it works:** You already have a favourite, and a one-sided research prompt returns support for it. Forcing the model to build the opposing case puts you in the shoes of a buyer who prefers the other option, which surfaces the attributes you are weakest on and the objections the copy will actually have to survive.

**Evidence offered:** Demonstration only — he runs it on GoPro vs DJI Osmo and says it 'weeds out your own personal biases'.

**Fit here (7/10):** Durable principle. RSC's strongest documented angle is us-vs-them against conventional antiperspirant, commercial soap and mainstream toothpaste, and the existing skills already require a comparison frame capped at three or four attributes you genuinely win on — this is the method for finding which attributes those honestly are, and for pre-loading the objection the losing side raises. One person, one free chat, runnable today.

## Ask the model to reformat its comparison answer as a table and then build the table up one column at a time ('add a column for price'), and treat the finished table as a shippable asset — a graphic inside the content or a downloadable handout attached to it.

**Why it works:** A table forces every option onto the same attribute axes, which exposes the gaps in your research; and because the rows and columns are already normalised, the same artefact can be exported as on-page comparison imagery rather than being thrown away after the script is written.

**Evidence offered:** Demonstration only — he reformats twice and adds a price column, then suggests exporting it as a video graphic or a downloadable link.

**Fit here (5/10):** Durable workflow, no platform dependency. RSC already needs a us-vs-them comparison frame in the Shopify gallery and the Amazon image set, so a normalised table is a genuine input to an asset that must exist. Mid score because the comparison graphic and its three-to-four-attribute cap are already specified elsewhere — the addition is the production route from chat to artefact, plus the honest constraint that anything claimed in the table has to be verifiable on-pack.
