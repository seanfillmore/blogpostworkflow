# Email List Health

## Capture contact information from every burst of attention, even when you have nothing to sell yet.

**Why it works:** Attention is perishable and offers are not. A list of people who raised their hand during a spike is the asset that makes a later offer sellable to a warm audience instead of a cold one. The offer can be built afterward, but the attention cannot be recovered afterward.

**Evidence offered:** Single anecdote: after a conference talk with nothing to sell, over one hundred people left phone numbers and business cards. That list later became the seed audience for the licensing offer. No comparison against a control.

**Fit here (6/10):** Directly applicable with a sub-1,000 list where the binding constraint is retention and repeat revenue. Every giveaway entrant, market-stall visitor and Amazon insert scan should land on the list, not just be a transaction. Runnable today by one person, free, and a durable principle. Not higher because the soap giveaway already implements the core of it. The incremental value is extending it to the non-campaign attention spikes (markets, viral posts, press mentions).

*Source: Alex Hormozi — "$100M Leads" (book, (part 1 of 16))*

## In the opt-in confirmation, sell the subscriber on staying subscribed before you hand over the incentive — tease what's coming, then deliver the promised reward near the end.

**Why it works:** Someone who opted in for the lead magnet alone will take it and disengage. The moment *before* you hand over the promised reward is the window of maximum motivation, so that is when to make the case for ongoing email. Leading with the reward spends the attention you needed to make that case. The failure it prevents is the immediate post-delivery unsubscribe.

**Evidence offered:** A line-by-line walkthrough of his own live opt-in email showing anticipation, then the resell, then the reward, plus the stated failure modes. Assertion only, no open, retention, or revenue figures.

**Fit here (7/10):** Targets the binding constraint directly. Repeat customers are 45–52% of revenue and the list is the only owned channel for driving a second order. Free, one email, solo-executable, and genuinely additive: the awareness-level and offer-construction skills cover what to say and which incentive to choose, but nothing owned the resell-before-reward *sequencing* inside the confirmation email. Applies to the live welcome flow, where FIRST20 is the incentive being handed over.

*Source: Alex Hormozi — "Learn Email Marketing in 39 Minutes!" (pLhQOYMGa88)*

## Probe a quiet list with Dean Jackson's nine-word email — "Are you still looking to [four-word desire]?" — plain text, no images, no links, nothing else, and treat every reply as a top-priority lead.

**Why it works:** A single short question with no sales apparatus reads as a personal note rather than a broadcast, so it gets replies where a designed campaign gets ignored. The replies then segment the list into engaged leads worth one-to-one follow-up. That is a different job from making an offer: this send finds who is still there, it does not try to close them.

**Evidence offered:** Attributed to Dean Jackson as a "timeless" template. The author says it is among the first things he does when investing in a new business. No response-rate figures.

**Fit here (8/10):** Durable principle and the strongest fit available. Sub-1,000 list, retention is the binding constraint, and repeat customers already carry 45–52% of revenue. A zero-cost plain-text probe to lapsed buyers is exactly the kind of send a solo operator can run this week in Klaviyo. Complements the existing win-back credit rather than duplicating it: this segments, that one offers. It is also a natural first send after a long silence, which pairs with the unsubscribe-spike expectation below.

*Source: Alex Hormozi — "$100M Leads" (book, (part 4 of 16))*

## Expect an abnormally high unsubscribe rate on the first send after a long silence — it is pulled-forward churn, not a signal to stop emailing.

**Why it works:** Subscribers who would have drifted off across several months all churn at once when contact resumes, so the spike is a one-time cleanup rather than a recurring cost. Reading it as failure and pausing the program just keeps a dead list dead. Continuing to hold dormant, disengaged subscribers is itself the thing that damages domain reputation, which decides whether the reorder and transition-period emails reach the primary inbox at all.

**Evidence offered:** Single-case anecdote: his own first send after a dormant stretch produced his highest-ever unsubscribe rate (just over 1%), settling to ~0.42% per email thereafter. No controlled comparison.

**Fit here (6/10):** Useful precisely because this list has been under-mailed, so the first real send will produce a scary number. The value of the tactic is that the number does not stop the flow build. A small list is more fragile than a large one here: a handful of spam complaints on a sub-1,000-subscriber list can measurably hurt inbox placement. Capped at 6 because it is an interpretive rule for a metric rather than a mechanism that produces revenue. Shopify and Klaviyo already ship a compliant unsubscribe link, so the additive work is not burying it and pruning persistent non-openers.

*Source: Alex Hormozi — "Learn Email Marketing in 39 Minutes!" (pLhQOYMGa88)*

## Run a sunset flow: after about 120 days of no engagement, send a short re-engagement series with a one-click unsubscribe, then remove anyone who still does not engage.

**Stage:** offer-aov — parked until the offer-aov phase opens.

**Why it works:** Dead subscribers inflate the ESP's subscriber-based bill, so pruning them keeps the paid list to engaged people. The last-chance series rescues some of them before removal. Deliverability is the stronger reason, though the source does not argue it. Removing chronically disengaged contacts is the systematic version of the pruning described in the unsubscribe-spike section above.

**Evidence offered:** Assertion only. The only rationale given is the cost of the email tool.

**Fit here (5/10):** Durable list hygiene. It is directly relevant to the 1,447 giveaway leads that produced no buyers. Two adaptations matter:

- **Key inactivity on clicks, site visits and orders, not opens.** Apple MPP inflates opens.
- **Never sunset past purchasers on email inactivity alone.** Route them to win-back instead. The nine-word probe above is a natural first touch for them.

The flow itself is runnable as soon as the stage opens.

*Source: Ecommerce Alley — "How We’d Scale to $100K/Month If We Started Over Today" (h2O0i1eHbkk)*
