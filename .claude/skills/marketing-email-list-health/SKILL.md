# Email List Health

## In the opt-in confirmation, sell the subscriber on staying subscribed before you hand over the incentive — tease what's coming, then deliver the promised reward near the end.

**Why it works:** Someone who opted in for the lead magnet alone will take it and disengage. The moment *before* you hand over the promised reward is the window of maximum motivation, so that is when to make the case for ongoing email; leading with the reward spends the attention you needed to make that case. The failure it prevents is the immediate post-delivery unsubscribe.

**Evidence offered:** A line-by-line walkthrough of his own live opt-in email showing anticipation, then the resell, then the reward, plus the stated failure modes. Assertion only, no open, retention, or revenue figures.

**Fit here (7/10):** Targets the binding constraint directly — repeat customers are 45–52% of revenue and the list is the only owned channel for driving a second order. Free, one email, solo-executable, and genuinely additive: the awareness-level and offer-construction skills cover what to say and which incentive to choose, but nothing owned the resell-before-reward *sequencing* inside the confirmation email. Applies to the live welcome flow, where FIRST20 is the incentive being handed over.

## Expect an abnormally high unsubscribe rate on the first send after a long silence — it is pulled-forward churn, not a signal to stop emailing.

**Why it works:** Subscribers who would have drifted off across several months all churn at once when contact resumes, so the spike is a one-time cleanup rather than a recurring cost. Reading it as failure and pausing the program just keeps a dead list dead — and continuing to hold dormant, disengaged subscribers is itself the thing that damages domain reputation, which decides whether the reorder and transition-period emails reach the primary inbox at all.

**Evidence offered:** Single-case anecdote: his own first send after a dormant stretch produced his highest-ever unsubscribe rate (just over 1%), settling to ~0.42% per email thereafter. No controlled comparison.

**Fit here (6/10):** Useful precisely because this list has been under-mailed, so the first real send will produce a scary number — and the value of the tactic is that the number does not stop the flow build. A small list is more fragile than a large one here: a handful of spam complaints on a sub-1,000-subscriber list can measurably hurt inbox placement. Capped at 6 because it is an interpretive rule for a metric rather than a mechanism that produces revenue, and Shopify and Klaviyo already ship a compliant unsubscribe link — the additive work is not burying it and pruning persistent non-openers.
