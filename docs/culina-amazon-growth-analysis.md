# Culina Amazon: Audit and Growth Analysis

**Prepared 29 September 2026. Measurement window: 30 days to 28 September 2026.**

All account figures are first-party, pulled directly from the Amazon Selling Partner API and the Amazon Advertising API, joined against supplier cost of goods. Nothing in the account was changed to produce this analysis.

---

## Executive summary

1. **The advertising program is unprofitable and the loss is structural, not tactical.** Contribution margin after Amazon's fees and cost of goods is 25.9% of gross. Advertising costs 30.4% of gross. The ads consume more than the products earn.

2. **Seven of eight advertised products are being advertised above their break-even ACoS.** The single product that clears break-even receives 1.5% of the budget.

3. **Advertising destroys about $3,600 per month of contribution.** The account's reported net loss is smaller than that only because organic sales are subsidising the advertising.

4. **About $3,800 of monthly spend, 43.3% of the total, sits on keywords that compete against each other** across two to eight campaigns each. That figure was 43.8% three weeks earlier, so it is not improving.

5. **Growth to an additional $50,000 per month is not available in cast iron care.** Culina already holds roughly 15% of that segment. The realistic path runs through adjacent categories and takes two to three years.

**The order of operations matters more than any single item. Fixing the margin comes before funding growth, because every incremental unit sold at today's price and today's ad cost loses money.**

## 1. Where every dollar goes

| | Share of gross |
|---|--:|
| FBA fulfilment | 24.8% |
| Amazon referral commission | 13.6% |
| **Amazon structural take** | **38.7%** |
| Cost of goods | 25.0% |
| Promotions and coupons | 9.0% |
| Refunds | 4.0% |
| **Contribution before advertising** | **25.9%** |
| Advertising (TACoS) | **30.4%** |
| **Net** | **−$1,310 / month** |

Gross revenue was $29,150 across 1,508 units, an average selling price of $19.33.

Two observations about this table:

**The 9.0% spent on promotions is a decision, not a cost of doing business.** It is concentrated on the three multi-piece kits, where it runs 24% to 26% of the selling price, against 3% to 5% everywhere else. Those are the products with the best underlying economics, and the coupons are removing most of that advantage. Section 2 quantifies it per product.

**FBA fulfilment does not scale with price, so it falls hardest on the cheapest products.** It costs $3.47 per unit on a $9.99 product and $7.57 on a $54.99 product: the price rises five and a half times while the fee roughly doubles. As a share of the selling price that is **35% against 14%**. This is the structural reason low-priced items in this catalogue struggle to carry an advertising budget, and it is why the single-item products show the worst gap between break-even and actual ACoS in section 2.

## 2. The advertising problem, product by product

Break-even ACoS is contribution margin expressed as a percentage of revenue. A product advertised above its break-even ACoS loses money on every advertised sale.

| Product | Pieces | Price | Break-even ACoS | Actual ACoS | Ad spend | Net |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Cleaning and Restoring Scrub | 1 | $14.99 | 33.9% | 69.6% | $2,288 | −$371 |
| Cleaning Kit (oil, soap) | 2 | $17.99 | 21.9% | 46.3% | $1,326 | −$606 |
| Cleaning Soap 8oz | 1 | $9.99 | 26.8% | 56.2% | $852 | −$277 |
| Conditioner Oil 8oz | 1 | $9.99 | 24.9% | 62.3% | $551 | −$313 |
| Six-piece set | 6 | $49.99 | 27.5% | 37.7% | $1,465 | −$212 |
| Four-piece set | 4 | $29.99 | 9.8% | 23.5% | $122 | −$22 |
| Supreme Care Set (oil, soap, scrub) | 3 | $24.99 | 23.5% | 32.3% | $2,134 | +$355 |
| Eight-piece set | 8 | $54.99 | 28.9% | **17.4%** | $133 | +$137 |

**Only the last product is advertised profitably, and it receives 1.5% of the budget.**

### The bundle ladder does not price coherently

Comparing each kit against the one below it, using supplier cost of goods:

| Step up | What it adds | Added cost | Added price |
|---|---|--:|--:|
| 3-piece to 4-piece | conditioning stick | +$2.05 | +$5.00 |
| 4-piece to 6-piece | brush, chain mail scrubber | **+$0.77** | **+$20.00** |
| 6-piece to 8-piece | plastic scrapers, silicone handle cover | +$0.50 | +$5.00 |

**A $20 price step is carried by 77 cents of goods.** The gap between those two kits is a pricing decision, not a cost difference.

### Coupons, not fees, are the largest controllable cost on the kits

Separating Amazon's fulfilment fee from the referral commission and from promotional spend changes where the problem sits. Fulfilment runs $3.47 to $7.57 per unit across the whole range, which is unremarkable. Coupon spend does not:

| Kit | List | Units / month | Coupon per unit | Contribution now | With coupon removed |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 3-piece | $24.99 | 453 | $1.25 | $6.26 (26%) | $7.93 (32%) |
| 4-piece | $29.99 | 37 | $6.57 | **$1.71 (6%)** | $10.34 (34%) |
| 6-piece | $49.99 | 97 | $12.83 | $13.06 (26%) | $25.89 (52%) |
| 8-piece | $54.99 | 18 | $13.75 | $14.80 (27%) | $28.55 (52%) |

**Removing the coupons on these four products is worth an estimated $2,570 per month of additional contribution at current unit volumes, with no change to any listed price.** That is roughly double the account's current monthly loss, and it costs nothing to do.

It also explains the four-piece set's 6% margin, which is not a cost problem: that product carries a coupon worth 24% of its price.

### Where the ladder actually fails

Unit volumes do not support the idea that the higher-priced kits are priced out of the market. The six-piece set at $49.99 sells 97 units per month, more than twice the four-piece at $29.99. The ladder works at the top and fails in the middle:

- **The four-piece set is the weak rung.** At $27.56 realised it sits about $3 above the three-piece set for one additional component, so there is little reason for a shopper to choose it. It should either be retired or moved to roughly $34 to become a genuine step.
- **The eight-piece set is the other weak rung**, only $5 above the six-piece for two more components.
- **The three-piece set is the volume anchor** at 453 units per month and the only consistently net-positive product. It is the last price to touch, not the first.

### What the advertising itself earns

Measuring the advertising in isolation, taking advertised sales multiplied by contribution margin, then subtracting advertising spend:

| | |
|---|--:|
| Advertising spend | $8,872 |
| Contribution generated by advertised sales | $5,260 |
| **Net effect of advertising** | **−$3,612 / month** |

The account's reported loss is $1,310 because organic sales generate contribution that partly covers the advertising loss. **The advertising is not slightly underperforming. It is consuming roughly $3,600 a month of margin earned elsewhere.**

## 3. Recoverable immediately, without new products or new spend

**Self-competing keywords: $3,838 of monthly spend, 43.3% of the total.** Ninety-eight targets run in two or more campaigns simultaneously. The keyword "cast iron cleaning kit" on exact match runs in eight separate campaigns. Amazon serves one advertisement per placement, so these campaigns bid against each other and raise their own cost per click. The equivalent figure measured three weeks earlier was 43.8%, so this is not resolving on its own.

**Cookware-intent search terms: 59 terms, $2,040 over 60 days.** Culina sells cast iron care. A shopper searching "cast iron skillets" wants a pan. Measured across 60 days:

| Search intent | Spend | Sales | ACoS |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| Care intent (clean, season, rust, oil, scrub, kit) | $10,419 | $27,694 | 37.6% |
| Cookware intent (skillet, pan, pot, cookware, lodge) | $3,104 | $4,789 | 64.8% |

Against a blended 26.9% break-even, the cookware cluster costs about **$634 per month** in lost contribution. A reviewed negation list of 59 terms is ready. Four terms that carry cookware wording but convert at or below break-even were deliberately excluded, because negating profitable traffic to satisfy a tidy rule would cost money.

**Coupons on the multi-piece kits: an estimated $2,570 per month.** Removing them lifts the six-piece and eight-piece sets from roughly 26% to 52% contribution, and the four-piece set from 6% to 34%, with no change to any listed price. This is the single largest controllable item in this report and the cheapest to act on.

**Together these are worth an estimated $3,000 to $5,000 per month of contribution, on a business currently losing $1,310 per month.**

## 4. Two structural blockers

**Brand Registry.** Until it is approved, the account cannot use Amazon Vine, A+ content, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display, or Search Query Performance data. The effect on reviews is measurable: Culina's best-selling product carries 435 ratings while selling roughly 440 units per month. The nearest competitor carries 6,763 ratings at 1.7 times the volume. Review volume is the primary conversion asset in this category and the account has been unable to build it. *(Application submitted 29 September 2026.)*

**Product categorisation.** The 8oz conditioning oil is listed in Dish Scouring Pads, where it competes against steel wool. This is the weakest product in the catalogue and the categorisation is a plausible cause.

## 5. Market position

Category sizing below is derived from Amazon's "bought in past month" badges, which are published as tiers rather than exact counts. Every figure is therefore a floor. The method was calibrated against Culina's own known revenue and captured approximately 90% of it, so category totals are roughly correct and slightly understated.

| Segment | Approximate size / month | Culina | Share |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| Cast iron care chemistry | $195,000 | $28,000 | **15%** |
| Griddle care chemistry | $288,000 | $1,000 | 0.3% |
| Griddle and grill cleaning tool kits | $162,000 | none | 0% |
| Chainmail, scrapers, durables | $180,000 | none | 0% |

**Culina already holds roughly 15% of the only segment it competes in.** Taking that to 25% is worth about $20,000 per month. Even an implausible 50% share of every cast iron chemistry product on Amazon is worth under $30,000 per month. **The segment cannot deliver an additional $50,000 per month and will not grow into it.**

### Category direction

Google Trends across 2019 to 2025 shows a clear sequence: cookware hardware peaked in 2021, branded care products peaked in 2023, and generic care search terms are still setting new highs in 2025. This is the installed base maturing, and it favours a care brand.

Supporting this, Blackstone's own filings show a consumables line growing from $3.0M to $7.7M to $19.9M across 2019 to 2021, against a cumulative installed base of 6.9 million griddles sold through 2021.

### Competitive threat

**Blackstone now sells a "Griddle Seasoning and Cast Iron Conditioner" at $9.49 in Target, with 1,233 reviews, inside a 53-product care line.** Blackstone merged with Weber in May 2025 and has taken exclusive distribution in the independent hardware channel from 2026. This is a mass-retail competitor arriving in roughly half of Culina's current revenue base, and Culina has no retail shelf position.

## 6. Growth pathways, ranked

| Rank | Pathway | Category size | Why it is winnable | Realistic contribution |
|--:|---|--:|---|--:|
| 1 | **Wood cutting board and butcher block care** | $1.2M / mo | Same beeswax and oil base, different carrier oil. The two entrenched brands in this category sell no cast iron care at all. The best-selling product belongs to a brand whose other products are wellness powders | $15,000 to $30,000 / mo |
| 2 | **Cast iron kits and hardware** | $250,000 to $350,000 / mo | Sourcing rather than formulation. Kits sell at $25 to $55 against $8 to $13 for a single tin. Requires no new customer and no new traffic | $10,000 to $20,000 / mo of order value |
| 3 | **Griddle subscription and flat-top liquid cleaner** | $313,000 / mo | Blackstone holds 72% of units but offers no subscription option and no care section in its own site navigation. A competitor prices 27% above Blackstone and still sells over 4,000 units per month | $5,000 to $15,000 / mo |
| n/a | Leather care | $1.25M / mo | Manufacturing fits well, customer overlap is close to zero, incumbents date from 1882 and 1921 | **not recommended** |
| n/a | General kitchen and cookware cleaning | $2.4M / mo | Category leader sells at $0.13 per ounce and runs 37% to 42% gross margin. Entry would be a margin reduction presented as growth | **not recommended** |

**No single pathway delivers an additional $50,000 per month.** The three recommended pathways together are worth an estimated $30,000 to $55,000 per month over a two to three year build, and only if they are treated as line extensions of an existing food-contact oil, wax and soap capability rather than as new category entries.

### Pricing evidence

The nearest direct competitor sells an almost identical three-product architecture at 36% to 50% higher prices, and moves over 2,500 units per month doing so:

| Product | Culina | Competitor |
|---|--:|--:|
| Soap | $9.99 | $13.59 |
| Oil | $14.99 | $14.99 |
| Two-piece set | $17.99 | $26.79 |
| Three-piece set | $24.99 | $35.69 |

Because Amazon's fulfilment fee is fixed per unit, a price increase flows almost entirely into contribution. Moving to competitor parity is worth an estimated **$6,300 per month of additional contribution at unchanged unit volume**, which is more than the account's entire current net result.

A second pricing observation, and a caution about how it was first read. Published competitor volumes suggest the category's kits cluster at $25 to $35, which initially looked like evidence that the $49.99 and $54.99 sets were priced out of the market. First-party unit data contradicts that: the six-piece set at $49.99 sells 97 units per month, more than twice the four-piece at $29.99. **The constraint on those two products is coupon spend, not price.** This is a worked example of why the badge-derived category figures in section 5 should be treated as directional and checked against first-party data wherever first-party data exists.

## 7. Recommended sequence

**Stage one, immediate, no new investment.** De-duplicate the 98 self-competing keyword targets. Apply the 59-term cookware negation list. Remove the coupons from the two premium kits. Estimated value $3,000 to $5,000 per month of contribution.

**Stage two, weeks.** Test a price increase toward competitor parity, beginning with one product. Complete Brand Registry and immediately enrol in Vine to address the review deficit. Correct the conditioning oil's product categorisation.

**Stage three, months.** Introduce kits and hardware to raise average order value on traffic already being paid for. This requires no new customer acquisition.

**Stage four, quarters.** Enter wood board care with a premium kit positioned at $25 to $35. Add a griddle subscription option.

**One low-cost credibility item applies across all stages.** NSF certification is used by at least one competitor across both its cast iron and board care lines. Culina holds none, and the certification would transfer across every category above.

## 8. Method and confidence

**High confidence, first-party.** All revenue, unit, fee, cost of goods and advertising figures come directly from the Amazon Selling Partner API and Amazon Advertising API for the 30 days to 28 September 2026, joined against supplier-provided cost of goods. Advertising figures use 7-day attribution, consistent with Amazon's own reporting.

**Medium confidence, observed but bucketed.** All competitor volumes are derived from Amazon's "bought in past month" badges, which are published in tiers. A reading of "1K+" means between 1,000 and 1,999. All category totals are therefore minimums. Review counts were deliberately not used as a volume proxy, because Amazon pools them across product variations and they measure listing age more than sales.

**Low confidence, estimated.** Channel share splits between Amazon, direct sales and retail. Any projection of contribution from a pathway not yet entered.

**Not established.** Private company revenues for the major incumbents, none of which disclose. Published market-size reports for this category were reviewed and discarded as unreliable: two widely cited sources differ by more than a factor of two on the same year.

**The single measurement that would most improve this analysis is free and becomes available on Brand Registry approval.** Amazon Brand Analytics Search Query Performance gives exact search volume and per-query purchase share for the account's own category. It would replace most of the medium-confidence sizing above with measured figures.
