# Bundle strategy — handoff

Context for picking this work up cold. Numbers live in [`bundle-economics.md`](./bundle-economics.md), regenerated by `node scripts/bundle-economics.mjs --write`. **Don't hand-edit that file.**

Everything below was measured, not assumed. Where something is still an assumption it says so.

---

## 1. The problem this work exists to solve

RSC sells a $30 coconut body lotion. Google Shopping spend on generic lotion terms produced 27 clicks and zero sales. The instinct was to fix bidding. The data said otherwise.

**Amazon SQP (7 weeks, 35 lotion sales) shows the generic market clears at $10–16:**

| Query | Volume | Market sales | Market clears | Ours |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| coconut lotion | 39,121 | 2,749 | $10.08 | $30 |
| organic body lotion | 23,465 | 1,181 | $16.58 | $30 |
| coconut oil for skin | 21,070 | 2,682 | $9.99 | $30 |
| coconut body lotion | 8,071 | 520 | $10.77 | $30 |

**It is a price problem, not a bidding problem.** A 3× price gap cannot be closed by bid caps or negative keywords. It also explains the 0.55% Shopping CTR — Shopping is a price-comparison surface.

Two further findings that shape everything:

- **Above $25, category demand on Amazon collapses to 0.3% of volume**, and every query at that price point is a *brand name* (`cocokind`, `la roche posay`, `pure grace`). Nobody searches "premium organic body lotion" and buys at $30.
- **57% of RSC's Amazon lotion sales come from brand queries**, at exactly our price, with no discount pressure. The non-brand winners are ingredient-led (`paraben chemical free body lotion`, `severe dry skin lotion free of petroleum chemicals`) and also convert at full price.

**The strategic conclusion:** you don't beat a category price ceiling by being premium. You beat it by changing the unit of purchase so per-unit comparison stops applying. A 90-day supply isn't compared against a $10 lotion; it's compared against running out. That is why the bundle architecture is the answer, and why the single $30 lotion should never be a paid-traffic destination.

## 2. Freight — measured, and it changes conclusions

`lib/shipping-costs.js` reads Shopify's `shipping_labels` ShopifyQL dataset (needs `read_reports`). **Label cost is not available anywhere else in the Admin API** — the `ShippingLabel` object has tracking and print status but no price.

232 labels / 365 days / **$1,700.02** / **$7.33** weighted average.

**Cost is driven by package, not weight.** Real labels are flat $6.50–8.50 from 0.4 lb to 3.1 lb — zone dominates. Two examples from the same week: a 2.97 lb seven-item order cost $5.83; a 0.94 lb Set cost $9.37.

The exception is decisive:

| Package | Labels | Avg |
|---|--:|--:|
| Bubble Envelope | 96 | $6.66 |
| 10x5x5 | 43 | $7.83 |
| **14x10x4** | **4** | **$21.31** |

The 14x10x4 is the box a 32oz refill needs, and it bills on **dimensional weight** — 3.2× a bubble envelope. This single fact is why both refill bundles lose money and why they were rejected.

## 3. Rules that fall out of the data

1. **Build bundles from light, high-margin SKUs.** Lotion (84%, 10 oz) and cream (82%, 5 oz) are the best objects in the catalog. Deodorant, toothpaste and bar soap are 73–74% and nearly weightless — terrible standalone (a $11 bar soap nets ~$1 after freight) but excellent bundled, because they add full margin at zero marginal freight.
2. **Order size is the lever, not price.** Every bundle pays freight once. $159 nets $100; $34 nets −$1.65.
3. **Never anchor a bundle on the 32oz refill.** It forces the $21.31 box. Refills are an *attach* — riding along in a box already shipping costs a tier bump (~$3.25), not $21.
4. **A pump-only multipack sits exactly on the CAC line.** At full $52 MSRP it contributes $25.32 — barely clearing $25. Discount it to $44 and it drops to $17.55. Pumps are 67% margin and 10 oz, so four of them need a real box. Adding one lotion (84%, 10 oz) roughly doubles contribution. **Push the 4-pack as reorder/AOV; anchor the paid-traffic version with a lotion.**
5. **Never send paid traffic to the single $30 lotion.** It's an anchor SKU (3×$30 + $28 = $118, which is what makes $99 an offer) and a reorder unit — not an acquisition product.

## 4. Current live configuration

Applied and verified on 2026-07-25.

**Shipping rates** (General profile, Domestic):

```
Standard   $0.00    when TOTAL_PRICE >= $45
Standard   $5.99    when weight 0–5 lb
Standard   $19.90   when weight 5–70 lb
Expedited  $9.99    when weight 0–1 lb
Expedited  $14.99   when weight 1–5 lb
```

$45 was chosen so the $46.80 hero Set clears free shipping on its own. It recovers $463.78/yr of absorbed freight — 92% of what $50 recovers. $60 would have earned $154 more and pushed the hero below the line.

**Second delivery profile: `Heavy Items — Weight-Based (Refills)`** holds the 4 refill variants with weight-based rates ($10.99 / $15.99 / $24.99) and **no free tier**. This exists because Shopify rejects mixing price and weight conditions on one rate (`Method definition cannot save conditions with different fields`), so the ≥$45 free rate could not be weight-capped directly.

**Free-shipping discounts** — all capped at `maximumShippingPrice $7.00` so Expedited is always a paid upgrade:

| Code | Minimum |
|---|--:|
| NEWCUSTOMER | $25 |
| WELCOMEBACK | $25 |
| SHIPFREE | $25 |
| SETSHIP | $45 |
| Subscription Free Shipping (automatic) | none (subscription only) |

**Where absorbed freight actually goes** (365d, $1,231.61 total). The mechanism matters, because only one bucket is fixable:

| Mechanism | Orders | Freight | Note |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| Threshold rate quoted $0.00 | 79 | $644.41 | policy — orders over the threshold. Intentional AOV lever. |
| Zeroed by a discount | 40 | $308.03 | of which **$238.37 came from two automatic discounts since deleted** (`Free shipping`, `FreeShippingLotion`) |
| Customer paid | 67 | −$16.03 | correctly priced ✓ |

Only **$30.35/yr** of discount-driven giveaway is still live, all of it `Subscription Free Shipping`. Discriminate these in order data with `shipping_lines[0].price > 0 && discounted_price == 0` — a `$0.00` quoted price is the threshold rate, not a giveaway, and Shopify confusingly labels it "Free shipping" in `discount_applications` anyway.

**Weights**: 46 bundle variants previously shipped declaring **0 lb**, including the live Sensitive Set and all 5 variants of the $99 Reset. All now set from component weights. Bundle products do **not** expand into component weights at checkout — verify any new bundle declares its own weight.

⚠️ **`inventoryItemUpdate` needs `write_inventory`; the legacy REST `PUT /variants/{id}` path sets the same field under `write_products`.** Both scopes now exist, but REST is the proven route.

### RULE: every bundle must be inventory-tracked

Bundles are **componentized** with `productVariantRelationshipBulkUpdate`, which is how the Sensitive Set and 90-Day Reset already work. Shopify then computes bundle availability from component stock — no double-counting, no phantom inventory.

Do **not** create a bundle as a standalone SKU with untracked inventory. A first attempt at the 4-pack did exactly that and had to be deleted and rebuilt.

Gotchas:
- Shopify overwrites the bundle variant price with the **component sum** at componentization time. Set the price *after* mapping components (the 4-pack came out $44 and had to be reset to $39).
- Bundle inventory takes ~10s to compute after mapping; an immediate read shows 0.
- Only the app that assigned components can manage them.

### Subscription cadence — flat 15%

Sean's rule: **flat 15% at every frequency.** A bigger discount for a longer cycle isn't the goal; removing the incentive to pick monthly is.

The **Shopify Subscriptions app was uninstalled 2026-07-25**, which removed the "Subscribe and save" group carrying monthly at **20%** — the biggest discount on the worst cadence, and the reason every live subscriber was monthly. All 17 Recurpay contracts were unaffected (active=4, cancelled=11, halted=2, unchanged before and after).

**Done as of 2026-07-26 — every plan on every product reads 15%.** Verified per-product across all seven SKUs (see the caveat at the end of this section: the shop-level `sellingPlanGroups` query cannot see Recurpay's groups, so a per-product read is the only honest check).

| Plan | Cadence | Discount |
|---|---|--:|
| `RP_PLAN_11134100` | 1 month | 15% ✓ |
| `RP_PLAN_11150631` / `11151619` | 6 week | 15% ✓ |
| `RP_PLAN_11150631` / `11151619` | 8 week | 15% ✓ (was 10%) |
| `subscribe-and-save` | 30 day | 15% ✓ |
| `BARSOAP_4MO` | 4 month | 15% ✓ |

What is left is **cadence** sprawl, not discount sprawl: most products still show three groups at the same 15%, offering 1-month, 6-week and 8-week. Monthly is still the over-supply cadence, so consolidating remains worthwhile — it just no longer costs money.

⚠️ **Recurpay's `PUT /plans/{id}` is positional and destructive.** Client and full constraints now live in **`lib/recurpay.js`**; audit with **`node scripts/recurpay-audit.mjs`**. Don't re-derive this API by hand again.

The root cause, confirmed live 2026-07-26: **the API rejects any request whose `selling_plans` array holds more than one item** — `422 "The selling_plans must contain exactly one item."` on both POST and PUT. So the single plan you send always becomes position 1, taking your delivery/billing policy with it. That is why a 12-week payload sent to plan `11150632` rewrote that plan's *4-week* position-1 plan to deliver every 12 weeks.

Rules for this API:
- Only safe on plans with a **single** selling plan (position 1) — that is how `11134099` was moved 5% → 15%.
- **Positions 2+ are genuinely unreachable, not merely awkward.** No payload shape fixes this; sending the full array is rejected outright. `updatePlan()` refuses multi-plan targets unless forced.
- **Shopify's API cannot substitute.** `sellingPlanGroupUpdate` on a Recurpay-owned group returns `"Selling plan group does not exist."` even though the group reads fine per-product. That route is closed.
- To test safely, `createPlan()` a throwaway, probe it, then `deletePlan()` — verified working. Don't test on an attached plan.
- Auth is `X-Recurpay-Access-Token`, **not** `Authorization: Bearer` (which 404s with an empty message and looks like a bad path).
- The discount field is spelled **`pricing_polices`** — Recurpay's typo, in both request and response.

Selling plan groups owned by another app are **not listable or editable** via `sellingPlanGroups` even when `appId` reads as null on `product.sellingPlanGroups`. Read them per-product; write them through the owning app's API.

## 7. Known gaps

- **`Subscription Free Shipping` has no minimum order value** — 5 orders/yr, $30.35 of freight, average order $15.81. Left in place deliberately: the root cause was the *subscription SKU*, not the shipping rule, and that has been addressed (below). Revisit only if small subscription orders reappear.
- **Single-bar subscription plans are still attached to `coconut-soap`.** The 20%-monthly option is gone, but two Recurpay groups still sell one $11 bar every 6 or 8 weeks at 15% — **−$0.87 per shipment**. The replacement 4-pack went live 2026-07-26, so detaching is now unblocked; it needs `read_own_subscription_contracts` to check for existing subscribers first.
- **Subscription plan sprawl.** Every product carries 3–4 overlapping selling plan groups from two apps ("Subscribe and save" 6wk/monthly, plus Recurpay `RP_PLAN_*` monthly/6wk/8wk). Customers see competing options. Worth consolidating.
- **`OCU Shipping Discount` (Zipify) has never applied to a single order** in 365 days. It is an inert function the app installs; it is *not* a leak. (An earlier version of this doc claimed otherwise — that was wrong.)
- **Comped orders ship more expensively than paid ones** — 33 orders at $0.00 subtotal averaged $8.81 freight vs $7.33 overall, including one **UPS Next Day Air Saver at $52.75** on a giveaway. Operational control at label-purchase time.
- **The Google feed title** is `global.title_tag` = "Coconut Body Lotion – Real Skin Care" — it leads with the exact term we lose on, while the storefront title already carries the clean-ingredient angle that converts. Changing it trades organic SEO on a ranking page for feed relevance; needs GSC data first.
- **Bucket B freight** ($422/yr, 50 orders ≥$45 shipping free) is an intentional AOV lever, not a leak.
- `agents/shopping-calibrator` runs weekly (Sundays) and auto-negates Google queries whose market clears below 60% of our price. It has two safety rails: never negate a query that produced a sale, and never add a PHRASE negative that would block a *longer* converting query.
