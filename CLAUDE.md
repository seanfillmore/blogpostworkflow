# SEO Claude Team

A fleet of AI agents that grow **Shopify revenue** for **Real Skin Care** (natural deodorants, body care, oral care, lip balm) on its storefront `realskincare.com`, using SEO as the means. RSC also sells on Amazon, where it shares a seller account with a sister brand, **Culina** (cast iron / Blackstone griddle care). Culina is on a separate website with its own SEO automation; this codebase's Shopify/content/SEO pipelines are RSC-only. The Shopify website is the primary revenue channel. Amazon is complementary and validates commercial intent for keyword/SEO decisions.

## Prime Directive — Revenue

**The purpose of this project is to generate revenue for Real Skin Care's Shopify store. SEO is the means, not the goal.** Every agent, report, and change exists to drive *traffic that converts to sales*. Traffic that doesn't convert, content with no purchase path, and rankings that don't produce revenue are not wins — they are unfinished work at best and wasted effort at worst. Before building or optimizing anything, answer: **how does this convert traffic to sales?** If it can't, it's a waste of time.

This reorders how every agent prioritizes:

- **Commercial pages first, but do not multiply them.** Collections and PDPs convert; informational blog posts mostly don't. **A collection exists only where a category holds 2 or more distinct products — single-product categories are PDP-only, and a collection is never created to chase a keyword.** Chasing rankings with new collections produced 62 live collections for 9 products, which split ranking signal and earned 51 clicks on 93,785 impressions in 90 days. Optimize the pages that exist before creating another.
- **Every page that earns traffic needs a conversion path** — a prominent above-the-fold link/CTA to the relevant collection or PDP, plus a working buy-box. A post with traffic and no buy path is a bug to fix, not a success.
- **Measure in dollars, not clicks — and be precise about WHICH dollars.** `agents/seo-impact` answers two different questions and one figure cannot mean both. **`clusters[].product_revenue_all_channels`** is what a CATEGORY SOLD, from raw order line items keyed on the product title; **`clusters[].entry_page_organic_revenue`** (alias `revenue`) is what a PAGE EARNED — organic-search-only, 28-day, entry-page-credited, bucketed by a word in the landing page's URL, reconciling only to `totals.organic_revenue`. Over 90 days **54% of the store's revenue ($1,152.50) lands on pages matching no cluster at all**, which the report shows as an explicit residual row instead of leaving invisible. Ask "is this category worth investing in" of the product figure and "which page earns" of the entry-page figure. **A cluster that sold $0 is put ON HOLD automatically** — see the cluster-hold section below; the verdict is made on the product figure over 90 days, because that is the question, and because a $0 over the report's own 28 days is noise (a genuinely-selling category records zero 37% of the time at 28 days and 4% at 90). Toothpaste is the case that taught this: entry-page attribution said $0 across 663 clicks and it was held for two days on that; its **products** sold **$71.50** over the trailing 90 days. Still the least efficient cluster in the catalogue — 59% of clustered organic clicks for 2.9% of revenue — but it is not a dud, and it is no longer held. Inefficiency and $0 are different findings needing different mechanisms, and **both mechanisms now exist**: `lib/cluster-hold.js` EXCLUDES a $0 cluster, `lib/cluster-efficiency.js` ORDERS everything that survives. See the cluster-efficiency section below.
- **CTR and conversion are first-class.** Impressions without clicks (low CTR) and clicks without purchases (no conversion) are the two revenue leaks. Closing them beats chasing new keywords.

When an agent has the data to make a revenue-improving decision, it makes it and applies it (see Autonomy Principle below).

**BRAND FACT — RSC sells a DEODORANT, never an ANTIPERSPIRANT.** Sean, verbatim (2026-08-24): *"We should never use the term antiperspirant to describe our product because it is not accurate."* An **antiperspirant is an FDA-regulated OTC drug** — it reduces sweating through an aluminum-salt active ingredient under the monograph at 21 CFR Part 350, and carries a Drug Facts panel. RSC sells an **aluminum-free coconut-oil deodorant**, a cosmetic that addresses odor. The word is wrong twice: **factually** (deodorant addresses odor, antiperspirant addresses wetness) and **as a regulatory claim** — describing a cosmetic with a drug CATEGORY name is the same intended-use failure PRs #634/#645/#648 cleaned up, except that here the category name *is* the claim rather than smuggling one in through a verb. Same class of rule as "made in the USA, never Blum".

**But the line is narrow, and over-correcting is the expensive mistake. RANKING FOR THE QUERY IS FINE; DESCRIBING THE PRODUCT WITH THE WORD IS NOT.** Measured read-only on 2026-08-24 across 204 live articles, 19 products, 89 collections and 42 pages, the word appears **539 times in rendered prose on 58 pages** (687 counting `href`/`title` attributes) — and essentially all of it is legitimate category reference: the FDA monograph, aluminum chemistry, deodorant-vs-antiperspirant comparisons, the two-to-four-week transition, "when was antiperspirant invented". **Exactly TWO of those 539 described an RSC product**, both buy-box headlines (see the product-category gate below). Five article titles and five URL handles are built on the word; **do not rename a slug** — a handle is a URL, not a description of the product, and renaming one breaks a ranking page for no accuracy gain. Posts may discuss antiperspirants as a category freely. What must never appear is *our* product being one: no "our antiperspirant", no "Real Skin Care's natural antiperspirant", no product name, formula description, CTA or buy-box copy using the word for the product.

Both brands ship from the same Amazon seller account. Until Culina Brand Registry approves, Amazon Brand Analytics + Search Query Performance reports return blended (BA) or zero (SQP) data for Culina. Classify ASINs by keyword in the product title:

- contains `culina` or `cast iron` → **Culina**
- everything else (including `REAL` sub-brand) → **RSC**

## Development Rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Always work on a branch** — never commit directly to `main`. Use `feature/<name>` or `fix/<name>`.
2. **Always merge via pull request.** Open with `gh pr create`. No fast-forward, no squash-merge locally, no pushing to `main` from the feature branch.
3. **Test locally before pushing to the server.**
4. **Test a fix on one post before bulk-applying.** End-to-end (edit → upload → check live site) before writing batch scripts.
5. **These rules apply even for one-line fixes.**
6. **Never work in the main checkout — take a worktree.** Multiple sessions run against this repo at once, and two sessions sharing `/Users/seanfillmore/Code/Claude` fight over HEAD. This has cost real work twice: 2026-07-24 a session switched the shared working dir onto its branch mid-task and a commit landed on the wrong branch; 2026-07-27 two sessions committed to `feature/marketing-tactic-lifecycle` within the same hour, one of them opening a PR whose body was stale inside the hour.

```bash
scripts/new-worktree.sh <name> [branch-name]     # branches from origin/main, symlinks .env + node_modules
cd .claude/worktrees/<name>                       # commit and open the PR from here
git worktree remove .claude/worktrees/<name>      # after the PR merges
```

Branch from `origin/main`, never from wherever the main checkout is sitting — it is 40+ commits stale as often as not. Before committing anywhere, re-check `git branch --show-current`; never assume the branch you left is the branch you are on.

## Autonomy Principle

Agents that have the data to make a decision should make it and apply the change. Surface cases to the user only when genuinely ambiguous (cannibalization-resolver auto-applies HIGH confidence, queues MEDIUM/LOW for review; meta-ab-tracker auto-reverts losing variants). When designing a new agent, default to apply-not-dry-run for production behavior. See `~/.claude/projects/-Users-seanfillmore-Code-Claude/memory/feedback_autonomous_by_default.md` for the full rule.

## Architecture (non-obvious patterns)

**Single-file daily orchestration.** `scheduler.js` at the project root runs every day at 8 AM PT (15 UTC) on the production server via cron. It dispatches the daily pipeline, plus weekly jobs on Sundays and monthly jobs on the 1st. Read `scheduler.js` for the actual order.

**Deferred-notification digest.** Agents call `notify({...})` from `lib/notify.js`. **Every** notification appends to `data/reports/daily-summary/YYYY-MM-DD.jsonl` rather than emailing — `status: 'error'` only changes how the digest renders the row, it does not escalate. The single exception is `immediate: true`, which emails at call time; use it sparingly. The `daily-summary` agent reads the JSONL at 5 AM PT and sends one consolidated HTML digest via Resend. `NOTIFY_DEFERRED=1` is set by `scheduler.js` and by the giveaway cron entry but is **read nowhere** — deferral is unconditional and the variable is vestigial. This paragraph previously claimed errors bypassed deferral; they never have, so anything that must not wait for the 5 AM digest needs `immediate: true`.

**Content pipeline order:** `content-strategist` → `content-researcher` → `blog-post-writer` → `image-generator` → `answer-first-rewriter` → `featured-product-injector` → `schema-injector` → `editor` → `publisher`. Orchestrated by `calendar-runner` (reads `data/calendar.json`).

**Retired structured-data types — `FAQPage`, `HowTo` and `Article`, all stopped on 2026-08-24, and the evidence is here so nobody re-adds them.** `agents/schema-injector` now emits exactly ONE node, `BreadcrumbList`, from the pure `buildPostSchemas` in `lib/schema-builders.js`. What was removed and why, each verified against the live web and the live site rather than recalled:

- **`FAQPage` — Google REMOVED the FAQ rich result from Search.** Not the 2023 narrowing to authoritative sites; outright removal. `https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/faqpage` returns **301 → `/search/updates#removing-faq-rich-result`** (the doc no longer exists) and FAQ is absent from the rich results gallery. Deprecation announced May 8, the feature stopped appearing May 7, the docs came down June 15.
- **`HowTo` — removed the same way in September 2023.** `.../structured-data/howto` returns **404**.
- **`Article` — a DUPLICATE, measured off the rendered pages.** All 182 blog article URLs in `sitemap_blogs_1.xml` were fetched and their JSON-LD parsed: the **theme** publishes `Article` + `BreadcrumbList` + `Organization` + `WebPage` + `Person` on **182 of 182**. The injector's Article node was a second copy layered on top. Do not check this against `theme/` in the checkout — that directory is a partial mirror; fetch the pages.

**`BreadcrumbList` stays, and not by omission.** Breadcrumbs are a live, supported rich result, and the theme's own `BreadcrumbList` is a degenerate **one-item stub** carrying nothing but "Home". The injector's is the real Home › News › Title trail, so it is the only thing on an article page this agent still contributes that nothing else provides.

**Existing blocks on live pages were deliberately NOT swept, and that is the decision, not an omission.** They are inert — Google ignores an unsupported type — so a sweep buys zero ranking and zero revenue while rewriting `body_html` on ~74 live indexed pages, which is this repo's highest-risk write class and is currently gated by the publisher's mirror-divergence refusal. Instead they **drain on their own**: `stripExistingSchemas` removes every JSON-LD block before the injector writes its own, so any post that passes through the pipeline again loses its dead schema as a side effect of work already happening on it. Measured live 2026-08-24 for whoever picks this up: **74 pages carry a content-embedded `FAQPage`, 63 an `Article`, 26 a `HowTo`** (the audit's 18/14/22 counted only the still-*parseable* ones).

**A separate live defect found while measuring, NOT fixed here: 58 of 182 pages carry INVALID JSON-LD.** `agents/internal-linker` has rewritten `<a href="…">` anchors *inside* JSON-LD string values, and the unescaped double quotes make the block unparseable — 56 `FAQPage`, 41 `Article`, 12 `HowTo`. Breadcrumb blocks are untouched because they contain no prose for a linker to hit. The theme's own valid `Article` node is separate and unaffected, so this is a Search Console parse warning rather than a ranking loss — but the fix belongs in the linker (never rewrite inside `<script>`), not in another mass body rewrite.

**Two consumers keyed on this output and BOTH had to move in the same change.** Stopping emission alone would have been worse than doing nothing:

1. **`agents/legacy-rebuilder`'s `isLegacy(slug)` was `!html.includes('FAQPage')`** read from `content.html`, and a "legacy" verdict is a full **paid** pipeline rebuild, five a day, unattended. Stop emitting FAQPage and every post the pipeline writes reads as legacy, forever. It now asks `lib/injected-schema.js`'s **`hasInjectedSchema`** — "does this body carry any injected JSON-LD", the direct statement of what the substring was only ever a proxy for. **The swap can only SHRINK the legacy set** (FAQPage can only occur inside a JSON-LD block), so no post is newly enrolled into spend: measured over the 93 eligible local posts, **39 legacy before, 36 after, 0 newly legacy**. The 3 that fall out were a live defect — FAQPage was CONDITIONAL on 2+ question headings while the injector ran unconditionally, so a post it had processed but which had too few question headings could never satisfy the test and was rebuilt and re-queued every morning.
2. **`agents/editor`'s rule 8 (COMPETITOR NAMES IN FAQ, a BLOCKER) parsed the FAQPage block for its Q&As.** With no schema it would have rendered "Pass · rule does not apply" on every new post — a compliance check switching itself off in silence. It reads the **prose** now, through the shared `lib/faq-blocks.js`. That also closed a live inconsistency: the editor AUTO-FIXES competitor names at step 1c by delegating to `agents/faq-rewriter`, which rewrites prose and never touched the JSON-LD, so the fixer and the checker read two different copies of the same FAQ and the schema copy was the stale one — a post whose prose had just been cleaned could still be BLOCKED for a name that no longer appeared on the page.

`lib/content-reconcile.js` keyed its rollback on the same string and moved with them (`schemaRegression`, re-exported from `lib/injected-schema.js`). Note the arm this fixes: re-injecting an old mirror now *replaces* FAQPage/HowTo/Article with a BreadcrumbList, which under the old key read as a LOSS and would have rolled back and held every mirror the reconciler touched.

**Still emitting `FAQPage`, deliberately out of scope: the COLLECTION agents** (`collection-creator`, `collection-content-optimizer`, via `buildFaqSchema`) and `agents/product-optimizer`. The rich result is just as dead for them; they are a different surface with a different blast radius (published commercial pages) and are their own change. `agents/content-researcher` also still writes a `schema_type` field into briefs naming `HowTo`/`FAQPage` — audited 2026-08-24, **nothing reads it**, so it is inert rather than harmful.

**Cannibalization auto-publish flow:** the `cannibalization-resolver` runs Sundays. CONSOLIDATE actions merge content via Claude → run editor → if editor finds no blockers (no `meta.needs_rebuild` set), the merge auto-publishes and the redirect is created immediately. If the editor flags blockers, the merge is HELD — draft saved, live winner untouched, surfaced in the report's "Drafts needing review" section — and **the redirect is not always created**: `agents/cannibalization-resolver/redirect-decision.js`'s `decideHeldMergeRedirect` still creates it when the loser's own GSC clicks (from the window already fetched for detection, no extra call) are provably zero, and skips it — same as before — when the loser earns real clicks, or when traffic data is missing (fails safe). This exists because "skip while held" used to be unconditional and a held merge has no expiry: the tattoo-soap merge sat held six days (2026-08-16 to 2026-08-22) on "overall quality: needs work" while its 0-click loser kept splitting ranking signal from a 10-click winner, until an operator redirected it by hand. **That hold turned out to be right for the wrong reason, and investigating it on 2026-08-22 found two independent defects, both now fixed.** (1) The merge itself was **truncated** — `consolidateContent` ran at a flat `max_tokens: 8000`, below the *median* article's own ~6.6k tokens once the prompt's "+30% length" allowance applies, so most merges were destined to truncate, not just this one. It returned HTML ending mid-sentence ("…fragrance-free (or scented"), having dropped the CTA, Sources, Related Articles and 3 FAQ entries. Nothing checked `stop_reason`. The ceiling is now derived from the winner's own length (`mergeMaxTokens`) and `assertHtmlComplete` runs on the result, so a truncated merge throws instead of becoming a publishable draft. (2) The hold reason was an artifact: the editor reconciles Overall Quality into the report it saves (`reconcileOverallQuality`), then derived `meta.needs_rebuild` from the **raw pre-reconciliation** LLM text — so the saved report read "OVERALL QUALITY: Pass" while the flag gating auto-publish read "overall quality: needs work", and no reader of the report could see why it was held. The gate now reads the same reconciled text everything else reads (`llmBlockerReasons`). Note the order these interact: fixing (2) alone would have **auto-published the truncated merge** over a live ranking page. The editor's LLM had explicitly asserted "Final section (FAQ) is complete, not cut off" about content that was cut off mid-word — an LLM assertion is not a check, which is why truncation detection is deterministic and lives in `lib/html-output-guards.js`. An earlier version of this paragraph said "Redirects always created" with no carve-out mentioned at all; an agent read that and shipped a redirect the resolver's actual code would not have made on its own — right call on the evidence, wrong premise. `--publish-pending-drafts [--apply]` clears any backlog of drafts the agent created on prior runs (it does not itself create the loser redirect on publish — that still only happens on the run that decided the merge).

**Optimization Queue auto-apply.** `agents/queue-autoapply` (scheduler step 4d, daily, `--apply` on the scheduled path and dry by default on the CLI) drains `data/performance-queue/` without a human clicking Approve — the queue had reached 21 pending items, the oldest 37 days old, and work that sits in a queue earns $0. Policy lives in the pure `lib/queue-autoapply.js`: it **applies** `seo-opportunity`, `quick-win`, `flop-refresh`, `page-meta-rewrite`, `low-ctr-meta` and `legacy-flop` after the existing editorial gate (`lib/edit-gate-repair.js`) passes — a blocked item goes into that library's repair loop and stays `pending`, it is never escalated to a human list — and **auto-dismisses** `collection-gap` items that hold fewer than 2 distinct products or sit in a cluster `data/reports/seo-impact/latest.json` shows earning $0 — read through `lib/cluster-hold.js`'s `corroboratedClassification`, never `classifyClusters` directly, so an uncorroborated $0 cannot dismiss a page for a category that really sells. **No cluster name appears in the code**; only the 2+ products rule is hardcoded, because it is the Prime Directive and must keep working when the revenue report is stale or missing. A collection-gap that passes both gates is still never auto-created — creating a commercial page is the decision that produced 62 collections for 9 products. **At most 5 live applies per run**, oldest first; dismissals are uncapped because they touch only a local JSON file. It applies **directly** rather than only stamping `approved`: the publisher's `publishApprovedQueueItems()` runs inside `agents/publisher/index.js`'s `main()`, which requires a post-metadata argument, so `approved` items only ship on days the calendar happens to publish something — leaning on that seam would have swapped a manual chore for a silent stall. The actual publish path is `lib/queue-apply.js`, shared with the dashboard's Approve route so the collection-gap gates (2+ products, `published: false` draft, `validateCollectionSpec`) cannot exist on one path and not the other; Shopify functions are injected there and in `lib/queue-revert.js` because `lib/shopify.js` throws at import time without OAuth credentials. Nothing is applied without a backup captured **before** the write and a `revert_plan` stamped on the item, and a refresh with no backup is refused outright rather than overwriting a live article irreversibly. Since 2026-08-24 `decide()` also runs the **health-claim gate** over an item's stored inline copy before any of that — see the SEO-copy gate section below for why that check sits in the pure planner and why a hit is a `skip`, never a `dismiss`.

**$0 clusters go ON HOLD — "if it doesn't drive revenue, we put it on hold" (Sean, verbatim) — but only when TWO sources agree.** A hold means **stop spending unattended LLM/refresh cycles on a cluster**. It does **NOT** mean unpublish, delete, redirect, deindex, or drop from a sitemap: these are live, indexed pages, they stay live and keep whatever traffic they have, and nothing on this path may call `lib/post-kill.js` or create a redirect (pinned by a test). The trigger was 2026-08-21, when one $0 cluster took 11 of 15 indexing "critical" slots and fired 11 content-quality refreshes — each a chain of paid LLM calls — on pages that have never produced a dollar. **The rule lives in `lib/cluster-hold.js` and names no cluster in its logic.**

**NO SINGLE MEASUREMENT PIPELINE MAY CONDEMN A CATEGORY ALONE.** That is the safety property, stated precisely, and the first version of this rule did not have it. Held on one source the rule paused **soap**, which really sells **$324.85 / 90d, 13% of all revenue, second only to lotion** — entry-page attribution filed $62.40 of it under `hand soap` and showed `soap` at $0, and the rule read the artifact as a verdict.

**The two sources SWAPPED ROLES on 2026-08-23 and a third was retired.** The rule is unchanged; what each side answers is not.

|  | **PRIMARY (source A)** | **CROSS-CHECK (source B)** |
|---|---|---|
| question | what did this CATEGORY SELL? | what did its PAGES EARN? |
| data | raw order **line items** | raw order **totals** |
| join key | product title → cluster | landing-page URL → cluster |
| channels | all | organic search only |
| window | 90 days | 90 days |
| applied by | `classifyClusters` (`lib/cluster-revenue.js`) | `corroborateClusters` (`lib/cluster-hold.js`) |

A cluster is `proven_dud` — and therefore held — **only when A says $0.00 AND B says $0.00**, on enough content exposure (≥125 clicks across ≥5 pages) and in a window big enough to judge (≥45 all-channel orders). Both fields come off `clusters_product_wide[]` in the same report, written in one atomic run, so the two sources can never be from different days.

**Why that is still two sources and not one number twice.** The independence that caught the soap bug was never "two different databases" — both sides always read Shopify orders. It was the **join key**: the same $62.40 order joined by product title landed in `soap` and joined by landing-page URL landed in `hand soap`, and the contradiction is what stopped the hold. A and B still differ on exactly that axis, so the defect class that produced the incident is still detectable — and **measurably so**: run that incident on the new basis and soap's pages earned **$110.49** over the judging window, which blocks the hold at soap's real **227** clicks. The old 400-click bar could not even reach the cross-check at that traffic.

**Why `topProducts[]` was retired as a source.** `data/snapshots/shopify/*.json` `topProducts[]` answered the SAME question as A by the SAME join key — it is a copy of A, capped by `agents/shopify-collector` at the top 5 products per day, and demonstrably wrong because of the cap: it put toothpaste at $39.00/90d where the raw line items say $71.50. Keeping it would have given the appearance of three sources while deleting the one axis of real independence. **A capped copy of the primary is not corroboration.**

**The AOV floor is gone, deliberately.** The old rule asked whether measured revenue cleared "one average order in the window", because `topProducts[]` was capped and noisy and a few dollars of noise should not block a hold. Both sources are now exact — line items reconcile to `subtotal_price` to the cent, order totals are the orders themselves — so there is no noise for a floor to absorb, and a floor would be a materiality judgement `classifyClusters` explicitly declines to make ("any amount counts"). Both steps now use the same test, `> $0`. The materiality notion did not vanish: it was **restated as a rate** and is what every threshold below is derived from.

The two vocabularies meet through `lib/keyword-index/cluster.js`'s ordered `assignCluster` — the **only** cluster taxonomy in the fleet since 2026-08-23, applied to both sides. Its first-match ordering (soap before lotion) is the only reason "Moisturizing Coconut Soap" counts as soap revenue rather than lotion revenue.

**Disagreement is a finding, not a shrug, and it survived the swap on purpose.** When A says the products sold $0 and B says the pages earned money, the cluster is **NOT held** and the mismatch is reported loudly — in `holdBanner()`, in `npm run cluster-holds`, and (because these agents run unattended at 3 and 8 AM) as `renderDisagreementLines()` in each gated agent's **digest body**. It means one of two things, both worth fixing and neither a dud: attribution is broken for that cluster, **or** its pages sell somebody else's category. A cluster with no SKU behind it does exactly the second — `coconut oil` is that shape, since RSC ships no coconut-oil product, so A is structurally $0 for it forever. (Note it is not currently an example of B *firing*: measured over the same window its pages earned $0.00 too, and it is spared by the 125-click precondition at 11 clicks. Do not repeat the earlier claim that its posts "send readers to lotion" — nothing measured says so.)

**Everything fails OPEN, and a disarmed gate now SAYS SO.** Missing report, **a report older than 4 days**, a report with no `clusters_product_wide[]` (source A absent — the pre-migration deploy state), no `entry_page_organic_revenue` on those rows (source B absent), a product map that is empty or all-zero (the shape a Shopify response with no `line_items` produces — `wide_orders_without_lines_all_channels` is emitted so a human can see it), a missing or below-`MIN_WINDOW_ORDERS` order count for the judging window, or a cluster name that maps to no product family → **nothing is held**, and the reason is printed. That last clause was false until 2026-08-23: a fresh report carrying `clusters_product_wide: []` gave `available: true`, every cluster `unproven` and an EMPTY banner — a fully disarmed gate rendering byte-identically to a clean run, which is precisely the "quiet loss of capability" the freshness rule exists to make loud. `buildClusterHold` now computes a `disarmed` reason and `holdBanner` prints `⚠ The $0-cluster gate is OFF this run: <why>`. **Never infer "clean" from `held.length === 0`** — read `hold.disarmed`.

**One thing the two-source rule does NOT promise.** A cluster absent from `clusters_product_wide[]` reads $0 on both sources and can be held. That is a *measured* zero, not a missing one — each rollup covers every order in the window, so a cluster neither mentions took no line item and hosted no landing page. But note the asymmetry it creates: source B is built from `shopifyRevenueByPage`, which drops orders with no `landingPath` (subscription renewals, direct carts), so B **under-reports** for clusters whose buyers arrive that way. B failing to block a hold it morally should have blocked is possible; B wrongly *causing* one is not, since it only ever adds a zero to a verdict A already reached. Two sources reduce false condemnations. They do not eliminate them. The top-5-per-day snapshot cap is no longer one of these conditions because `topProducts[]` is no longer read; the equivalent guard is now the order count, which is the only thing standing between a degenerate order pull and every category being condemned at once. Pausing the fleet on an absent measurement is the opposite of the Prime Directive. `agents/queue-autoapply` reads the same loader (it used to classify the report inline), so the queue's $0 collection-gap dismissal and the refresh hold can never drift apart.

**One staleness policy, in `lib/seo-impact-freshness.js`, because there were eight and six of them were "none" (2026-08-23).** The same `latest.json` was read with a 3-day guard (`pipeline-prioritizer`), a 9-day one (`daily-summary`), a 35-day one (`priority-tuner`) and **no guard at all** in `content-strategist`, `calendar-runner`, `lib/brief-triage.js`, `ad-brief` and the dashboard. The unguarded ones were the dangerous ones: a months-old report still blocked drafting, still silently dropped LLM proposals, and still `unlinkSync`'d paid-for briefs. **`SEO_IMPACT_MAX_AGE_DAYS` is 4** — the producer is daily, one day of that is structural lag (seo-impact is scheduler step 5z.1 while `calendar-runner` and `queue-autoapply` are steps 1 and 3, and `daily-summary` runs at 5 AM PT, three hours before that day's report exists), leaving ~2 days of grace, so age > 4 means at least three consecutive missed runs — the signature of the 2026-06 disk-full outage, never a transient. The arithmetic is **not** re-implemented: the module delegates to `lib/snapshot-health.js`'s `checkFreshness`, the fleet's one answer to "how old is too old".

**Two behaviours, and they are not interchangeable.** Anything that **blocks or deletes** fails safe: `loadClusterHold` treats a stale report *exactly* like an absent one (`available: false`, empty classification, `stale: true` kept for the banner), so every cluster reads `unproven` and nothing is held, blocked, deferred or deleted — `scripts/triage-orphan-briefs.mjs --drop-non-earning` refuses to run at all. That is the same fail-open shape `classifyClusters` already has for a missing product-revenue reading or a missing judging-window order count, one more input the gate declines to guess at, not a second mechanism beside it. Anything that **ranks or displays** degrades instead: `pipeline-prioritizer` skips the revenue-cluster boost *and logs that it did*, `ad-brief` drops the report and scores `commercial` neutral (never throws — ranking destroys nothing), the dashboard card renders with a staleness banner rather than hiding, because a three-week-old revenue table otherwise reads exactly as authoritative as today's.

**The alert threshold is the same constant, deliberately.** `daily-summary`'s `checkSystemHealth` is the only channel a stale report speaks through — these agents run unattended at 3 and 8 AM and nobody reads their stdout — so it must never alert LATER than a consumer degrades. It sat at 9 while `pipeline-prioritizer` dropped the revenue signal at 3, so ages 4-9 were a window where the fleet ran degraded and the digest said all was well. Its row now imports `SEO_IMPACT_MAX_AGE_DAYS` and carries a note saying the gates have *fallen open* rather than the generic "running on stale data" — a stale report is a quiet loss of capability, not a wrong action, and the two need different fixes. **`agents/priority-tuner` is the one documented exception at 35 days** (`PRIORITY_TUNER_MAX_AGE_DAYS`): it does not read the report's current state, it joins `action_wins`, which describe a trailing 28-day outcome window that only becomes measurable after `tuning.measureLagDays`. Looser than the alert is safe — a looser consumer can never degrade before the digest has already complained. A *stricter*-than-alert consumer is the bug. No consumer may spell its own number; a test pins that each one imports the policy module.

Gated — the six points that actually spend money unattended, each **skipping and counting**, never silently dropping: `agents/indexing-fixer`'s `crawled_not_indexed` → content-quality refresh (the 11/day), `agents/performance-engine`'s four pickers, `agents/legacy-rebuilder`'s pick list, `agents/blocked-post-resolver`'s candidates, `agents/refresh-runner`'s three bulk pick lists, and — added 2026-08-23, missed by the original change — `agents/meta-optimizer`'s low-CTR candidate list. **The hold is always applied BEFORE the per-run cap** — holding afterwards would let five held posts eat a budget of five and leave every earning-cluster post untouched, which is the bug this rule exists to prevent wearing different clothes. **meta-optimizer is the case that shows why "before the cap" is the whole rule.** Its weekly cron is `--apply --limit 5`, spent in `sortByValidation` order (Amazon-validated first, then by impressions). Simulated against the real 2026-08-23 pool: **4 of the 5 slots fell in the held cluster, and `best soap for tattoos` — the site's biggest CTR opportunity, and the very page the winner-lock work above exists to make improvable — ranked SIXTH and was never reached at all.** Gated, it moves to second and the cap lands entirely on earning clusters. Nine held queries collapse to four held pages in the digest (`dedupeHeld` keys on the page, since several queries point at one article), which is the number that honestly answers "how much is this hold withholding". Its selector is the pure `agents/meta-optimizer/lib/hold.js` — split out because `agents/meta-optimizer/index.js` calls `loadEnv()` and can `process.exit` at import, so a selector living there could not be tested; `lib/sort.js` and `lib/grounding.js` were already split for the same reason. Clustering tries the QUERY first (what seo-impact attributes revenue on) and falls back to the page the rewrite would land on, so a query like "best options for 2026" that names no cluster is still placed by its page.

Deliberately NOT gated: indexing-fixer's Tier 1/Tier 2 (a sitemap ping and an Indexing API submission cost nothing but a Google quota, and a hold is never a deindexing — held pages stay submitted); `agents/content-refresher`, which is never on cron and is only ever reached with an explicit `--slug` from a caller already gated above; **meta-optimizer's `--refresh-stale-years` pass**, which is a deterministic regex with no model call and where holding would leave "2025" in the title of a live indexed page — a hold pauses spend and is explicitly not allowed to degrade a page; and any single-slug CLI invocation anywhere, because a hand-typed slug is not unattended spend. Every gated agent reports its held count in the console, in its `renderHoldLines()` digest body and in its `notify()` subject — one **deferred** notification, never `immediate: true`, and a hold never sets `status: 'error'`, because it is the policy working rather than a failure. Two agents that would otherwise fall silent (`performance-engine`, `refresh-runner`) fire a notification specifically for the "did nothing BECAUSE everything was held" run: a hold nobody can see becomes a mystery outage six weeks later.

Observable and overridable: **`npm run cluster-holds`** (`scripts/cluster-holds.mjs`, `--json` for machines) prints every cluster side by side as **SOLD $ (source A) vs PAGES $ (source B)**, with the report's own 28-day entry-page figure shown for context and judged on by nothing, plus each cluster's product family, its state (`ON HOLD` / `DISAGREE!` / `no data` / `earning` / `unproven`), and the agents that honour the hold. `PAGES $` is per **family**, so sibling clusters legitimately repeat the same figure. It also prints the **EFFICIENCY RANKING** (below) in the same output and under `--json`, because a hold is now a rare event and the ranking is what actually decides where a budget goes on an ordinary day — splitting them across two commands is how the second one stops being read. **`--include-held`** on any gated agent runs the held items anyway and still records that they were held. There is **no flag to override the ranking**, because it excludes nothing.

**Cluster EFFICIENCY RANKING — `lib/cluster-efficiency.js`, the mechanism that replaces a hard block with an order (2026-08-23).** The hold answers *did this category earn literally nothing?* and, at ~50 all-channel orders per 90 days against `MIN_WINDOW_ORDERS` 45, the honest answer is now almost always "no cluster did". **This store can barely condemn a category on revenue evidence, and the gate is right to admit that.** What did not go away is the business fact: toothpaste takes **640 of 1,114 clustered organic clicks (57.5%) and returns $71.50 of $2,435.45 (2.9%)**. Sean's instruction — "if it doesn't drive revenue, we put it on hold" — expressed honestly for a cluster that earns *a little* is **rank it last, not block it**. A hard block is wired to brief archival, a path that has already permanently destroyed three paid-for briefs; it is far too blunt for a signal this noisy.

**The measure: `score = product_revenue_all_channels ÷ (clicks + PRIOR_CLICKS)`.** The numerator is **source A** — what the CATEGORY SOLD over the 90-day judging window, from raw order line items. Ranking on `entry_page_organic_revenue` instead is the same category error that had soap condemned at $0 while it sold $324.85, wearing a softer consequence. The denominator is **clicks, not pages**: clicks are an OUTCOME (demand the cluster attracted), pages are a DECISION (how much we chose to publish). Dividing by pages rewards a cluster for our own restraint and forgives it for our own over-publishing — toothpaste scores better per page (24 pages) than per click (640 clicks) precisely *because* we over-published it, and the operator's own statement of the problem is a click statement. `revenuePerPage` is reported on every row so the 24-vs-6 page disparity stays visible; **nothing sorts on it**. A blend was computed and moves exactly one pair (soap/lip balm) while making the number unexplainable. **The ratio is an INDEX, not a price** — 90-day all-channel dollars over 28-day organic clicks is not literally "dollars per click"; every cluster carries the identical mismatch so comparing clusters *on one report* is valid, and the absolute level means nothing.

**The small-sample guard, and the direction it shrinks.** Lip balm sold **$117 behind SIX organic clicks**. Raw revenue-per-click makes it the most efficient cluster in the catalogue ($19.50/click vs lotion's $16.58) — it ranks **first**, on a sample of six. The cause is structural: product revenue is all-channel while clicks are organic search, so a cluster whose buyers arrive elsewhere divides a real numerator by a near-zero denominator and the number is not measuring conversion at all. `PRIOR_CLICKS` adds a fair shot's worth of clicks that earned nothing to every denominator, and **it IS `MIN_CLICKS` (125), imported from `lib/cluster-revenue.js` and never re-declared** — that constant is already derived as the click count at which a cluster's own pages expect three orders over the judging window, i.e. exactly where its click data starts carrying evidence. **It shrinks toward ZERO, never toward the site average, and that is the whole design decision.** Regressing a thin sample to the mean is the textbook move and is wrong here: `coconut oil` sold $0 across all 50 orders in the window, and mean-shrinkage ranks that as merely average — third of six, **above soap**, which is 13% of revenue with a paid campaign live. The evidence for a $0 comes from the ORDER POOL, not from the cluster's own clicks (the same insight that moved the dud gate onto product revenue), so "we have not seen many clicks" may never be read as "it is probably fine". Same shape as the dud gate's guard, opposite tail: there a $0 is not evidence until the window is big enough, here a high rate is not evidence until the click count is big enough.

**How it composes with the hold: the hold EXCLUDES, the ranking ORDERS what survives.** No hold logic is re-implemented and no cluster is named in code. `rankClusters` reads `hold.heldSet` for exactly two purposes — to mark a held row (it sorts last anyway, since a held cluster's product revenue is $0 by definition) and to guarantee the anti-starvation reserve can **never** target a held cluster, which would quietly undo the hold. Under `--include-held` a held cluster is ranked last, not promoted.

**It must not starve a cluster to zero — that would be the hard block again under a new name.** A strict order plus a per-run cap is a block by arithmetic: four lotion candidates and a budget of four means toothpaste is untouched this week, next week and every week. So `orderByEfficiency(items, ranking, { limit })` **reserves one slot inside the cap** for the lowest-ranked cluster PRESENT in that list (worst-present, not worst-that-exists, or the reserve would almost never fire). It applies only at `limit >= RESERVE_MIN_LIMIT` (**3**): below three slots a reserved one is a third or more of the budget, which is a co-equal share rather than "what is left" and inverts the ranking it is meant to soften. At the caps that actually run — 3 (`refresh-runner`), 5 (`legacy-rebuilder`, `blocked-post-resolver`, `meta-optimizer`), 6 (`performance-engine`) — that is 33%, 20% and 17%. **The reserve is a floor, not a ceiling:** when the pool is dominated by the bottom cluster it still takes the leftover slots, which is the intent — leftovers, not nothing.

**It DEGRADES, it does not fail safe, because it destroys nothing.** Per the freshness split above: a missing report, a stale one, or one carrying no `clusters_product_wide[]` yields **no ranking**, and every pick list comes back in exactly the order its own picker built it. It never falls back to the entry-page figure to have something to sort by. `efficiencyBanner()` prints `· Clusters are NOT ranked by efficiency this run: <why>` for the same reason `hold.disarmed` exists — a ranking that quietly stopped ranking is byte-identical to one with nothing to reorder. Every ranked agent puts `renderEfficiencyLines()` in its console output AND in its existing **deferred** `notify()` body; nothing here is `immediate: true` and nothing sets `status: 'error'`, because ordering is the policy working.

**Ranked — five of the seven agents that consult the report, each ordering BEFORE its own cap** (same rule as the hold, and for the same reason): `agents/performance-engine` (inside `heldAware`, the one chokepoint all four pickers pass through, against each picker's own cap of 3/2/1/3 — the cross-picker concatenation order, flops > quick-wins > meta > legacy, is a deliberate source priority and is left alone), `agents/legacy-rebuilder`, `agents/blocked-post-resolver`, `agents/refresh-runner`, and `agents/meta-optimizer` (in the pure `agents/meta-optimizer/lib/hold.js`, whose `sortByValidation` order is blind to what a cluster earns). One behaviour change rode along: **`performance-engine`'s `pickMetaRewrites` used to `break` at `MAX_META` inside its loop**, so its single meta slot went to whichever low-CTR query GSC happened to list first; it now collects every eligible candidate before the hold and the cap, which also makes its held count mean the same thing it means in the three sibling pickers.

**Deliberately NOT ranked, and skipped rather than wired as dead code.** `agents/indexing-fixer`: its `crawled_not_indexed` → content-quality list has **no per-run cap** — every actionable post is refreshed whatever order it is in — so an ordering there changes nothing. `tests/agents/cluster-efficiency-wiring.test.js` pins both halves of that: that the agent does not import the ranking, and that no `.slice()` cap has appeared on the list (if one ever does, the agent has to be ranked). `agents/queue-autoapply`: it drains **oldest-first**, which is already an anti-starvation policy, over work another agent has already decided to do; reordering an approval queue by cluster is a different decision with its own blast radius. A single-slug CLI invocation is never reordered anywhere, for the same reason it is never held.

**`proven_dud` blocks content and DELETES briefs, so it holds to the hold's evidence bar — this was a live defect until 2026-08-23.** `proven_dud` is not just an input to the spend hold. It is a hard block in four places, none written to be reversible: `agents/content-strategist` silently drops LLM proposals in that cluster and clears existing calendar items on re-plan; `agents/calendar-runner` blocks work items from ever being drafted; `agents/queue-autoapply` dismisses collection-gaps; and `lib/brief-triage.js`, reached from `scripts/triage-orphan-briefs.mjs --drop-non-earning --apply`, **removed the brief files from disk**. Three of those four read `classifyClusters` DIRECTLY — so the corroboration that keeps the spend hold honest never applied to the paths that destroy work. The deletion path was strictly *less* evidence-hungry than the pause path. All four now read **`corroboratedClassification(loadClusterHold(...))`** from `lib/cluster-hold.js`, which downgrades any uncorroborated `proven_dud` to `unproven` and keeps the reason; a test pins that none of them calls `classifyClusters` itself. Since 2026-08-23 `classifyClusters` is also strictly harder to satisfy than it was: it refuses to call anything a dud without a product-revenue reading AND the judging window's order count, so a report written before `clusters_product_wide` existed — which is exactly what sits on the server the moment before this deploys — switches the gate off rather than falling back to the entry-page number.

**Dropping a brief is a MOVE, not a delete — because the guard above arrived one run too late.** Between 2026-08-19 15:00 UTC and 2026-08-23 13:17 UTC the taxonomy bug fixed in PR #624 had `soap` stamped `proven_dud`, `--drop-non-earning --apply` ran, and `unlinkSync` **permanently destroyed three paid-for briefs**: `vegan-soap.json`, `oatmeal-soap.json` and `coconut-oil-soap-benefits.json` (probably a fourth, `soap-making.json`, undatable). `lib/brief-archive.js` replaces `unlinkSync` with a move into **`data/briefs/_dropped/`**, plus a `<slug>.drop.json` sidecar recording when, why, the cluster verdict and its evidence string verbatim, which seo-impact report it came from, and which run did it. **Why `_dropped/` is invisible to the pipeline, and why that is tested.** All six readers of `data/briefs/` do a **non-recursive** `readdirSync` filtered on `.endsWith('.json')` (`blog-post-writer`, `content-researcher`, `gsc-opportunity`, `unmapped-query-promoter`, `seo-reporter`, the triage script), and the per-slug `existsSync(join(BRIEFS_DIR, '<slug>.json'))` checks in `calendar-runner`, `rank-tracker`, `post-performance` and the dashboard cannot see into a subdirectory at all. So a directory with no `.json` suffix cannot be re-read, re-briefed or re-counted as coverage. That is a convention six files happen to share, not something the language enforces — `tests/lib/briefs-dir-readers.test.js` scans the source and fails on a seventh reader without the filter or an existing one switched to a recursive walk. (A source scan rather than a behavioural test because importing `agents/*/index.js` RUNS the agent.)

**No retention sweep, deliberately.** A brief is ~25 KB of JSON; the 73-brief orphan backlog is 1.8 MB and ten thousand drops would be ~250 MB against the server's ~9.9 GB free. The disk outage that cost four days of cron was GB-scale Amazon dumps, not JSON. Nothing prunes `_dropped/`; every `--apply` run measures it and says so in the digest past 256 MB, so it can become a known problem but never a silent one. It is **not gitignored** for the same reason: an untracked archive is the condition that made the original loss unrecoverable.

**The thresholds, RE-DERIVED for the product basis (2026-08-23). Show the working or do not move them.** Under the entry-page basis a $0 was a direct function of the cluster's own clicks — no clicks, no entry-page revenue — so clicks had to carry the whole statistical load. Product revenue does not work that way: a category earns from carts that entered anywhere, on any channel, so a $0 product reading is **stronger** evidence than a $0 entry-page reading and the load moves off clicks entirely. Measured inputs, all production, 2026-08-21:

```
90-day window:  50 all-channel orders, 26 organic, $2,435.45 line subtotal
28-day window:  18 all-channel orders,  8 organic, 1,067 organic sessions
per-organic-click conversion: 8/1067 = 0.75%
smallest category that genuinely sells: lip balm, 4 orders / 90d
```

**A note on that rate, because it moved between two consecutive reports and the numbers below depend on it.** The 2026-08-22 report read 1,067 organic sessions against 8 conversions (0.75%); the 2026-08-23 report read 984 against the same 8 (0.81%). Every derivation below uses the **lower** figure, which is the conservative side for a bar that must be high enough — at 0.81% the click precondition would come out at ~115 rather than 125. `lib/cluster-revenue.js` and `tests/lib/cluster-revenue.test.js` both use 0.75% and the test recomputes it rather than asserting the constant, so a real move in this rate fails the test rather than silently ageing the bar.

**The materiality floor, restated as a RATE.** The old rule carried the operator's "materially a channel" notion as one average order *per window*, which is circular — it is 1 by definition however long the window is. As a rate it becomes a real null hypothesis, and it is what every number below rests on: **a category we would refuse to condemn sells at least ONE AVERAGE ORDER PER 28 DAYS.**

**`JUDGING_WINDOW_DAYS` = 90.** Over a W-day window such a category expects W/28 orders, so P(it records zero) = e^−(W/28): **28d → 36.8%** (a coin toss and a half), 60d → 11.8%, **84d → 5.0%**, **90d → 4.0%**. So 84 days is the minimum honest span and 90 is what the report already computes. **This answers "which window makes a $0 verdict honest": the 90-day one, and the report's own 28 days never can.** Toothpaste is the demonstration — $0 at 28 days, $71.50 at 90. The 28-day window remains the report's primary view for everything else; it is simply not evidence for a $0 verdict. (Belt and braces: the 28-day window carries 18 orders, itself below `MIN_WINDOW_ORDERS`, so even a caller that passed it could not produce a dud.)

**`MIN_WINDOW_ORDERS` = 45** (was 5, and counted *organic* orders in the 28-day window — the wrong denominator for a number built from all-channel line items). The same statement in orders rather than days, because days and orders decouple if store volume moves, and because this is the only guard against a degenerate order pull reading as $0 in *every* category at once. A floor-rate category takes share s of the store's orders: s = (1/28d) ÷ (50/90d) = 0.03571/0.55556 = **6.43%**. It is absent from N orders with probability (1−s)^N: N=25 → 19.0%, **N=45 → 5.0%**, N=50 → 3.6% (the observed window — it passes, barely). **Eight orders of headroom is deliberate: a slow quarter should switch this gate OFF, not condemn on thinner evidence.**

**`MIN_CLICKS` = 125, and its JOB HAS CHANGED** (was 400). It is no longer the statistical evidence — the window is — it is the *"had a fair shot"* **precondition**: a category whose content nobody has read has not been tested, and stamping it a dud is how a new category never gets a chance. Calibrated on the same measured rate: a cluster with k clicks in the 28-day GSC window sees ~3.214k over the 90-day judging window, expecting 3.214k × 0.0075 = 0.0241k orders from its OWN pages, so k = 125 is where that expectation reaches 3 — the same 5% point. **Lower than 400 is correct rather than lax**, because clicks no longer carry the load alone; it is also honest to record that the measured click→category-sales relationship is far weaker than the model in *both* directions (toothpaste turned 663 clicks into 5 orders where the model predicts 30; lip balm turned 6 clicks into 4 orders), so treat 125 as a floor on exposure and not as a probability statement. The probability statement lives in the window now. **A side effect worth having:** the soap incident is now reproducible in tests at its real 227 clicks instead of a fixture inflated to 500 to clear the old bar.

**`MIN_PAGES` = 5, unchanged — and re-examined rather than carried over.** Under either basis it has no statistical content; one anomalous page could never have produced the $0 under the product basis anyway, since the $0 comes from the order pool and not from any page. It is a structural floor on "this category has content", and at 125 clicks it also asserts ~25 clicks per page — enough that the pages are indexed and read.

**Honest labelling, because the name is what caused the misreading.** `clusters[].entry_page_organic_revenue` is the canonical field (`revenue` is kept as a back-compatible alias holding the same number, and `classifyClusters` prefers the canonical). `lib/seo-impact.js`'s `residualRollup` adds the **residual row** the table never had — `$77.94 — no cluster (homepage / cart / subscription)` on the 2026-08-22 report, $1,152.50 / 54% of the store over 90 days. $462.14 clustered + $77.94 residual = $540.08 = `totals.organic_revenue`, exactly. The headings that oversold it are gone: the markdown section is "Entry-page organic revenue by cluster — the LANDING-PAGE view" with the caveat inline.

**Product-level cluster attribution — built 2026-08-23, and it is a SECOND number, not a replacement.** A landing page and a product category are different things and one figure cannot mean both, so the report now answers each question at the granularity it is actually asked at: **pages keep entry-page credit, clusters gain product credit.** `lib/product-cluster-revenue.js` rolls up raw order **line items** (`price × quantity` less allocated discounts) by `clusterForText` on the product title, and `clusters[]` carries `product_organic_revenue`, `product_organic_revenue_delta`, `product_revenue_all_channels`, `product_orders_all_channels` and `organic_share` **alongside** the untouched `entry_page_organic_revenue` / `revenue` / `revenueDelta` / `clicks` / `pages`. A 90-day companion view lands in `clusters_product_wide`, and `cluster_product_meta` states the basis, both windows and the reconciliation identity.

Measured on the 28 days to 2026-08-21 (18 orders / $1,079.46), entry-page → product-organic → product-all-channels: `lotion` $313.49 → $357.00 → $755.30; `soap` $62.40 → $62.40 → $123.50; `lip balm` $48.00 → $60.00 → $96.00; `deodorant` $38.25 → $36.00 → $46.50; `toothpaste` $0 → $0 → $0 (but $71.50 over 90 days). The residual tells the story: entry-page leaves **$77.94 on `/`, `/cart/...` and subscription orders**, while the product side leaves **$0.00** — every line item in 200 days of orders maps to a product cluster except a $0.00 "Cut and Scrape" freebie.

Four decisions are baked in and should not be quietly reversed:

1. **It sums to LINE SUBTOTALS, not order totals.** Shipping and tax are order-level; allocating them across categories would be invention. The gap ($24.68 organic / $58.16 all channels that window) is *reported* as `non_product_revenue_*`, never reconciled away. So these figures do **not** tie to `totals.organic_revenue` and are not meant to. `discount_allocations` is the only discount source — line `total_discount` is already inside it, and adding both understates every discounted line (verified: line revenue reconciles to `subtotal_price` to the cent on all 131 orders in a 200-day pull).
2. **Both channel views ship, and the organic one is well defined.** Channel is a property of the ORDER (entry page + referrer), and that classification never depended on the entry page matching a cluster — so a soap bought in a homepage-entered organic session *is* organic soap revenue here, which is exactly the case entry-page attribution loses. Organic-only keeps the row comparable to the headline and to the prior window; all-channel is what "did this category sell" actually means and is the only view with enough orders to judge on. Showing both is the same organic-vs-all-channels pairing the weekly trend and the store-context section already use — one BASIS per row, two channels, both labelled.
3. **The report window stays 28 days; the 90-day view is NOT a companion any more — it is the table the $0-cluster gate judges on.** At ~0.5 orders/day a category's slice of 28 days is one or two orders, so a $0 there is noise: a genuinely-selling category records zero 37% of the time at 28 days and 4% at 90. `toothpaste` reads $0 at 28 days and $71.50 at 90. `clusters_product_wide[]` therefore carries BOTH sources of the hold rule for that one window — `product_revenue_all_channels` (A) and `entry_page_organic_revenue` (B) — and `lib/cluster-hold.js` reads that array and nothing else; an empty one means the gate holds nothing. `WIDE_WINDOW_DAYS` is re-exported from `lib/cluster-revenue.js`'s `JUDGING_WINDOW_DAYS`, so the two cannot drift. It costs no extra Shopify call — the trend's 84-day pull was widened to 90 and serves all of it.
4. **Bundles are expanded, not title-matched.** Four of the ten live bundles (`The 90-Day Clean Swap`, `Head-to-Toe`, `The Clean Swap`, `Gift Box`) match **no** cluster by title and would fall into the residual, and `The 90-Day Coconut Reset` matches `coconut oil` while its contents are entirely lotion. A bundle line is split across its `config/bundles.json` components pro rata by list value (`qty × the component's price in data/brand/product-catalog.json`), per VARIANT where variants differ (`hand-soap-set`'s "4 pumps" is soap-only; "3 pumps + body lotion" is not). Falls back to quantity share with no catalogue, and to title clustering with no config. Note `Sensitive Skin Moisturizing Set` is **not** cross-cluster — both components are lotion — so it credits lotion either way.

**The gate WAS migrated, in a second change on the same day, and one consumer deliberately was not.** `lib/cluster-revenue.js`, `lib/cluster-hold.js`, `calendar-runner`, `content-strategist`, `queue-autoapply`, `lib/brief-triage.js` and `scripts/cluster-holds.mjs` all judge on the product figure now (via `corroboratedClassification`, never `classifyClusters` directly). `clusters[]`'s `entry_page_organic_revenue` / `revenue` / `revenueDelta` are still emitted byte-for-byte and are still what answers "which page earns"; the gate simply stopped asking that field a question it could not answer. `clusters[]` may contain a row for a category with product sales and no clustered entry page (`clicks: 0, pages: 0`); that is safe by construction — with no clicks and no pages such a row can only ever be `earning` or `unproven`, never a dud, whatever `MIN_CLICKS` is.

**`lib/ad-brief-score.js` is the explicit deferral.** Its `scoreCommercial` still reads `c.revenue` — the 28-day entry-page figure — and that is now a stated decision rather than an omission, pinned by a test that fails if the product fields start being read. Two reasons: (1) its revenue component **saturates at $200 in the window**, which entry-page figures rarely reach and product figures usually do (soap: $62.40 vs $324.85; deodorant: $38.25 vs $165.00), so swapping the field alone would push most categories straight to the 20/20 cap and flatten the very component meant to discriminate — the ceiling has to be re-derived in the same change; (2) this score only **ranks** briefs and never kills one, so a stale basis costs ordering, not work. A silent scoring reshuffle would have been worse than a stated deferral.

**The corroboration machinery went partly redundant the moment product attribution existed, and that was RESOLVED rather than collapsed.** The old cross-check (`productRevenueByFamily`, reading `topProducts[]`) answered the same question as the new primary by the same join key, and was demonstrably wrong because of the top-5-per-day cap — toothpaste $39.00/90d where raw line items say $71.50. The tempting move was to delete the second source and run on the better number alone; that would have destroyed the property that stopped a $324.85 category being condemned, since one number cannot contradict itself. Instead the roles **swapped**: raw line items became the primary, and entry-page organic revenue — the field that used to be the verdict — became the cross-check, because it is the one thing on hand that answers a genuinely different question by a genuinely different join key. `topProducts[]` was retired outright. Two sources in, two sources out; see the cluster-hold section above for the full table and for why "same file, two fields" is not the same as one source.

**A calendar re-plan REPLACES the calendar, so every cleared item has to be named.** `agents/content-strategist` writes `data/calendar/calendar.json` from the brief queue it just extracted; anything the planner does not re-propose, or that one of its filters drops, simply stops existing. Between 2026-08-19 and 2026-08-21 that cleared **12 of the 19 scheduled items** on the calendar, and the only trace anywhere was eight `[SKIP]` lines in `data/reports/scheduler/scheduler.log` — an unattended cron log nobody reads. The real attribution: **4** dropped by the already-covered check, **3** by the soap `proven_dud` verdict (since fixed, see above), **4** never re-proposed but with a live post — finished work the calendar had gone on carrying — and **1**, `travel-size-deodorant`, never re-proposed with no post and no filter: a real loss, and the only one that mattered. Five of the twelve had no log line of any kind.

The rule and the reconciliation now live in the pure `lib/calendar-coverage.js`. **The check itself had two defects, both of which made it drop items it should have kept**; both are reproduced against real production fixtures in `tests/lib/calendar-coverage.test.js`, and every change here makes the check LESS aggressive, never more — a coverage check that over-fires silently kills planned content, which is the failure being fixed.

1. **The published pool counted posts that were never published.** It was every `data/posts/<slug>/meta.json` carrying a `target_keyword`, with no check that anything had been written. `agents/blog-post-writer` writes that file at **draft** time, so from the moment `calendar-runner` starts drafting an item, the item's own keyword sits in the "published" pool — and the next re-plan drops the item as `already covered` by the post generated FROM it, same slug, same keyword. `coveragePool` now applies the written-or-published rule `scripts/triage-orphan-briefs.mjs` has always applied (`content.html` on disk **or** a `shopify_article_id`, the latter for the legacy corpus synced in without local content) and prints the excluded scaffolds rather than silently believing them. On the server 2026-08-23 that is 201 post dirs → 150 real coverage, 2 excluded (`toothpaste-without-sls-what-to-know-best-options`, `best-soap-for-tattoos-what-to-use-for-safe-healing` — both consolidation leftovers whose content was merged away), 49 with no `target_keyword` at all. Note what this did **not** cause: all four already-covered clearings above were against genuinely live pages, so the pool fix does not change those verdicts. It closes a structurally guaranteed defect, not the one that fired.
2. **The calendar self-match exemption was defeated by a tie.** The old rule let `findSemanticDuplicate` pick one winner and then compared *that winner* to the proposal (`dup !== keyword`). `findSemanticDuplicate` breaks ties with `>`, so the first entry to score 1.0 wins. The live calendar carries "sls-free toothpaste", "toothpaste sls free" and "toothpaste no sls", whose core token sets are identical — Jaccard **1.0** to each other — so re-proposing any one of them matched its twin instead of itself and the already-scheduled item was cleared as a duplicate. `findCoverage` now asks "is this exact keyword already scheduled?" and answers **before** any similarity is computed.

**Visibility is the other half, and it is the half that lets this class of bug hide.** `classifyClearedItems` reconciles the calendar before a re-plan against the calendar after it and gives every disappeared item a reason — the filter that dropped it, or `completed` when no filter fired but its post is live, or **`not_reproposed`**, the unexplained loss. A skip is joined to an item three ways in order (the proposal's slug, the proposal's keyword, and the slug of the POST it collided with), because the 2026-08-19 run dropped a proposal called "deodorant without aluminum" against post `aluminum-free-deodorant` and thereby cleared the calendar item `aluminum-free-deodorant`; matching on the proposal alone files that as "no reason recorded". Rows are printed naming the **page** (`data/posts/<slug>`) and the rule, not just a keyword — `[SKIP] already covered: "x" ~ existing "x"` could not distinguish a live competitor page from the item's own draft — and a clearing that matched the post at the item's own slug is flagged `SELF-MATCH: that post is this item`. One **deferred** `notify()` per re-plan that cleared anything, never `immediate: true`; `status: 'warning'` only when something was lost without a reason, because tidying finished work off the calendar is the policy working.

**Deliberately NOT changed: the matching itself.** `agents/gsc-opportunity`'s `loadCoveredKeywords` reads a single `target_keyword` per post and `isMapped` does bidirectional substring against it, which produces false negatives in the other direction — the live tattoo winner's target keyword is "best soap to use on new tattoo", which neither contains nor is contained by any of the eight tattoo queries it actually ranks for, so all eight were promoted as "unmapped" new topics (`scripts/consolidate-tattoo-soap-calendar-2026-08-23.mjs`). Widening coverage to the queries a page really ranks for (`data/keyword-index.json`, GSC) would fix that — and would make every coverage check in the fleet **more aggressive**, which is precisely how planned content gets killed silently. It is a separate change with its own blast radius, and it should not ride along with the fix for over-firing.

**Zigpoll on-site surveys — the fourth voice-of-customer source, and the only one that hears from a buyer who never wrote a review.** `lib/zigpoll.js` is the REST client (`https://v1.zigpoll.com`, cursor-paginated, `Authorization: <key>` with **no `Bearer` prefix**), reading `ZIGPOLL_API_KEY` from `.env`. It is best-effort exactly like Tavily and DataForSEO: a missing key or a failed request degrades the corpus to `partial: true` rather than failing the monthly run.

**Do not reach for the MCP server here.** Zigpoll also ships one at `https://mcp.zigpoll.com/mcp`, and it is the right tool for interactive work — 49 tools, 26 of them read-only analytics the REST API does not expose (`analyze_dropoff`, `correlate_slides`, `get_verbatims`, `analyze_shopify_line_items`). But it authenticates with a per-user OAuth token in the operator's macOS Keychain, which nothing on the production cron box can read. **Anything unattended uses the REST client and a dashboard-issued private key.** That key can only be minted from the Zigpoll dashboard UI (Integrations → Private Keys); there is no API for it and the MCP exposes no tool for it.

**A bad or missing key returns `422`, not `401`** — verified 2026-08-24 against `/me`, `/responses` and a path that does not exist, all three. Auth is rejected before routing, so a 422 tells you nothing about whether your path or parameters are right, and a probe with a bogus key cannot validate an endpoint. `lib/zigpoll.js` says so in the thrown message.

**Three shape traps, all established by calling the live API rather than reading the docs.** (1) `metadata.shopify_line_items` is a **comma-separated STRING** of variant titles, not an array — and titles carry `|` and `-` of their own, so comma is the only usable separator. (2) `response` holds the option's own TITLE for a fixed-choice vote, which is our copy and worthless as customer voice; **`valueType` is the discriminator** (`vote` vs `dynamic-response`), and the docs' line that a write-in "counts as a vote" is about which `filter` it matches, NOT its `valueType` — all 17 write-ins on this account are `dynamic-response`. Do **not** screen free text by comparing it against the answer options' titles: a real customer typed "Google" into the Other box on a survey that already offered Google, and that rule would have silently discarded the verbatim. (3) **The envelope is not uniform across endpoints** — `/responses` returns `{ data, hasNextPage, endCursor }`, but `/accounts` returns a **bare array** and `/me` a bare object. Only the paginated endpoints wrap in `data`. Assuming otherwise is how `resolveAccountId` passed every unit test and then threw on its first live call; the stub had been written to match the assumption rather than the API.

**Scoping is by ORDER, through `clusterForText`, not by handle.** Zigpoll records a product title and never a handle, so `filterSkinCluster` cannot see these records — the same split `lib/demand-questions.js` documents for GSC leaks, and the same fix: free text goes through `assignCluster` (here via `clusterForText`, the wrapper `lib/product-cluster-revenue.js` already applies to Shopify line-item titles). `ZIGPOLL_CLUSTERS` is `lotion`, `soap` and the `coconut oil` ingredient fallback — `SKIN_CLUSTER_HANDLES` restated in the cluster vocabulary. **Deodorant, lip balm and toothpaste are excluded because voice-of-customer is scoped to the skin cluster, NOT because they earn nothing** — toothpaste earns $71.50/90d and is no longer held, and re-justifying this list on revenue would make it drift the moment the revenue does. A mixed order counts as in-scope when ANY line is. A response with **no order attached** (exit-intent, cart) is dropped and counted separately from an out-of-scope one, because those two numbers mean different things; when those surveys have volume, scope them by `metadata.url` rather than by an order they will never have.

**What it is good for, and what it is not.** The live post-purchase survey is mostly "how did you hear about us?", so it is the best evidence in the corpus for `trigger_points` and for how buyers FIND us, and thin evidence for objections — these people had already bought when they wrote it. The prompt says exactly that, so the model does not weigh a two-word channel name as a considered opinion. Measured on the 17 open-ended answers live on 2026-08-24: **Amazon ≈29%** (including "Originally found on Amazon and then wanted to support you on your own actual site"), **AI assistants ≈18%** ("Claude", "Chatgpt", "Chat" — independent corroboration of the AI-assistant channel), Google and generic search ≈24%, repeat buyers ≈18%. Directional at n=17, and the fixed-choice options that would have captured the first two did not exist until they were added that day — an "Other" bucket holding 17 of 41 votes was the finding.

**Demand mining — top-of-funnel questions voice-of-customer structurally can't reach.** `agents/demand-miner` runs monthly, immediately after `voice-of-customer` in the same scheduler block — it reads `data/context/personas.json`, which `voice-of-customer` rewrites in that same run, so running it first would seed from last month's personas. The pure logic lives in `lib/demand-questions.js` (no I/O, same split as `lib/voice-of-customer.js`, testable without stubbing): it seeds Google People-Also-Ask + related-search harvesting from two empirical sources — GSC impression leaks and persona objections — under a hard `SEED_CAP` of 40, because each seed is one paid DataForSEO SERP call made unattended from cron, and an uncapped run turns a bad GSC week into hundreds of unauthorized calls. `--limit <n>` (read in the shell, not a change to `SEED_CAP`) trims a run to fewer seeds for a cheap rehearsal, clamped so it can never raise the cap above 40. The cap is split as a **reserve per origin** (`LEAK_RESERVE`/`PERSONA_RESERVE`, 20 each), not a single ordered slice: a real production run yielded 253 impression leaks against the 40-seed cap, and taking leaks first would fill the whole budget from leaks alone — starving `persona_objection` to zero and leaving `persona_id` null on every question, silently breaking the join to `personas.json` on `stage` + `persona_id` that this artifact exists for. That same join is why `AWARENESS_LEVELS`, the five-stage funnel vocabulary, is **imported from `lib/voice-of-customer.js` in `lib/demand-questions.js`, never redefined** — a second copy would drift and break the join the same way. Persona objections stay lotion+soap only by construction (`personas.json` IS voice-of-customer's `SKIN_CLUSTER_HANDLES` research), but GSC leaks are unfiltered site-wide queries — `lib/demand-questions.js`'s `filterLeaksToSkinCluster` (reusing `lib/keyword-index/cluster.js`'s ordered `assignCluster`, not `voice-of-customer.js`'s handle-based `filterSkinCluster`, which doesn't fit free-text queries) keeps only `SKIN_LEAK_CLUSTERS`-cluster leaks before `deriveSeeds` ever sees them — `lotion`, `soap`, `coconut oil`, `deodorant` and `lip balm`, always excluding `toothpaste` (≈268 clicks / $0 revenue, Prime Directive) and `hair` (RSC sells no hair products) — because roughly a third of a real leak report's top entries were oral-care queries (e.g. "coconut oil as toothpaste") that would otherwise be mined on a paid budget for a cluster that already earns $0. The artifact's `clusters` field (sourced straight from `SKIN_LEAK_CLUSTERS`, never hardcoded) is an **array**, not the single `cluster: "skin"` string this used to stamp: deodorant and lip balm were added to the leak-mineable set on 2026-08-22, and no one-word label describes lotion+soap+coconut-oil+deodorant+lip-balm honestly. That array only bounds leak-origin seeds, though — persona-origin seeds stay lotion+soap regardless of what `clusters` lists, so a reader has to check each question's own `seed_origin`, not the header, to know which scope actually produced it. Both `demand-questions.json` and `demand-questions.md` carry that caveat inline rather than assuming a reader works it out. `demand-miner` is also the **fifth reader** of `personas.json` (`realApplyPersonaOverlay`): it applies `data/context/operator-angles.json`'s overlay before `sanitizePersonas`, same order and same reasoning as the other four readers documented under `data/context/operator-angles.json` below — skipping it would let a retired persona angle keep consuming a paid seed while an operator-authored replacement is never seeded. The agent refuses to write `demand-questions.{json,md}` when a run produces zero questions, leaving the previous artifact in place: this covers both "every seed's SERP call failed" and the more dangerous case where every call succeeded but returned no PAA/related-search items, which would otherwise look like a clean run while silently replacing good data with nothing. The one Claude call classifying every harvested question by funnel stage merges its response back onto records **positionally, by the `[n]` index the prompt assigns** — never by echoing question text, which broke on a curly apostrophe, a retained `[n]` prefix, or collapsed whitespace — and retries once on malformed/schema-invalid output (so "one Claude call" is up to two per run), but a `stop_reason: 'max_tokens'` response throws immediately without retrying, because truncated JSON cannot become valid on a second attempt against the same ceiling. Upstream, `lib/dataforseo.js`'s `getSerpResults` now also returns `paa` and `relatedSearches` alongside the original `organic`/`serpFeatures` — **additive only**. Its item-shaping logic was extracted into an exported pure `extractSerpPayload(items)` so it's testable without a network call, and all nine existing production callers destructure only `organic`/`serpFeatures` off the same return object, so they are unaffected. `agents/gsc-query-miner` writes `data/reports/gsc-query-miner/impression-leaks.json` on **every** run, including empty ones, so a consumer's staleness guard can tell "ran, found nothing" from "did not run" — the same reasoning `untapped-candidates.json` already documents.

**Publish state has one source of truth, because "no status" is not "not published".** `lib/post-publish-state.js` (`resolvePublishStatus` / `isLivePost` / `isLiveOrScheduled`) answers "is this post live?" for the whole fleet. **52 of 93 posts carry a `shopify_article_id` and no `shopify_status` key at all** — the legacy corpus, synced in by `scripts/sync-legacy-posts.js` without ever passing through `agents/publisher`, which is what stamps that field. Every consumer requiring a strict `shopify_status === 'published'` therefore treated live, indexed, traffic-earning pages as unpublished: `post-performance`, `refresh-runner`, `publish-drift` and `draft-refresher` could not see them at all, and the 5 AM digest reported three of them as "Action Required — hard-blocked" every morning from 2026-08-16 while all three returned HTTP 200. The helper is deliberately **not a third rule** — it is the inference `agents/indexing-checker` and `agents/legacy-triage` had each already hand-rolled (explicit status wins; otherwise an article id plus a parseable publish date decides published vs scheduled), written once. `'unknown'` is returned rather than guessed, and callers must treat unknown as not-live. **The real fix is `scripts/backfill-shopify-status.mjs`** — it asks LIVE Shopify per article and writes the true status down (dry by default, `--apply` to write). Shopify has no `status` field on an article: `published_at` is the whole story (null = draft, future = scheduled, past = live). A 404 writes **nothing** — a deleted article is not a draft, and stamping it as one buries the real problem under a plausible field. The helper is the safety net for whatever the backfill cannot resolve, and for posts synced in later.

**`data/posts/<slug>/content.html` is a MIRROR of a live article body, and 27 of 89 of them are a different article (2026-08-23).** `agents/publisher` replaces `body_html` with that file whenever `meta.shopify_article_id` is set, so a drifted mirror is a queued overwrite of a live page. PR #645 noticed in passing that two of the five files it edited were not stale copies but *wholly different, older articles*. Measured properly against live Shopify — read-only, all 203 articles, 89 comparable posts — it is far worse than two of five:

| | count | what it is |
|---|--:|---|
| identical | **0** | not one local file matches its live body byte for byte |
| cosmetic | **7** | text-identical; only markup differs (the restyled product CTA) |
| divergent | **55** | a real, recognisable edit apart — 51 of them share ≥85% of their text blocks |
| **different article** | **27** | share **under a quarter** of their text blocks with what is live |

**The fuse is `scheduler.js`'s daily link-repair step**, which repairs links in the local file and republishes it with `--force` for any post already on Shopify — unattended, every morning. On any of those 27 that silently replaces a live, indexed page with an older draft. Four callers push this file (`scheduler.js:121`, `agents/refresh-runner`, `agents/calendar-runner`, `pipeline.js`) and none of them looked at what was already there.

**The fix is to REFUSE, not to resync — and refusing is the whole decision.** `lib/content-mirror.js` is the pure comparison; `agents/publisher`'s update branch reads the live body first and refuses when the push would replace the page rather than edit it. **`--force` does not disarm it** — `--force` is what the unattended caller already passes, and a gate its routine caller turns off is not a gate; the override is `--allow-divergent-mirror`, typed by a human who has looked at both bodies. An **unreadable** live article refuses too, the same call `lib/post-lock.js` makes for an unreadable lock. A create (no article id) and an empty live body are allowed — there is nothing to destroy.

**A blanket resync would have been wrong, and the reason is not caution.** `content.html` is also the INPUT to legitimate work: `agents/refresh-runner` writes a refreshed draft over it and then publishes, so a file *ahead* of live is a normal state for the minutes between those two steps, and overwriting from live there destroys a paid LLM rewrite. Nor is stale-vs-ahead reliably answerable: 80 of the 82 divergent files were last touched by one bulk-import commit, every working tree is clean, and mtimes under `data/posts/` are checkout times rather than edit times (the same reason `blocked-post-resolver` fingerprints an editor report instead of stat-ing it). What content alone *can* say is the **direction of containment**, and on 2026-08-23 it said the same thing every time: no post had local ⊇ live, and the pipeline's genuine work-in-progress lives under its own filename (`content-refreshed.html`, 6 present) rather than in the mirror. **That was evidence about one corpus on one day, not a law, and it stopped being true the next day** — re-measured 2026-08-24, `best-toothpaste-for-sensitive-teeth-2025` came back `local-superset` (1 local-only block, similarity 0.991, no live-only block at all). So a blanket resync is still wrong, and the containment test is re-derived per post rather than quoted from the survey. See the reconciler below, which holds that post rather than overwriting it.

**Reconciling the mirrors is a SEPARATE, `--apply`-gated script, and it holds four posts back — `scripts/reconcile-content-mirrors.mjs` / `npm run reconcile-content-mirrors`, 2026-08-24.** Direction is live → local: Shopify is authoritative for published content, which `scripts/remediate-live-post.js` and `scripts/regate-live-posts.js` already assume when they pull `body_html` down over `content.html`. Dry by default; every file is copied to `data/posts/<slug>/backups/content-reconcile-<stamp>.html` before it is overwritten, and the backup is kept even when the write is rolled back. It touches `content.html` and nothing else — `content-refreshed.html`, `meta.json`, `editor-report.md` and the rest of the post directory are never written, moved or deleted. The decision logic is the pure `lib/content-reconcile.js`, so every hold is a case a test constructs rather than a claim in a comment.

**Default scope is exactly what the checker flags** — the `different-article` tier and the 0.25–0.75 warn band, its exit 2 and exit 1. The ~50 ordinary divergent mirrors above 0.75 have drifted too, but they are recognisably the same article and a republish there edits rather than replaces the page; sweeping them is `--all`, an explicit choice. **A HOLD is reported whatever the scope**, because the one genuinely-ahead post sits *above* the warn band and scoping holds would be the single arrangement that hides it.

**Four holds, each from something that would actually have broken.** `live-empty` (nothing to copy down). `local-ahead` (live ⊆ local — the containment test above, re-run per post). `pinned-mirror` — the file is a byte-pinned fixture of a committed health-claim remediation plan. `faq-regression` — decided AFTER the write and rolled back, see below.

**Injected JSON-LD is load-bearing for a PAID rebuild, and nothing about the mirror says so.** `agents/legacy-rebuilder` runs daily from `scheduler.js` at `--limit 5 --apply`, and its `isLegacy(slug)` reads `content.html` and asks whether the body carries any injected JSON-LD at all (`lib/injected-schema.js`'s `hasInjectedSchema`; it was literally `!html.includes('FAQPage')` until 2026-08-24 — see the retired-schema-types section below for why that key had to move). **36 mirrors carry schema their live article does not** — it was injected locally and the push never happened — so a plain copy-down hands that agent 36 fresh "legacy" posts and it starts paying for full pipeline rebuilds of live pages, unattended, five a day. Nobody asked for that and nothing would have said it was happening. So a mirror that had JSON-LD gets `agents/schema-injector` re-run on it (never with `--apply`; local write only, no Shopify call), which rebuilds the schema **from the new prose** — strictly better than preserving the old blocks, since a different-article mirror's schema describes an article the live page is not. The check is a **verify, not a prediction**: what the injector emits is the injector's business and a second copy of that decision would drift, so each post is written, re-injected, then checked, and one that comes back with no JSON-LD is restored from its backup and held. **Idempotence depends on this**: "in sync" is judged with JSON-LD stripped from BOTH sides, so the mirror a run leaves behind — live prose plus regenerated schema live lacks — is in sync on the next run and a second `--apply` writes nothing. `compareBodies` ignores `<script>` entirely, so re-injected schema can never re-open the gate that was just closed. (The measured "35 of the 36 regain it, `best-natural-bar-soap-for-men` does not and is rolled back" is **no longer the shape of this check**: that post failed only because FAQPage needed 2+ question headings and it has one. The breadcrumb the injector emits now is unconditional, so every reconciled mirror regains schema and that hold should not fire again.)

**Everything else that reads `content.html` was audited and is unaffected — three of four suspected breakages measured ZERO.** Across all 89 comparable posts, live bodies introduce **0** stray `<h1>` (an `agents/editor` BLOCKER), **0** losses of the `class="rsc-featured-product"` sentinel `featured-product-injector` dedupes on (a double buy box), **0** cases of relative-only `/products/` hrefs (`agents/editor` parses with `new URL()` and files a relative href as non-product, which fires its `no_product_cta` BLOCKER → `needs_rebuild` → the publisher gate), and **0** `target="_blank"` without `rel="noopener"` (the one documented case of Shopify rewriting a link on the way in — `scripts/fix-target-blank.js`). Every CTA and FAQ HTML comment in the corpus is decorative; no code reads one. The reconciler does **not** re-run `agents/editor` afterwards, so `editor-report.md` is stale for every reconciled post and the run says so — `scripts/regate-live-posts.js --all` regenerates it. That is deliberate: the editor makes Claude calls and can set `meta.needs_rebuild`, and neither belongs inside a file reconciliation.

**The residual is 4 posts and it is honest, not a rounding error.** Dry run 2026-08-24: 34 considered, **30 reconcilable, 4 held** — which empties the different-article tier from 27 to 1 and the warn band from 5 to 1. Three of the four are pinned mirrors (`11-benefits-of-incorporating-tea-tree-oil-into-your-everyday-life`, `incorporating-vanilla-skin-care-into-your-beauty-regimen`, `why-choose-coconut-skin-care-products`), and the reason is worth stating precisely because the *content* verdict points the other way: **every one of the 15 `kind: 'file'` plan entries across both remediation scripts is ALREADY APPLIED TO LIVE** (verified per entry — live carries the AFTER at the expected count, and all 14 tea-tree `KEPT` excerpts are present live), so reconciling would leave the claims fixed on both sides. What it would also do is make 15 plan entries unfindable at once and fail the "every mirror BEFORE occurs exactly `expectedOccurrences` times" assertion in both plans' tests — and one entry, `mirror-coconut-why-antibacterial-stale-draft`, could never match again by literal at all, because that mirror is a different, older article than what is live. **Retiring compliance-plan entries is a compliance decision and does not ride along inside a mirror reconciliation.** The follow-up is small and belongs to whoever owns those plans: relax the two BEFORE-present assertions to the invariant the tea-tree test already uses (`before + after === expectedOccurrences` — "never a third value"), retire the dead coconut entry, then re-run the reconciler, which will then take all three. `PINNED_MIRROR_SLUGS` is pinned by a test against both plans' exported `PLAN`, so the list cannot drift from the plans it describes. The fourth hold is `best-toothpaste-for-sensitive-teeth-2025` (`local-ahead`), which is outside the default scope anyway. **All four residuals are safe**: the publisher already refuses the different-article one, and the warn band blocks nothing by design.

**The two thresholds are measured, not picked, and the working is in the module header.** Similarity is Dice over the set of normalized **text blocks** — paragraphs, list items, headings — because word n-grams are unusable here: at n=8 a lightly copy-edited *same* article scored 0.024, since one changed word breaks eight overlapping grams. Blocks are the unit an edit operates on and the score is checkable by hand ("these share 18 of their 20 paragraphs"). Blocks under 40 characters are dropped, or the product CTA every post carries makes unrelated articles look related. **`DIFFERENT_ARTICLE_MAX` is 0.25**: the worst genuinely-different pair measured 0.221, the next post up 0.324, and the *deepest legitimate rewrite* on record — `agents/content-refresher`'s own queued drafts against the file they were generated from — scored 0.775 (then 0.923, 0.932, 0.964, 0.985). So it sits inside a real gap and three times below a real refresh. **`DIVERGENT_WARN_MAX` is 0.75** and blocks nothing: the 0.25–0.75 band warns loudly and proceeds, because blocking it would stop a legitimate deep refresh, and this project has already destroyed three paid-for briefs by letting a gate decide work was worthless. Four posts sit in that band today.

**`npm run check-content-mirrors`** (`scripts/check-content-mirrors.mjs`) asks the same question over the whole corpus on demand — read-only, `--json`, `--slug <slug>`, exit 0/1/2/3 on the same vocabulary as `check-post-meta-drift.mjs`. **It is also on cron at 12:20 UTC through `scripts/check-content-mirror-drift.mjs`; see "The content-mirror drift gate" under Server Deployment.** It has **no resync mode in either direction**, and `--apply` without `--snapshot-live` is refused with exit 64 rather than running a report someone reads as an apply. `--snapshot-live --apply` captures the live bodies so a human can diff them; they land in `data/reports/content-mirror/` and **never inside `data/posts/<slug>/`**, because everything under a post directory is a pipeline input and a live body dropped there is the accidental resync the tool exists to prevent. `publishApprovedQueueItems()` inside the publisher is a stated **deferral**, not an omission: it pushes a queue item's `refreshed_html_path` rather than the mirror, and the live path for that work is `lib/queue-apply.js`, which already captures a pre-write backup and stamps a `revert_plan`.

**The winner lock guards the BODY, not the metadata — and for its whole life it guarded nothing at all.** `agents/legacy-triage` stamps `legacy_locked: true` on posts it buckets as winners (51 posts on production as of 2026-08-23, 50 of them `legacy_bucket: 'winner'`). Three agents were each supposed to honour it and each hand-rolled its own read. `agents/meta-optimizer`'s copy built the path as a **flat `data/posts/<handle>.json`** — the layout documented in `docs/signal-manifest.md`, and one this repo has not used for a long time (production: **0 flat files, 201 `data/posts/<slug>/meta.json`**). Its `readFileSync` therefore threw on every single post, its bare `catch { /* proceed */ }` swallowed the throw, and **the guard was inert from the day it shipped** while reading, in source, exactly like a working guard. `content-refresher` and `refresh-runner` had the same code against the correct path and did block. A fourth agent, `legacy-rebuilder`, routed on `legacy_bucket === 'winner'` and never read the lock at all — which is not the same thing: production carries `natural-soap-bar` at `legacy_locked: true` / `legacy_bucket: 'flop'`, so a re-triage that moves the bucket without clearing the lock sent a protected post through a **full pipeline rebuild**. The identical flat-path bug lived a second time in `agents/meta-ab-tracker`, where `meta` was always null and the `shopify_url` fallback could never fire.

All four now go through **`lib/post-lock.js`**, the single reader, which answers two named questions instead of exposing one boolean read three ways: **`mayRewriteBody()`** (blocked on a winner) and **`mayTestMetadata()`** (never blocked). That split is the point. Winner protection exists so a page that ranks is not *rewritten* into one that does not; a title/meta test does not touch the body, is two reversible fields, and is auto-reverted by `agents/meta-ab-checker` when CTR regresses past `lib/meta-ab-decision.js`'s 0.5pp dead-band. Blocking it meant a winner could never have its CTR improved — the main lever a winner has left, and the tattoo-soap winner (position #9, ~38k impressions, 0.62% CTR) was the site's single biggest CTR opportunity sitting unimprovable. **Judge a proposed change by whether it rewrites the body, not by whether it writes to Shopify.**

**Four lock states, because "I could not read it" is not "it is not locked".** `unlocked` and `no-post` allow; `locked` and `unreadable` refuse a body rewrite. `no-post` allows because the flag is only ever stamped on a local `meta.json` and agents keyed off live URLs routinely see articles with no local directory — refusing there would make the guard refuse everything it cannot see, the same silent stall wearing the opposite sign. `unreadable` refuses because the evidence exists and cannot be read, which is exactly when "might be a winner" has to win. Resolution goes through `resolvePostSlug()`; a Shopify article handle is **not** the local slug (the tattoo winner lives in `data/posts/best-soap-for-tattoos/` behind the handle `best-soap-for-tattoos-what-to-use-for-safe-healing`), and using the handle as a slug was the second half of the same bug. That resolver also gained a `shopify_handle`/`shopify_url` pass: **93 of 94 local metas carry neither `handle` nor `url`**, so almost every real post had been falling through to the prefix heuristic.

**Permitting a metadata test on a winner is only safe because auto-revert is, so two holes in auto-revert were closed in the same change.** (1) `meta-optimizer` wrote its A/B tracker **once, after the whole loop** — a crash mid-run left Shopify mutated with no baseline recorded, and an unrecorded mutation is one `meta-ab-checker` will never evaluate and never undo. It now writes after every applied change, and `mayTestMetadata().requiresAbTracking` (true for a locked or unreadable post) escalates a failed write to an `immediate: true` notification rather than the 5 AM digest. (2) The baseline and the measurement were **different metrics**: `baselineCtr` was the *keyword-level, 90-day* CTR that made the page a candidate, while the checker measures `getPagePerformance(url, 28)` — *page-level, 28-day*. On the tattoo winner that gap alone (0.56% vs 0.62%) reads as "improved" before a new title has done anything. `meta-optimizer` now records `baselinePageCtr` on the same basis, captured **before** the mutation, and `pickBaselineCtr()` prefers it while leaving pre-existing entries on the old basis rather than silently re-basing them; the report names which basis was used.

**Nothing about this opens a floodgate, and the arithmetic is worth keeping.** Of the 20 candidates currently clearing meta-optimizer's thresholds in `gsc-opportunity/latest.json`, **6 map to locked winners** — and all 6 were *already* being rewritten every week, because the guard was inert. Relative to actual behaviour the metadata change is a no-op; what genuinely changes is that body rewrites are blocked for the first time. The weekly cron is `--apply --limit 5`, which is the pacing. **4 of those 6 are toothpaste**, the one cluster ON HOLD, which is why `meta-optimizer` was added to `lib/cluster-hold.js`'s gated set in the same change — see the meta-optimizer paragraph in the cluster-hold section above for the before/after. Unlocking metadata on winners and gating the held cluster are two halves of one move: the first makes the tattoo winner improvable, the second is what actually lets the weekly budget reach it.

**The CTR program — and why the answer to a 0.47% blog CTR was NOT to run meta-optimizer harder (2026-08-24).** Blog CTR is **0.47% on 521,159 impressions / 90d** (2,501 clicks, 190 pages, window 2026-05-24 → 2026-08-21, aggregated from `data/snapshots/gsc/`). The flagship, `best-soap-for-tattoos-…-2`, sits at position 8.0 with 0.56% CTR on 37,531 impressions. The obvious move is to raise `--limit 5`. Measured, that is wrong on every count, and the three findings below are what `agents/ctr-program` and the guards in `lib/meta-ab-decision.js` exist to act on.

**1. Throughput is not the constraint — the population is 20 pages.** Impressions are extremely concentrated: the **top 10 of 190 pages hold 59.4%**, the top 20 hold 73.9%, the top 30 hold 82.4%. Excluding toothpaste, only **20 pages carry 4,000+ impressions/90d and they hold 73.8% of non-toothpaste blog impressions**. At `--limit 5` weekly that entire population is covered in **four weeks**, which is also the length of one measurement window — the cap and the 28-day conclusion cycle are already matched. Raising it exhausts the pool in two weeks and then spends the budget on pages where nothing is measurable. Historical throughput was never the cap's fault either: **18 tests in 5.5 months**, and the cap was first actually reached on 2026-08-24. **`--limit 5` is KEPT, on evidence.**

**2. The candidate ranking looks at the wrong object.** `sortByValidation` ranks **queries** (Amazon-validated first, then query impressions) and rewrites the **page** each query lands on. The flagship earns its 37,531 impressions across **666 distinct queries**; its single biggest is 1,045, **6.1% of the page**. A query's impressions are therefore a poor proxy for a page's opportunity, and two queries can select one page twice. But ranking by raw PAGE impressions is not the fix either: **toothpaste is 41.4% of blog impressions and 54.7% of blog clicks for 2.9% of revenue**, so impression-ranking hands ~41% of the budget to the least efficient cluster — the Prime Directive violation wearing a new hat. `lib/ctr-opportunity.js` ranks pages by **recoverable clicks × what the cluster earns**, reusing `lib/cluster-efficiency.js`'s ordinals; it DEMOTES and never drops, and on the real pool toothpaste takes 2 of 10 treatment slots rather than 4 of 5. Its position→CTR benchmark curve is a **RANKING INSTRUMENT, NOT A FORECAST** — every page on this site sits far below it, and the gap must never be quoted as recoverable revenue. Same doctrine as cluster-efficiency's "the ratio is an index, not a price".

**3. NOTHING MEASURED SO FAR IS READABLE, and this is the finding that decides whether the program is worth running.** Nine A/B tests have concluded. Replayed against `data/snapshots/gsc/`, **not one is attributable to the rewrite**:

- **No noise floor on the win side.** `delta > 0` meant `improved`, forever, on any margin. The dead-band guarded regressions only. **All five verdicts ever recorded as improved sit INSIDE that same 0.5pp band** (+0.38, +0.13, +0.10, +0.19, +0.25 pp) and three are on fewer than four clicks. The band is now symmetric — which breaks no existing test, because it only changes deltas in (0, 0.005), precisely the noise.
- **Corpus drift.** Blog-wide CTR went **0.166% → 0.203% → 0.282% → 0.433% → 0.502% → 0.505%** across six consecutive 28-day blocks ending 2026-08-21, with nobody touching most of those pages. The four tests concluded `improved` ran 2026-03-09 → 2026-06-14, straddling the steepest stretch — roughly **+0.27pp of free tailwind, larger than three of the four deltas it was credited for**. `controlDrift` now subtracts what the untouched corpus did over the same window (`gsc.getBlogPerformanceForRange`).
- **Position.** The 2026-07-27 `sls free toothpaste` test auto-reverted on a 0.46% → 0.17% fall. Over that window the page's average position went **13.2 → 27.6** and its impressions fell 9,934 → 3,462. CTR follows rank before it follows copy; `decideOutcome` fetched `currentPosition` and never looked at it. A page that moved beyond tolerance now returns **`confounded`** — not concluded, not reverted.
- **Power.** No impression floor existed. The 133-impression test ("tom's of maine toothpaste alternative", two clicks) was concluded `improved` on +0.38pp when nothing under **+4.2pp** is detectable at that sample.

**The treatment is as weak as the instrument.** On 2026-08-24 this agent rewrote `Best Soap for Tattoos: Clean, Gentle, Fragrance-Free` to `Best Soap for Tattoos: Gentle, Clean & Fragrance-Free` — **the same three adjectives reordered** — spending a live Shopify mutation, a tracker slot and 28 days of the store's only measurement capacity. Across the eight most recent rewrites, **none introduced a number, a year, a count or any new concrete specific**. Fixing measurement against a treatment that does not treat anything buys nothing, so `lib/ctr-copy-distinctness.js` gates the rewrite: material change (token-overlap ceiling) AND at least one new CTR-bearing element. One retry naming what is missing, then skip — the same policy and the same reasoning as the health gate, and counted against the same skip budget so "doesn't count against `--limit`" stays bounded.

**Two new outcomes, and neither concludes or reverts.** `confounded` (invalid measurement) and `underpowered` (insufficient measurement) leave the test OPEN with its variant live, stamping `lastCheckedAt` so a stuck test is visible rather than looking like one nobody reached. Precedence is **invalid before insufficient**: no sample size repairs a page that moved twenty places, so reporting that as `underpowered` sends an operator to fix the wrong thing. Acting on an unreadable measurement destroys good work exactly as eagerly as it undoes bad work — that is the 2026-07-27 revert, and it is a one-way door. `meta-ab-checker` also **refetches the baseline window on the page basis** (`gsc.getPagePerformanceForRange`) before comparing positions: `entry.baselinePosition` on the 13 pre-2026-08-24 entries is the KEYWORD's 90-day average while `perf.position` is the PAGE's over 28 days, and comparing those would manufacture `confounded` verdicts out of a basis mismatch — the same different-denominator error PR #630 removed from the CTR side. The CTR baseline is deliberately **not** re-based; `pickBaselineCtr`'s documented choice stands. Its report also stopped re-deriving buckets from `ctrDelta`, which was a second copy of the decision rule.

**The unit of measurement changes: cohort waves with a HOLDOUT, because per-page before/after has no control and, for 187 of 190 pages, no power.** At a +50% relative target over 28 days, exactly **three** pages clear the bar alone — `toothpaste-without-sls` (102,816 imps/90d at 0.79%), `best-soap-for-tattoos-…-2` (37,531 at 0.56%) and `best-toothpaste-without-sls-2025` (36,238 at 0.66%) — and two of those three clear it by under 10%, a knife edge an ordinary week of traffic variance erases. Those three keep the ordinary per-page path. Everything else is pooled: a **ten-page treatment cohort against a matched ten-page holdout** (100,817 vs 105,627 impressions, skew 2.3%) reads a 0.152pp move against a 0.232pp target — **53% of margin**, and measured at size 5 the same wave is UNDERPOWERED. The holdout lives through the same algorithm updates and the same corpus drift and is not rewritten, which answers drift and position at once. `lib/ctr-cohort.js` holds the assignment, the difference-in-differences and its verdict.

**`partitionByPower` exists because pooling a page that does not need pooling is harmful in BOTH directions.** `toothpaste-without-sls` is 19.7% of the entire blog and would be 51% of a ten-page arm: a cohort containing it is a single-page test wearing a cohort's name, its ranking moves swamp the DiD, and the page's own clean measurement is thrown away. Note the trap the power floor in `lib/ctr-power.js` closes: a page with no clicks has a near-zero baseline and the required-sample arithmetic collapses as p → 0, so without the floor a 0-click page reads as the easiest thing on the site to measure when it is the hardest.

**Cluster mix beats arm size in cohort assignment, and a pure greedy balance got this wrong on real data.** Sending each page to the lighter arm produced beautifully balanced impressions (6.0% skew) and split toothpaste **ONE against FOUR**, because the 102,816-impression page goes first and every later toothpaste page falls to the other arm. A difference-in-differences is robust to the arms sitting at different CTR LEVELS; what it cannot survive is different category MIXES, since then a trend in one category is indistinguishable from the treatment. Assignment now deals each cluster alternately, starting on the lighter arm — every cluster splits within one page, and arm-size imbalance only costs power the wave has to spare. Assignment is **deterministic, not random**: at n=20 a single bad draw puts the flagship and its near-duplicate in one arm and there is no second experiment to average it out.

**`agents/ctr-program` writes `data/reports/ctr-program/wave.json` and NEVER touches Shopify.** Every mutation still happens in `meta-optimizer`, behind the health gate, the distinctness gate and the A/B tracker. It runs Mondays 14:55 UTC, between `meta-ab-checker` (14:50) and `meta-optimizer` (15:00) — order is load-bearing in one direction: the optimizer reads `wave.json` as its **DO-NOT-TOUCH list** via `excludeHoldout`, applied to the pick list BEFORE the cap for the same reason the $0-cluster hold is, except that the cost of missing it is worse than a wasted slot. **Rewriting one holdout page does not degrade the measurement a little — it removes the control for the whole wave, unreconstructably.** It fails open (no wave file, an unreadable one, or an empty holdout → nothing withheld), which is right for "the planner has not run yet" and is not something to rely on. Matching is on the article **handle**, never the whole URL: the wave is built from GSC rows (www) and candidates resolve through the blog index, which has carried the myshopify host — comparing whole URLs matches nothing, and matching nothing looks exactly like a clean run.

**The honest accounting is in the report, not in a comment.** `npm run ctr-audit` re-decides every concluded test under the current rules and states how many survive as evidence. On the live tracker that number is **zero of nine**. Position is deliberately NOT fed into that retro-decision, for the basis reason above — the audit re-decides only on the dead-band and the power floor, which is what it can honestly compare. And "unchanged" is not "surviving": a `flat` verdict never counts, or a program that has learned nothing scores well for consistency.

**The revenue arithmetic, with its assumptions stated.** Blog CTR 0.47% → 0.9% across 561k impressions/90d is +2,400 clicks/quarter; at 0.82% CVR and $50.46 AOV that is **≈$1,000/quarter**. Every term is load-bearing and two are shaky. The CVR is a **site-wide** figure and `reference_order_level_attribution.md` puts **blog at 7% of revenue** on per-order `landing_site` — blog traffic converts far below site-wide, so the honest range is materially lower. And the toothpaste cluster, which is 41.4% of the impressions, converts at ≈$0: excluding it, the addressable pool is **305,510 impressions at 0.371%**, and the same lift there is ≈+1,600 clicks/quarter. Treat ≈$1,000/quarter as the **ceiling of a directional estimate**, not a forecast — the same caution `reference_seo_impact_cluster_revenue_understates.md` already demands of cluster revenue. This is why the program is sized to be **cheap and measurable** rather than large: 10 rewrites a wave, one wave per 28 days, at the existing cap.


**Hard-blocked posts resolve themselves.** `lib/blocked-posts.js` holds the one pure `classifyBlockedReport` rule, shared by the digest, the dashboard card and `agents/blocked-post-resolver`; it moved out of `agents/dashboard/lib/data-loader.js` (which drags in the whole dashboard path tree and is not importable from an agent, though it still re-exports the function so existing callers are unchanged). Two audiences, one rule, split by **`includeLive`**: the 5 AM email passes `false` — a live page is never an "Action Required" row, because the resolver owns fixing it and reports its own outcome into the same digest — while the dashboard and the resolver pass `true`. Explicitly-published and inferred-published posts now get **identical** treatment there; the old split (skip explicit `published` outright, let an unset status fall through to the freshness rule) is precisely what made the 52 legacy posts behave differently from the other 41. `agents/blocked-post-resolver` runs daily from `scheduler.js` right after `legacy-rebuilder`, capped at 5 posts (each is a chain of paid LLM calls). It does not reimplement repair: it schedules `scripts/remediate-live-post.js <slug> --push`, which already pulled the live body, gated it, repaired up to 3× and pushed only a PASSING revision — and was simply never on a timer. What it adds is what happens when that loop runs out: **soften, never delete.** It forces a `citation-finder` softening pass, re-gates, pushes if that cleared the gate, and otherwise clears `needs_rebuild` and leaves the page LIVE. It never calls `lib/post-kill.js` and never unpublishes — these are indexed pages that earn traffic. A written-off post stamps `meta.blocked_resolution.report_fingerprint` (a content hash of the editor report, **not** an mtime — file mtimes under `data/posts/` are pull/checkout times, not review times), so tomorrow's run does not pay to fail again on identical text while a genuinely new editor verdict re-opens it automatically. Two leaks that made a blocked post immortal are closed alongside it: `agents/legacy-rebuilder`'s `broken` bucket returned success **without** clearing `needs_rebuild`, so `findLegacyPosts()` re-queued the same post daily forever (the `winner` branch had always cleared it), and `agents/content-remediator`'s link-drop guard threw and gave up on the first sample — it now gets **exactly one** retry with the missing anchors named in the prompt (`lib/content-revision.js`), while the guard itself stays hard: a second sample that still drops a link is still never saved, and only the link-drop failure is retryable (retrying a `max_tokens` truncation against the same ceiling cannot succeed).

**Giveaway confirmation has TWO mechanisms and they disagree about every entrant at once.** `config/giveaway.json`'s `confirmMechanism` is the switch, and `lib/giveaway/reconcile.js`'s `resolveMechanism` is its only reader — an unrecognised value **throws** rather than defaulting, because a typo resolving to whichever branch happens to be the default silently rewrites the entry ladder for the whole promotion. Under **`double_opt_in`** (the launch behaviour, live until 2026-08-24) Klaviyo only adds a profile to the list once the opt-in link is clicked, so membership of the SUBSCRIBED set **is** the confirmation. Under **`flow_link`** the list is single opt-in, the entrant is subscribed the instant they submit the form, and a branded flow email carries the link instead (Klaviyo's `update_property_link`, writing `gv_confirmed`) — so subscription proves **nothing**, and reading it as confirmation pays the advertised +2 to every submitter *and* inflates every §5 referral rung with it (§5 pays "+5 per confirmed friend"). `p.subscribed` as a synonym for "confirmed" had been copied into six places; they all now go through `confirmedEmailSet`, and a seventh copy is a bug, not a shortcut. Two records are deliberately **mechanism-independent** — the `gv_confirmed_at` stamp and a legacy `gv_breakdown.confirmed` — because they are what carries an already-confirmed entrant across the cutover and what makes a rollback cost nobody their +2; the draw (`lib/giveaway/draw-snapshot.js`) keys off the stamp alone and needs no change either way. `gv_confirmed` is compared against the **string** `'true'` as well as the boolean: `update_property_link` takes its value as a quoted literal, so a `=== true` test passes every unit test written with a boolean fixture and rejects every real confirmation. Two traps worth naming: `scripts/giveaway/nudge-unconfirmed.mjs` **refuses to run** under `flow_link` (re-issuing a subscribe to a single opt-in list sends no email at all, so it would report "nudged 40" while sending zero and burn each profile's nudge cap), and the nurture flow triggers on **list-add**, which under `flow_link` means "submitted" rather than "confirmed" — it must be re-triggered off the confirmed segment first, or `01-confirm` opens by congratulating people who never clicked. Klaviyo's own double opt-in email is why the cutover exists at all: its subject line and button label **cannot be edited**, so the two elements that earn the click could never mention the prize.

**Dashboard.** `agents/dashboard/` is a Node app (PM2 process `seo-dashboard`, port 4242). Browser HTML/CSS/JS lives in `agents/dashboard/public/` — edited directly, no template literal escaping rules apply.

**Dashboard route contract — three rules, because a bad request used to kill every tab.** `agents/dashboard/lib/router.js`'s `dispatch()` calls handlers without awaiting them, and `seo-dashboard` is a single shared PM2 process on a public URL, so before hardening any uncaught throw or rejection was fatal to the whole process. Now: (1) `dispatch()` catches **both** a synchronous throw (try/catch around the call) and an async rejection (`.then(undefined, ...)` on whatever the handler returns) and answers a fixed `500 {"ok":false,"error":"internal error"}`, never echoing the exception — neither arm substitutes for the other, since the try/catch can't see a rejection and the `.then()` can't see a throw with no promise yet. That makes **returning** the promise load-bearing: a handler that fires an async call without `return`ing it hands `dispatch()` nothing to attach to. This was a live bug in `agents/dashboard/routes/agents.js`, whose handlers now `return ctx.runAgent(req, res)` / `return ctx.bgRunStart(req, res)`. (2) Every route module that reads a request body now reads it through `await readJsonBody(req, { maxBytes })` from `agents/dashboard/lib/responses.js` — `grep -rn "req.on('data'" agents/dashboard/` returns only that one file, everywhere else in the tree — and that includes `agents/dashboard/lib/run-agent.js`, not just files under `routes/`. The cap is per-call, not a shared constant: `DEFAULT_MAX_BYTES` is 1 MB for the authenticated routes, but the two unauthenticated ones keep their own smaller ceilings — `/api/rum` 8 KB, `/api/giveaway/*` 4 KB, the giveaway upload `MAX_UPLOAD_BASE64 + 2048` — because a shared 1 MB default would have silently raised the limit on the routes that most need one. `readJsonBody` also takes `destroyOnOverflow` (default `true`, which just preserves prior callers' behavior); `giveaway.js` passes `false` and defers the destroy to `res.on('finish', ...)`, because destroying the socket before the 413 flushes makes nginx serve the entrant a bare 502 instead — pinned by `tests/dashboard/giveaway-routes.test.js:182`. (3) `agents/dashboard/index.js` wires `agents/dashboard/lib/fatal-reporter.js` to `unhandledRejection`/`uncaughtException` as the last-resort net: it logs, calls `notify({ immediate: true, ... })`, and **keeps serving** — no exit, no restart. Its firing is a bug report, not a routine event, and it's hardened against its own failure modes (a rejecting `notify`, a hostile `err` object) because during this work the net itself found three separate ways to become the outage it exists to prevent. **Never do work in a callback whose promise the handler does not return.** `req.on('end')` is the clearest case — it runs on the emitter's stack, outside the handler's promise, where the `dispatch()` guard cannot reach it — but multer's callback-style API is the same shape: `ctx.upload.*(req, res, callback)` does its work inside `callback`, not inside whatever the route handler itself returns, so `dispatch()` has nothing to attach to there either. That unreachability, not `null` bodies, is why every body-reading route module was migrated onto `readJsonBody`. The three multer routes in `routes/creatives.js` are the exception to the single-reader rule, each guarded by its own multer error argument instead of `readJsonBody`: `/api/creatives/templates/from-image` (`ctx.upload.single('image')`, an `async` callback, reads `req.file`), `/api/creatives/reference-images` (`ctx.upload.single('image')`, a sync callback, reads `req.file`), and `/api/creatives/generate` (`ctx.upload.array('referenceImages', 20)`, an `async` callback, reads `req.body`/`req.files`) — all three carry the shared multer instance's 20 MB `fileSize` limit rather than `readJsonBody`'s byte cap.

**Shopify API version — one constant, because a stale pin does not fail, it lies.** `lib/shopify-api-version.js` holds `API_VERSION` for the whole fleet (currently **`2026-07`**, the current stable quarterly). Everything that builds an admin URL imports it: `lib/shopify.js`, `agents/technical-seo`, `agents/apply-optimization`, `agents/meta-ab-tracker`, and the ~20 standalone `scripts/*` that hand-roll their own `fetch`. It is deliberately a **separate module from `lib/shopify.js`** — that file reads `.env` and throws at import time without OAuth credentials, so scripts authenticating differently could not reach the constant through it; this one reads nothing and has no side effects. Requesting a **retired** version does not error: Shopify returns HTTP 200 and silently serves the oldest supported version instead, disclosing the substitution only in the `X-Shopify-API-Version` response header. That is how the fleet spent three quarterly releases believing it was on `2025-01` while actually running `2025-10` — nobody chose that, and nothing said so. Only a version that does not exist *yet* fails loudly (404). So every response through `lib/shopify.js` runs `checkServedApiVersion`, which **warns to stderr and fires one deferred `notify()` per process, and never throws**: fall-forward hands back a newer *supported* version, so the run is degraded rather than broken, and hard-failing would take the unattended 8 AM pipeline — and the collectors that write the only copy of snapshot history — down over a condition the response survived. **Do not pin the next quarter early.** The version one quarter ahead is the release candidate, published *three* months before it stabilises, and it can change without notice — which is the same bug wearing a newer number.

**REST is legacy, but not on a clock.** The Admin REST API has been legacy since 2024-10-01 and is in maintenance mode; the hard rule ("must use GraphQL") binds **new public apps only**, and this is a custom app, so every REST endpoint the fleet uses still works on `2026-07`. Two carry an explicit deprecation notice and return `X-Shopify-API-Deprecated-Reason` on a live 200 — **`/products*`** (deprecated as of REST 2024-04) and **`/products/{id}/images/*`** (2025-01). Those are the endpoints to migrate to GraphQL first if REST ever gets a removal date. `productByHandle` is likewise deprecated-not-removed in `2026-07`; its replacement is **`productByIdentifier`**, not `product(handle:)`.

**Ad creative pipeline — two agents, two jobs.** `agents/ad-studio/` produces the **ad base**: a text-free, icon-free PLATE with the product rendered in-scene at ad scale and position, plus a safe-zone guide SVG and the claim-gated copy as text. The operator sets type and icons onto it in Photoshop. A throwaway **comp** is derived from each Meta plate so a human can see the intent — it is a picture of what the ad could be, never the ad. `agents/creative-packager/` produces **placement crops from an already-approved master**; that job is unchanged.

**Why plate-first.** Ad Studio used to bake finished ads and gate them hard on text. Chasing 100% text accuracy from an image model was a losing game — most expensively on 9:16, where Meta draws UI over the top ~14% and bottom ~20%, every layoutBrief runs a headline to the frame edge, and the model fills the frame whatever the prompt says (measured: 6 of 6 attempts failed at 3 paid attempts each). Moving type to Photoshop dissolved that: a plate has no copy to misplace, so 9:16 renders first-attempt and the safe zone ships as `guide-<ratio>.svg` instead of as three failed renders.

**Where strictness lives now.** **TWO copy gates, different questions, neither substituting for the other.** `claims.js` asks *can this be traced to a source we hold?* — an unsourced factual claim stops the run before anything renders, no override. It holds four sources — `pdp`, `catalog`, `brandKit`, `reviews` — plus a fifth, `giveaway`, that exists ONLY while a giveaway's Entry Period is open. That fifth one is the plain text of the **published** Official Rules and nothing else: `lib/giveaway-claim-source.js` uses `config/giveaway.json` purely to cross-check that the entry-open and entry-close calendar dates literally appear in the rules, and **throws rather than picking one** when they disagree. Folding the config's dates into the searchable body instead would let a writer cite a deadline the published rules contradict *as sourced evidence* — the claim gate defeated through its own front door. Adding a source is never relaxing matching: a giveaway claim absent from the rules is rejected exactly as before, and outside the Entry Period `sourceId: "giveaway"` fails as an unknown source. `health-claims.js` asks *is a COSMETIC allowed to say it at all?* and is equally hard: no disease name, no drug or prescription reference, no heal/cure/treat/prevent, no clinical or FDA backing, in any zone of any format. A **verbatim customer review passes the first and can still fail the second** — that is exactly what happened on 2026-08-16, when a correctly-sourced Judge.me quote ("tried prescription strength lotions, steroids... to no avail. Until Real Skin Care") sailed through sourcing. "It came from a review" is not a defence: the FTC holds an advertiser responsible for claims an endorsement *conveys*, and the FDA treats marketing material including testimonials as evidence of intended use, which is what turns a cosmetic into an unapproved drug. Reviews carrying such language are also withheld from the copy writer up front (`selectQuotableReviews`), because detection without prevention burns retries. **The same withholding now covers `data/context/personas.json`**, which is copy input read by four agents: the generated 2026-07-27 file named steroids, prescriptions and eczema in every copy-facing field of persona `p1` — the top-ranked one, so the *default* angle — which meant the default creative brief was seeded with the language this gate exists to reject. `lib/voice-of-customer.js`'s `sanitizePersonas` removes a violating angle (or the whole persona, when its own name or summary is the violation) at BOTH ends: when `agents/voice-of-customer` writes the file, and again when `creative-packager`, `ad-studio` and `ad-brief` read it, because the file is regenerated monthly and a bad month must not reach a copy prompt. It removes, never rewrites — an LLM rewrite would be the agent inventing research — and `source_quotes` is never screened, because it is the evidence record and no consumer feeds it to a writer. **Product fidelity** (`verify.js`) is still a hard fail: the reference photographs go to the verifier and a bottle that contradicts them on shape, cap, label element order, printed graphics or base colour is rejected, because Photoshop cannot fix a wrong product. Photographic styling — lighting, gloss, shadow, crop — is explicitly never a mismatch; relaxing that rejects good renders at $0.13 a retry. **Stray text on a plate** is a hard fail too: you cannot remove pixels. Everything about the comp is advisory, including its 1-5 quality score, which feeds `run.json`'s `ranking[]` and the rolling baseline in `data/reports/ad-studio/scores.jsonl`. `ok: true` means checked, not good.

**The health-claim gate is not only an ad-copy gate — SEO copy is a second regulated surface.** On 2026-08-22 `agents/meta-optimizer` generated and published, live, the title `Best Soap for Tattoos: Clean Ingredients That Heal` and a meta description saying the soap "supports real healing". It did not import `health-claims.js` at all. A page title and meta description are the SERP snippet a cosmetic brand shows beside its own name — marketing material, and therefore evidence of intended use in exactly the way a testimonial is. **`lib/seo-copy-health-gate.js` is the gate for that surface**, and `agents/meta-optimizer/lib/gate.js` is its retry orchestration. It **imports `HEALTH_CLAIM_PATTERNS` from `agents/ad-studio/health-claims.js` and never re-declares a regex** — same single-source rule as `AWARENESS_LEVELS` — and only decides which of two tiers each hit lands in. It also matches every occurrence rather than calling `findHealthClaims`, which returns the first match per category: a string carrying "remedy" before "heals" would otherwise arrive as one hit whose match text is the harmless word.

**Why the ad gate is not simply reused whole, and how that was decided.** Measured before it was applied, per the 2026-08-18 vocabulary rule: across **892 real strings** (203 live article titles, 195 summaries, 37 `description_tag`, 17 `title_tag`, 19 product titles, 89 collection titles, 82 collection bodies, 42 page titles, plus every local `data/posts/*/meta.json` field and the `meta-ab-tracker.json` proposals) the unmodified ad gate flags 28 distinct strings, and **15 of them are the `toxicity` category** — "Toxic Chemicals In Soap To Keep An Eye On", "Zero Toxins", "free from harmful chemicals", "7 harmful ingredients hiding in natural toothpaste". None of those says the product treats anything; they are ingredient-avoidance editorial and absence-of-ingredient reassurance, which is this brand's central content position and the same generic reassurance the operator already ruled acceptable when "non-toxic" was deliberately unblocked. Blocking them would not merely lose a rewrite — a page whose ranking query IS "toxic chemicals in soap" cannot be given a title without the word and still match its own query, so **every** regeneration would trip and the page would leave CTR work permanently, silently, on a weekly unattended cron. So: **BLOCKING = the unapproved-drug categories** (`disease`, `drug`, `therapeutic`, `systemic-absorption`, `substantiation`) — all 17 blocking-tier hits in the measured corpus are genuine claims. **ADVISORY = the category-verdict ones** (`toxicity`, plus the therapeutic NOUNS `remedy`/`remedies`, whose three live hits all frame somebody else's material informationally) — reported in the run report, never blocking. The therapeutic **verbs** stay blocking, because a verb takes the product as its subject, and that is the whole line: *"the product does X"* is a claim, *"here is information about X"* is not. **Do not promote `toxicity` to blocking without re-measuring that blast radius.**

**"OVER-THE-COUNTER" IS THE ONE CONDITIONAL TIER, and it exists because two correct rules contradicted each other (2026-08-24).** `health-claims.js`'s `drug` pattern blocks `over-the-counter`/`OTC` outright, which is right for AD COPY and is **unchanged** — an ad has no legitimate reason to reach for a drug-status word, and `hasHealthClaim` / `assertNoHealthClaims` still refuse it, pinned by `tests/lib/regulatory-otc-phrasing.test.js`. But `drug` is in the SEO gate's blocking tier, so the phrase was also refused in generated titles and metas — on the pages that exist precisely to explain that **an antiperspirant is a regulated OTC drug while a deodorant is a cosmetic**, which is the same distinction `lib/product-category-terms.js` exists to keep accurate. The gate was refusing the accurate statement of the rule it enforces.

Measured read-only over **1,389 live strings** (204 articles, 19 products, 89 collections, 43 pages, 171 `title_tag`/`description_tag` metafields, 94 local `data/posts/*/meta.json`): **66 OTC occurrences, every one of them in `article.body_html`** — a surface this gate does not screen — and **21 of the 40 deodorant-cluster articles (52.5%) carry one**, against 7.9% of everything else. The recurring sentence is literally *"The FDA classifies antiperspirants as over-the-counter drugs, not cosmetics."* **So it had not yet FIRED on a live gated string; what it did was make the phrasing unreachable going forward** — and that pressure is real, not hypothetical: `data/briefs/best-aluminum-free-deodorant.json` instructs the writer verbatim that antiperspirant is "regulated as an OTC drug by the FDA", and `PRODUCT_CATEGORY_COMPLIANCE_RULE` (carried into **every** gated prompt by `SEO_COPY_COMPLIANCE_RULE`) tells the model it "MAY and SHOULD discuss antiperspirants as a CATEGORY". The gate instructed a behaviour and then refused its most accurate expression, with `seoCopyConstraint` naming the phrase as the offence — teaching the retry to delete the regulatory fact rather than fix a claim. Nine deodorant collections and one live deodorant PDP are standing targets of `collection-content-optimizer` and `product-optimizer`, both of which generate 450-650 words of exactly this prose behind this gate.

**The rule is the line drawn twice already: "our over-the-counter formula" BLOCKS; "Antiperspirants are regulated as over-the-counter drugs" does not.** The predicate is **`findBrandGovernedPhrases`, imported from `lib/product-category-terms.js` and not re-implemented** — Arm A's machinery generalised over the term (`buildBrandGovernedPatterns`), so the gap rule, `GAP_STOP_WORDS`, and the `PRODUCT_NOUNS` / `PHRASE_END_FOLLOWERS` whitelists carry over along with their measured 0-false-positive record. Tiering is by **span**, per occurrence, because one string can carry both shapes; `findSeoCopyClaims` uses `matchAll` rather than `match` for that reason alone. It is **not** a fourth `PRODUCT_CATEGORY_MISNOMERS` entry: that table's `correct` field is mandatory and feeds Arm B, the rewriter, and there is no accurate one-word substitute for "over-the-counter" — Arm B would have to invent one. Blast radius, re-measured after: **66 of 66 live occurrences demoted to the advisory `regulatory-reference` category, 0 still blocked, and every other category byte-identical** (drug 167 → 101, therapeutic 803, disease 696, substantiation 20, systemic-absorption 4 all unchanged). Against 17 hand-written brand-governed phrasings it misses **0**; against 18 category references and negations it fires **0**.

**Two misses are accepted on purpose and are pinned as tests.** `"our deodorant is an over-the-counter drug"` is not caught, because the determiner stop-word that makes the NEGATION (`"…is NOT an over-the-counter drug"`) safe cannot tell the two apart — and the negation is copy this brand genuinely writes and must never lose, while the positive is a sentence no writer produces. `"our over-the-counter drug"` is not caught, because `drug` is not in `PRODUCT_NOUNS` and adding it would flag *"Real Skin Care explains over-the-counter drugs"*. **Unknown-means-allow is the safety property, not a gap.**

**The rest of the `drug` vocabulary was measured the same way and deliberately left alone.** `prescription`/`prescribed` (30 live occurrences) stays fully blocking: a cosmetic positioned against a prescription drug is the classic unapproved-drug shape and is the exact language of the 2026-08-16 incident quote. `medicinal` (23), `medication` (17), `antifungal` (13), `antibiotic` (8), `medicated` (5), `pharmaceutical` (3) and `hydrocortisone` (2) name drugs or drug CLASSES rather than a regulatory STATUS, and only **2** of those occurrences sit on a gated surface at all (both in unpublished collection drafts). **`FDA`, `monograph`, `active ingredient` and `Drug Facts` do not bite** — bare `fda` is in no pattern (only `fda-approved`/`fda cleared`, under `substantiation`) and the other three are not in the vocabulary at all. Nothing was widened to make a point.

**PRODUCT-CATEGORY ACCURACY — a THIRD gate, and the only one whose question is "is this the right word for our product?" (`lib/product-category-terms.js`, 2026-08-24).** The health-claim gate could not have caught the defect it was built for a second time: "Our pick for travel size antiperspirant: Best Coconut Oil Deodorant" carries no disease, no drug reference and no therapeutic verb. It calls a cosmetic by an OTC drug **category** name, which is an intended-use claim with no claim vocabulary in it at all. Two live articles carried exactly that line inside the buy box, directly above an Add-to-Cart button (`travel-size-antiperspirant-…`, `aluminum-free-antiperspirant-…-2`) — the same shape as the 2026-08-22 CTA-heading find, and equally invisible to a title/meta scan.

**Two arms, failing in opposite directions on purpose.** **ARM A — `findProductCategoryMisnomers` — BLOCKS**, in three shapes, all of which require the BRAND to govern the phrase: **HEAD** ("our antiperspirant", "Real Skin Care's natural antiperspirant"), **ATTRIBUTIVE** ("our antiperspirant formula", "Real Skin Care antiperspirant stick") and **RECOMMENDATION** ("Our pick for travel size antiperspirant: <product>"). It is wired into `findSeoCopyClaims` as the blocking category `product-category`, so every writer already gated through `checkSeoCopyFields` inherits it for free, and `PRODUCT_CATEGORY_COMPLIANCE_RULE` rides in `SEO_COPY_COMPLIANCE_RULE` so the FIRST generation is told. **ARM B — `sanitizeProductCategoryTerm` — REWRITES and can never block**, for copy whose subject is definitionally our product. `agents/featured-product-injector`'s `buildCtaCopy` builds `Our pick for ${target_keyword}: ${product}` and now sanitizes the keyword, which is what makes the live fix stick: the line is GENERATED, so a hand fix would have been undone by the next injector run. It sanitizes rather than refusing because "travel size antiperspirant" is a real query those pages rank for, and refusing there would strip the buy box off a page that has traffic — by the Prime Directive a worse outcome than the inaccuracy.

**The first version of Arm A required the term to be the HEAD of the phrase, and that was too narrow — measured, 5 misses.** In "Real Skin Care's antiperspirant formula" the term is **attributive** (it modifies `formula`), so the head test skipped what is both an unambiguous product description and the exact phrasing an editor pass flagged as an ingredient-accuracy BLOCKER on `natural-antiperspirant`. `Real Skin Care antiperspirant` (brand token, no possessive) missed the same way. Three widenings, each with its own guard: `PRODUCT_NOUNS` (a whitelist of things we physically sell, so "our antiperspirant **guide**" stays a miss), `BRAND_BARE`, and `RECOMMENDATION_HEADS`. **`PHRASE_END_FOLLOWERS` and `PRODUCT_NOUNS` are both WHITELISTS, never blacklists of nouns** — an unrecognised follower means *not flagged*, so a word the file has never seen can only ever produce a miss, never a killed ranking page. Widen either and re-measure.

**MEASURED BEFORE IT SHIPPED, and RE-MEASURED after the widening, per the 2026-08-18 vocabulary rule: Arm A's false-positive rate is ZERO.** Run over all 539 live prose occurrences, all 102 repo files mentioning the word, and the five server-side post mirrors: **4 hits, all TRUE positives** — the two live buy-box lines and their two server mirrors. Against 17 hand-written bad phrasings it misses **0**; against 18 hand-written category references and negations it fires **0**. (The eight files *this change itself* authored are excluded from the denominator and measured separately — the rule's own examples, its test fixtures, the plan's literals and the CLAUDE.md note all quote the forbidden phrasings on purpose, and the rule recognising its own documentation is not a copy defect.)

**Four strings constrain the rule and are all pinned as regression tests.** Two are live and killed the obvious first attempt ("the word within N words of a brand token"): *"…in **our deep-dive on aluminum-free antiperspirant**: what it is and how it works"* and *"check **our natural deodorant vs antiperspirant guide**"* — in both the possessive's head is an ARTICLE we wrote, so `GAP_STOP_WORDS` (no preposition, conjunction, determiner or document-noun in the gap) plus `TERM_TAIL` (the term must end its phrase or modify a PRODUCT noun) kill them. The third was found BY the widening and is the sharpest: *"never call our product, our formula or our collection **an antiperspirant**"* — **the rule fired on the sentence that states the rule**, a negation inside `PRODUCT_CATEGORY_COMPLIANCE_RULE` itself. A determiner opens a new noun phrase exactly as a preposition does, so `a`/`an`/`the` joined `GAP_STOP_WORDS`, which makes every "our X is not an antiperspirant" safe. The fourth: *"**Our pick for antiperspirant quitters**"*, a recommendation whose object is a kind of person — `TERM_TAIL` applies to the recommendation arm too, which is exactly what separates it from the two live lines.

**Both arms cover the buy-box line, deliberately redundantly.** Arm B sanitizes the keyword so the generator cannot emit it; Arm A blocks the shape if any other caller builds it or if Arm B regresses. Note that reaching it needed the recommendation idioms specifically — in "Our pick for travel size antiperspirant", `Our` heads `pick` and `for` opens a new phrase, so simply allowing `for` into the gap re-acquires the "our deep-dive on" false positive. "Our pick for" takes a product as its object by construction; "our deep-dive on" takes a document. **The live corpus was cleaned by `scripts/remediate-antiperspirant-product-copy.js`** (dry by default, `--apply`, per-value backups, run record, occurrence counts asserted, six entries: two buy-box lines, one borderline buying-guide `<h2>`, and a paired local mirror for each). Its plan records `caughtBy: 'arm-a' | 'arm-b' | 'arm-a+arm-b' | 'judgement'` per entry, checked against the code by a test rather than trusted as a label — the four buy-box entries are `arm-a+arm-b`, and the `<h2>` is `judgement`, meaning **nothing prevents it recurring**. That is the one documented remaining miss, stated honestly rather than papered over by bending a rule until it produced the answer somebody wanted: it names no RSC product and carries no brand token, so reaching it would mean flagging the bare phrase — which is the 539-occurrence blanket ban this whole design exists to refuse. **All three mirrors are server-only and untracked**, so a local `--apply` fixes Shopify and leaves the box's `content.html` stale; `scheduler.js`'s daily link-repair republish would then push the old line back (a one-line diff scores ~0.99, far above `DIVERGENT_WARN_MAX`, so the content-mirror gate reads it as an ordinary edit and allows it). The script says so loudly and records `mirror_warning`; run it on the server too.

**Fail closed on the WRITE, not on the candidate.** A blocked first attempt costs a **regeneration with the offending words named in the prompt**, not the candidate — dropping it on the first hit would quietly delete CTR work from an unattended run, which is the same invisible-failure class as the bad write the gate exists to stop. Exactly one retry; a second failure skips the page, leaves it untouched, and is **counted and named in the report body** (which IS the digest body), on the normal deferred success path — a gate skip is the policy working, so never `immediate: true` and never `status: 'error'`. Gated candidates do **not** count against `--limit` (the cap is a budget for pages optimised) but carry their own equal cap, or "doesn't count" becomes an unbounded walk of the candidate list at two model calls each. `SEO_COPY_COMPLIANCE_RULE` is in the **first** prompt too, so most runs never reach the retry.

**The other three unattended writers were gated on 2026-08-24 — and the gate has two shapes, because two of them cannot regenerate.** No SEO-copy writer in the repo imported `health-claims.js` before 2026-08-23. `agents/product-optimizer` (daily: `--publish-approved`, `--pages-from-gsc`; also `--from-gsc`, `--optimize-titles`, `--expand-faq`, and the default `--apply` mode), `agents/collection-content-optimizer` (daily `--publish-approved`, plus `--queue`) and `agents/queue-autoapply` → `lib/queue-apply.js` (daily, cap 5) now all go through it. **The retry loop is `lib/seo-copy-gate-loop.js`'s `gateGeneratedCopy`, extracted from `agents/meta-optimizer/lib/gate.js` when the second caller appeared** — a hand-copied retry policy is a retry policy that drifts, the same rule that makes `lib/demand-questions.js` import `AWARENESS_LEVELS`. `meta-optimizer`'s gate is now a five-line wrapper holding nothing but its own field mapping.

**Named fields, not a `{title, meta}` shape, and that is a correctness fix not a tidy-up.** These agents write things that are not a SERP snippet: a live **product title** (`new_title`), a product `body_html`, a 450-650 word collection body, FAQ answers, a page `summary_html`. `checkSeoCopy` takes an OBJECT and **returns `ok: true` on a bare string** — there is no `.title` on a string to read — so forcing those onto two fields would have meant either mislabelling a product body as "meta" in the digest or handing the gate the wrong shape and collecting a silent free pass. `checkSeoCopyFields({ 'product title': …, body: … })` takes arbitrary named fields, HTML-stripped through `plainText` (tags become a SPACE, so `<li>pre</li><li>vent</li>` cannot be glued into a claim nobody wrote), and the field NAME is what appears in the report. `checkSeoCopy` is now that function's two-field special case. A test pins the bare-string trap so it stays visible.

**Where the gate actually bites, measured 2026-08-24 before it was wired, per the standing rule.** Across the live catalogue: **0 of 19 product titles, 0 of 89 collection titles, 0 of 42 page titles, 2 of 107 `title_tag`, 3 of 121 `description_tag`** carry blocking-tier language — and every one of those five is a genuine claim (`cut-and-scrape`'s "Wound"/"Heal", the tattoo-soap pair's "healing"/"healed", i.e. the 2026-08-22 incident itself). **Bodies are a different surface entirely: 32 of 82 live collection bodies, 1 of 19 product bodies, and 5 of the 10 collection bodies `collection-content-optimizer` has already generated** trip it — eczema, rosacea, dermatitis, "antifungal", "curing", "treatment", "prevention". So the retry on a body generation is load-bearing, not decorative, and roughly half of those runs should expect a second call. This is **not** the toxicity over-restriction the tier split exists to avoid: a collection whose ranking query is "unscented lotion" does not need the word "eczema" to match its own query, and RSC sells no eczema product and may not target one.

**Blast radius on the live production queue (94 files / 77 items, 2026-08-24): the gate empties nothing.** `queue-autoapply` still plans the same 5 applies; **0 of the 15 auto-apply-eligible pending items are newly skipped** (14 of them carry no inline SEO copy at all — a `seo-opportunity` runs an executor and a refresh points at an HTML file). **0** approved items exist for either `--publish-approved` drain today. Retrospectively, **9 of the 77 items ever queued** carry a blocking claim and would have been refused — including both tattoo-soap collection gaps, which are the pages the incident was about. The one live item that would be refused *if approved* is the pending `unscented-lotion` collection body ("eczema", "acne").

**`lib/queue-apply.js` is the case with no prompt, and the decision there is REFUSE, never dismiss.** It applies copy another agent generated, possibly days earlier; there is nothing to regenerate with. So `applyItem` calls `assertItemCopyIsCompliant` as its first statement and throws a typed `SeoCopyClaimError` — the write is refused and no Shopify call happens. It does **not** dismiss the item, and neither do the two `--publish-approved` drains, which stamp `health_gate` on the item and leave its status alone. A gate may decide copy cannot ship; it may not decide the work is worthless — that answer is what permanently destroyed three paid-for briefs on 2026-08-19 (see `data/briefs/_dropped/`). Re-running the producing agent regenerates it under the gate, and re-checking daily is free because the check is regexes, not a model call, so no attempt counter is needed to bound it. **`agents/queue-autoapply` gates in the PURE planner (`decide()` in `lib/queue-autoapply.js`), before the editor gate, before the repair loop and before the per-run cap** — same "gate before the cap" rule as `lib/cluster-hold.js`, because a gated item that reached the apply list would eat one of five daily slots and produce nothing. It is reported as its own `health_gated[]` row and its own digest block, never as `failed`, never `status: 'error'`, never `immediate: true`.

**Two things the queue gate deliberately does NOT cover, both because their blast radius is different and unmeasured.** File-backed article bodies (`refreshed_html_path`) stay with `lib/edit-gate-repair.js`: a refreshed blog post is *editorial*, not the product speaking, and the blocking vocabulary legitimately appears in it — a post about sensitive skin says "eczema" because that is what the reader typed. And `decide()` is pure, which is precisely what lets the gate run before any spend, so it reads no files; the collection body behind `proposed_html_path` is gated instead by its own producer's `--publish-approved`, the only thing that publishes it. Extending this gate over 1,500-word article bodies is a separate change with its own measurement.

**Still ungated, and each is its own review.** Not cron-driven: `agents/apply-optimization` (copy authored upstream by `agents/competitor-intelligence`), `agents/technical-seo --fix-meta`, `agents/collection-creator`, `agents/publisher` (writes `summary_html` from the post's LLM-authored `meta_description`), and `agents/meta-ab-tracker` (replays stored variants). `tests/agents/seo-copy-writers-gated.test.js` is a source scan — the agents cannot be imported without running them — pinning that each gated writer imports the gate, uses the shared loop, and carries `SEO_COPY_COMPLIANCE_RULE` in **every** prompt rather than only the retry.

**Editorial framing is the majority case and rewriting it is the expensive mistake.** 62 published-article headings trip the blocking tier and almost all of them stay: `Can I use unscented products if I have eczema?`, `Oily or Acne-Prone Skin`, `Signs of Infection — When to See a Doctor`, `Treat yourself and your skin to…` (an idiom). It re-gates every `after` through `checkSeoCopy` at run time (one failure aborts the run), backs the live value to `data/reports/health-claim-remediation/backups/<stamp>/` before writing, and **skips rather than overwrites when the live value matches neither `before` nor `after`** — that guard immediately earned itself, catching a trailing **U+00A0** in the vanilla excerpt that had been transcribed as a plain space; the plan now sources it as an explicit `\u00A0` escape and a test pins that no rewrite may silently normalise a non-ASCII character. **Prose claims are out of scope for this path** and belong to `scripts/remediate-live-post.js` (pull live → gate → repair → push).

**The six ingredient-benefit headings that script left as borderline were toned down the next day — `scripts/remediate-ingredient-benefit-headings.js`, 2026-08-23.** `Naturally Antibacterial and Antifungal`, `Supports Wound Healing`, `Fights Fungal Infections`, `Antifungal Support`, `As an Overnight Spot Treatment` and `Supports Skin Healing`, all `<h3>`s in `body_html`, all verified live (five articles; note the slug is `tea-tree-oil-soap-benefits-uses-what-to-look-for`, not `tea-tree-oil-soap`, which 404s). **"It's about an ingredient, not the product" is a weaker defence than it sounds**: FDA reads intended use from the whole page, and every one of these five carries a buy box a few hundred pixels below the heading — including the tea-tree posts, which sell the **Tea Tree Bar Soap variant of `/products/coconut-soap`**, so "RSC sells no tea tree product" is false. **Second generative passes: what the rule actually protects.** The rule was "never feed a render back in", from a probe where a second pass shifted ingredient photos one row against their captions — jojoba captioned as coconut oil. The real hazard is **image/label pairing drift on an artifact that ships**. The comp pass is allowed because it violates neither half: the plate it derives from is already verified and is not modified, the comp is throwaway, and a plate carries no icons or captions for anything to drift against. **`--formats` is required.** The default run is one format, one variation, all three Meta placements. Demand Gen plates are opt-in (`--targets all`). **`--flexible` builds one Meta 3-2-2 flexible ad** — 3 plates from 3 distinct formats at ONE ratio, plus 2 primary texts and 2 headlines from a second gated copy call — because twelve combinations sharing a single learning pool is what lets a $30/day campaign exit the learning phase at all (three ad sets ≈ 28 entries/week each, under Meta's ~50; consolidated ≈ 84). It refuses any argument set that would silently yield a different structure, and it writes a manifest only — **nothing in this repo creates, edits or launches anything on Meta.** The fleet's image-generation model IDs in `config/creative-models.js` are the **GA** releases, `gemini-3-pro-image` and `gemini-3.1-flash-image` — verified 2026-08-14. ## Data Layout Conventions

- `agents/<name>/index.js` — one agent per directory. Read the header docstring for usage.
- `lib/<name>.js` — flat namespace of shared libraries (Shopify, GSC, GA4, notify, posts helpers, retry, etc.). Subdir `lib/amazon/` for the SP-API client.
- `data/posts/<slug>/` — per-post intermediates: `content.html` (**the local MIRROR of the live article body — `agents/publisher` republishes from it, and 27 of 89 were a different article until `scripts/reconcile-content-mirrors.mjs`; see the mirror-gate note above before touching one, and reconcile with that script rather than by hand — a `backups/content-reconcile-<stamp>.html` is written before every overwrite**), `meta.json` (Shopify article IDs, target keyword, `needs_rebuild` flag, `shopify_status` — see the publish-state note above; `blocked_resolution` when `blocked-post-resolver` has written off a verdict; `legacy_locked` / `legacy_bucket` from `legacy-triage` — read the lock **only** through `lib/post-lock.js`, never by hand, and never from a flat `data/posts/<slug>.json`, which does not exist), `editor-report.md`, `answer-first.md`, `internal-links.md`, `backups/`, `content-refreshed.html` (queued). **`meta.json` is tracked in git AND rewritten by cron**, so a deploy that carries a change to one collides with the box — see "The stash dance is RETIRED" under Server Deployment, and `lib/post-meta-reconcile.js` for which of its ~56 fields the repo owns and which the server owns. **Every writer of this file MERGES onto what is already there** — `{ ...existing, ...changes }` or a read-modify-write — and a writer that rebuilds it from a fresh object literal is a bug; see the next bullet.
- **`meta.json` is written PRESERVE-BY-DEFAULT, and the list that governs it is a list of what an agent OWNS, never a list of what to KEEP.** Until 2026-08-24 `agents/blog-post-writer` built a **new** object on every redraft and carried the previous file across through an **allowlist of 11 keys**. Everything else was destroyed. Measured over the 94 local `meta.json` files: **91 of them would lose at least one field on a redraft, across 23 distinct fields** — `legacy_bucket`/`legacy_triage_reason`/`legacy_triaged_at` on 91 files, `shopify_image_url` on 40, **`legacy_locked` on 21** (a redraft silently unlocked a protected winner — the tattoo-soap winner is one of them), `indexing_state` on 20, `published_at` on 17, `indexing_submissions` on 10, plus `needs_rebuild`, `blocked_resolution`, `performance_review`, `shopify_status_verified_at`, the redirect record and the image record. The direction is the whole point: **an allowlist of what to keep has to be updated by everyone who adds a field, and loses data silently when they forget** — which is what happened, repeatedly, invisibly. A list of what the agent *owns* fails the other way, because the merge is `{ ...existing, ...authored }`: forget to update it and the worst case is a field nobody classified, never a field nobody kept.

  **The owned-field list is NOT hand-maintained as a third list.** It lives in `lib/post-meta-reconcile.js` as `AUTHORED_BY`, beside the `FIELD_OWNERS` table the deploy reconcile reads, and `authoredFields(agent)` **refuses a field `FIELD_OWNERS` has not classified** — so "this agent writes a new field" and "decide who owns it across a deploy" are forced to be the same edit, which is precisely the edit people forget. `composeAuthoredMeta(existing, authored, agent)` is the merge, and it throws on an authored key the agent has not declared. That throw is a static check over two frozen constants: it either always throws or never does, so it fires on the first local run after an edit and can never surprise a production morning.

  **`AUTHORED_BY` is a SECOND axis over that table and must not be collapsed into the first.** `FIELD_OWNERS` answers "in a deploy conflict, whose value wins"; "which fields does this agent produce" is a different question, and the two disagree in both directions — `post_type` is repo-owned and `blog-post-writer` never touches it, while `word_count`, `generated_at`, `brief_path` and `tokens_used` are server-owned (stamps and measurements, not content) and it rewrites all four every draft. Using the `repo` set as the owned set would clobber `post_type` and preserve a stale `word_count`. The table already carried this provenance in comments (`blog-post-writer:728`, `:732`–`:736`); `AUTHORED_BY` is that promoted to data so something can read it.

  **Nothing downstream relied on the old clearing.** `agents/editor` clears a stale `needs_rebuild` itself when a post reviews clean, and `agents/image-generator` regenerates and clears `image_blocked` whatever it finds — both run *after* the writer in the content pipeline. And an unreadable previous `meta.json` (conflict markers from a bad deploy is the realistic case) is now **warned about** instead of `catch {}`-swallowed, because it is the one case where the merge preserves nothing. **Audited 2026-08-24: `blog-post-writer` was the only wholesale rebuilder in the tree.** All ~30 other write sites already read-modify-write or spread the existing object; `lib/ensure-local-post.js` builds a fresh object but only for a post that has no local file at all.
- `data/snapshots/{gsc,ga4,clarity,shopify,google-ads}/YYYY-MM-DD.json` — daily metric snapshots. `data/snapshots/bing/` is the one exception to "one file, one day": Bing's API takes no date parameter and returns its whole ~6-month window on every call, so each file is a full history stamped with the day it was **collected**, written **weekly** from the Sunday block of `scheduler.js` (Bing's own data refreshes weekly — `GetQueryStats` returns 26 dates across 177 days, so a daily job would write six duplicate files). Its `coverage` block records that query/page rows are a weekly sample capped at 100 rows per date; those totals are deliberately far below the site totals and must never be reconciled against them. ## Code Review Checklist — any agent that writes LLM-generated post HTML

**These checks are `assertHtmlComplete` in `lib/html-output-guards.js`. Call it; do not hand-roll them.** They must throw (not warn) before the HTML is saved or pushed:

1. **`stop_reason === 'max_tokens'`** — output was cut at the token limit; the post is incomplete. **Throw, do not save.** The file will be truncated mid-tag and produce broken links when published.
2. **Unclosed `href` attribute** — regex `/href="[^"]*$/` on the HTML. Output truncated mid-link. **Throw, do not save.** Shopify auto-closes the broken tag into a malformed URL (e.g. `https://domain.com/blogs/news/best`) that 404s.
3. **Unclosed block tag** (`unclosedBlockTags`) — a `<p>`/`<li>`/`<h2>`… opened and never closed means the output was cut **mid-prose**, where checks 1 and 2 can both miss it: check 2 only fires when the cut lands inside an attribute, and check 1 only when the caller actually inspected `stop_reason`. This is the shape the tattoo-soap merge had. Validated against all 203 live articles — every one balanced, so a failure here is real.

All three must be fatal — truncated HTML on Shopify creates broken links that take a manual audit cycle to find, and a truncated *merge* silently overwrites a proven ranking page with a page that stops mid-sentence.

Current callers: `agents/blog-post-writer`, `agents/citation-finder`, `agents/content-remediator` (via `lib/content-revision.js`), `agents/cannibalization-resolver`, `agents/content-refresher`, `agents/technical-seo`, `agents/specificity-audit`, `agents/answer-first-rewriter`, `agents/collection-content-optimizer`. **An agent that generates article HTML and does not appear in that list is a gap, not an exemption.** Never substitute an LLM's own "the final section is complete" assertion for these — the editor made exactly that assertion about content cut off mid-word.

A 2026-08-22 audit of every local post intermediate and every Shopify article (269 files, 203 articles) found **3 truncated artifacts, none of them live**: the tattoo merge, a second merge from the same 2026-08-16 resolver run (`toothpaste-without-sls-…/content.html`), and a queued `content-refreshed.html` from 2026-07-06 — which is what put `content-refresher` on the list above; it ran at a flat `max_tokens: 8192` with no `stop_reason` check, the same defect. Live and draft Shopify articles were clean (0/203), so nothing truncated ever reached readers.

**The 2026-08-23 sweep of the remaining 13 HTML-generating agents settled which actually need this, and the answer was 4, not 13.** The distinction is *what the model returns*, not what the agent eventually writes:

- **Guarded** — the model's own output IS the HTML that ships: `technical-seo` (a collection-page rewrite that had **no truncation check of any kind**, not even `stop_reason` — the worst of the set, and collections are the pages that convert), `specificity-audit` (live product description), `answer-first-rewriter` (handed straight to cheerio's `replaceWith()` against a live article body), and `collection-content-optimizer` — where the guard is applied to the **`body_html` field**, not the raw response, because the response is a JSON envelope and a block-tag check against the envelope is meaningless.
- **Correctly NOT guarded** — the model returns something that is not HTML, and guarding it would throw falsely in production: `collection-creator`, `collection-linker`, `internal-linker`, `competitor-intelligence`, `product-optimizer` all return **JSON** (the `body_html` is assembled afterwards by deterministic code); `link-repair` returns **a URL**; `editor` returns **review prose**; and `faq-rewriter` returns **plain text** in a `QUESTION:/ANSWER:` envelope — the agent preserves the wrapping `<p>`/`<h2>` tags itself, so the model never emits a block tag.

The lesson worth keeping: "this agent writes `body_html`" is not the test. Read the call site and ask what the model returned.

Two of the JSON callers (`collection-linker`, `internal-linker`) silently `return []` when `JSON.parse` fails, so a truncated response there degrades to "no suggestions" rather than corrupt HTML — a real but different bug (silent degradation, not corruption), deliberately left alone.

## Product Imagery — two rules that have already cost real work

**Deleting a Shopify product image destroys the CDN file.** `DELETE /products/{id}/images/{id}.json` removes the underlying file, not just the product association — even for a `/files/` path. On 2026-08-12 three bundle photos were deleted from the foaming soap PDP and are unrecoverable (absent from the Files library, 404 on the CDN, no Wayback capture). **Download full-resolution to `data/archive/` before any destructive image call, and grep the repo first** — product images turn out to be embedded in live blog posts, Klaviyo flow scripts, and `assets/digital/image-map.json`. Prefer reordering or reattaching, which are reversible. A detached image can still return 200 for a while with its original `?v=` param; that is CDN lag, not survival.

**The theme scopes gallery images to variants two mutually exclusive ways.** In `sections/main-product.liquid`:

1. **Variant attachment** — set `hide_variants: true` on the template's `main` section. Attached media renders only for the selected variant; unattached media renders for all (this is how shared marketing frames work).
2. **alt-text `#` "gang" scoping** — images whose `alt` contains `#<token>`. **This path only runs when `hide_variants` is false.**

Before flipping `hide_variants` on any template, check whether that product's images carry `#` in their alt text. If they do it is already gang-scoped and the flag will break it. `scoped-gallery` and `bundle-landing` templates use the gang mechanism; the `landing-page-*` templates use variant attachment.

### The post-meta drift gate — `DAILY_POST_META_GATE`, 12:40 UTC, DETECT ONLY

`scripts/reconcile-post-metas.mjs --ref origin/main` is the deploy gate documented below. `scripts/check-post-meta-drift.mjs` is the same question **on a timer**, and it exists because that gate is only ever consulted by whoever happens to be deploying: between deploys the box can grow a field nobody has classified, or a `meta.json` that no longer parses, and neither is visible until a deploy trips over it at 2 AM. A parse failure is the worse of the two — every reader in the fleet `catch {}`s one and carries on as though the file were empty.

**It can never fix anything.** The arguments it passes are a frozen constant (`GATE_ARGS = ['--ref', 'origin/main', '--no-run-record']`), `--apply` is refused with exit 64, and no branch of it writes a `meta.json`. A reconcile applying on a timer would resolve the CONTESTED fields — `title`, `meta_description`, `target_keyword` — unattended, where the losing value is somebody's compliance fix or somebody's A/B-tested title. `scripts/triage-orphan-briefs.mjs --drop-non-earning` is what a scheduled write looks like when it is wrong: it ran on a fresh-but-wrong report and permanently destroyed three paid-for briefs.

How each exit code is reported, all through one **deferred** `notify()` — never `immediate: true`, and the wrapper always exits 0 so cron has nothing to say that the digest does not:

| exit | digest `status` | why |
|---|---|---|
| 0 in sync | `success` | routine |
| 1 diverged | `success` | **also routine** — cron rewrites these files all day, so the box is *expected* to be ahead of git. Reported quietly on purpose; a daily failure row for the normal state is how a digest stops being read. |
| 2 unclassified field | `error` | the case a human genuinely needs. Some agent started writing a field `FIELD_OWNERS` has never heard of, and the next deploy cannot resolve it. Renders in the digest's **Failures** block. |
| 3 will not parse | `error` | conflict markers or a truncated write, already on the box, silent today. |

`status: 'error'` on this fleet changes **rendering only** — it has never escalated to an email — so it is the right way to put something in the block a human reads without paging anyone.

Two details that are decisions, not accidents. It runs `git fetch --quiet origin` first (remote-tracking refs only — never HEAD, never the working tree), because comparing against a stale `origin/main` silently under-reports; the fetch's outcome is stated in the digest body rather than assumed. And it passes **`--no-run-record`**, a flag added to `reconcile-post-metas.mjs` for this caller alone: a deploy runs that script a handful of times and its `run-<id>/` record is the audit trail, but a daily cron would leave one every morning forever, and this box has already lost four days of cron to a full disk. The detector writes no `meta.json`, so there is no decision to audit — its human report goes to the cron log on stdout. The flag is **ignored when `--apply` is set**: a run that wrote files always leaves the record naming every value that lost.

**12:40 UTC, and the hour is chosen against two UTC landmarks rather than a Pacific wall-clock time**, so DST cannot walk it out from between them: 20 minutes before the 13:00 UTC `daily-summary`, so the row lands in the **same** morning's digest instead of tomorrow's; and ~2h20m before the 15:00 UTC scheduler, so it reports a settled tree rather than one mid-pipeline. Per the UTC-only rule above it carries **no `TZ=` prefix** — one would schedule nothing — and `tests/scripts/post-meta-drift-gate.test.js` now pins that *no* line in `setup-cron.sh` carries one, so the five that were stripped on 2026-08-23 cannot creep back.

### The content-mirror drift gate — `DAILY_CONTENT_MIRROR_GATE`, 12:20 UTC, DETECT ONLY

`scripts/check-content-mirrors.mjs` is the read-only mirror check. `scripts/check-content-mirror-drift.mjs` is the same question **on a timer**, routed into the 5 AM digest, because a detector nobody runs is not a detector: the 27 different-article mirrors found on 2026-08-23 had been wrong since an April bulk import and nothing anywhere said so for four months.

**It may never resync, and that is a structural guarantee rather than a promise.** `scripts/reconcile-content-mirrors.mjs` exists and is `--apply`-gated, so this cron line is one careless edit away from a nightly overwrite of `content.html` — which would eventually fire inside the minutes when `agents/refresh-runner` legitimately has a paid LLM rewrite sitting in that file. So `GATE_ARGS` is frozen, `--apply` and `--snapshot-live` are refused with exit 64, and a test counts the child processes to pin that the one thing this file spawns is the read-only check. (The reconciler IS named in the digest body — a human reading the row needs to be told where to go — which is why the test counts spawns rather than scanning for the string.)

**`GATE_ARGS` is the empty list, and that is the `--no-run-record` decision.** `check-post-meta-drift.mjs` passes that flag so a daily cron does not leave a `run-<id>/` directory every morning forever on a box that has already lost four days of cron to a full disk. Here the check writes nothing at all *unless* given `--snapshot-live --apply`, which would drop ~80 full live article bodies into `data/reports/content-mirror/` daily. Adding a flag is the hazard; the frozen empty list is the fix. The human report still reaches the cron log on stdout.

| exit | digest `status` | why |
|---|---|---|
| 0 every mirror matches | `success` | routine |
| 1 deep divergence (0.25–0.75 band) | `success` | the operator's decision on 2026-08-24 was to leave that threshold **advisory** rather than promote it to a refusal. A daily failure row for a band we have deliberately chosen not to block on is how a digest stops being read; the posts are still named in the body. |
| 2 a different article | `error` | `agents/publisher` already refuses to republish it, so the live page is safe — what is broken is that the post can never be republished at all until somebody reconciles the mirror. Renders in the **Failures** block. |
| 3 a post could not be read | `error` | silent otherwise: every reader `catch {}`s a read failure and carries on as though the file were empty. |

One **deferred** `notify()`, never `immediate: true`, and the wrapper always exits 0 so cron has nothing to say the digest does not — the single exception is refusing a write flag, which is a usage error rather than a finding.

**12:20 UTC, expressed in UTC because a `TZ=` prefix schedules NOTHING on this host.** The hour sits between three UTC landmarks, none of them a Pacific wall-clock time, so DST cannot walk it out from between them: 40 minutes before the 13:00 UTC `daily-summary`, so the row lands in the **same** morning's digest; 20 minutes before the 12:40 UTC post-meta gate, so the two cheap detectors do not share a slot; and ~2h40m before the 15:00 UTC scheduler, **whose link-repair step is the very republish this drift endangers** — reporting before it runs is the point. Cost is one `getBlogs` + one `getArticles` per blog (~204 articles) and a local pass, all GETs.

Both inconsistencies found while building this mirror are now **resolved**, and both turned out to be a doc that was wrong rather than a schedule that was:

- **`unmapped-query-promoter` — cutover finished 2026-08-23.** Its header claimed `DEPRECATED (2026-06) … no longer scheduled` while the live crontab ran it daily at `45 13 * * *`, fifteen minutes before `pipeline-prioritizer`, both writing to the same content calendar. The header was corrected first, then the schedule was retired on evidence: the prioritizer reads the **same** `gsc-opportunity.unmapped[]` feed at the **same** 500-impression floor, and the promoter's final runs qualified **zero** items (18 under the floor, 5 already covered). Retiring it removed a daily no-op, not a capability. The agent file is kept and still runs by hand.
- **`competitor-watcher` — header corrected 2026-08-23.** It runs `0 2 * * 1` (02:00 UTC Monday) while its header claimed "weekly Sun 7:00 PM PT" — true only during PDT, silently 6 PM PT once PST resumes. **The tempting fix, a `TZ=` prefix, does not work on this host** (see the UTC-only note above), so the header now states the UTC contract and names the DST drift instead of pretending it away. A weekly competitor crawl does not care which hour it lands on.

### Deploy hygiene — backfills run AFTER `git pull`, never before

The `data/` tree contains tracked files (e.g. `data/posts/<slug>/meta.json`) that get modified in-place by backfill scripts. If you `git stash push` before pulling, those uncommitted updates land in stash and get forgotten. Run any data backfill **after** the pull — scripts are idempotent so re-running on top of fresh code is safe.

### The stash dance is RETIRED for cron-written tracked files — it produced invalid JSON on production twice in one day

This section used to say: if `git pull` fails on a dirty tree, `git stash push` → `git pull` → `git stash pop`, and resolve by hand if the pop conflicts. **On 2026-08-23 that procedure corrupted production data twice.**

- **Incident 1 (PR #629, `data/rejected-keywords.json`)** — the pop conflicted and left **20 conflict markers** inside a tracked JSON file that nine agents parse. Resolved by hand.
- **Incident 2 (PR #634, five `data/posts/*/meta.json`)** — `git pull` aborted, the five files were stashed, pulled, popped; **all five conflicted, all five were invalid JSON on the production box.** And "resolve by hand" could not mean taking a side: HEAD was missing `indexing_state`, `indexing_submissions`, `published_at` and `shopify_status: published` — **including a backfill that had run hours earlier** — because the committed copies are stale by construction. Taking git's side reverts production; taking the box's side reverts the compliance fix the deploy existed to ship. It had to be resolved field by field.

**Why it fails, plainly:** `git stash pop` runs a three-way **text** merge, and git's merge driver for `.json` is the line-based default. Two sides that edited nearby lines of the same pretty-printed object produce `<<<<<<<` markers *inside the JSON*. The file is then unparseable to every reader on the box — including the cron jobs that run before anyone notices, several of which `catch {}` a parse failure and carry on as though the data were empty. The stash is not the bug. **Running a text merge over a machine-written data file is the bug**, and no amount of care in resolving the conflict prevents it, because the corruption happens before you see it.

So: **never let git text-merge `data/posts/*/meta.json`.** Take the pull cleanly, then merge semantically.

```bash
# 0. GATE — before anything, does the box diverge from what is about to land?
ssh root@137.184.119.230 'cd ~/seo-claude && git fetch origin && node scripts/reconcile-post-metas.mjs --ref origin/main'
#    exit 0 = clean, just pull. exit 1 = divergence, use the sequence below.
#    exit 2 = a field changed on both sides with no owner — classify it first.
#    exit 3 = something on the box is already unparseable — fix that by hand first.

# 1-4. The safe deploy, when step 0 said 1. `SNAP` is resolved on the box, so the
#      snapshot path never has to be copied back and forth by hand.
ssh root@137.184.119.230 'cd ~/seo-claude \
  && SNAP=$(node scripts/reconcile-post-metas.mjs --snapshot | sed -n "s/.*→ //p") \
  && echo "snapshot: $SNAP" \
  && git checkout -- "data/posts/*/meta.json" \
  && git pull \
  && node scripts/reconcile-post-metas.mjs --against "$SNAP" --apply; \
  pm2 restart seo-dashboard'
```

**The quotes around the pathspec are load-bearing.** Unquoted, `data/posts/*/meta.json` is expanded by the SHELL against the filesystem, which includes the ~110 post directories whose `meta.json` is UNTRACKED — git then errors `pathspec ... did not match any file(s) known to git` on the first one, and because the chain is `&&`, **the pull never runs**. That failure is safe (nothing is modified) but silent-looking: it scrolls a hundred error lines and leaves the box on old code. Quoted, git does the matching itself against tracked files only. Verified 2026-08-24.

Two details in that chain are deliberate. The reconcile is joined with `;` rather than `&&`, because it exits **2** on an unclassified conflict *after* writing a correct merge — a condition that needs a human to classify a field, not a box left unrestarted on old code. And `git checkout --` names `data/posts/*/meta.json` specifically, not `data/posts`: `content.html`, `editor-report.md` and the rest of the per-post tree are not covered by this reconcile and must not be discarded by it. If the pull still aborts after step 2, something *other* than a meta.json is dirty — deal with that file on its own merits; do not widen the checkout to make the error go away.

`--snapshot` copies every working-tree `meta.json` plus a manifest recording the **pre-pull SHA**, which becomes the merge base. That base is what makes this a real 3-way merge: a field only one side moved simply takes that side, and ownership is consulted **only** where both sides genuinely moved. `git checkout --` is then safe *because the snapshot exists* — never run it without one.

`scripts/reconcile-post-metas.mjs` is dry by default, backs up every file before writing, validates the serialized JSON and re-reads it from disk before renaming into place, and **refuses rather than writing a broken file**. It is idempotent (a second run exits 0 and writes nothing) and safe both before and after a pull. Run record with every field-level decision — including the value that lost — lands in `data/reports/post-meta-reconcile/`, which is **gitignored on purpose**: a tracked run record written by the deploy would recreate this exact hazard.

**Who owns which field** is in `FIELD_OWNERS` in `lib/post-meta-reconcile.js`, derived from an inventory of all 27 writers of a meta.json field (2026-08-23), not from memory. The rule is not "who runs on the server" — the authoring pipeline runs there too:

- **Repo owns what a human authors and a deploy exists to ship**: `slug`, `title`, `meta_description`, `target_keyword`, `tags`, `post_type`.
- **Server owns what a machine observes or stamps**: Shopify identity and publish state, `indexing_*`, `legacy_*`, refresh/rebuild stamps, `needs_rebuild`, image-generation records, `word_count`/`generated_at`/`brief_path`/`tokens_used`. `brief_path` settles the principle — its real values are `/root/seo-claude/data/briefs/…` on the server and `/Users/seanfillmore/…` locally. It is a fact about the box, not content. **`republished_at` and `republish_reason` joined this list on 2026-08-23**, and how they got there is the useful part: they entered the corpus through commit `dddd0e74` (the `best-boka-alternatives-2025` silent-draft-drift remediation), and the dated one-off script in that commit calls `updateArticle` and **nothing else** — the meta.json stamp was hand-written in the same commit, so there is no code writer anywhere. That is why PR #646 could not find one, and it is **not** a reason to leave a field unclassified: `unpublished_at`/`unpublished_reason` have no writer either and were already server-owned. **What decides ownership is what the field records, not who writes it.** These record an observation about the live world (the article was found unpublished) and the action taken against it — machine state, never authored copy — so a deploy carrying an older `meta.json` must not delete an incident's audit trail while the incident itself stays fixed on Shopify.
- **`title`, `meta_description` and `target_keyword` are CONTESTED** and the table says so: they are authored in git *and* rewritten unattended by `agents/editor` (stale-year bump), `agents/meta-optimizer` (daily stale-year pass, weekly CTR rewrite) and `agents/cannibalization-resolver`. Repo still wins — that is how a compliance fix reaches the site, and the live value already sits on Shopify regardless — but the conflict is always printed with both values instead of resolving quietly.
- **A field in neither list is `unclassified`.** One-sided changes still merge normally. If both sides moved it, the merge keeps the live box's copy (the one a pull would destroy) and **exits 2** so somebody classifies it. Nothing resolves silently, and no field is ever dropped: every key either side held is in the merged file, and every value that lost an arbitration is in the run JSON. **You should not first learn about an unclassified field mid-deploy** — `DAILY_POST_META_GATE` runs this same check every morning at 12:40 UTC and puts exit 2 (and exit 3) in the digest's Failures block; see "The post-meta drift gate" above.

**These rules survive unchanged:** `git stash pop` is still non-negotiable *if* you stashed — never leave a stash dangling, `git stash list` should be empty after a deploy. **Never** `git stash --include-untracked` on the server — it destroys ALL untracked data files (queued performance items, draft posts, generated reports), not just conflicting ones; delete specific files instead. **Never** `git reset --hard` with a dirty tree.

**The reconcile is a patch over a design problem, and should not be mistaken for the fix.** `data/posts/<slug>/meta.json` mixes authored copy (6 fields) with machine state (50+), in one file that is both tracked in git and rewritten by cron. The durable fix is to split them — authored copy stays tracked, machine state moves to a gitignored, server-authoritative sibling (e.g. `data/posts/<slug>/state.json`), after which a deploy can never collide at all. That is a ~200-file migration touching all 27 writers and every reader of `getPostMeta`, and it is deliberately **not** in this change. Gitignoring the current file instead is the wrong shortcut: it would strand the authored copy, which is real content that must be reviewable in a PR.

### `data/rejected-keywords.json` — MERGE it, never pick a side

A tracked file production writes on its own — one of two, the other being `data/posts/*/meta.json` (see the deploy-hygiene section above; that one needs a per-field merge, this one a list union). Audited 2026-08-23: **39 entries on the server, 2 committed** — last commit `6c89b2f7`, **2026-04-08**, so 37 entries existed nowhere but the production box. Four writers put them there: 18 from `agents/content-strategist`'s `appendRejection` (an off-scope proposal auto-rejects itself), 8 from `scripts/consolidate-tattoo-soap-calendar-2026-08-23.mjs`, 5 from `lib/post-kill.js`, 5 from the dashboard Ideas inbox, 1 from the dashboard DataForSEO route, 2 hand-authored.

**Nothing has been lost yet, and that is a verified fact rather than an assumption.** The 18 `content-strategist:product-scope` entries match the 18 `[SKIP] Off product scope` lines in `data/reports/scheduler/scheduler.log` one-for-one; the server's reflog (back to 2026-05-10) holds only fast-forward pulls and mixed resets, never `--hard`; and both committed keywords are still present. It survives only because **no commit has touched the file since April**, so `git pull` has never had to reconcile it — and because a broad `git add` left it (and 175 other paths) staged but uncommitted on the server. The hazard is live and un-fired, not absent.

The destructive outcome is a deploy reverting those 37 and the strategist re-proposing keywords Sean already rejected — each one a full paid research + writing pipeline. So the answer is **merge**, not gitignore (it records human decisions and cannot be regenerated) and not "commit it back sometimes" (that is what did not happen for four months):

```bash
# after `git pull`, never before — same rule as any other data backfill
node scripts/reconcile-rejected-keywords.mjs                 # report; exit 1 if the two sides diverge
node scripts/reconcile-rejected-keywords.mjs --apply         # write the UNION to the working tree
node scripts/reconcile-rejected-keywords.mjs --against <file> [--apply]   # merge a copy pulled from the server
```

It never drops an entry either side holds: `lib/rejected-keywords.js`'s `mergeRejections` is order-independent by construction (whichever side "wins" is the question that loses data), keeps the **earliest** `rejected_at` and the **broadest** `matchType` on a duplicate — widening blocks more, narrowing lets a keyword back through, and a reconcile must never be the thing that lets one through. The server's 37 were merged into git in this change, so the drift is currently zero.

**All four writers now go through `lib/rejected-keywords.js`'s `appendRejection`, which re-reads before writing.** Each used to do `readFileSync → push → writeFileSync` with no lock and no re-read, and two of them run concurrently on the same box — `content-strategist` from the 15:00 UTC cron while the long-lived PM2 `seo-dashboard` process writes from two routes — so whichever finished second silently erased the other's entry. The write is a temp file + `rename`, because a crash mid-write leaves a truncated array that every reader's `catch { return [] }` reads as "nothing is rejected". Two writer-specific bugs died with the consolidation: the Ideas route deduped **case-sensitively**, and the DataForSEO route had **no dedupe at all** and stamped `rejectedAt`, a camelCase spelling none of the nine readers consult.

**What this deliberately does not touch: the matching.** Nine agents hand-roll `isRejected` with three different semantics for `matchType: 'exact'` (raw string / slug-normalized / exact-only-ignoring-`matchType` in `lib/brief-triage.js`), a divergence pinned by `tests/agents/rejected-keywords.test.js`. Unifying them changes which keywords are blocked fleet-wide, and — through `scripts/triage-orphan-briefs.mjs`, where the rejected list is what makes an orphan brief droppable — cuts in the destructive direction. It belongs in its own change. The module governs the FILE, not the rule.
